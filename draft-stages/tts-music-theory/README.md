## Course approach

The first twelve lectures move from musical pattern, pitch and rhythm through harmonic and contrapuntal construction, phrase/form analysis, modal center, chromatic harmony, polyrhythms and revision of a sixteen-bar study. The next four lectures extend that foundation into changes of tonal home, **carefully scoped** cross-cultural comparison, timbre and production, and a complete 48-bar two-line study. Lectures 017000–020000 then explain PCM audio, DAW routing and scheduling, the musical problem of combining independent stems, the differences between offline DawDreamer processing and Ardour live mixing, and the decisions involved in mastering and delivery. Lectures 021000–024000 deepen the distinction between MIDI events and actual sound, work through filters and dynamics quantitatively, explain phase and stereo imaging, and give an end-to-end reproducible workflow for multi-agent music production. The final lectures intentionally have the length needed for their subject rather than a runtime target. Lectures 025000–028000 add capture and monitoring physics, the mathematical and routing models of room effects, spectrogram limitations, and source-grounded mix assessment. Lectures 029000–032000 develop hearing physiology and pitch, energetic and informational masking, perceptual stream formation, spatial localization, and controlled listening. Their reading maps distinguish primary experimental evidence from author-designed DAW exercises. Lectures 033000–036000 connect those ideas to ear-training, tuning calculations and tempered compromises, two-voice counterpoint, and a complete original eight-bar transcription/revision cycle. The companion listening lab can render 18 original comparisons as WAVs locally; no WAVs are shipped in the source pack. Lecture 037000 compares eight original eight-bar developments of one motif: dialogue, sentence-inspired continuation, sequence, transformation, ostinato layering, octave canon, additive process, and contrast-and-return. Its separate text-only demonstrator provides event-level validation and local audio rendering; those teaching examples are not presented as cultural performance replicas or empirical rankings. [Development listening lab](examples/README-development-037000.md). Lecture 038000 extends the sound-grouping discussion into orchestration decisions: foreground and support, unison doubling, register competition, staged entrances, and timbral handoffs. Six original eight-bar studies keep a common melody and bass reference and use a [local orchestration lab](examples/README-orchestration-038000.md) to validate note events and generate reproducible optional WAVs; those are synthetic demonstrations, not acoustic instrument emulations.

Lecture 039000 extends the imitative forms in 035000 into a historically scoped study of the tonal fugue. It distinguishes real and tonal answers, full exposition, recurring versus free accompanying counterpoint, episodes, invertible counterpoint and stretto. The [original fugue laboratory](examples/README-fugue-039000.md) has nine annotated listening renderings including a 28-bar three-voice miniature, and is explicitly not presented as Bach transcription or historical-style certification.

Examples in `examples/` contain earlier score grids and the original WAV-rendering lab for 033000–036000; all are optional machine-readable companions. There is no claim that written pitch grids capture the intonation, ornamentation, idiom, or artistic effect of every musical practice. For traditions best transmitted through performance and listening, further training and direct encounters with sound remain essential.

5. Analyze additional forms and styles, including non-major/minor systems, with genuine performance evidence and careful attribution.

## Detailed attribution practice for 025000–038000

Lectures 025000–039000 each contain a **source-to-claim reading map** with direct links and section-level explanations, not just a generic bibliography. Their metadata sidecars have a `claim_evidence` array recording a verbatim narration anchor, source IDs, why the source is relevant, and provenance. An `original_calculation`, `original_example`, or `editorial_inference` is *explicitly not* represented as an experimental result in the cited document. The sources are primarily official manuals, standards, university books, and peer-reviewed research. This is a stronger, local attribution contract; it does not automatically certify every editorial judgment or imply that an online source contains a particular illustrative score.

## Reference and integrity conventions

- `track.json` lists **only existing** numbered manuscripts; each has one spoken marker pair and a corresponding `metadata/<id>.json` citation sidecar.
- `metadata/sources.json` records per-track references, including references to musical traditions by specialist sources rather than treating the original classroom exercises as transcriptions.
- Do not embed copyrighted music or binary audio in overlay ZIPs. The local renderer for lectures 033000–036000 uses only original scores and standard-library synthesis; any other renderer must respect source rights and record configuration.
