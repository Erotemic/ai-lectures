# 20th-Century Literature: Story, Then Analysis

The track currently covers **thirty-three literary works** (one receives extended multi-lecture treatment). For each work, complete plot narration precedes criticism: usually one continuous story lecture, with extended multi-volume treatment where the work itself warrants it. The story account introduces the people, setting, main events, and ending; it does not require that the listener has read the book. The analysis can then explore form, historical context, and competing critical readings without explaining the plot again.

## Science fiction as literature: Sagan and Vonnegut

- `018200` then `019000`: Carl Sagan, *Contact* (1985): a full story orientation to the **novel** (five international travelers, the base-eleven pi ending), followed by analysis of scientific evidence, witness testimony, science and religion, public institutions, and communication. Do not substitute the 1997 film plot.
- `019200` then `020000`: Kurt Vonnegut, *Slaughterhouse-Five* (1969): the authorial frame, Dresden, Billy Pilgrim's life and reported Tralfamadorian encounters, followed by analysis of traumatic narrative time, fatalism, black comedy, and historical memory. Do not interpret fictional time travel as established physics or repeat the novel's inflated Dresden death toll as a fact.

The two works offer contrasting science-fiction methodologies: a speculative scientific-process novel and a fragmented anti-war novel. Story and analysis remain separate. Each recording is as long as the material needs; length metadata are measurements only.

## Mainstream twentieth-century landmarks: Fitzgerald, Orwell, Herbert

The next three complete pairs deliberately strengthen mainstream and genre-defining coverage without undoing earlier geographic and formal breadth:

- `020200` then `021000`: F. Scott Fitzgerald, *The Great Gatsby* (1925). The story follows Nick's account from his arrival at West Egg to the deaths of Myrtle, George and Gatsby and the nearly empty funeral. The analysis examines time, narrative mediation, wealth, class impunity, racism and antisemitic stereotypes in the narrated world, and the contradictions in Gatsby's dream.
- `021200` then `022000`: George Orwell, *Nineteen Eighty-Four* (1949). The story follows Winston's Ministry work, private record, relationship with Julia, the trap, torture and final capitulation. The analysis distinguishes evidence from command, explores historical roots and language, and treats the appendix's historical tense as debatable rather than proof of an offstage revolution.
- `022200` then `023000`: Frank Herbert, *Dune* (1965). The single story lecture covers **the first novel**, not its film versions or sequels, from the imperial trap through Paul and Jessica's Fremen lives, the spice monopoly and Paul's victory with its unresolved religious consequences. The analysis treats ecology, political economy, Bene Gesserit engineering, Indigenous and Islamic resonances, narrative perspective and the danger of a messianic leader.

In each case, narration is a complete spoiler-containing account before interpretation. There is no runtime or word quota: the plots determine how much explanation and pacing they require. Later books and adaptations are not silently imported into first-novel summaries. The pack is wholly within Agent A's authoring boundary.

## Editorial principles

- No target, minimum, or maximum duration. One complete story can be short or long, according to its needs; do not split to hit a time box.
- Story orientation before analysis, including spoilers and an intelligible ending. Do not assume the listener knows character names or context.
- Original expository narration, not a narrated copy of copyrighted prose. Preserve the identity of the plot and avoid invented dialogue attributed to the author.
- Distinguish historical facts, plot details, critical arguments, and present-day synthesis. Culture, language, caste, colonialism, and gender need specific treatment rather than a single global template.
- Use sub-thousand numeric IDs for insertions while retaining existing analysis IDs and URLs.
- `spoken_words` and duration are descriptive measurements, never stylistic constraints. `track.json` lists only manuscripts that exist and can be played.
- Source catalog and sidecars are outside the spoken region. Every source ID must resolve and every claim anchor must match the extracted narration exactly.

## Special treatment: Tolkien's *The Lord of the Rings*

**`026200`–`027200` — eleven dedicated lectures.** Tolkien's novel is one continuous work internally arranged in six books and published in three volumes. We use those volume boundaries as natural narrative divisions, rather than splitting at an arbitrary speaking duration:

- `026200`: an accessible entrance to Middle-earth without requiring *The Hobbit* or lore familiarity
- `026300`: *The Fellowship of the Ring*, complete story of Books I–II, including Bombadil and the Barrow-downs
- `026400`: *The Two Towers*, Books III–IV, both the Rohan/Isengard and Frodo/Sam branches
- `026500`: *The Return of the King*, Books V–VI, including the Paths of the Dead in the novel, the Scouring of the Shire, and the Grey Havens
- `026600`: the Ring and the corruption of apparently virtuous political aims
- `026700`: mercy, Gollum, providence and Frodo's limit, grounded in Tolkien's correspondence
- `026800`: languages, ruins and how deep time becomes perceptible
- `026900`: fellowship, class, service and friendship across inherited hostility
- `027000`: war, environmental injury, the Scouring and the different possibilities of return
- `027100`: kingship, gender, peoples, the moral status of Orcs and the representation of outsiders
- `027200`: the creation of the book, philology, interlacing, fantasy inheritance and Tolkien's rejection of one-to-one allegory

This is a single literary work, not three separate novels. The narrator introduces geography and characters when needed; the plot lectures contain full spoilers and run in sequence *before* criticism. The analysis lectures are thematically self-contained after that context. No rigid length target or word quota was used. Metadata lengths describe the files after authoring.

Historical and bibliographic anchors include the Tolkien Estate, manuscript records at Marquette, primary Tolkien correspondence, Carl Hostetter's philological scholarship, Dimitra Fimi's published account of the legendarium, Vincent Ferré on contemplative pacing, the 2022 academic companion, and environmental scholarship. Sources restricted to public abstracts are explicitly marked; claims from the primary novel are differentiated from authorial intention and our own interpretation. ## Handmaid, Fahrenheit, and Bulgakov: narrative plus criticism

The next three pairs extend the course through three kinds of twentieth-century fiction. All complete their plots before critical discussion, and none is divided to achieve a fixed listening duration.

- `027400` / `028000`: Margaret Atwood, *The Handmaid's Tale* (1985). The novel's own ambiguous van ending and 2195 *Historical Notes* remain intact; neither the television series nor *The Testaments* supplies an invented resolution.
- `028200` / `029000`: Ray Bradbury, *Fahrenheit 451* (1953). Montag's loss of Clarisse, reading crisis, killing of Beatty, escape, media fabrication, and the destroyed city; then the tension between book censorship and distracted public attention.
- `029200` / `030000`: Mikhail Bulgakov, *The Master and Margarita* (written before 1940; censored publication 1966–67). The Moscow, Margarita, and Pilate strands are told together through their final resolution. Analysis differentiates authorial manuscript history from the text's supernatural fantasy and rejects a single compulsory theology of 'peace' versus 'light'.

Primary works are copyrighted; story lessons are independent, original expository summaries, not narrated reproductions of their prose. Source catalog records distinguish primary novels, freely available reader guides, original author statements, and scholarship accessible only through abstracts. ## Agent A continuation: Woolf, Kafka, Walker (030200–033000)

Three new complete story-first pairs add different narrative techniques to the same literary course:

- `030200` then `031000`: Virginia Woolf, *Mrs Dalloway* (1925). The single day in 1923 London, Clarissa's friends and party, Septimus and Rezia's parallel tragedy; then an interpretation of public clock time versus lived time, free indirect perspective, gender, empire, class, and the ethics of another person's inward life. Clarissa never meets Septimus, and her response to the report of his death is not privileged knowledge of his intentions.
- `031200` then `032000`: Franz Kafka, *The Trial* (written 1914–15, published by Max Brod in 1925). Josef K.'s arrest, court offices, lawyer, Titorelli, Block, cathedral parable and death, followed by analysis of procedural illegibility, shame, the costs of indefinite accusation, and the manuscript's unfinished editorial state. Common chapter order is **not** claimed to have been fixed by Kafka.
- `032200` then `033000`: Alice Walker, *The Color Purple* (1982). Celie's voice, Sofia and Shug, the buried letters and Nettie's African experiences, family revelations, work and final reunion. Analysis of epistolary voice, chosen intimacy, social and racial power, spiritual revision, independent labor, and contested portrayals. This is the novel, not screen or stage adaptations; Olinka are **fictional**, not a real-world ethnographic sample.
