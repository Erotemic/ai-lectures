## Editorial decisions

- The first lecture of each pair is a full-spoiler narrative explanation for listeners unfamiliar with the novel. It introduces the main relationships, follows causal developments, and reaches the ending in a single recording.
- The second lecture analyzes narrative construction, historical forces and contested readings, with documented distinctions between primary-author testimony and our synthesis.
- Baldwin: the British publisher agreement is distinguished from first-edition chronology (New York Dial 1956; London Michael Joseph 1957). David's reconstruction of Giovanni's fatal meeting with Guillaume is conjecture, not testimony.
- Dangarembga: the main story is set in colonial Rhodesia; publication in 1988 follows independence. The title's famous phrase comes from Jean-Paul Sartre's preface to Frantz Fanon, not from a book by Sartre.
- Kundera: the plot narration is mostly chronological for accessibility while describing how the novel announces death earlier and concludes with the final dance. The novel and the later film are distinct versions.
- Source records describe whether the reference is primary author testimony, university criticism, scholarship with only an abstract available, or a plot-summary cross-check. This is not a claim to have collated the full primary novels in this pass.
- Spoken word counts are metadata only; none are used to accept or reject a lecture.
