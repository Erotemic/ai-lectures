## Why these two

Sagan's *Contact* (1985) adds a science-driven novel with an international research cast and public reasoning as dramatic action. Vonnegut's *Slaughterhouse-Five* (1969) adds an anti-war novel that deploys science fiction to disrupt linear time and heroic narratives. Together they expand the course's treatment of what speculative forms can do beyond extrapolating technologies.

## Fact and interpretation boundaries

- The published 1985 *Contact* is authoritative for the story. The 1997 film has a different crew and ending. The pi circle is a fictional discovery, not a verified mathematical property.
- *Slaughterhouse-Five* contains a historically inflated Dresden death count. The municipal historians' commission instead documents a much smaller magnitude, with up to about 25,000 deaths. Avoid repeating outdated figures as historical fact.
- Interpretations involving PTSD, alien reality, authorial fatalism, and religion are discussed as readings, not settled diagnoses or theorems.
- Secondary sources supplement the copyrighted original novels; no publisher text was reproduced into the narrations. Some research pages expose only abstracts; source entries state the access level.

## Future possible links

*Contact* can be taught alongside *The Left Hand of Darkness* as two models of intercultural communication. *Slaughterhouse-Five* can be taught alongside *Kindred* and *Beloved* as distinct novels about the past's refusal to stay past. These are conceptual links, not mandatory prerequisites.
