# Tolkien special treatment — editorial record

Agent A scope: `draft-stages/tts-modern-literature/` only. Added eleven lecture records with stable local IDs `026200–027200`. One listening-oriented entrance, complete story narrations for the three published volumes (corresponding to Books I–VI), and seven separate analytical essays. The analysis lectures are *after*, not interspersed within, the story sequence.

## Why the volume break?

A continuous novel of this length needs comprehensible listening arcs. The three published volumes have clear transition points: the breaking of the Fellowship, Frodo's capture by Orcs, and the final homecoming. These are not time-based partitions. An alternative division into the six internal books is equally defensible for close reading; this sequence keeps the paired parallel stories in *The Two Towers* together as one lecture, so listeners can appreciate the interdependence of events.

## Differences from film adaptations that must remain correct

- The Old Forest, Tom Bombadil, and the Barrow-downs matter in *The Fellowship*; the old barrow-blade helps Merry against the Witch-king.
- The *Two Towers* ends with Frodo paralyzed and taken into Cirith Ungol; Sam learns he is alive only after taking the Ring.
- Faramir refuses to take the Ring to Gondor, and his choice must not be given the film's plot.
- Aragorn releases the oath-bound Dead after their help in the south at Pelargir; the army fighting at Pelennor Fields with him is living men.
- The Scouring of the Shire, Bywater, the fate of Saruman and Wormtongue, and Frodo's inability to remain in the Shire are central to the published novel.
- The last instant at Mount Doom is *not* Frodo voluntarily throwing the Ring into the fire; Gollum's intervention and Frodo's limits are central to interpretation.

## Source judgment

The published novel remains the authority for plot; the publicly accessible secondary summary is cross-reference, not a substitute for the book. The estate page supports the composition and publication record. Tolkien's own letters support *his* interpretation of Frodo and allegory; they do not prohibit alternate scholarship. Marquette's manuscript collection is documented, but no claim is made to have inspected the restricted scans. John R. Holmes and the environmental paper are used to the extent publicly accessible abstracts support; the complete scholarly chapters were not asserted as read.
