## Selection and scope

Virginia Woolf's *To the Lighthouse* is a central text for the literary history of modernist time, free indirect perspective, and the relation of women's work to literary and visual form. The work was selected for its formal achievement, ongoing scholarly importance, and significance to literary pedagogy; this is **one work**, not three different works because it has three movements. The section adds 12 original TTS manuscripts to the existing modern-literature track, in two parts: a continuous spoiler-complete retelling first, then eight analytical lectures. Length follows explanatory need, not a time or word quota. No audio, binary art, or runtime code is included.

The 1927 novel has **three** titled parts: *The Window*, *Time Passes*, *The Lighthouse*. The first and third concentrate on less than two days of experienced time, while the short middle section covers roughly a decade. The first story movement is split into afternoon and evening because the promised voyage, domestic and intellectual activity, dinner, nursery scene and quiet marital ending are distinct continuous phases of one day; **the retelling does not invent a fourth part in Woolf's novel**.

## Primary text and bibliographic source hierarchy

**Primary narrative witness:** The three chapters of the 1927 American edition are available as verified public-domain transcriptions at Wikisource: [The Window](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/To_the_Lighthouse/The_Window), [Time Passes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/To_the_Lighthouse/Time_Passes), and [The Lighthouse](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/To_the_Lighthouse/The_Lighthouse). These are primary support for fictional events and narrative design, but their exact wording and paragraphing should not silently be called definitive for the 1927 British edition. The [Cambridge introduction by Marina MacKay](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-introduction-to-the-novel/virginia-woolf-to-the-lighthouse-1927/C0D38691E022D3EBD8EE11687C089D00) independently verifies three-part arrangement, prewar Skye setting, short narrative time in the long opening, decade-long interval, and the final morning; only the publisher summary was directly available, not the whole chapter.

**Gender, class, empire, and form:** [Helen Carr's chapter, "Virginia Woolf, Empire and Race," Cambridge Companion to Virginia Woolf](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-virginia-woolf/virginia-woolf-empire-and-race/D69E50B1D9CDEF98625B88C3C5E7C426) explicitly discusses the daughters' imagined independence and the text's joint invocation of the Bank of England and Indian Empire. The publisher excerpt and the primary text are available; the entire chapter is not claimed to have been inspected. Yale's [Modernism Lab overview](https://campuspress.yale.edu/modernismlab/to-the-lighthouse/) gives scholarly orientation about Lily's painting, the family, and wartime rupture; the page may return 502 on direct access. The analyses of domestic power, subjective knowledge, and painting are readings argued from identified scenes, not attributed to a secondary scholar who has not been read in full.

**Author, life and publication:** The [Virginia Woolf Society's biographical account](https://virginiawoolfsociety.org.uk/resources/virginia-woolf-a-short-biography/) grounds Woolf's childhood summers at Talland House, St Ives, and the reimagined Hebridean setting; it is not proof that every action of Mrs Ramsay reproduces an action of Julia Stephen. The [Cambridge Companion chronology](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781107481732_A23761529/preview-9781107481732_A23761529.pdf) gives Woolf's start in January 1926 and the 1927 publication date. [Princeton's exhibition on Woolf and the Hogarth Press](https://dpul.princeton.edu/company-of-good-books/feature/beach-woolf-making-modernism) provides publishing and artistic context, and the [National Portrait Gallery's Woolf profile](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/search/person/mp04923/virginia-woolf-nee-stephen) records her significance for twentieth-century modernist fiction. These are general context sources, not evidence of an exhaustive direct-influence genealogy.

**Textual witnesses and limits:** [Smith College's exhibit of corrected page proofs](https://www.smith.edu/libraries/libs/rarebook/exhibitions/penandpress/case10b.htm) documents the archival evidence. [Edward Mendelson's annotated collation and facsimiles](https://mendelson.org/VirginiaWoolf/tothelighthousetext.html) distinguish the first American and British printings (both 1927), the 1930 revised British impression, variant words and section numbers, and the existence of marked proof sets. Assertions that a particular printing is **preferable** are the editor's judgment rather than an undisputed archival fact. An exchange in the [New York Review of Books](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2023/03/23/respecting-second-thoughts/) addresses how the first part's ending may be read through Mrs Ramsay's private belief rather than as a universally authoritative report that Mr Ramsay understands her. The spoken story acknowledges this interpretive issue without forcing an edition-specific resolution.

## Interpretive boundaries and fact checks

1. **The narrative is set before, during and after World War I, but the middle section does not put the reader on a conventional battlefield.** Andrew dies when a shell explodes in France. Mrs Ramsay dies suddenly at night. Prue marries, and then dies in a condition related to childbirth. The brevity and bracketed form of the reports are meaningful, not omissions of detailed medical histories known elsewhere in the text.
2. **The servant characters are not substitutes for Mrs Ramsay.** Mrs McNab, Mrs Bast and George restore the house through physical work. The story gives them agency rather than describing the house's return as a spontaneous miracle of memory. These people are not all interchangeable and not necessarily given the same narrative interiority as the upper-class guests.
3. **Mr Ramsay is not an omniscient truth-teller.** His weather prediction is his confident forecast, not an ethical entitlement to treat another's hope roughly. His Q-to-R alphabet is an inward analogy for intellectual progress, not a philosophical publication or theorem. Lily's kitchen-table image is her remembered interpretation of his philosophical work.
4. **James's and Cam's resistance is real; the final praise does not constitute total reconciliation.** The father praises his son's competence near the lighthouse. The novel does not narrate a permanent psychological resolution, a return trip, or a later adult relationship.
5. **Lily's final line completes her composition as she sees it.** The text does not claim exhibition, commercial success, marriage, or fame. The painter's relationship to Woolf is useful for analogy and not a diary transcription.
6. **The novel's narrated minds are not adjudications of every motive.** Mrs Ramsay's hopes for Paul and Minta do not guarantee a happy marriage. Charles Tansley's social anxiety does not excuse his disparagement of women artists. A final inward thought about marital understanding may not establish the husband's unspoken mind.
7. **Empire references are in the primary text**, especially Mrs Ramsay's thoughts about men who rule India and her daughters' resistance to the Bank of England and the Indian Empire. The section does not assume all the Ramsay family members share a single anti-imperial philosophy or make the lighthouse a single allegorical sign of empire.
8. **Differences among textual witnesses matter for exact readings.** The first British and first American editions were published in 1927 but differ. The British 1930 uniform edition introduced further authorial changes. The public-domain primary witness used for plot validation is the first American text; exact quotations or textual close readings should identify their edition.
9. **Plot retelling and interpretation remain distinct.** The first four are original expository retellings; eight critical lectures follow. No one-to-one copies of copyrighted modern secondary writing occur in spoken text. The primary 1927 novel is public domain in the United States.
10. **No duration policy, audio or images.** Word counts are measurements of actual extracted speech, not targets or acceptance gates. The section includes only text, source metadata, and a track catalog update.
