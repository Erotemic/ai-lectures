## Plot and interpretation distinctions

* *The Hour of the Star*: Rodrigo S. M. is a fictional narrator; the narrator's account is not a direct statement by Lispector. The novella and its narration must both be told to explain the form. The 13-titles critical account is supported by the visible abstract of Gomes; manuscript evidence is documented by the author's archive. Neither is a license to quote extended copyrighted text.
* *This Earth of Mankind*: stop at the ending of *Bumi Manusia*, **not** the later Buru Quartet plot. Colonial legal and racial categories are represented through the novel's fictional suit, not a definitive reconstruction of statutory Dutch East Indies law. Pramoedya's own interview describes oral circulation of general outlines while writing was barred; avoid the claim that every final sentence was first memorized and recited. The book was first published in 1980; first-volume bans followed in 1981.
* *Rebecca*: Maxim admits he shot Rebecca. Dr Baker's medical evidence rules out pregnancy; it does not establish Rebecca's mental intentions beyond doubt. The novel's Manderley fire is strongly associated with Mrs Danvers by context, but readers are not given conclusive proof of ignition or Danvers's death. Do not substitute 1940 film particulars.

## Method

A visible author/estate archive or publisher record, public plot summaries in the original language where practical, and scholarly secondary material were used for each work. Evidence catalog `access` and `notes` fields distinguish full page, abstract, and published copyrighted work. There is no assertion that a copyrighted primary text was available in full online. Authors' personal backgrounds and distinct critical arguments are not conflated with the fiction.
