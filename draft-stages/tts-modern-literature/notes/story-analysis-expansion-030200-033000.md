## Source strength and interpretive questions

**Woolf.** The original 1925 text is available through Project Gutenberg (#71865) and the University of Virginia annotated first-edition transcription. British Library pages support the one-day structure, postwar London and the Clarissa–Septimus counterpoint. Cambridge/Edinburgh and Oxford chapters have public abstracts; do not claim their whole texts have been read. The analysis treats Clarissa's reading of Septimus's death as a *reading*, not validation of suicide or a statement that the two met.

**Kafka.** Project Gutenberg's German first edition (#69327) is a Brod-edited book, not proof of fixed authorial chapter order. The Kafka Project offers manuscript-oriented critical material; a peer-reviewed article on Kafka's reception and publication documents the 1925 posthumous history. K.'s moral failings are not an implied charge, and neither the court's identity nor the parable's single definitive solution is invented. The story distinguishes final events Kafka wrote from uncertain editorial sequencing.

**Walker.** Smithsonian Libraries establishes the bibliographic record and the Pulitzer website records the 1983 prize. Public study guides cross-check plot; Mae G. Henderson's Oxford chapter abstract supports the epistolary / women's cultural forms analysis, and Cambridge's public scholarly entry documents longstanding differences in reception. None of these links is represented as online full text of Walker's copyrighted novel. The analysis explicitly separates fictional Olinka from actual African populations and keeps domestic violence, racist state authority and colonial economic disruption analytically distinct.
