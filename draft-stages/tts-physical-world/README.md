# The Physical World: Motion, Energy, Fields, and Matter

A first course in measurable physics, with limits and assumptions stated. Each lecture is self-contained for audio and provides display-only equations, exercises, and sources.

## Available lectures

- 001000 — How Can Motion Become a Law? Measurement, Models, and Forces (1,722 spoken words; approximately 11.9 min at 145 wpm)
- 002000 — Where Does the Energy Go? Conservation, Work, and Heat (3,012 spoken words; approximately 20.8 min at 145 wpm)
- 003000 — Why Does Heat Flow One Way? Entropy and Irreversible Change (1,657 spoken words; approximately 11.4 min at 145 wpm)
- 004000 — A Force at Every Point: Fields, Charges, and Interactions (1,578 spoken words; approximately 10.9 min at 145 wpm)
- 005000 — How Does a Disturbance Travel? Waves, Sound, and Resonance (1,618 spoken words; approximately 11.2 min at 145 wpm)
- 006000 — What Changes Across Scales? Atoms, Radiation, and Mass–Energy (1,607 spoken words; approximately 11.1 min at 145 wpm)

These are the six completed lectures in the first foundations sequence. Begin with 001000 for measurement and mechanics; 002000 provides the energy accounting needed for 003000; 004000–006000 connect fields, waves, and microscopic matter. All six can also be listened to independently.

Spoken narration is between the marker pair in each manuscript. Examples described as hypothetical are illustrations, not invented reports of historical measurements. Reference IDs and historical claim anchors are in `metadata/`.

## Further directions

Thermodynamic ensembles and statistical fluctuations; fluids and continuum models; optics and measurement; basic circuit models; relativity and reference frames; atomic binding and nuclear reaction energetics. The completed foundations do not claim to replace dedicated courses in these subjects.
