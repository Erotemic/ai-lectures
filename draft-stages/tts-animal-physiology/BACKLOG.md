# Comparative physiology: further lectures

The first five manuscripts are complete and indexed. Later topics should remain coherent, substantive spoken lectures with specific source-backed metadata, not a predetermined number or runtime target. Build chapters when the science supports a complete argument and useful comparisons.

## Next candidate topics

1. **Muscles and the cost of movement.** Actin–myosin crossbridges and ATP; skeletal mechanics; muscles versus hydraulic or ciliary movement; walking, flying and swimming; scaling and locomotion economy. Differentiate biomechanical forces from subjective effort.
2. **How nerves carry information.** Membrane potentials, voltage-gated channels, action potentials, conduction and synapses; distinguish signal propagation from information processing; comparative examples with jellyfish, insects, cephalopods and vertebrates. Coordinate with the broader neuroscience course rather than replicate it.
3. **Sensing the world from inside a body.** Mechanoreception, hearing, vision, electroreception, thermoreception, proprioception and sensory tradeoffs. Complement, rather than rewrite, `tts-living-world/002000-animal-sensory-worlds.md` by pursuing physiology and measurement.
4. **Heat, cold and metabolic rate.** Energy budgets, thermogenesis, thermoregulation, ectothermy/endothermy, insulation, circulatory exchange, torpor, and constraints of size and environment.
5. **Hormones and long-distance coordination.** Glands and receptor specificity, endocrine feedback, stress, development, metabolic regulation, timescales, and limitations of simplistic single-hormone stories.
6. **Defense within a living body.** Barriers, innate immunity, inflammatory communication, adaptive immunity in jawed vertebrates, and non-vertebrate defense mechanisms. Develop enough mechanism to support a subsequent dedicated immunology track.
7. **Reproduction, growth and life-history costs.** Sex and asexual processes, gamete allocation, pregnancy and egg investment across taxa, parental care, senescence, and the physiological costs of reproduction. Coordinate with the survey's existing life-cycle lecture.
8. **A body as an integrated system.** If the preceding chapters earn a synthesis, use an animal coping with feeding, movement and environmental change to integrate transport, energetic, neural, endocrine and excretory control. Do not publish a mandatory capstone just to close a list.
