# Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy: Can one nearby projection be carried to another by a controlled unitary?

An orthogonal projection describes a subspace without choosing a basis. Suppose two projections are close as operators. Can we construct a rotation, or more generally a unitary map, that carries the range of one onto the range of the other?

Béla Szőkefalvi-Nagy, often cited as Béla Sz.-Nagy, was a Hungarian mathematician associated with the Szeged school of functional analysis. His work ranged across operator theory and approximation theory. A 1947 paper on perturbations of self-adjoint transformations belongs to the history of controlling how operator-generated subspaces change.

The simplest case is two lines in the plane. One is the horizontal axis; the other is tilted by an acute angle. Their orthogonal projections are close when the tilt is small. A plane rotation carries the first line to the second, but there is an important choice: rotating the ambient plane is different from merely naming a new basis along the tilted line.

For general subspaces, a useful construction begins with the two projections themselves. Combine the product of the projections with the product of their complementary projections. This gives an operator that maps the source subspace toward the target and the source complement toward the target complement. When the projections are sufficiently close, its polar factor provides a unitary intertwiner.

Why mention the complements? An ambient unitary must carry an orthonormal basis of the entire space to another orthonormal basis. It is not enough to map one selected subspace correctly; the action on its orthogonal complement must also be consistent.

The threshold matters. If one subspace has a direction exactly orthogonal to the other, the natural near-identity construction encounters a right-angle obstruction. The special case is visible even in two dimensions: at a ninety-degree angle the preferred continuous choice of near-identity transport is no longer controlled by a small perturbation.

Sz.-Nagy's place in this story is the passage from a distance between projections to an actual geometric motion of the underlying Hilbert space. Later canonical direct rotations and perturbation estimates refine this question, but the fundamental issue is already present: can closeness of subspaces be realized by a well-controlled transformation of all vectors?

## Mathematical and historical companion (display only)

For orthogonal projections \(P,Q\), set
\(S=QP+(I-Q)(I-P)\). Then \(SP=QS\).
When \(\|P-Q\|<1\), \(S\) is invertible, and its polar unitary
\(U=S(S^*S)^{-1/2}\) satisfies \(UP=QU\).
This is a nearby-projection intertwining construction, with the stated
strict smallness hypothesis.
