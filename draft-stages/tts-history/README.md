# History · People and ideas

The playable History track contains **eight researched long-form lectures**:
Camille Jordan, Lord Rayleigh, Walther Ritz, Hermann Weyl, David Hilbert,
Tosio Kato, Chandler Davis, and William Kahan.

Ritz's **4,418-word lecture** develops a full trial-function calculation,
the difference between eigenvalue bounds and eigenvector residuals, the
1908–1909 plate research, the spectral-line combination principle, and his
radiation-asymmetry debate with Einstein. Mathematical calculations and
bibliography are in the display-only companion.

Estimated listening lengths are about **27–35 minutes** at 145 spoken words
per minute; actual audio timing varies by voice and playback speed. No new
audio was synthesized.

The remaining **20 short drafts** are under `stubs/` with their metadata and original IDs.
They are excluded from the playable catalog until expanded and checked.

The History reader shares the audio-player implementation but isolates its
cache, listening progress, feedback and generated-audio availability from
other tracks. The recordings respect mathematical prerequisites without referring
to other recordings as earlier or later videos.
