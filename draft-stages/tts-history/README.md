# History · People and ideas

The playable History track contains **28 researched, substantive, standalone historical portraits**, from James Joseph Sylvester (`001000`) through Richard J. Samworth (`028000`). The archive of shorter sketches is now empty. Each portrait includes audio-first narration, worked mathematics, a display-only companion, and claim-level reference metadata.

**The two portraits completing the original archive:**

- **James Joseph Sylvester** (`001000`), *How did matrices become objects that could be solved for?* His London upbringing, Cambridge religious restrictions, friendship with Arthur Cayley, Woolwich, Johns Hopkins and Oxford appointments, 1850 matrix terminology, algebraic resultants, the 1884 matrix-equation papers and law of inertia support a complete historical narrative. The companion proves the finite-dimensional Sylvester criterion and distinguishes existence from numerical conditioning.
- **Richard J. Samworth** (`028000`), *How can statistical methods remain trustworthy when data are scarce or complicated?* His Cambridge research career connects log-concave density estimation, complementary pairs stability selection, the 2015 population-gap theorem with Yu and Wang, random-projection ensembles, permutation independence testing and incomplete data. The companion develops spectral and statistical examples while preserving the assumptions behind each theorem.

Stable lecture IDs remain unchanged. Spoken markers determine TTS content; derivations, citations, and historical references after those markers are display-only. Audio is not bundled. New shorter draft biographies should be placed in `stubs/` until expanded and verified.
