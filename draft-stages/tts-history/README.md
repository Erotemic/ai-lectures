# History · People and ideas

The playable History track contains **23 researched long-form biographies**, from Camille Jordan through Karl Pearson. The lectures cover geometry, variational methods, operator theory, numerical computation, and statistics. Every recording is a standalone, sourced historical narrative with worked mathematics and a display-only companion.

**Karl Pearson** (`024000`) is a **5,949-word** portrait (approximately **41.0 minutes** at 145 words per minute). It follows his early education, interdisciplinary work, UCL appointment, collaboration with Weldon and Galton, the emergence of correlation, regression and chi-square testing, and the geometric closest-fit problem of 1901. The mathematical companion derives best-fit subspaces, includes a four-point PCA example, SVD correspondence and spectral-gap sensitivity, and separates historical contributions from the later Hotelling formulation. It also documents Pearson's active eugenics advocacy, his role establishing the Galton Laboratory, and the UCL historical reassessment, alongside the work of Alice Lee and other collaborators.

Previously expanded portraits include Stewart (`023000`), Golub (`022000`), Björck (`021000`), Wedin (`020000`), Rosenblum (`017000`), Dixmier (`015000`), Halmos (`014000`), Szőkefalvi-Nagy (`013000`), and Ky Fan (`012000`).
