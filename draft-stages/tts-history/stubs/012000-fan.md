# Ky Fan: How can a whole list of singular values be compared at once?

A statement about the largest singular value tells us what happens in the worst direction. It does not say how movement is distributed among the other directions. To control a full list, it is useful to add the largest two singular values, then the largest three, and continue.

Ky Fan was a mathematician whose work included matrix inequalities, operator theory, and fixed-point theory. In the spectral story his name is attached to extremal characterizations of eigenvalue and singular-value sums. The Ky Fan k norm of a matrix is the sum of its k largest singular values.

Take two decreasing lists of nonnegative numbers. The first is four, one, one. The second is three, two, one. Their totals are the same. But the first has a larger leading value, while their first two sums both equal five. Whether one list dominates another depends on every relevant prefix sum, not only on the total or the largest entry.

This is the language of weak majorization. If every leading sum for one singular-value list is no larger than the corresponding leading sum for another, we have a coordinated comparison of the distributions. Many unitarily invariant norms respect that ordering. The result usually called Ky Fan dominance says that control of all the relevant Ky Fan sums supports control of all unitarily invariant matrix norms.

Notice the quantifier. One prefix sum is not enough. A Frobenius-norm inequality, or even an operator-norm inequality, does not automatically give every Ky Fan inequality. The separate prefix sums encode information that one scalar norm can miss.

Why does this matter for subspace angles? The singular values of a projection leakage operator are the sines of the principal angles. If we control their leading sums, we control an entire pattern of angular movement. This is stronger information than a bound on the single largest angle, especially when a large-dimensional subspace moves in several different directions.

Ky Fan's contribution helps turn spectral perturbation estimates into norm-independent statements. Instead of proving one theorem for the operator norm and another for the Frobenius norm, we can sometimes establish a singular-value ordering from which many norm estimates follow.

## Mathematical and historical companion (display only)

For singular values \(s_1(A)\ge\cdots\ge s_n(A)\ge0\), the Ky Fan
\(k\)-norm is \(\|A\|_{(k)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}s_j(A)\).
Weak majorization \(s(A)\prec_w s(B)\) means
\(\sum_{j\le k}s_j(A)\le\sum_{j\le k}s_j(B)\) for each \(k\).
The list \(4,1,1\) weakly majorizes \(3,2,1\): its first prefix sum is larger and its remaining prefix sums are equal. The reverse weak-majorization relation fails at the first entry.
