# Yi Yu: Can a statistical eigenspace bound use a population gap alone?

An applied statistician estimates a covariance matrix from data and computes its leading eigenvectors. The population covariance matrix is fixed but unknown. To judge the result, it is natural to ask for assumptions expressed in population quantities, rather than in the particular random eigenvalues the sample happened to produce.

Yi Yu is one of the three authors, with Tengyao Wang and Richard Samworth, of the 2015 Biometrika paper A Useful Variant of the Davis–Kahan Theorem for Statisticians. The paper had appeared as a preprint in 2014. Its central practical contribution is a version of a subspace perturbation estimate whose separation condition can be expressed using population eigenvalues alone.

Consider a population covariance matrix with leading eigenvalue ten and next eigenvalue four. A sample estimate differs from it by a symmetric error matrix. A gap of six in the population is a natural resource for controlling how far the sample's leading eigendirection moves. In contrast, a bound requiring a gap between one population eigenvalue and a selected sample eigenvalue can be awkward to apply before we have already controlled the sample spectrum.

The mathematical problem is still about principal angles. The leading eigenvector is one case; a group of leading eigenvectors defines a higher-dimensional subspace. The error is measured through the sine of its principal angles to the population subspace. A perturbation norm forms the numerator, while a suitable population gap forms the denominator.

A subtlety is worth hearing clearly. 'Population gap' does not mean any distance between arbitrary eigenvalues. It is the separation of the retained eigenvalue block from the eigenvalues immediately outside the target block, under the theorem's ordering assumptions. Using an internal difference between two eigenvalues in the retained block would answer the wrong geometric question.

The 2015 article is jointly authored; its mathematical results should be attributed to all three authors, not assigned to one author on the strength of the order of names. This portrait concentrates on why the population-gap formulation is useful in the statistical problem the paper addresses.

The historical development is a change in how a classical theorem is packaged for a new kind of evidence. In statistics, the computed subspace is random, the reference subspace is a population object, and the hypotheses should be quantities that can be incorporated into a probabilistic analysis.

## Mathematical and historical companion (display only)

For symmetric population \(\Sigma\) and sample \(\widehat\Sigma\),
\(E=\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\). A population-gap variant bounds
\(\|\sin\Theta(\widehat V,V)\|\) in terms of a suitable norm of \(E\)
and a gap between the retained population spectral block and its complement.
The exact constants, norms and ordering hypotheses are specified in Yu,
Wang and Samworth (2015), DOI 10.1093/biomet/asv008.
