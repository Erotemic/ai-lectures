## Editorial principles

- Aim for comprehensive practical competence by identifying missing mechanisms, worked diagnostics and genuinely new applications, not by filling sequential IDs automatically.
- A lecture can be as long or short as needed by its subject. Word counts are descriptive, not authoring constraints.
- Narration teaches core reasoning; exact equations, checklists, adopted code and equipment-specific limits belong in the companion.
- Claim-to-source records support later fact review; structural inventory success is not independent clinical or engineering verification.
- Safety-critical, clinical and jurisdiction-dependent claims require expert review, with original standards bodies and original authors credited.
- Preserve prior manuscripts and the existing electrical curriculum; do not alter shared source code or add binary assets.
