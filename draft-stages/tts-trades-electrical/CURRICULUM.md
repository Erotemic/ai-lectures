# Electrical Systems: Principles, Practice, Codes, and Judgment

A trades-foundations curriculum for homeowners and curious readers. The subject is broad: understanding ordinary electricity, recognizing hazards, interpreting installation rules, maintaining and repairing equipment responsibly, designing better systems, and communicating with competent tradespeople. It is **not** a replacement for supervised practice, licensing, equipment-specific instructions, or required inspection.

## Editorial contract: three distinct questions

For each consequential design choice, ask:

1. **Physics:** What path can charge take, what energy can be released, what can fail, and with what consequence? These statements should survive differences between national codes.
2. **Engineering practice:** How do system architecture, isolation, protective devices, materials, measurement, documentation, and maintenance reduce those hazards and improve reliability, efficiency, and repairability?
3. **Local compliance:** Who has authority over the particular installation, what edition or amendment is incorporated, what equipment approval/marking is required, and who may design, install, test, and inspect it? These are facts to check for every project, not permanent universal rules.

A code may embody decades of accumulated engineering experience and still contain administrative requirements that differ by jurisdiction. Do not label an installation safe merely because an inspector accepted it, or call a legitimate permit or inspection pointless because it is administrative. **No lecture should quietly substitute general U.S. practice for a global rule.** Use U.S. split-phase 120/240 V as one common example, alongside 230 V single-phase, 400/230 V three-phase, different earthing arrangements and RCD conventions where relevant. Nominal voltage, colors, frequency, and permissions vary.

**Length rule:** There is no ten-minute target or any other lecture-duration quota. A treatment of a technical problem should be as long as the explanation, relevant failure modes, qualifications and real examples require. Spoken counts are descriptive metadata, never a reason to shorten or pad.

## Learning surfaces and competence

Every lecture can stand alone but fits a cumulative progression. The audio portion teaches vocabulary, mechanisms, choices, and failure reasoning. A separate non-spoken companion provides schematics described in text, equations, lookup guidance, an evidence map and a practical exercise. Each exercise is explicitly classified:

- **Desk / simulation:** paper calculations, reading nameplates, circuit drawings, component data sheets, assessing a hypothetical installation. No electrical hazards introduced.
- **Current-limited training bench:** purpose-built, enclosed, isolated extra-low-voltage educational modules, with manufacturer-recommended current limiting and supervision appropriate to the learner. *Extra-low voltage is not universally safe*; batteries can cause severe burns or fires when shorted.
- **Visual-only site survey:** look at labels, outlets, nameplates, exposed cord condition and accessible control features without removing covers or touching conductors.
- **Supervised practice:** hands-on skills in an approved, de-energized training assembly with a qualified instructor; correct preparation, tool selection, cable support, terminations, workmanship, testing and inspection.
- **Qualified / authorized field work:** panel/service entry, exposed live testing, damaged or uncertain systems, generator transfer/interconnection, battery banks and photovoltaic string work, utility-side components, complex fault location. The course teaches how to specify, evaluate, and witness work rather than pretending narration confers qualification.

No simulated exercise should normalize intentionally defeating protective devices, using household mains as a training supply, or connecting a source to building wiring without engineered source isolation and a compliant transfer/interconnection arrangement. Homeowner work rights differ between jurisdictions; the same task may be permitted to an owner-occupant in one place and reserved to credentialed practitioners in another.

## Running system example

A home has an induction cooking appliance rated at 2,000 W, a solar array, an inverter, and optional storage. This is first a quantitative example, **not wiring instructions**. At ideal conversion, one hour of 2,000 W operation needs 2.0 kWh; if a hypothetical inverter delivers 90% efficiency at the operating point, approximately 2.22 kWh must be supplied from its DC side. A hypothetical 48 V nominal battery supplying 2,222 W would average about 46 A, and actual current and transient peaks depend on the battery voltage, inverter and controls. That immediately raises nontrivial conductor, protection, connector, thermal, autonomy and enclosure questions. Photovoltaic panel nameplate watts do not describe constant output; the system also needs inverter power and surge capability and an operating architecture that avoids energizing any external supply unintentionally. **Never describe connecting panels directly to a household outlet or backfeeding a house.** Later lessons expand this example into a documented, professionally reviewed design with appropriate transfer, protection and approval.

## Reference architecture and freshness

Stable scientific concepts are taught in the narration. Standards and editions belong in track-local bibliography and *dated* jurisdiction companion pages. Primary reference families: IEC 60364, NFPA 70, OSHA electrical work practice rules, NFPA 70E safety work practice guidance, CSA C22.1, BS 7671, AS/NZS 3000, product markings/instructions, and energy-system research. A publication's edition date does **not** prove it has been legally adopted locally. Do not copy copyrighted code tables into the lectures; teach the hazard they address and how to find the controlling table in the applicable text.

## Attribution and corroboration gate

Every lecture must satisfy [`EVIDENCE_POLICY.md`](EVIDENCE_POLICY.md). Give named researchers and writers appropriate attribution when known, and name the exact standards body, edition and clause when verified. Explain what each source can and cannot support. Corroborate hazardous, decision-relevant claims with independent evidence where possible. Publisher abstracts and employer safety rules alone do not establish requirements for a particular home. For installation, energized testing, source transfer or first-aid procedures, record an outstanding qualified professional review rather than claiming certification. Lecture length remains unrestricted.

## Review questions for future authors

Before any lesson is indexed: (1) is the hazard model correct, (2) are the assumptions about source, grounding and fault current explicit, (3) could a listener mistake conceptual explanation for permission to work on live equipment, (4) are national or regional examples labeled, (5) is a manufacturer's instruction or engineering assessment being silently substituted with a blanket rule, (6) does the laboratory exercise remain genuinely low energy and controlled, (7) does the narration teach a concept rather than recite code section numbers, and (8) do citation IDs and extracted word counts match? No fixed length threshold.
