# World History audio lectures

This is a separate, audio-first collection within AI Lectures. It does not
change the mathematical curricula or their course graph. Open the **World
History** tab in the audio reader, or visit `/audio?track=world-history`.

The substantive, standalone lectures are:

- **001000 · The Epic of Gilgamesh: The King Who Could Not Defeat Death** —
  narrative, archaeology, transmission of the text, flood traditions, and mortality.
- **002000 · Mary Leakey: Reading the Footprints of Deep Time** —
  early human evolution, field methods, Olduvai Gorge, Laetoli, and the
  attribution of team discoveries.
- **003000 · Uruk: How a City Learned to Count** —
  late fourth-millennium urbanism, irrigation, institutions, proto-cuneiform,
  regional exchange, and the later traditions of Gilgamesh.
- **004000 · Mansa Musa: Gold, Pilgrimage, and the Empire of Mali** —
  Sundiata and the formation of Mali, trade and political power, the 1324–1325
  pilgrimage, Cairo diplomacy, Timbuktu, and the historical sources.
- **005000 · Hatshepsut: The Woman Who Became King of Egypt** —
  Eighteenth Dynasty kingship, the co-regency with Thutmose III, royal imagery,
  Deir el-Bahri, Karnak, the expedition to Punt, and later monument erasure.
- **006000 · Fu Hao: The General, the Oracle Bones, and the Shang World** —
  Shang kingship, divination and early writing, military leadership, bronze
  craftsmanship, ancestral ritual, sacrifice, and the excavated tomb at Anyang.
- **007000 · The Austronesian Expansion: How People Crossed an Ocean World** —
  Taiwan and Island Southeast Asia, Near Oceania and Lapita, navigation,
  Micronesia, Polynesian settlement, Madagascar, and long-distance contact.

## How We Know: archaeology and the making of historical evidence

- **029000 · Reading the Ground** — excavation context, stratigraphy, site formation, and the credited work of Kenyon, Harris, Schiffer, and other researchers.
- **030000 · Dating the Past** — tree rings, radiocarbon, calibration, Laetoli geology, luminescence, and original scientific teams.
- **031000 · Recovering Gilgamesh** — the ancient scribal record, excavators including Hormuzd Rassam, George Smith, decipherers, critical editions, and digital corpora.
- **032000 · Searching for Troy** — Calvert, Schliemann, Dörpfeld, Blegen, Korfmann, and the evidence behind competing interpretations of Homer's city.

- **033000 · The Rosetta Stone** — the 196 BCE priestly decree, Åkerblad, Young, Champollion, Coptic, and the testing of hieroglyph readings.
- **034000 · Tutankhamun and the archaeological record** — Carter, Egyptian foremen, Burton, Lucas, the excavation archive, and the limits of royal burial evidence.
- **035000 · The Indus cities** — Sahni, Bandyopadhyay, Kenoyer, Parpola, undeciphered seals, urban archaeology, and independent material evidence.
- **036000 · Fu Hao's tomb and the oracle bones** — Zheng Zhenxiang, Anyang archaeology, inscription interpretation, and joining material and written records.

- **037000 · The Popol Wuj: How a Maya Book Survived** — K'iche' authorship and colonial copying, Ximenez's bilingual manuscript, contested provenance, editorial decisions, and the scholarship of Sam Colop and others.
- **038000 · Finding Mali's Trade Networks in the Ground** — Jenne-jeno urbanism, Tadmekka gold-working equipment, Timbuktu manuscripts, and how archaeologists check medieval trade narratives.

- **039000 · Deciphering Maya Writing** — Knorozov, Proskouriakoff, Berlin, Stuart, and living-language approaches to identifying political history in inscriptions.
- **040000 · Finding Voyagers** — Lapita excavation and dating, Teouma, Vanuatu ancient DNA, and evidence from traditional navigators and experimental voyaging.
- **041000 · Olduvai and Laetoli** — fossil context, volcanic dating, tool/bone associations, named field contributors, and the reanalysis of footprint trails.

- **042000 · Uluburun: A Bronze Age Shipwreck and the Geography of Things** — Turkish sponge diver Mehmet Çakır, Bass and Pulak's underwater excavations, the scientific provenance of metal ingots, dating uncertainty, and the limits of cargo-based route reconstruction.
- **043000 · Berenike and the Monsoon** — Red Sea excavation teams, Cappers's pepper cache, Tomber's pottery analysis, the *Periplus*, and competing evidence for Roman-period Indian Ocean connections.
- **044000 · The Belitung Shipwreck** — Tang-period ceramic production, Flecker's sewn-plank hull, the dated 826 bowl, commercial recovery, and Pearson's analysis of its heritage afterlife.

- **045000 · The Swahili Coast** — Shanga, Kilwa, Songo Mnara, revised dating, local pottery, the house-foundation coin deposit, and the distinct evidence of archaeology, genealogy, and ancient DNA.
- **046000 · The Lost Harbor** — Portus and Ostia sediment cores, geophysical survey, dredging, and underwater archaeology and photogrammetry at Caesarea.
- **047000 · Saving a Shipwreck** — the Mary Rose and Vasa, treatment of waterlogged timbers, iron and sulfur chemistry, documenting artifacts, and ongoing conservation.

## Late-Imperial Chinese Literature: Journey to the West

This sequence lives in a **separate browsing section from Ancient Literature**.
The novel's fictional Tang-era setting draws on a historical seventh-century
journey, while its earliest surviving complete hundred-chapter printing is
from 1592. The author is traditionally identified as Wu Cheng'en, with the
attribution still debated.

- **048000 · Part I: The Stone Monkey and the Court of Heaven** — the waterfall
  cave, immortality, heavenly offices and peaches, Erlang, and the Buddha's hand.
- **049000 · Part II: The Monk, the Vow, and the Companions** — the mission, the
  monk's calling, Guanyin, the fillet, dragon horse, Pigsy, Sandy, and first tests.
- **050000 · Part III: The White Bone Demon and the Problem of Appearances** —
  ginseng fruit, Monkey's dismissal, Precious Elephant, Wuji, and Red Boy.
- **051000 · Part IV: The Kingdoms of Desire and the Two Monkey Kings** — Chechi,
  the River of Heaven, women's kingdom, the double Monkey, and Flaming Mountain.
- **052000 · Part V: False Paradise and the Kingdom of Children** — Yellow Brow,
  Zhuzi, the spiders, Lion-Camel country, and the threatened children.
- **053000 · Part VI: The Last Trials and the Return with Scriptures** — final
  kingdoms, the jade rabbit, blank scrolls, the turtle and the return.
- **054000 · The Historical Pilgrim and the Making of a Novel** — Xuanzang's
  actual journey and translation career, story cycles, editions, the 1592 book,
  chapter nine, authorship, interpretations, and adaptations.

The six narrative lectures follow the complete novel in large arcs; the
seventh focuses on evidence and transmission. Every recording stands alone,
includes historical qualifications in the narration where they clarify a
particular episode, and has non-spoken reading references. See the
[Journey to the West roadmap](JOURNEY_TO_THE_WEST_ROADMAP.md) for boundaries,
source and edition conventions, and the intended reading order.

These lectures include named original contributors in the spoken narrative when
supported by the source record. Each also has non-spoken attribution and
research notes; the metadata link cards point to original methods, field
reports, archives, and scholarly reassessments. See
[How We Know: Sources and Research](HOW_WE_KNOW_ROADMAP.md) for the continuing
methodological strand and attribution guidelines.

The [ancient-literature roadmap](ANCIENT_LITERATURE_ROADMAP.md) describes further candidates and the editorial approach. These are full narrative essays with contextual analysis rather than annotated readings or verbatim reproductions of modern translations.

The source Markdown files contain a spoken lecture between explicit markers.
Historical notes and references after the spoken region are for reading and
editorial review; they are not synthesized. Non-spoken reading links for the
reader live in `metadata/`, with stable IDs in `metadata/sources.json`.

`track.json` lists only lectures ready for sustained listening. The manifest
order is a browsing order, not a required playback sequence. New lectures can
be added by creating a six-digit-prefixed transcript, manifest entry, and
optional reading metadata. Use a new identifier within this track; IDs may
be reused across other tracks because player, speech cache, progress, and
feedback are namespaced by track.

Run `make serve` at the repository root, and select **World History** in the
reader. The server synthesizes audio on demand with the existing TTS profiles.
