# Shahnameh: Persian Epic and the Historical Record

**Listening-ready:** 075000–087000; part of World Literary Epics and Classics.

This thirteen-lecture series tells the story in the order of its large narrative cycles,
from the first kings to the Sasanian dynasty's fall. The spoken narration stands alone;
the non-spoken reference notes and metadata distinguish legend, later inherited
literary tradition, historically attested rulers, and surviving manuscript evidence.

The final lecture addresses Ferdowsi's life, Abu Mansur's prose Shahnameh,
Daqiqi's included verse, the 1217 Florence witness, later lavish manuscripts,
translations, and the limits of the written record.

## Narrative and evidence conventions

Each narrative lecture has a complete spoken manuscript with dialogue and imagery
expressed in original paraphrase, not copied from a modern translation.
Royal legends about the first kings and the heroic cycle are not treated as
dated eyewitness history. In the Alexander episode, the literary brotherhood
of Iskandar and Dara is distinguished from the Macedonian and Achaemenid
historical record. From Ardashir onward, historical kings and events can often
be evaluated through inscriptions, coins, material evidence and other texts,
while particular private adventures remain literary constructions.

Core story and verse order: Ferdowsi's Persian poem. Related historical and
scholarly studies appear in `metadata/sources.json`; at least four curated
reading links accompany each lecture. See `WORLD_LITERARY_CLASSICS_ROADMAP.md`.
