# The Making of the Modern World

Global history since 1500, built around multiple centers, changing networks, institutions, ecological exchange, and people's choices under unequal power.

## Revolutions and industrial transformation

- **011000 — [Who Is the People? Revolutions, Constitutions, and the Boundaries of Freedom](011000-who-is-the-people.md).** Comparative sovereignty and citizenship across the United States, France, Haiti, Spanish America and Brazil; attends to slavery, Indigenous sovereignty, gender and contested political membership.
- **012000 — [Haiti Made Freedom: The Revolution That Reshaped the Atlantic](012000-haiti-made-freedom.md).** A chronological and internally differentiated Haitian Revolution account from Saint-Domingue to the 1825 recognition debt, including land, labor, resistance, gender and the evidentiary limits of inherited narratives.
- **013000 — [The Factory and Its World: Cotton, Coal, Skill, and the Origins of Industrial Growth](013000-the-factory-and-its-world.md).** Textiles, skills, power, factor prices, ecological constraints, colonial raw materials and debated explanations of Britain's early industrialization.
- **014000 — [A City Built to Work: Industry, Childhood, Collective Action, and the Environmental Bill](014000-city-of-work.md).** Paid and unpaid labor, early factory regulation, Chartism, housing, urban mortality, sanitation, pollution and uneven global industrial development.

**Editorial rule for this track and the wider lecture project:** The subject matter determines how much explanation is needed. **There is no 10-minute goal or any other mandatory lecture duration.** Manifest word counts and time estimates are measurements of finished text, never constraints, defaults, or pass/fail checks.

### Granular citation convention for this batch

Each spoken paragraph is linked to a source-ledger entry **after** the narration markers. Entries identify an exact source record, a direct URL, the relevant document/chapter/section, what the accessible source can support, and an explicit caution where interpretation outruns direct documentation. Sidecar `metadata/<id>.json` has one `historical_claims` entry per paragraph. Check the source-access scope (`direct_page`, `direct_article`, `abstract_only`, `catalog_only`, `excerpt_only`) before treating a supporting reference as inspected full text. The scope does **not** assert that every clause of a long source has been independently audited.

The project does **not** have a ten-minute (or any other fixed) lecture length target. Word counts and duration estimates are descriptive metadata.

## Empire, mobility, Indigenous sovereignty, and networks (019000–022000)

- **019000 — [When the Company Became a Crown](019000-the-rebellion-and-the-raj.md).** The 1857 Indian uprising, its diverse participants, the 1858 shift from Company to Crown, and the contested politics of historical memory.
- **020000 — [The Contract and the Ocean](020000-the-contract-and-the-ocean.md).** Indentured migration, the meaning and limits of contracts, migrant choices and coercion, women’s petitions, and diasporic historical sources.
- **021000 — [The Treaty and the Land](021000-the-treaty-and-the-land.md).** Comparative Indigenous sovereignty: Te Tiriti o Waitangi, Māori land struggle, U.S. treaties and allotment, and Australian frontier records.
- **022000 — [The Shorter Distance](022000-the-shorter-distance.md).** Indian railways, cables and telegraphs, the Suez Canal, and unequal distribution of benefits, labor, and control.

All four lectures carry paragraph-level display-only evidence ledgers with direct links and the narrowest available passage locators. They distinguish public full articles and primary texts from abstracts, publisher descriptions, archival catalogs and indexed excerpts. No target length or duration rule applies. Content authorship remains wholly inside this pack.


## Nationalism, migration, extraction and political membership (023000–026000)

- **023000 — [Who Speaks for a Nation? Associations, Constitutions, and the Politics of Representation](023000-who-speaks-for-a-nation.md).** Political organizations and claims to speak for peoples under empires, from India and Egypt to the Philippines and the Ottoman Empire.
- **024000 — [Across the Ocean, Against the Border: Migration, Exclusion, and Diaspora Politics](024000-across-the-ocean-against-the-border.md).** Transpacific Chinese migration, mutual aid, racially restrictive borders, diaspora political organization, and the limits of state archives.
- **025000 — [The Forest, the Mine, and the Ledger: Extraction at the Edges of Empire](025000-the-forest-the-mine-and-the-ledger.md).** Rubber in Congo and Putumayo, mineral production in southern Africa, concession power, colonial violence, documentary limits and Indigenous resistance.
- **026000 — [The Right to Count: Citizenship, Gender, Race, and the Vote](026000-the-right-to-count.md).** Citizenship versus enfranchisement, Reconstruction in the United States, Māori and Pākehā suffrage campaigns, and early twentieth-century Indian women’s political demands.

Every spoken paragraph in these four lectures maps to a display-only numbered evidence entry with direct source links, passage locators and limitations. Publisher summaries are not labeled as fully reviewed books. No lecture duration or word-count policy is in force. This content pack belongs exclusively to Agent B.

- **030000 — [The Public Enters the Argument: Labor, Protest, and Mass Politics After 1918](030000-the-public-enters-the-argument.md).** ILO institutions, Winnipeg strike, qualified suffrage, Egypt 1919, Indian non-cooperation, Caribbean veterans and political membership.

All four lectures separate narrated prose from display-only paragraph citation ledgers with direct URLs, passage locators and evidence-limit labels. Full-text scholarship, source translations, primary diplomatic texts and summaries are identified at their real review scope. No word-count or duration policy is in force.

## Interwar economic and political choices (031000–034000)

- **031000 — [The Price of Recovery: Debt, Credit, and the Uneven Twenties](031000-the-price-of-recovery.md).** Postwar reparations and loans, 1923 German hyperinflation, U.S. installment credit, Japanese banking, agricultural export markets, and contested gold-standard stability.
- **032000 — [Who Could Claim a Country? Anticolonial Organizing Between the Wars](032000-who-could-claim-a-country.md).** Civil disobedience in India, New Culture debates in China, constitutional West African organizing, women’s resistance in Nigeria and qualified Egyptian sovereignty.
- **033000 — [When the Price Falls Everywhere: The Great Depression Across Borders](033000-when-the-price-falls-everywhere.md).** Bank runs, debt deflation, gold-standard transmission, Gold Coast cocoa, Australian exports, colonial Indian debt, and Japanese and U.S. responses.
- **034000 — [Who Can Order a Society? Dictatorship, Planning, and Democratic Reform](034000-who-can-order-a-society.md).** Italian Fascism, Nazi dictatorship, Soviet collectivization and famine, Japanese expansion, and competing democratic, labor, and agrarian reforms.
