# The Living World: How Nature Works

This is a new, **in-progress** natural-history audio track. It is meant to complement, not duplicate, `tts-deep-time` (the history of the universe and of life) and `tts-physical-world` (foundational physical laws). Its organizing question is how contemporary organisms and ecosystems work, and how experiments reveal those mechanisms.

## Listening and editorial design

- Each essay is a complete audio lecture, not an abbreviated list of facts or a planned stub. No prior installments are required.
- Explain enough biology for an intelligent newcomer without hiding technical mechanisms from science enthusiasts. A mechanism, a concrete organism or experiment, a rival explanation, and the limits of evidence should matter more than a quota of examples.
- Speak naturally. Keep bibliographic details, source links, technical distinctions, and suggested readings outside the spoken section when they are more useful as a reference than as narration.
- Source records live in `metadata/sources.json`; lecture-specific evidence anchors live in `metadata/ID.json`. Every anchored passage is present verbatim inside the spoken region. Each companion has accessible Wikipedia orientation links alongside research and agency sources.
- No fixed minimum, maximum, or target duration. All topic and length decisions are editorial.
- Content is text only. No downloaded images, TTS artifacts, server changes, or global registries.

## Boundaries and connections

The forest lecture discusses fungal networks with a source-critical qualification, rather than treating popular 'mother tree' narratives as established general biology. The sensory lecture distinguishes receptor anatomy from behavior and subjective experience. The soil lecture distinguishes nutrient forms, microbial processes, and carbon stocks. The evolution lecture distinguishes selection from drift, analogy from homology, and historical contingency from a predetermined direction. The insect lecture keeps quorum experiments separate from claims about queen leadership. Plant electrical signaling is presented without anthropomorphic consciousness claims. The fungi lecture distinguishes symbiotic exchange from universal mutual aid. The microbial lecture distinguishes genetic capacity from measured metabolism and biofilm tolerance from genetic resistance.

The new hidden-interactions listening route connects the insect, plant, fungal, and microbial essays.
