# The Decameron — Alessandro and the Disguised Abbot

Three Florentine brothers inherit wealth and squander it through lavish spending. For a time they seem to enjoy the signs of success; then credit fails and their resources disappear. Their young nephew Alessandro has been helping to conduct their business abroad, in England. His own hopes now become entangled in political disorder there. With the old arrangement no longer secure, he decides to leave the country and make his way toward Rome.

On the road he joins a traveling party led by a person dressed as an abbot. The supposed abbot is young, commanding, and surrounded by attendants who treat the traveler with extraordinary respect. Alessandro's courtesy and competence make him welcome. An attraction grows between him and the person he believes to be a churchman. The abbot finally finds a moment to speak privately with him, and the two arrange to share a room. Alessandro is disturbed by what seems to be an inappropriate proposal from a superior cleric. The stranger then reveals the secret: the abbot is in fact a woman disguised in religious clothing.

She is a daughter of the king of England, fleeing a marriage that has been arranged for her. Her costume and her household's obedience have allowed her to travel without being recognized. She chooses Alessandro as the man she wishes to marry. Once he understands who she is and what she intends, their connection changes from bewildering intimacy into a plan for marriage. They arrange the necessary steps, and political circumstances that had ruined Alessandro's household become the route by which he enters a royal family.

The pair eventually return to England. Her father accepts the marriage and Alessandro gains position and wealth enough to repair the misfortunes of his uncles. A family that had moved from extravagant prosperity into ruin has found an ending so favorable that no business calculation could have predicted it. Where the brothers exhausted their own resources, the nephew's fortunes turn on flight, a disguise, a chance meeting, and a woman's decision to trust him.

Pampinea's tale is full of the conventions of romance: hidden identity, royal parentage, perilous travel, and marriage as restoration. It is also a story about who has the power to choose. The princess must take on the public appearance of a man and a religious superior to evade a marriage she does not want. Alessandro must learn to distinguish a frightening surface from the person behind it. The story treats the revelation as liberating, but its happiness also depends on a royal parent's eventual willingness to recognize a match made without his initial permission. Fortune brings the lovers together, but the woman's escape from a planned marriage has created the possibility that Fortune can act upon.
