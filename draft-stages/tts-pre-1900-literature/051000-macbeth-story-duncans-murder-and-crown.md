# Macbeth — Story: Duncan’s Murder and the Crown

It is night at Inverness. Banquo cannot sleep easily. He mentions troubled dreams of the three witches, while Macbeth pretends not to have been thinking about them. Macbeth sees a dagger suspended in the air, its handle pointing toward his hand. He reaches for it and finds nothing. The blade appears to acquire blood. He calls it a product of his fevered mind, yet also follows the direction it indicates. The scene does not require us to decide whether the vision is supernatural or psychological. It puts an image of murder before the murderer's eyes while the victim is sleeping offstage.

Lady Macbeth has drugged Duncan's attendants. She hears an owl and waits for Macbeth to return. The murder itself is not displayed to the audience. Macbeth comes back carrying the guards' daggers, terrified by what he has done. He reports hearing voices and becoming unable to say a word of prayer. He believes that he has destroyed his own sleep. Lady Macbeth focuses on the immediate problem: the bloody knives must be placed with the sleeping attendants, and their faces marked. When Macbeth cannot return, she does it herself. She tells him that water will wash the evidence from their hands. It is a statement about practical concealment, not a reliable forecast of conscience.

The castle's gate is battered by knocking. A porter staggers toward it and jokes that he might be opening the entrance to hell, admitting sinners of different kinds. This ugly little intermission matters. A crime committed in private is about to enter the morning's public order, and comic speech prepares the audience for the terrible discovery. Macduff and Lennox have come to wake the king. Macbeth directs Macduff toward Duncan's chamber. Macduff comes back horrified. Alarm spreads; guests rush from their rooms; the king is found murdered in the house of a loyal lord.

Macbeth then announces that he killed the two servants found beside Duncan's body. He claims anger and loyalty: how could he restrain himself when he saw the murdered king? But those dead men can no longer speak. Their killing has conveniently eliminated the only immediate suspects whom the conspirators arranged to frame. Lady Macbeth collapses or appears to faint, and the room divides its attention. Shakespeare does not settle whether the faint is strategic. The audience knows that the evidence has been manufactured, while the characters can only reconstruct the morning from fragments and testimony.

Duncan's sons understand the danger. Malcolm resolves to flee to England, and Donalbain to Ireland. Their departure is defensible self-preservation, but it can be made to look like guilt. The state has no trustworthy process for deciding who committed the murder. Macbeth can exploit the suspicion created by their absence, even though the audience has watched him arrange the crime. We have arrived at a troubling political question: once an insider controls the facts available to everyone else, how does anyone distinguish the loyal witness from the criminal?

Outside, reports of unnatural darkness and alarming behavior among animals make Scotland feel disordered. They are presented as signs of a world disturbed by regicide, not as meteorological evidence for modern investigators. Ross and an old man exchange news; Macduff says that Macbeth has gone to Scone to be crowned. The young princes are far away, and the killer is moving into the seat of legitimate authority. Macduff's decision to return home rather than attend the coronation begins to show that not everyone will accept the new settlement.

The play's first killing has created a need for more killing. Macbeth has secured the crown, but he does not possess the calm he imagined a crown would bring. Duncan's murder required him to silence his hesitation; its concealment required him to kill the attendants; its political outcome depends on the flight and vulnerability of Duncan's heirs. Lady Macbeth's image of water has not produced peace. The noise at the gate opened a world in which the murdered ruler's house continues to function, but every public explanation of its new government has been poisoned.
