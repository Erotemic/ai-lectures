# Literary Classics Before 1900

The guided survey follows works by publication period, from medieval texts through the nineteenth century. Complete story orientation precedes interpretation; connected fragments have been combined into one manuscript. Longer works retain meaningful divisions between narrative movements or between the story and criticism. The main route selects one to three lectures per work; the Decameron and Journey to the West also have separately curated entrances to substantially longer studies.

The Canterbury Tales main route chooses the company/selected-tales lecture and the interpretation lecture; the other two tale lectures remain in the full study. The Decameron keeps ten full day lectures and two criticism lectures, reached from its compact entrance. Flatland remains one complete short lecture because its material is coherent. Journey to the West is in progress through the selected witness’s chapter 87; continue with [the writer handoff](notes/journey-west-continuation-handoff.md), not with an assumed unassigned playback ID. Its section selector includes subsequent indexed additions automatically.

The full pre-1900 source index retains its authoring order while the Journey writer is active. Listening order is explicitly set by the survey and work studies below.

## Sources, narration, and continuation

`track.json` owns the current local manuscripts and measured lengths. `navigation.json` defines the survey and study routes; numeric IDs identify sources and do not determine teaching order. Absorbed parts have been removed from the playable index and filesystem. Their attribution, source claims, and original component identities remain in the surviving sidecars’ consolidation records and Git history.

Each manuscript has one spoken marker pair. Bibliographies, source limitations, exercises, and other companions remain outside the narrated region. `metadata/ID.json` records exact spoken anchors and source IDs; `metadata/sources.json` records access limits and bibliographic identities. Retold events, historical claims, an author’s documented views, and editorial interpretations remain distinct. Do not silently substitute a film, sequel, or another edition for the named literary work.

Follow [LECTURE_STYLE_RULES.md](../../LECTURE_STYLE_RULES.md) when revising narration. Add new IDs only after checking the current pack; preserve inherited provenance and attribute new writing to the actual runtime model. No TTS run, media build, or repository-wide test is required for a routine source addition.
