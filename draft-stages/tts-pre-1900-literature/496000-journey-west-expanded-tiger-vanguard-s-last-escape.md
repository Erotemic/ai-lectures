# Tiger Vanguard's Last Escape

Wukong stood before the entrance to Yellow Wind Cave and called for its master to send out the monk. The sentries began to shout. Deep inside, the noise reached the chambers where Tiger Vanguard had been congratulating himself on his capture.

The guard who hurried to him described a small, fierce monkey with a staff, demanding the release of the Tang monk and threatening to break the cave apart. Tiger Vanguard took up his copper blades. He had tricked Sun Wukong once. Surely a creature clever enough to seize the master could deal with the disciple at the door.

He went out with a company of lesser demons carrying flags and drums. The flags flapped in the mountain air; the drums beat against the stone. It was an attempt at a proper military display, but the sight of Wukong waiting alone seemed only to provoke him further. Tiger Vanguard demanded to know why an insignificant monk had the nerve to disturb his king's household.

Wukong answered that a bandit who abducted his master had lost the right to complain about bad manners. He knew perfectly well who stood before him. The two blades were the same ones that had met Bajie's rake. The skin was the same empty disguise that had drawn them from Xuanzang's side. The creature would return the monk or answer for the deceit.

Tiger Vanguard confirmed that he had taken the pilgrim and boasted that the monk would soon become a meal for his ruler. Wukong's anger sharpened. He lifted the staff and advanced.

This contest was shorter than the earlier fight. Tiger Vanguard's blades flashed in front of his face. Wukong pressed through the defenses and drove him backward. The demon threw up gusts and dust, but no imitation tiger could now persuade Wukong to forget the opponent in front of him. The staff struck against the copper swords again and again. Before long Tiger Vanguard's arms weakened and his steps became clumsy.

The creature fled. He did not turn back toward the cave, where he had already claimed an easy victory over the Great Sage. Instead he ran up the slope, hoping to vanish into the rocks beyond the fighting. Wukong followed, shouting threats over the clatter of the demon's retreat.

There was a chance here that neither warrior could see from the narrow path. Higher on the ridge Bajie had settled in a concealed hollow with the white horse. The bags lay close at hand, and he was waiting for word from his senior disciple-brother. His instructions had been to stay where he was. He had obeyed them.

Bajie heard the pursuit before he saw the figures. A rush of feet came over the stone, then the clang of a blade striking iron. Tiger Vanguard appeared around the bend, running so hard that he scarcely looked toward the hollow. Wukong followed him, staff raised.

Bajie recognized the fleeing demon at once. He set the horse safely aside, stepped out of concealment, and brought down the nine-toothed rake across the creature's path. Tiger Vanguard had escaped one attack by trading places with a tiger skin. He could not escape this one. The rake struck him, and the wound was fatal.

Wukong reached the body a moment later. He saw Bajie standing over their enemy, breathing heavily, the rake still in his hands. The Great Sage was delighted. If Bajie had not been there, the demon might have found some new way to slip away. Instead the two of them had caught him between pursuit and a guarded retreat.

Bajie wanted to be certain. Was this really the creature who had carried off Xuanzang? Wukong assured him it was. He added that the younger disciple deserved the credit for finishing the fight. It was a compliment Bajie enjoyed hearing from a brother who had mocked his appetite and ears at every opportunity.

They had, however, won only part of what mattered. Tiger Vanguard was dead, but the monk was still a prisoner. Wukong explained that the cave's ruler intended to eat him. The death of the patrol officer would not in itself undo the ropes in the rear garden.

Bajie agreed to continue guarding the horse and the baggage. He offered to intercept the king as he had intercepted the vanguard if Wukong could drive him back up the ridge. Wukong took hold of the dead tiger and dragged it toward the entrance as proof that his threats could not be dismissed.

Behind the gate, survivors of the escort scattered through the tunnels with torn flags and useless drums. They had gone out behind a confident commander and returned without him. The message they brought to Yellow Wind King was plain: Sun Wukong was alive, the first attack had failed, and the great monkey was standing outside the cave with the body of its best fighter.

The king now faced a different choice. He could stay hidden while the monkey broke his gates, or go out and fight. He called for his armor and his long three-pronged fork.

Wukong waited at the entrance. The dead tiger lay at his feet. Somewhere behind those walls Xuanzang was still tied to a post, wondering whether the voices outside belonged to the servants who would cook him or the disciples who might save him.
