# The Decameron — Ruggieri and the Chest

After nine stories of love frustrated, endangered, or destroyed, Dioneo claims his customary freedom to end the day with a story of a different kind. The company has heard of secret marriages, an execution at sea, a pot of basil, and a dish whose meaning was concealed from the person eating it. Dioneo's own tale also features secrecy and a body that seems dead. But in his hands these ingredients produce a farce in which the feared death proves temporary.

In Salerno, an elderly physician named Master Mazzeo della Montagna has a much younger wife. She takes as her lover a man called Ruggieri da Jeroli, whose reputation is not especially good. Their meetings must be concealed from her husband. One day the physician prepares a powerful sleeping draught for a patient who is to undergo treatment. The medicine is left where Ruggieri can find it during a visit, and he drinks it without knowing what it is.

The effect is alarming. Ruggieri falls into a deep, motionless sleep. Neither the wife nor her servant can wake him. Believing he has died, the two women panic. They fear that the discovery of a man's body inside the physician's house will expose the affair and invite a charge of murder. Their next decision is improvised rather than wise: they put him into a large chest that stands near the house and arrange to remove the apparent corpse from sight.

During the night, two moneylenders who have been looking for a useful chest steal it. They carry it away and bring it into their own lodging. When Ruggieri recovers consciousness, he finds himself in a strange room. His efforts to leave bring him into a fresh calamity. The people whose property surrounds him take him for a thief. The man who was mistaken for dead is now accused of entering a house to steal.

The accusation gathers force because every witness knows only one stage of the story. The doctor's wife knows that Ruggieri collapsed after drinking from the wrong vessel. The people who moved the chest know that it held what appeared to be a corpse. The moneylenders know they acquired the chest by theft but cannot publicly admit that fact. The officers encounter a confused young man in a room where he has no evident right to be. Each interpretation is plausible from a small angle and false when treated as the whole account.

The wife's servant eventually provides the crucial explanation. Her account of the mistaken death and the sleeping draught makes sense of the locked chest and the bewildered prisoner. Ruggieri escapes execution, and the moneylenders' own conduct becomes part of the comic reversal. What looked like the prelude to an irreversible tragedy is revealed as a chain of concealments, misunderstandings, and lucky delays.

Dioneo is not pretending that everything in the story is admirable. The lovers deceive a husband; they dispose of a body they have not verified to be dead; the chest is stolen; judicial authorities initially misunderstand the evidence. Yet a life is saved because somebody can finally reconstruct the sequence of events. The story's exuberance comes from the widening gap between how events appear and what actually happened.

The sleeping potion gives the fourth day a deliberate counter-image to its earlier deaths. Gabriotto died suddenly and was carried away by Andreuola; Ruggieri seems to die suddenly and is hidden away by his lover. Andreuola's truth was met with suspicion; Ruggieri's defense also seems impossible until a witness tells the whole story. But Dioneo's dead man wakes. His comic privilege allows a return from the edge of tragedy that the other narrators have withheld.

As the company prepares to leave Filostrato's day behind, its ruler passes authority to Fiammetta. The next day will be devoted to love that reaches happiness after troubles and reversals. Dioneo has already moved the listeners toward that brighter prospect. His concluding tale does not undo the nine previous losses. It shows instead that the arrangement of stories can change how an audience feels about the same hazards. A chest can be a coffin or the hiding place of a man who will stand up again. The next story will decide which.
