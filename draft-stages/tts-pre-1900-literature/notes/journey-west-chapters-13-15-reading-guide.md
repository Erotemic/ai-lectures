## Narrative details worth retaining

**The first loss is permanent.** Xuanzang leaves with two unnamed human attendants. General Yin's guests eat them while he remains bound. Taibai rescues Xuanzang, not the two men. Their deaths explain his later fear when Boqin must turn back.

**The hunter is not an exotic miracle.** Liu Boqin knows the terrain, hunts dangerous animals, and protects a traveler within the region he inhabits. His mother and wife solve the practical problem of a vegetarian guest in a meat-centered household. Xuanzang, in turn, provides religious service for Boqin's dead father. The exchange cuts both ways.

**Wukong is already named Wukong.** Xuanzang does not invent that religious name at his release. Subodhi gave it to him long ago. Xuanzang calls him Sun Xingzhe, Sun the Pilgrim, as a new traveling title. A giant monkey with a lifetime of rebellion becomes a disciple who carries packs, washes, and sews his tiger skin.

**The six highwaymen have allegorical names.** They bear names attached to the six forms of sensation and perception: seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, bodily experience, and mental intention. But the physical violence is also part of the story. They attack, Wukong kills them, and Xuanzang protests that a religious disciple cannot settle every threat by slaughter. The allegory does not cancel the dispute.

**The headband is painful and deceptive.** Guanyin supplies the disguise and the formula. Xuanzang misrepresents the cap and uses the spell to enforce obedience. The scene gives him authority over a being far stronger than himself but does not remove the conflict between his non-killing ethic and Wukong's violent protective instincts.

**The dragon is a failed introduction, not simply another monster.** The son of the Western Sea Dragon King has been waiting to serve the pilgrimage after Guanyin saves him from execution. He swallows Xuanzang's original white horse because he and Wukong fight without identifying themselves as members of the scripture mission. Guanyin changes the dragon into a *new* white mount; the swallowed animal does not return to life.

**The horse needs an actual saddle.** The river-spirit ferryman and the shrine where Guanyin sends saddle, bridle, and whip are practical parts of chapter 15. The shrine disappears after the donation is made, and Wukong teases Xuanzang for repeatedly bowing to a departed spirit. After about two peaceful months, the road reaches the next temple; do not begin the theft of the robe until chapter 16.
