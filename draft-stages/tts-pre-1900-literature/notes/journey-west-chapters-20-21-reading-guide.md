## Scene continuity and textual checks

1. Chapter 20 opens after Crow-Nest Chan Master and the Heart Sutra, with Bajie debating hunger and his vow. The household that feeds them before Yellow Wind Ridge is an **ordinary farming family**; it is not the divine apparition that later heals Wukong.
2. The old host advises turning east, Wukong refuses, the returning farm family is frightened by Bajie’s appearance, and Bajie eats roughly a dozen bowls of rice. His commitment to the journey and his appetite appear together.
3. Wukong samples the tail of the mountain wind and detects an animal odor. Tiger Vanguard first appears as an ordinary tiger, takes off his striped hide, and fights Bajie with a pair of red-copper knives.
4. The abduction requires **two appearances of the tiger skin**: first a bodily disguise and then a skin placed on a rock to lure both disciples away. The real creature returns on a gust of wind to seize Xuanzang while he recites the Heart Sutra.
5. Yellow Wind King is wary of Wukong despite his officer’s boast. He does **not** eat Xuanzang immediately; he orders the monk tied to a wind-stilling post in the rear garden and proposes waiting three to five days.
6. Wukong drives Tiger Vanguard up the slope, but **Bajie kills him** with the rake while guarding the horse and baggage. The chapter closes with Wukong dragging the body back to challenge Yellow Wind King.
7. Chapter 21 opens with the king himself. The king and Wukong fight about thirty rounds. Wukong’s hair-doubles are scattered by the **samadhi divine wind**; the second blast into Wukong’s face injures his fiery golden eyes. This is not a clean martial defeat by the trident.
8. The apparent farmstead in chapter 21 is a **temporary divine transformation**. The disguised elder provides sesame food and Three-Flower Nine-Seed eye ointment. At dawn it has vanished, and a note identifies the protectors. Bajie points out they deserve thanks rather than Wukong’s scolding.
9. Wukong enters the cave as a **mosquito**, confirms Xuanzang is alive and still bound, and hears Yellow Wind King reveal his own fear of **Lingji Bodhisattva**. Taibai Jinxing then appears as an elderly traveler and points toward Little Sumeru, about three thousand li away.
10. Lingji has already subdued the wind demon once, using the power entrusted to him by the Buddha, and allowed it to retire under conditions. The flying-dragon staff, transformed into an eight-clawed golden dragon, catches the king and reveals a **yellow-furred marten**. Lingji stops Wukong from killing it and takes it to the Buddha for judgment.
11. The protagonists then **kill the cave’s remaining demon inhabitants** before freeing Xuanzang; the source does not portray a bloodless clearing of the cave. They eat vegetarian food there and resume the pilgrimage. Chapter 22 begins the Flowing-Sand River adventure.
