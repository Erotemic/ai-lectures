## Witnesses and teaching text

The general retelling follows a contemporary combined *Hamlet* reading text,
not an asserted author-final script. The First Quarto (Q1, 1603), Second
Quarto (Q2, 1604/05), and First Folio (F1, 1623) differ materially. Q1 is far
shorter and has different names and scene placement; Q2 and F1 have unique
readings and passages. The Folger edition openly identifies its decisions.
Do not imply that a quotation not present in a particular early witness must
be a later error, or read a modern composite as evidence for one staged text.

## Literary inheritance

The Amleth story appears in Saxo Grammaticus, was reshaped by Belleforest,
and reached earlier English stages through a now-lost *Hamlet*. A lost play is
not an inspectable primary text; a confident attribution of it to Thomas Kyd
would overstate the evidence. Shakespeare's distinctive treatment of interior
speech, theatre and the ethics of revenge should be explained alongside those
inherited elements.

## Plot and interpretive cautions

- A ghost reports the old king's murder; the audience later hears Claudius
  privately acknowledge guilt. Hamlet does not hear that prayer.
- Hamlet's apparent derangement includes deliberate performance, but neither
  the audience nor other characters can assign every utterance to a single
  category without interpretation.
- Polonius is killed behind the hanging; Hamlet cannot know the victim when he
  thrusts his sword.
- Gertrude reports Ophelia's death offstage, and the funeral raises unresolved
  questions about intentionality. Avoid a categorical pronouncement of suicide
  or accident. See the 2022 *Essays in Criticism* article in `sources.json`.
- Gertrude's knowledge of the murder, and which stage actions imply knowledge,
  remain choices of argument or performance rather than established narration.
- Claudius plans Hamlet's execution in England; Hamlet redirects the death
  warrant toward Rosencrantz and Guildenstern; the final fencing scheme uses
  both a poisoned blade and poisoned wine.

## Evidence access and limitations

Directly consulted Folger plot, edition information, and excerpts of the play,
plus Royal Shakespeare Company plot, key-moments, and source-history pages.
This is a structurally checked pedagogical retelling; it is not a verse-by-
verse collation against Q1/Q2/F1 and it does not purport to resolve disputed
editorial reconstructions. The critical lectures make original interpretive
arguments and identify staging possibilities rather than claims of fact.
