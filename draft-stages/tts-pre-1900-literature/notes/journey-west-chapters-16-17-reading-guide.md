## Details to preserve and discuss

- **The elder is human.** Chapter 17 calls him Jinchi (金池) and says the black bear's teachings helped him with breathing and longevity. Chapter 16 gives his age as 270. The monks' costly robes and the elder's dazzling possessions form an ironic setting for the guest's sacred robe.
- **The original murder proposals differ.** Guangzhi proposes a knife attack and hidden burial; Guangmou argues that the hairy attendant might be too strong and suggests destroying the guest quarters by fire, while blaming the visitors. Wukong detects the plot in bee form.
- **Wukong does not start the original fire, but deliberately makes it worse.** He borrows a fireproof covering from the Wide-Eyed Heavenly King to protect Xuanzang, the horse, and baggage. Then he sends wind into the other buildings. The bear, initially arriving to help, takes advantage and steals the robe. Retelling the fire as pure rescue would distort Wukong's role.
- **The elder dies, and the monks are not all interchangeable.** Jinchi kills himself after the failed attack and disappearance of the robe. Wukong's search covers 230 people. The plot's responsibility and Wukong's retaliatory destruction should not be flattened into a collective moral verdict on all monks.
- **The black bear is strong and cultivated.** The novel gives him a refined cave, religious associations, and enough fighting skill to resist Wukong. He also steals the robe and plans a birthday banquet around it. Wukong kills the white-flowered snake and a demon courier; he subsequently kills the wolf-spirit Lingxuzi before Guanyin reproaches him. Neither witty religious conversation nor magical power makes the bear innocent.
- **Guanyin corrects and recruits rather than executes.** She criticizes Wukong's boasting and the wind-driven fire, then uses Lingxuzi's disguise and a pill into which Wukong transforms. The bear receives a constraining band and becomes guardian of Guanyin's mountain. The episode parallels the tight band already controlling Wukong.
- **The last episode ends before chapter 18.** Wukong departs with the robe; the next episode should dramatize his return to the monastery and the journey onward. Do not claim that Guanyin or Wukong burned the monastery from the outset, or that Wukong won a fair one-on-one duel against the bear.
