# Journey to the West — chapters 46–51: reading and evidence guide

**Prepared 16 November 2026.** Companion to six independently written, continuous English narrative manuscripts `519000`–`524000`, covering **six consecutive chapters** of the selected transmitted Chinese-language *Journey to the West*. These chapters are **fictional events**, not events in the historical life of the Tang monk Xuanzang. The spoken prose is original retelling, not quotation from a modern English translation. Each `metadata/ID.json` supplies exact scene-level narration anchors and chapter-specific source IDs.

## Chapter-by-chapter scene checks

### Chapter 46 — a contest whose rules keep changing

- The scene starts **after chapter 45's visible appearance of the four Dragon Kings** and does not repeat the entire rain contest. **Tiger Strength, Deer Strength, and Goat Strength remain alive at the beginning** and propose further trials.
- The first high-platform contest puts Xuanzang's learned stillness in tension with Wukong's inability to remain motionless. The original includes **fifty stacked tables**, the Daoist's biting insect, and Wukong's retaliatory **centipede**; the master remains seated while his opponent falls.
- The covered-object contest is *not* clairvoyance by Xuanzang: Wukong secretly changes the items. The royal garment becomes a poor garment; the peach becomes a pit after the monkey eats it; a hidden Daoist boy appears as a shaved novice.
- Tiger Strength's beheading is a real dangerous spectacle: Wukong restores his own severed head; a **yellow dog** conjured by Wukong carries away Tiger Strength's severed head, preventing its recovery. Tiger Strength's body reveals a tiger.
- Deer Strength opens his abdomen; Wukong's hawk steals the entrails, exposing a deer. Goat Strength survives boiling oil only while a **cold dragon** protects him; the **Northern Sea Dragon King** removes the cooling helper, and the goat dies. Wukong's moment of appearing dead in the cauldron is itself a sustained joke on Bajie and the court.
- **Chapter 46 ends with the king grieving for his former teachers.** The order releasing the captive monks and the diplomatic send-off belong to the opening of **chapter 47**. Do not claim that the king celebrates their deaths.

### Chapter 47 — Chechi's settlement and the sacrifice household

- Wukong advises the king not to replace Daoist religious persecution with Buddhist persecution; the novel invokes concord among **Buddhism, Daoism, and Confucian learning**. The court restores the freedom of **five hundred monks**.
- The new river is **Heaven-Reaching River** (*Tongtian He*) and is described as **eight hundred li across**. Its enormous width is a narrative obstacle, not a surveyed modern measurement.
- The **Chen** family is affluent yet subject to the river spirit's yearly **boy-and-girl child tribute**. The boy **Chen Guanbao** is seven and the daughter is eight, often named **Yichengjin / A Scale of Gold**. The family has begun mourning while both are alive.
- Wukong's apparently cruel proposal to use different children is a preliminary exchange, not the solution. He takes Guanbao's shape himself. Bajie reluctantly imitates the little girl; his conspicuous belly is part of the scene's humor, and Wukong corrects the transformation.
- The boys' and girls' offerings go to the shrine on **red lacquer ceremonial trays**. Chapter 47 stops with the counterfeit children waiting for the spirit; the fish encounter is **chapter 48**.

### Chapter 48 — the fish scales and the frozen road

- The river monster is named the **Great King of Numinous Response**, or *Linggan Wang*, and first comes to eat the children. Bajie asks the supposed little boy to be eaten first; he then attacks with his rake. The monster flees after **two large scales** are knocked loose, a clue to his fish nature.
- In the underwater palace the **mandarin-fish woman / attendant** conceives the second scheme: freeze the vast river and supply reassuring sights of apparent merchants crossing it, drawing the monk out. The onset of cold is a **supernatural sudden freeze**, not a natural winter crossing.
- Xuanzang's hurry is motivated by his promise of **three years** to the Tang court, already exceeded. The Chen family urges waiting for proper passage. The companions' debate is substantive; it is not a scene in which everyone accepts the ice uncritically.
- The preparations include **straw on the horse's hooves**, rakes or staffs used for balance, and attempts to test the surface. The monster breaks the ice **after the travelers have committed themselves** and takes Xuanzang into his underwater palace, confining him in a **stone chest/coffer**.
- Chapter 48 ends with the master captured, **not** with Guanyin's rescue.

### Chapter 49 — the goldfish, a basket, and an old question

- Wukong cannot fight underwater with the freedom he has on land. He uses a **small insect/louse disguise in Bajie's ear** to observe the underwater search; Bajie and Sha Wujing try to lure out the fish king. Xuanzang laments inside the stone coffer.
- The monster's palace originally belonged to an **old white turtle**, displaced **nine years earlier**. Its restoration explains the turtle's later gratitude; it is not a boat provided by the same fish spirit.
- The mandarin-fish advisor recognizes the danger and recommends barricading the palace. Force does not liberate Xuanzang. Wukong turns to **Guanyin**, who is found **working on a purple bamboo fish basket without her formal religious finery**. The practical simplicity of her appearance matters to the episode's later image-making.
- The Great King is **a goldfish that escaped from Guanyin's lotus pond**. The copper weapon is associated with an unopened lotus. Guanyin's basket captures him and removes him from the river; do **not** import the later explanation from the next monster's story into this one.
- The disciples release Xuanzang. The old turtle bears the party and horse across the river and asks that Xuanzang inquire of the Buddha **when he can shed his turtle shell and obtain human form**. Xuanzang **promises**. This is important later in the novel. Do not give the answer here.

### Chapter 50 — an effective boundary that no one stays inside

- Wukong draws a **protective circle using his staff** around the waiting party while searching for alms. Its failure is not a failure of the circle's power: Xuanzang and his companions **walk out of it** under Bajie's persuasion.
- An old householder **refuses food and strikes Wukong**; the monkey returns invisibly and **takes cooked rice** without permission. Retain the moral complication of the food-gathering instead of converting it to consensual hospitality.
- The attractive house is an enchanted trap. Xuanzang refuses to take strangers' rich clothing; **Bajie and Sha Wujing** take the quilted waistcoats, which magically bind them. The whole party is captured despite Xuanzang's refusal.
- The local spirits identify the **One-Horned King of Golden Cave on Golden-Helmet Mountain**. Wukong fights him but the **white shining ring** snatches the golden-hooped staff. **Do not reveal the One-Horned King's connection to Laozi's blue ox** before its source-chapter resolution in **chapter 52**.

### Chapter 51 — repeated defeats without resolution

- Wukong's despair at losing his staff is not merely slapstick. He seeks celestial intervention and asks the Jade Emperor for an inventory of missing divine personnel. **No heavenly official is missing**, which fails to solve the mystery.
- **Heavenly King Li, Prince Nezha, and the thunder gods** assist. Nezha assumes his **three-headed, six-armed form**, multiplies his assault, and loses all six weapons to the ring. It is not a battle won by superior ordinary swordsmanship.
- **Celestial fire** and its equipment are also taken. The **Yellow River Water Earl's half-basin of river water** cannot get through the demon's defenses; the spill harms the surrounding fields, and Wukong is told that released water cannot simply be collected again. Keep the collateral damage.
- **Unarmed combat** and Wukong's multiplied hair-figures do not solve the problem. Wukong enters Golden Cave in stealth, finds and **recovers his staff**, and escapes, but **Xuanzang and the other disciples remain captives**. The demon still has his ring and most of the stolen equipment. The source chapter does **not** conclude the confrontation.

## Continuity, evidence, and next step

The expanded story has now reached **selected-witness chapter 51** in **36 playable manuscripts**. The next six-source-chapter installment is **chapters 52–57**. Chapter 52 begins with the unresolved One-Horned King conflict; preserve the recovery of Wukong's staff at the end of 51 but **do not treat that as recovery of the demon's ring or liberation of the companions**. Follow chapter 52 before explaining the ring's origin. After its resolution, the journey turns toward the next episodes; verify source-chapter endpoints before dividing arcs.
