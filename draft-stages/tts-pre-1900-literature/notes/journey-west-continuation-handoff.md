## Where the original writer continues

Continue at the **opening of chapter 64**, immediately after lecture `536000`. At the end of chapter 63 the pilgrims left Jisai Kingdom, where the old Golden Light Monastery had been renamed **Dragon-Taming Monastery (Fulong Si)** following recovery of a Buddhist relic stolen by the Nine-Headed Son-in-Law's household.

In chapter 58 the **Six-Eared Macaque** was identified before the Buddha and killed by the real Wukong. Guanyin returned the monkey to Xuanzang. In chapters 59–61 Wukong borrowed, lost, recovered, and finally returned the true plantain fan. The Bull Demon King was captured and his second companion Princess Jade-Face was killed. The family's grievances about Red Boy's seizure continue to shape the relationships; his story was told in chapters 40–42. After cooling the Flaming Mountains with the fan, Wukong obeyed the local instructions to fan **forty-nine additional times** to extinguish the underlying fire.

In chapter 62 Xuanzang found monks punished for years in Jisai when their monastery's sacred relic vanished following a rain of blood; **two groups of innocents had died**, and surviving monks were shackled. Xuanzang and Wukong swept the **thirteen-story tower**. Wukong found the fish spirits sent as watchers by the Dragon King of All Saints at **Emerald Waves Pool**. The prisoners confessed that the king's son-in-law stole the relic and the princess separately stole a nine-leaf spirit fungus from Heaven to preserve its radiance. The king of Jisai released the accused monks.

In chapter 63 Wukong and Bajie fought the **Nine-Headed Son-in-Law**, whose bird form seized Bajie and carried him into the water; Wukong rescued Bajie disguised as a crab. The old dragon king was killed. **Erlang Shen and his six hunting brothers** joined the battle; his hound tore off one head, but the Nine-Headed Son-in-Law **escaped alive toward the northern sea**. Disguised as the wounded son-in-law, Wukong tricked **Princess All Saints** into surrendering two boxes: the stolen relic and the nine-leaf fungus. Bajie killed the princess, while Wukong spared the old dragon woman to bring her back as a witness. The king and pilgrims installed the relic at the top of the pagoda, swept each level with the fungus, and imposed on the dragon woman a **lifelong chain-guard duty**, with food once every three days. The tower shone again and was renamed. The pilgrims declined royal rewards and went west.

**Chapter 64 begins on the road beyond Jisai**, with the thorn-covered ridge and Xuanzang's literary conversations with tree spirits; no unresolved dragon-palace battle should carry over.
