# Journey to the West: chapters 76–81 — reading and source guide

**Scope:** Six original, scene-first English narrative manuscripts `549000`–`554000`, corresponding to chapters 76–81 of the linked Chinese selected witness of *Xiyou ji*. These are not English translations copied from an existing translation: spoken dialogue, descriptive bridges, and pacing are independently composed. Word counts and estimated listening time are descriptive, not editorial limits. The events belong to a novel and are not claims about historical Xuanzang.

**Primary witness and textual context:** [*Xiyou ji* on Chinese Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98), with individual chapter links below. [*Journey to the West* (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_West) introduces the novel and its transmission but is not used to determine scene boundaries. See [the expanded-retelling source map](journey-west-expanded-retelling-source-map.md) for edition questions, including the River Child insertion.

## Chapter 76 — A rope around the lion’s heart (`549000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第076回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC076%E5%9B%9E).

- **Exact opening:** Wukong is still in the lion's belly, drunk on the wine intended to force him out; Xuanzang and the other disciples believe he may be dead. This is the end state of lecture `548000`, not an event to resolve before this lecture.
- Wukong threatens the lion's insides and resists attempts to bring him out or kill him. The demon must negotiate rather than digest him.
- The monkey makes a **rope from his hair**, attaches it to the lion's heart, and emerges by way of the demon's **nose**, retaining power over him through the rope.
- The three rulers appear to yield and undertake to escort the travelers through the mountains in comfort. Wukong is suspicious but wants to take the party out of danger.
- The deceptive hospitality lasts long enough to make a peaceful passage appear plausible. At **Lion-Camel City**, a fully demon-occupied urban realm, the rulers reveal the false escort as a method of separating the three disciples and taking Xuanzang.
- **Closing limit:** The three disciples are fighting outside Lion-Camel City; Xuanzang has been carried inside. The capture and rescue continue in chapter 77.

## Chapter 77 — The city and the iron chest (`550000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第077回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC077%E5%9B%9E).

- The three disciples are overpowered amid the demon-controlled city, while Xuanzang remains vulnerable in the captors' hands.
- Attempts to escape fail even after aid involving the **Northern Sea's dragon king**; the monster city's organization is itself an obstacle.
- Reports that Xuanzang has been eaten shake Wukong. He cannot treat rumor as proof, but his usual power has failed against the third ruler, the vast roc.
- Wukong seeks help from **the Buddha**. The two elder demon kings are connected to holy mounts; the **roc** has a more entangled relationship to the Buddha's own family. The powers behind them must intervene directly.
- The lion and elephant are recovered, the roc is confronted, and Wukong searches for his master rather than simply celebrating the destruction of an army. **Xuanzang is found alive inside an iron chest**.
- **Closing limit:** The Lion-Camel ordeal ends here and the pilgrims resume the road. Bhikshu Kingdom begins in chapter 78.

## Chapter 78 — The 1,111 cages of Bhikshu (`551000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第078回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC078%E5%9B%9E).

- A new kingdom contains **1,111 children held in goose cages**, rather than imprisoned adults. It is the sight of the cages that forces the travelers to ask about the condition of the state.
- The king has been misled into believing a horrific cure for lost vitality requires the hearts and organs of young boys. His adviser is called the **state father**, and a beautiful woman has supplanted the responsibilities of the court.
- Wukong sends guardian spirits to move or conceal the children before the intended sacrifice; their apparent disappearance is part of the plot, not a mass killing.
- The king's interest turns to Xuanzang as a supposedly stronger source of restorative medicine. Wukong **assumes the monk's appearance** to enter the court in his place.
- The court debate contrasts an ethical teaching about governing and human welfare against a magical promise that treats the children as resources. Xuanzang remains safe outside while Wukong confronts the ruler.
- **Closing limit:** Wukong is ready to expose the false state father but has not yet rescued the whole kingdom. Chapter 79 contains the revelation and return.

## Chapter 79 — A kingdom of many hearts (`552000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第079回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC079%E5%9B%9E).

- Wukong confronts the king in Xuanzang's likeness, using a **magical display of multiple hearts** to challenge the premise that health can be purchased with other people's organs.
- The corrupt adviser flees and is pursued to his retreat. His consort is a **fox spirit**, and he himself is revealed as a **white deer**.
- The **Old Man of the South Pole** recovers the white deer, who belongs to his celestial household. The story does not present that retrieval as absolving the king of the decision to sacrifice children.
- The threatened boys are restored to their parents. Preserve the **1,111** figure and the public relief: the children did not die at the end of chapter 78.
- The court reforms and thanks the pilgrims; Xuanzang and companions leave Bhikshu at the chapter's end.
- **Closing limit:** The white deer episode is finished. The Black Pine Forest and its pleading captive belong to chapter 80.

## Chapter 80 — The captive in the Black Pine Forest (`553000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第080回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC080%E5%9B%9E).

- In the dark **Black Pine Forest**, Wukong sees ominous demonic vapor while the others rest. A young woman is found tied to a tree and says robbers bound her and left her to die.
- Wukong identifies danger and stops Bajie from untying the woman, but Xuanzang refuses to abandon someone apparently helpless. **The woman persuades him by an appeal only he hears.**
- The quarrel between protection and compassion should not be reduced to Wukong simply being correct and Xuanzang foolish: both characters are acting from established traits that have helped the pilgrimage in earlier episodes.
- The disciples arrange for the woman to travel with them rather than immediately leaving her in the forest. After a scare involving an old bell they find **Zhenhai Monastery**—ruined outer buildings, better-maintained inner quarters.
- Monks agree to shelter and feed the woman, giving her a place behind the image of a **Heavenly King**.
- **Closing limit:** The monastery has not yet suffered the deaths revealed in chapter 81. The woman is not unmasked and Xuanzang has not yet been abducted.

## Chapter 81 — The shoe and the Bottomless Cave (`554000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第081回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC081%E5%9B%9E).

- Xuanzang becomes ill at **Zhenhai Monastery** and lies sick for days. He worries that he cannot complete the pilgrimage. Wukong's teasing about a past-life grain of rice is a comic effort to encourage him.
- The resident monks reveal that **six novices have been killed over three nights**, with remains behind the monastery. They were not murdered before the party admitted the woman in chapter 80.
- Wukong takes the appearance of a **young novice** and waits awake in the hall. The young woman approaches and lures him toward the rear garden, intending to kill him as she has killed others.
- Wukong resumes his shape and attacks. She escapes by changing an **embroidered shoe** into her double; the fallen apparent woman becomes a shoe. **Wukong has not killed the demon.**
- While he pursues that false body, the demon **abducts the ill Xuanzang**. The disciples quarrel, but Sha Wujing urges cooperation instead of recrimination.
- The three return to the **Black Pine Forest** and question local earth gods. They learn she has taken Xuanzang south to **Trap-Sky Mountain (陷空山)** and **Bottomless Cave**. They transport the horse and luggage southward.
- **Exact closing limit:** They reach the mountain, and **Bajie is sent ahead to scout the cave**. The descent, deeper recognition of the demon, and rescue belong to chapters 82 and later. Her identity should not be announced as an established fact before the plot reveals its evidentiary basis.

## Next narrative hinge: chapters 82–87

Begin with **Bajie scouting the Bottomless Cave**, not with a rescue already achieved. Xuanzang is captive and the woman's full ties to divine figures remain to be uncovered. The demon's apparent death at Zhenhai Temple was only an embroidered shoe; do not describe her as killed. Chapters 82–87 are the next six selected-witness chapters to research, draft and annotate at their authentic boundaries. Keep dialogue independently reconstructed, retain primary chapter references and associated scene anchors, and do not impose a fixed lecture length.
