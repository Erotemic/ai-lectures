## Trace the handkerchief, not merely its final location

1. Othello had given Desdemona the handkerchief. It has symbolic value to him, not a publicly verified magical power.
2. When it falls, **Emilia picks it up**. She knows Iago has asked for it but does not know his full intended use.
3. **Iago takes and plants it** where Cassio can find it. This is deliberate fabrication of material evidence.
4. **Cassio finds it** in his lodgings and later asks **Bianca** to copy the embroidery. Bianca does not thereby become an accomplice.
5. **Iago stages an overheard conversation** with Cassio about Bianca, having conditioned Othello to hear talk of Desdemona. Bianca's appearance with the cloth seems conclusive only after this false frame is installed.
6. **Emilia reconstructs** the missing provenance in the final scene. She is killed while exposing her husband. The truth arrives after Desdemona's murder.

The complete story is narrated before close analysis. **Desdemona is innocent**; the audience has access to facts Othello lacks, and Iago's lies are never treated as reliable testimony about her.

## Dates, source and textual witnesses

- A performance of *Othello* at the court of James I is recorded for **1 November 1604**. Composition is usually estimated around 1603–1604, not known to a precise day. See [RSC dates and sources](https://www.rsc.org.uk/othello/past-productions/dates-and-sources/) and [Internet Shakespeare Editions textual introduction](https://hcmc.uvic.ca/eol/internetshakespeare.uvic.ca/doc/Oth_TextIntro/complete/index.html%3Fe%3DOth.html).
- Shakespeare drew major narrative material from **Giraldi Cinthio's** story in the *Hecatommithi*, published in the mid-1560s. Similarity in plot does not make the source's characterization, lessons, or final punishments identical to the play.
- Surviving early witnesses are the **1622 Quarto** and **1623 First Folio**, with the Folio around 160 lines longer in aggregate, plus smaller regions present in Quarto and absent in Folio. Origins of these differences are debated. The [Folger editorial introduction](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/othello/an-introduction-to-this-text/) discusses its editorial base and visible markers of variants.
- The stage's racial discourse is part of the evidence, not the lecturer's standpoint. **Moor** is a historically variable term, and claims about the protagonist's culture should not be reduced to modern one-to-one identity labels. Folger's [overview](https://www.folger.edu/explore/shakespeares-works/othello/about-shakespeares-othello/) supplies scholarly context. The [Internet Shakespeare Editions performance history](https://internetshakespeare.uvic.ca/doc/Oth_PerfHistory/index.html) and [Folger's casting scholarship](https://www.folger.edu/blogs/collation/color-of-character/) address later staging conventions, including the harmful history of blackface.
- Iago is responsible for the manufactured evidence and lethal schemes; that does not relieve Othello of responsibility for the decision to kill Desdemona. Othello's final self-account is not the independent verdict of the play.
- Roderigo dies in the street attack, Cassio survives, Emilia is murdered after disclosing the plot, Iago is captured and refuses further explanation. His ultimate punishment is **not enacted** in the ending.

## Questions for an active listener

1. What does Cassio actually say to Iago in the eavesdropping scene? What does Othello believe he has heard?
2. Who has independent evidence about the handkerchief at each step? Where does Iago conceal or invent the provenance?
3. What different protections do military rank, marriage, class and reputation provide Othello, Cassio, Desdemona, Emilia and Bianca?
4. At what points could Othello revise his conclusion? Why does he begin treating every denial as another sign of deception?
5. Does the 1622/1623 text you are reading contain the passage invoked by a given production or critic? Identify the edition before drawing conclusions.
