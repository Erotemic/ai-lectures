# Journey to the West: chapters 88–93 — reading and evidence guide

**Scope:** Six original English scene-first manuscripts, `561000`–`566000`, corresponding to selected Chinese-witness chapters 88–93. The novels' characters and supernatural incidents are fictional events, not factual testimony about the historical Xuanzang. All English dialogue and descriptive transitions are independently composed. Times, word counts, and chapter divisions are descriptive; lecture lengths follow the substance of each scene.

**Primary text:** [*Xiyou ji*, Chinese Wikisource chapter index](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98). Context and literary history: [*Journey to the West* (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_West) and [Xuanzang (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang). The online witness is a transmitted Chinese text, not a claim to reproduce the 1592 Shidetang edition leaf for leaf. The separately maintained [source and edition map](journey-west-expanded-retelling-source-map.md) documents the chapter-nine insertion and authorship caveats.

## Chapter 88 — Three princes of Jade Flower (`561000`)

Primary source: [西遊記/第088回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC088%E5%9B%9E).

- **Opening continuity:** The four pilgrims leave Fengxian after the successful rain and return to the western road. Their banter about Wukong's merit and Bajie's lost chance to feast belongs to this chapter's opening. Xuanzang asks an elderly traveler about the city ahead, **Jade Flower (Yuhua)**, a district of Tianzhu administered by a royal relative.
- The three royal sons train with a staff, rake, and pole and at first have little idea that the pilgrim's three companions are powerful supernatural practitioners. Bajie, Wukong, and Sha show the princes the **immense weight** and true nature of their weapons. The airborne display causes the princes and the entire city to venerate the visitors.
- The princes beg to learn. Wukong **refuses a monetary fee**, and the three disciples request **Xuanzang's permission** before accepting their own pupils. Wukong grants the young men strength through rites and breath, but wielding the real magical implements leaves ordinary people breathless.
- The court sets smiths to forging **lighter copies**, and the real staff, rake, and pole remain under the forge canopy while the smiths measure them. Their rays are visible at a distance; **the Yellow Lion** from Leopard Head Mountain's Tiger Mouth Cave notices, steals all three at night, and leaves them missing at the end of the chapter.
- **Closing boundary:** The theft takes place in chapter 88. Discovery by the smiths and the search for the thief begin chapter 89.

## Chapter 89 — The Yellow Lion's rake banquet (`562000`)

Primary source: [西遊記/第089回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC089%E5%9B%9E).

- The smiths find three **empty forge places**. Bajie initially accuses ordinary workers; Wukong acknowledges the disciples' carelessness in leaving treasured arms away from their usual protection.
- Wukong discovers two servants, **刁鑽古怪 and 古怪刁鑽** (names represented in narration as Crafty Oddity and Odd Craftiness), sent to buy animals to feed a celebration centered on the stolen **nine-toothed rake**. Wukong freezes the messengers, takes their identities and money, and plans the disguise.
- Wukong and Bajie take the two servants' appearances while Sha travels as a livestock dealer with pigs and sheep. On the road a second servant carries a formal invitation to the Yellow Lion's **grandfather, Jiuling Yuansheng (Ninefold Numinous Primal Sage)**.
- The three enter Tiger Mouth Cave. The Yellow Lion hesitates to show a stranger the stolen treasures, but the impostors persuade him. In the inner hall the rake is displayed centrally; the three pilgrims **reclaim their own weapons** and rout the defenders. The cave is burned; servants are killed, and the Yellow Lion escapes to his grandfather.
- The elder recognizes Wukong's reputation and comes with a company of lion demons. At the destroyed site, the real servants recount the imposture. The Yellow Lion's mourning is **explicit** in the Chinese text, not a seamless villain's defeat.
- **Closing boundary:** The combined lion force advances on Jade Flower while the city shuts its gates. The siege and capture of family and monk are in chapter 90.

## Chapter 90 — The nine-headed lion (`563000`)

Primary source: [西遊記/第090回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC090%E5%9B%9E).

- The three disciples engage the lion army outside the city; Bajie is taken captive. Wukong's multiplied bodies allow him and Sha to capture some lions for bargaining.
- The old lion sends its juniors out again, then flies over the city and seizes **Xuanzang, the prince of Jade Flower and all three sons** with multiple mouths, carrying Bajie as well. Wukong defeats and captures the juniors, but the civilians' hostage crisis deepens.
- Wukong and Sha seek the cave and are themselves captured or trapped in confrontation with the nine-headed ruler; Wukong later escapes and learns from local protectors that the elder is the escaped mount of **Taiyi Tianzun, the Celestial Worthy of Supreme Unity**. The lion-keeper had drunk Laozi's heavenly liquor and slept for three heavenly days, several years on earth.
- The Celestial Worthy descends; the lion prostrates himself on hearing its master. Wukong frees the party; the cave is burned. **The narrative retains the killing, flaying, and distribution of the other lions**; the elder lion alone is returned as a mount.
- The prince and sons thank the travelers; the disciples give further lessons; the court provides new robes rather than the refused gold. The city escorts the pilgrims west.
- **Closing boundary:** The Jade Flower episode concludes in chapter 90. The Lantern Festival in Jinping starts chapter 91.

## Chapter 91 — The false Buddhas of the Lantern Festival (`564000`)

Primary source: [西遊記/第091回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC091%E5%9B%9E).

- The pilgrims enter **Jinping (Gold-Level Prefecture)** and stop at **Compassionate Cloud Temple**. Xuanzang sweeps a tower and accepts the monks' invitation to view the Lantern Festival. The many lantern designs, moonlit crowds, and celebratory streets are given narrative space.
- At **Golden Lamp Bridge**, three enormous lamps burn **costly fragrant oil**; people believe **three Buddhas** descend to accept the offering. The burden of the oil falls on the prefecture. Wukong sees impostors, but Xuanzang bows with the crowd and is abducted with the oil.
- Wukong follows to **Green Dragon Mountain / Mysterious Flower Cave**. The three demons take the names **Cold-Averting, Heat-Averting and Dust-Averting** and have horned bodies; Xuanzang recounts his life and disciples to the frightened captors. Wukong fights the three for many exchanges; they summon bull-headed followers and eventually force him to retreat.
- Wukong returns to the temple, identifies the opponents as **rhinoceros spirits**, and consults Bajie and Sha. Sha insists that they must **return that night**, not rest until morning, because Xuanzang is captive.
- **Exact closing boundary:** The three disciples **set out to Green Dragon Mountain together** at the end of chapter 91. Wukong's firefly infiltration **begins chapter 92**, not chapter 91.

## Chapter 92 — Four wood stars and the rhinoceros kings (`565000`)

Primary source: [西遊記/第092回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC092%E5%9B%9E).

- Wukong enters as a **firefly**, finds Xuanzang shackled, and briefly opens his lock. The guards awaken, so **Xuanzang is recaptured** and Wukong fights his way out.
- The brothers challenge the three kings; an influx of bull-headed troops captures **Bajie and Sha**, leaving Wukong to fetch celestial assistance. He goes to Heaven, learns the rhinoceroses' relation to the **four Wood Creature Stars**, and secures the Jade Emperor's order for their deployment.
- The stars' appearance causes the rhinoceroses to flee; some stars clear the cave, rescue the captives, and burn the den. Wukong and the other stars pursue the horned beasts into the **Western Sea**, where Dragon King **Ao Shun**, Prince **Mo'ang**, and marine troops intervene.
- **One rhino is killed** in pursuit despite Wukong's request that all be taken alive. Two return for judgment at Jinping; their horns are removed and the living prisoners executed. The fake-Buddha oil tribute is ended, and the townspeople lavish thanksgiving hospitality on the pilgrims.
- **Closing boundary:** The pilgrims leave **quietly before dawn** to escape another long round of feasts. Chapter 93 begins with the monks regretting their departure.

## Chapter 93 — The princess behind the wall (`566000`)

Primary source: [西遊記/第093回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC093%E5%9B%9E).

- The pilgrims approach Tianzhu's capital by way of **Hundred-Leg Mountain** and **Gold-Spreading Temple**, whose monks connect its grounds to **Jetavana (Anathapindika's garden)** and the story of gold laid over the site. Traders wait for dawn to pass a road troubled by centipede spirits.
- A **centenarian monk** tells Xuanzang and Wukong of a young woman found on the grounds after a sudden wind. She claims to be **Tianzhu's real princess**. The monk has imprisoned her in a narrow room, ostensibly to protect the temple from a possible demon, but he acknowledges her nightly weeping for her parents. Xuanzang and Wukong agree to investigate rather than presume guilt.
- At the capital an official tells them of the princess's public **embroidered-ball marriage selection**. The witness **explicitly tells the reader** that a supernatural impostor supplanted the true princess to exploit Xuanzang, but the court is deceived and the characters do not yet know the impostor's true identity.
- In the crowd she **deliberately throws the ball at Xuanzang**. He is frightened and accuses Wukong of conspiring; Wukong proposes that he go to court and summon his disciples so they may investigate the princess. The court sends a messenger to bring Bajie, Sha and Wukong.
- **Exact closing boundary:** The disciples are **summoned to court**, not yet released from the marriage predicament. The wedding negotiations and final recovery of the true princess belong to **chapters 94–95**. Do not name the impostor's real animal identity before chapter 95 in the retelling.
