## Keep these conceptual and textual distinctions

1. **Two wagers:** the Lord's permission to test Faust occurs without Faust's participation; Faust's own bargain with Mephistopheles involves satisfaction and a reciprocal service contract. The text should not be compressed into the time-limited contract familiar from Marlowe.
2. **Gretchen is not a disposable moral lesson:** she is a fully voiced character whose imprisonment and salvation close an independently tragic Part One. Her later spiritual presence does not undo the deaths of her mother, brother, and child.
3. **Faust's last words are spoken under error:** he is blind and imagines workers creating a public future while the Lemures are digging his grave. The formulation about asking a moment to stay has conditional force; interpretive disputes about fulfillment of the compact remain legitimate.
4. **Act V must include Philemon and Baucis:** the elderly couple and the saved wanderer are killed during the forcible removal of their home. Faust's wish to offer another estate does not absolve his command or erase their resistance.
5. **Do not make the Homunculus a modern scientific prediction:** he is a creature of poetic scientific fantasy and mythology; the sea scene depicts transformation, not ordinary human gestation.
6. **Do not treat the Helena act as literal history:** the juxtaposition of ancient Greece, medieval castle culture, Arcadia and Euphorion is a dramatic crossing of artistic worlds. Byron comparisons are interpretations, not a literal dramatic identity.
7. **Do not claim the paper-money satire explains all currency:** it criticizes political wishfulness and representation detached from material obligations in a particular play.
8. **Do not turn the eternal feminine into a sociological claim:** the ending is rich in Christian and poetic imagery; competing interpretive traditions exist, and the imagery does not fix universal gender traits.

## Source and version orientation

- Original German: Project Gutenberg ebooks [2229, *Faust I*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2229) and [2230, *Faust II*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2230), consulted as free reading texts. They are not diplomatic transcriptions of all print states.
- English orientation: [Bayard Taylor, *Faust* Part One](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14591) and [Part Two](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Faust_(trans._Bayard_Taylor)/Second-Part). This translation is in the public domain in the US; English metrical effects are translator choices.
- Earlier tradition: the 1587 *Historia von D. Johann Fausten* can be located in the [SLUB Dresden bibliographic record](https://katalog.slub-dresden.de/id/0-611932423); Marlowe's *Doctor Faustus*, 1604-based edited text, is at [Project Gutenberg 779](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/779). Marlowe also has a different surviving 1616 quarto; do not merge them without annotation.
- Goethe's development: early Faust manuscript material and the **1790 Fragment** preceded Part One's **1808** publication; Part Two was completed late in life and published **posthumously in 1832**. See the [University of Pennsylvania bibliographic guide](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/work?id=olbp109982) and the [Goethe University Frankfurt digital-edition discussion](https://aktuelles2.uni-frankfurt.de/english/digital-edition-of-faust-a-deep-insight-into-how-goethe-worked-on-his-masterpiece/index.html). The early manuscript copies and textual states are research topics, not one single canonical Urfaust volume.
- Biblical allusion: Mephistopheles explicitly calls attention to Naboth's vineyard in Act V; see [1 Kings 21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2021&version=KJV).

## Close-reading prompts

- Contrast the three theatrical prologues: who expects an audience, who expects a poem, and who expects an experiment in the value of human striving?
- At the compact, locate the exact grammar of Faust's promise. What must happen for the devil to claim victory? Compare it with the final declaration made while Faust is blind.
- Contrast Faust's reverence in Gretchen's empty room with his later decision to intrude into her life. What does he know, and what does he decline to acknowledge?
- In Part Two, compare the emperor's paper money, Helen's conjured image, and Faust's last vision. How does an image shape action even when its referent is absent or incomplete?
- In Act V, compare the language used about the public benefit of the new land with the surviving couple's attachment to their home. Which of those relations does Faust allow to have authority?

## Scope of verification

These are original pedagogical retellings and interpretations, not substantial reproductions of Goethe's verses or a critical edition. Selected structural and high-stakes details were checked against public-domain primary texts and bibliographic sources; this pass did **not** collate every German manuscript and print variant, did not evaluate all interpretive scholarship, and did not produce theatrical scripts or recordings. Historical facts and critical hypotheses are marked by their language; primary-source quotations and spellings should be checked in the reader's chosen edition before publication or close philological use.
