## Character continuity and difficult passages

1. **The fire is not over with chapter 17.** Chapter 18 begins with Wukong deliberately setting Black Wind Cave on fire after Guanyin takes the bear away. The rescued robe then returns to Xuanzang; the monastery's monks offer thanksgiving before the pilgrims depart. That action is separate from the earlier monastery fire.
2. **Gao Cai is not a voluntary guide at first.** He tries to leave on his employer's errand. Wukong detains him physically, making the scene comic by his own standards but not a willing recruitment.
3. **Old Gao's marriage arrangement has an economic rationale.** He has three daughters and no son, and wants the youngest daughter's husband to reside in the family and work the land. Zhu's feats at farming are real within the story, as are his escalating appetite and apparent transformation. The squire's concern for family reputation should not displace his daughter's confinement.
4. **Cuilan's confinement is not a consensual marriage scene.** The story establishes that she has not been allowed to visit her family for about six months. Wukong frees her *before* taking her appearance. The figure whom Zhu tries to embrace on the bed is Wukong in disguise, not Cuilan.
5. **The former marshal's offense is serious.** Chapter 19's autobiographical verse depicts a drunken Tianpeng forcibly pursuing Chang'e, who repeatedly refuses him; the episode should not be romanticized as an innocent romance. Taibai pleads for commutation of the capital sentence, and Tianpeng receives two thousand blows and is cast down. His pig rebirth is explained as a mistaken womb on descent.
6. **Neither man's story makes him virtuous.** Wukong argues that Zhu brought genuine agricultural value to the family; this is an ironic counterargument after the rescue, not a moral defense of imprisoning Cuilan. Their fight reveals high abilities and an affinity as former heavenly figures, but their wrongdoing differed.
7. **The name sequence matters.** Zhu's former name is Zhu Ganglie. Guanyin has already bestowed the Buddhist name Zhu Wuneng. Xuanzang, on accepting him, adds the familiar name Bajie, referring to the precepts. Wukong entered discipleship earlier and is Bajie's *senior* disciple-brother.
8. **Conversion has conditions and uncertainty.** Zhu takes an oath, burns his cave on Wukong's demand, surrenders his rake, and is bound before presentation to Xuanzang. Wukong gives some of Gao's proposed gold/silver to the servant Gao Cai; Xuanzang refuses the larger gift. Bajie requests new clothes and jokes about returning if the pilgrimage fails. The narrative does not provide Cuilan with a spoken reconciliation at departure.
9. **The Heart Sutra is a turning point, not merely a route marker.** Crow-Nest Chan Master knows Bajie and transmits the teaching orally to Xuanzang. The modern English wording in lecture 492000 is an independent rendering and narrated explanation of the Chinese chapter's cited sutra, not a reproduction of a copyrighted modern translation. The warning about the future water monster belongs to chapter 19; the next adventure itself does not begin until chapter 20.
