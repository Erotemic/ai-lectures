# The Tire That Went Soft

"Psssss," said Rex.

"What was that?" asked Gus.

"A tire," Rex said.

"You are not a tire," Gus replied.

"I am practicing being one. Pssss."

They were sitting outside the Red Door Garage with Jack and Aunt Jo. Ms. Pam had set out a clean old tire on a low stand beside the window so the friends could see it.

It belonged to Aunt Jo's yellow work van.

The tire looked round, but its lower edge drooped.

"It used to be much fatter," Rex said.

"It was filled with air," Ms. Pam explained. "Now a little hole has let the air out."

Jack studied it. "Air is invisible. How can it make a big tire?"

Pam pointed to the round shape of a properly filled tire nearby.

"A tire holds air inside it, and the air helps it hold its shape."

Gus stared at the two tires.

"So one tire has a big lunch of air," he said, "and the other had its lunch taken away."

"Air isn't lunch," Jack said.

"Good," said Gus. "Because I am hungry for a sandwich."

Rex sniffed the old tire, then the new one.

"They smell exactly like tires," he announced.

"That's a helpful report," Aunt Jo said.

"It is my best nose report today."

Ms. Pam showed Jack a small tool with a dial. She called it a pressure gauge.

A little gauge told Ms. Pam how much air was pressing inside the new tire.

The pointer moved while Pam checked the reading.

"It has numbers!" Jack said.

"It does," Pam replied. "Different vehicles need the right amount. I check the van's instructions."

Jack drew a tire in his blue notebook. Inside the tire he drew tiny dots.

"That's the air," he said.

"Your air has spots," Gus observed.

"Only in the drawing."

"I would like spotted air," Gus said. "Then we could see where it went."

Molly came along with Farmer Ruth, carrying her blue rope toy in her mouth. She set it down on the bench.

"What happened to the yellow van?" she asked.

"One tire got soft," Rex told her.

Molly pressed a paw gently into the grass. "Like this?"

The grass bent under her paw and sprang back.

"Not quite," Jack said. "The tire lost some of its air."

Molly's ears perked up. "Could we put all the air back in?"

"This old tire is damaged," Pam said. "It won't be going back on the van. I have a proper new one ready."

Rex looked from one tire to the other.

He had an idea.

"When a tire goes flat, does a truck sit lower on that side?"

"It can," Ms. Pam answered. "That's one clue that something isn't right."

Gus crouched until his green cap almost touched the ground.

"Am I flat?"

"No," Rex said.

Gus crouched lower.

"Now?"

"You are a bear," said Molly.

"A very squashed bear," said Gus.

Everyone laughed, including Pam.

Then she moved the tires back to the work area. The new one was prepared, but the yellow van still needed its wheel fitted and checked.

"We'll finish the job next time," she said.

Outside, the afternoon breeze stirred the flowering shrub beside the bench.

Jack closed his notebook with a soft tap.

At home Rex tried saying pssss one more time. His dark ear went up and his light ear flopped down.

Then he curled up on his rug, perfectly happy to remain a puppy and not a tire.
