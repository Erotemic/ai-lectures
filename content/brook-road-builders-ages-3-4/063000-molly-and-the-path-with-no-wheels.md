# Molly and the Path with No Wheels

Molly knew the walk from Green Gate Farm to Pine Park very well.

First came the farm path. Then the little wooden bridge. Then the short path by the brook. And after that, the park with Pancake Hill and the tall pine.

She could make the trip with her eyes almost closed.

Almost.

Farmer Ruth said it was better to keep them open.

That morning Rex, Gus, Jack, and Aunt Jo came over Little Bridge to visit. Rex and Molly gave one another their one-wag greeting.

"We're making a map for the park," Rex said.

"Jack says it needs two kinds of lines," Gus added. "One for roads and one for bears."

"One for walking paths," Jack corrected.

"That's what I said. Bears walk."

Molly looked at Jack's drawing. The dotted line went across the brook. The solid one bent around a big loop toward the long farm road.

"Your dotted line stops right here," she said, putting one white paw on the paper.

Jack frowned. "Does it?"

"The farm path goes all the way from the bridge to our green gate. You left that little bit out."

"I did."

Jack took his pencil and added the missing piece.

Rex sniffed the paper thoughtfully.

"It doesn't smell like the farm."

"It's a drawing," Jack said.

"I can help with the smelling part," Rex said.

They set off along the walking route with Aunt Jo, leaving the drawing safely inside Jack's notebook until they reached the bridge.

The farm's red tractor stood by the barn, its big back wheels washed clean from its last job. The hay was stored under cover. Beside the wagon door, the repaired little shelter kept Farmer Ruth's green wheelbarrow dry.

"The wheelbarrow's back in its old place," Molly said.

"Just where it belongs," said Rex.

They passed the three hens, who seemed to have a meeting about a beetle. Gus listened for a moment.

"I think they've decided the beetle is in charge."

One hen pecked the ground and walked away.

"Meeting finished," Gus declared.

At the green gate, Molly stopped.

"This is where the path begins from our side."

Jack drew a little green gate at the end of the dotted line. He looked back toward the barn, then toward the brook.

"From here, the road for big trucks is out that way," Farmer Ruth said, pointing toward the longer farm lane. "The walking path to Pine Park goes the other way."

Gus looked at the two ways.

"So if I had a tractor, I'd go that way. But if I had four paws—"

"You already have four paws," said Molly.

"Then I am ready for the bridge."

They followed the path to the brook. The wooden rails of Little Bridge were still smooth and strong. Mr. Lee had built them long ago, when Rex and Molly first wanted to meet.

Beneath the bridge, water moved between the stones. A single yellow leaf floated under them and came out on the other side.

"That leaf didn't use the walking path," Gus said.

"It used the brook," Jack replied.

"Should we put a leaf road on the map?"

"We can draw the brook in blue," Jack said. "Then people will know where the bridge crosses."

He opened the notebook and added a thin wavy blue line.

Molly peered down from the bridge, then up at the paper.

"It's the right shape," she said. "But your brook is much quieter than ours."

Rex leaned over the rail to listen.

Shhhh, shhhh.

"The map should say that," Rex suggested. "Little Bridge: has a brook that goes shhhh."

"It might not fit," Jack said.

"Then make the letters little," said Gus.

On the park side, they turned around and looked back. From there the farm appeared as a red patch between the trees. Jack could see why someone new might be glad of a picture instead of guessing where to go.

"A map has to show places where they are," he said. "Not just where I remember them."

He compared the real path with his drawing one more time.

At Pine Park, Mr. Lee was waiting beside the original park sign with Aunt Jo's measuring tape. He had brought a wooden sample board small enough to hold in his hands.

"I hear there's an idea for a map," he said.

"Two kinds of ways," Molly answered at once. "And the green gate at the end of the dotted one."

Mr. Lee studied the notebook. "That's helpful. If we make a public board, grown-ups will check the routes and lettering. The pictures can show the main places, and the walking path can be different from the vehicle road."

Gus put a paw on the sample board.

"Can it show my cap?"

"Is your cap a place people need to find?" Mr. Lee asked.

Gus thought for a moment.

"Only when I lose it."

They decided not to put the cap on the public map.

That afternoon Molly walked home the same way she had come. The blue brook murmured beneath Little Bridge, the green gate waited at the farm, and the repaired shelter cast a small cool shadow beside the barn.

Jack's drawing now had the whole walking path on it. Molly was pleased. A little piece that had been missing was not missing anymore.
