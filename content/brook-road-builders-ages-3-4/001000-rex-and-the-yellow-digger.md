# Rex and the Yellow Digger

On Brook Road, there was a little house with a blue front step.

A puppy named Rex lived there with his Aunt Jo, a grown-up dog who knew all about trucks.

Rex was brown and white, with one dark ear and one light ear. When he was excited, his tail wagged in such a hurry that the rest of him had to stand very still to keep up.

On Monday morning, a big yellow machine rolled past the house.

Rumble, rumble, rumble.

Rex ran to the window.

"A truck!" he cried.

"Not quite," said Aunt Jo. "That's an excavator. It has a bucket for digging."

The machine went to the empty field at the end of Brook Road, where one tall pine tree grew beside a patch of weeds.

Rex hurried to tell his friends.

Gus, a small brown bear in a green cap, was sitting on his front step, watching an ant walk over his shoe.

"A yellow digger!" Rex told him. "A very, very big one!"

The ant stopped.

Gus stood up. "Even bigger than your aunt's sofa?"

"Much bigger."

"Then we'd better look."

They called for Jack, the little boy who lived next door.

Jack was a little boy who liked being a scientist. He always carried a blue notebook. He liked to ask questions and draw what he found out. He didn't know everything. That was his favorite part.

"What is it digging?" Jack asked.

"That's what we're going to see," said Rex.

Aunt Jo walked with them to the field. She wore an orange work vest, because she was helping lead the building crew.

A blue fence stood around the work area. On the sidewalk side was a wide place where neighbors could watch.

"You three can stay here with me," Aunt Jo said. "The machines have the other side."

Rex put his front paws on the ground and sat so carefully that his tail swept the sidewalk.

Inside the fence, a grown-up driver sat in the excavator.

The long arm stretched out.

The bucket went down.

Crunch.

It scooped up a big mouthful of earth.

Then the upper part of the excavator turned, while its wide tracks stayed in place.

The bucket tipped.

Plop, went the dirt into a neat pile.

"It's a giant spoon!" Gus said.

"A spoon for the ground," said Jack.

"It would make terrible soup," Gus decided. "Too many stones."

Jack opened his notebook and drew a long arm, a bucket, and two wide tracks.

"I thought the whole digger would drive over to the pile," he said. "But it turned the top instead."

They watched again.

Down went the bucket. Up came the dirt. Around turned the upper part.

"It can dig in one place and put the dirt somewhere else," said Jack.

Rex watched the big metal bucket open and close.

"Does the digger have teeth?"

"Only on the bucket," Aunt Jo said. "Those little points help it bite into the ground."

Gus showed his own teeth.

Rex showed his.

Jack laughed. "My teeth can't dig a pond."

"Mine can't either," said Gus. "I tried eating a carrot once. That was plenty of work."

Aunt Jo pointed toward the tall pine tree.

"We're making Pine Park here. It will have a path, a little hill, and a place to sit beneath that tree."

Rex stared at the field.

A park? Right where there had only been weeds and a muddy puddle?

"Will there be somewhere for a dog to walk?" he asked.

"A whole path," said Aunt Jo.

"For a bear?" asked Gus.

"For bears, too."

"For scientists?" asked Jack.

"Especially curious ones," said Aunt Jo.

Rex looked at the pile of earth and the tall pine tree. Nothing looked like a park yet.

But he could imagine a path going around the tree. Gus could imagine the little hill. Jack could imagine putting a mark in his notebook each time something changed.

The excavator took one more careful scoop.

Crunch. Lift. Turn. Plop.

By late afternoon, the driver parked the machine, lowered the bucket, and switched it off.

The rumbling stopped.

The field was quiet except for the pine needles whispering overhead.

At home, Jack drew the first page of his notebook: a yellow digger and one small pine tree.

Gus drew an enormous spoon beside it.

Rex added a little paw at the bottom of the page.

"That's our park," he said.

"It's the beginning of our park," Jack answered.

After supper, Rex curled up on his rug.

He thought about the digger's long arm and the pile of brown earth.

He thought about a path that wasn't there yet.

His tail gave one last, sleepy wag.

Outside on Brook Road, the excavator was still and the pine tree stood under the stars.

Tomorrow, the building crew would be back.

Tonight, the big yellow digger was resting too.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Continuity:** Pine Park is being built in the old field at the end of Brook Road, with its existing pine tree preserved. Aunt Jo is Rex's aunt and helps lead the site. Jack begins a blue notebook with the excavator drawing; Rex adds a paw and Gus a spoon. The excavator has made the first earth pile, but there is no finished path or hill.

**Editorial note:** An excavator's boom, stick, bucket, and rotating upper structure are described with child-sized words; these are different from the work done by the machines in later episodes. Children watch with Aunt Jo from outside the work fence. No child handles machinery.

**Optional prompt:** "What part of the digger moved when its big tracks stayed still?" Or just enjoy the rumble words.

**Sources / limits:** Caterpillar's excavator documentation supports the distinction between bucket/arm and swing movement. AAP shared-reading guidance supports optional questions and repeated stories; neither source tests this fictional series. No educational or sleep outcome is claimed.
