# Jack and the Tube That Waited for Rain

Jack was sitting on the bench at Pine Park, looking at the sky.

It was blue.

There was one white cloud, shaped rather like a lumpy potato.

"Maybe the sky is growing potatoes," Gus said.

"I don't think clouds grow potatoes," said Jack.

"Not very good ones, anyway," Gus replied.

From the park gate came the sound of a truck slowing down.

Rex leaped to his paws. His dark ear stood up. His light ear fell across one eye.

"Dan!"

The blue box truck parked in the roadside delivery space beside Pine Park. It did not drive onto the winding gravel path. Aunt Jo walked over to meet Dan while the children waited at the bench.

Dan took out a long, thin box.

"This one is for the park," he said. "Careful: there's a clear tube inside."

"A tunnel for ants?" Gus guessed.

"A house for a very skinny fish?" Rex asked.

Jack shook his head. "A fish would need more room."

Aunt Jo opened the package at the park worktable. Inside was a clear measuring tube, with little marks going up one side, and a holder for a post.

"It's a rain gauge," she said.

Rex peered at it.

"But there isn't any rain in there."

"Not yet," said Jack.

"A rain gauge catches rain so we can see how much fell."

"Could I put my nose over the top and catch some?" Rex asked.

"Then we'd be measuring noses," said Jack.

"That might take a bigger tube," Gus said.

Aunt Jo carried the gauge to a flat grassy patch near the park entrance, where the sky was clear above it. The tall old pine tree was farther away.

"Why not put it under the big tree?" asked Rex. "The tree has a very nice roof."

"The top needs open sky, not a roof of pine branches."

Jack looked up through the branches and imagined raindrops tapping the needles before reaching the ground.

Aunt Jo fixed the holder firmly to a post and set the tube upright. Its opening faced straight up.

"We can come and look after a rainy day," she said.

Gus bent his head to inspect it without touching.

"It's an empty cup with important stripes," he said.

"Those are measuring marks," said Jack. He drew the tube in his notebook, carefully adding small lines up its side.

Rex sniffed the grass. He sniffed the post. He sniffed his own paw.

"My nose says it will rain biscuits," he announced.

"Your nose said that yesterday too," Gus reminded him.

"It was very nearly right," Rex said. "Aunt Jo had biscuits in the kitchen."

The cloud drifted past without leaving a single drop. The clear tube stayed empty.

Jack wasn't disappointed. He liked having a question that wasn't answered yet.

"Tomorrow we can look again," he said.

"Even if it's still empty?" asked Rex.

"Even if it's still empty."

They took one slow walk around Pancake Hill before going home. A butterfly rested on a wildflower beside the path. The bench beneath the pine was warm from the sun.

That night Jack drew the cloud-shaped potato at the top of his page, above the empty gauge.

The tube in the park waited quietly, ready for the first real rain.
