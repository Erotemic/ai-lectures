# The Path to Space — 18-episode mission companion (not spoken)

**Pack:** `brook-road-builders-ages-3-4`  
**Arc:** Seasons 12–14, episodes 067000–084000, one continuous story.  
**Primary audience:** Ages 3–4; one narrator; full original stories without imposed duration limit.

## Creative promise

This is a true Moon visit *inside the fiction*, not a dream, a cardboard fantasy or a rocket that only flies to a hill. It is also **not** literal present-day child astronaut training: the animal society and specialized large six-passenger spacecraft are speculative story-world premises. Keep the children active through questions, observation, careful simple tasks and exploration, but never reassign professional rocket design, launch safety or heavy equipment control to them. A grand outcome depends on a large adult team and many unglamorous machines.

The conflict is primarily **distance, coordination, uncertainty and patience**. A crane waits for favorable weather; an engine measurement triggers a repeat check; a practice door indicator interrupts a simulation; a launch countdown holds briefly. None is treated as a catastrophe. The payoff is real arrival, two short suited Moon walks, a persistent robotic research station, safe orbital reunion and a return home. Each chapter still has its own concrete question, joke, satisfying action and gentle ending.

## Exact spacecraft and material state

- Two launches: **Scout** cargo rocket first from a distinct smaller facility in 078000, then **Star One** crew launch in 079000. Scout is an automated landing craft with a folded four-wheel rover and instruments. It lands at a chosen sunlit site on the Moon's Earth-facing side. It stays there.
- **Star One** is the primary large white launch vehicle and mission name. It includes launch stages, a pressurized return cabin kept in lunar orbit during descent, and a separate six-person Moon lander. No staged booster or crawler goes to the Moon. No extra people or animals are onboard after departure. Max and Aunt Jo are the adult crew.
- On the Moon, all six transfer to and remain in their six-person lander, step onto the Moon **only in pressure suits**, return from two short outings, and then ascend in the lander. The lander meets and docks with the orbiting return cabin; all six board return cabin for Earth return.
- The rover/solar array/antenna/temperature-light station persists after mission end. It communicates by radio and has power/operational limitations, including lunar darkness. No one builds a full crewed Moon house or unconditionally perpetual power supply.
- Return vehicle uses heat protection, parachutes and ocean recovery by an adult team. The friends eventually return to Brook Road. Dr. Ada remains on Earth and can report rover news.

## Scientific and narrative distinctions

- Earth launches require roads, specialist assembly, pad, ground power and liquid-handling systems, acoustic protection, controls, crew life support, and communication; no individual town resident 'builds a rocket overnight'.
- In orbit, the crew floats because spacecraft and contents are in free fall together, not because gravity vanishes. The Moon has weaker gravity, **not zero** gravity.
- The Moon has effectively no atmosphere for wind, breathing or parachute braking. Suits are personal life-support systems; helmets stay closed during surface excursions. Daytime sky looks dark even under sunlight.
- Radio waves can travel in space; talking without a radio across a vacuum does not work. The chosen near-side site can send radio toward Earth in the fiction.
- Staged rocket sections are not trailers. A ground crawler carries Star One slowly and **does not launch**. The first space road is a real heavy-access road on Earth; the last 'path' is a planned spacecraft trajectory.
- No 'space biscuits' literally provide fuel, no giant Moon rabbits, and no magical atmosphere. Gus's jokes are jokes.
- The map at Pine Park remains a local picture map beside its original sign. Jack draws the Moon route **in his notebook**, leaving the public board and its solid/dotted walking key unchanged.
