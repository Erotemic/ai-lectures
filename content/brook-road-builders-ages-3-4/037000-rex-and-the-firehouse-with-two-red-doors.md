# Rex and the Firehouse with Two Red Doors

Rex could smell three things on Brook Road that morning.

Fresh bread from somebody's kitchen.

Wet leaves drying by the curb.

And paint.

New paint.

He followed the smell past his blue front step, with Aunt Jo and his friends close behind.

"Where are you going?" asked Gus, jogging to keep his green cap on.

"I don't know yet," Rex said. "My nose hasn't told me."

Jack carried his blue notebook. He had drawn the road near Pine Park plenty of times, but today he noticed a side street he hadn't put on his latest page.

It turned north, away from Brook Road.

At the end stood a brick building with two enormous red doors.

"Those doors are big enough for a giant sandwich," Gus said.

"They're big enough for trucks," said Aunt Jo.

Just then, one door began to rise.

Up went its bottom edge. Up, up, up.

Rex sat down so quickly that his red collar gave a little jingle.

Inside stood a bright red fire engine.

It had big wheels, a windshield, and shiny doors along its sides.

"A truck with a whole wall of cupboards!" said Rex.

The second red door was already open. Behind it was another red truck. This one was longer. A long metal ladder lay folded along its roof.

Gus put a paw over his eyes and pretended to search the distance.

"I see a roof. And a truck. And another roof on the truck."

"That one's the ladder," Jack said, opening his notebook.

A fox in a navy work shirt came out of the building. She wore sturdy boots and carried a yellow helmet under her arm.

"Hello," she said. "I'm Captain Ann. Welcome to the Brook Road firehouse."

"I'm Rex. Is that a truck garage?"

"Partly," Captain Ann said. "We look after our trucks here. We also work here, eat here, and rest here between calls."

Rex looked at the two doors again.

"Does each truck have its own bedroom?"

Captain Ann smiled. "Its own bay, yes."

Gus nodded as though he'd expected this all along.

"I hope they don't snore."

Jack pointed his pencil at the shorter truck.

"Why don't they both have ladders?"

"An engine carries water, a pump, and hoses," Captain Ann explained.

She opened one side compartment, showing neatly folded hose. Then she closed it again.

"The long truck carries a ladder that can rise high," she said.

"Two trucks for different jobs," Jack said.

"That's right."

Rex sniffed the clean concrete at the edge of the driveway.

"And who washes their paws?"

"We wash their wheels when they need it," Captain Ann replied.

Gus looked delighted.

"A bath for a truck. How many towels?"

"Fewer towels than you'd think."

Captain Ann showed them a small notice attached to a post beside the visitor path.

OPEN DAY, it said, with a picture of the two red trucks.

"We're inviting neighbors to look around this weekend," she told them. "There'll be demonstrations in the yard and time to see how our equipment works."

"Can Molly come?" Rex asked. "She's a puppy too. She lives across Little Bridge."

"Of course. Bring Farmer Ruth along."

On the walk home, Jack drew a short line off Brook Road, then two big red rectangles at its end.

"Station Lane," he said, reading the street sign one more time.

"Draw a biscuit cupboard," Rex suggested.

"There wasn't a biscuit cupboard."

"There might be. They have lots of doors."

Gus looked back at the firehouse until it disappeared around the corner.

"Two red trucks," he said. "One with water. One with a ladder."

That night Rex lay on his familiar rug beside his repaired wooden dump truck.

His home had one blue step and a little door.

The firehouse had two enormous red doors.

Somewhere behind them, the trucks were resting too.

Rex closed his eyes and wondered whether either one snored.
