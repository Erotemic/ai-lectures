# The Rain in Jack's Little Tube

The first raindrop landed on the roof of Rex's house at bedtime.

Tik.

The next one landed beside it.

Tik-tik.

And then came a whole crowd of drops, pattering together.

Rex lay on his rug with one ear pointed up and one ear folded down.

"Someone is tapping on the roof," he said.

Aunt Jo listened from the kitchen.

"Rain," she said.

"A lot of tiny visitors," said Rex. "They don't even knock at the door."

Outside, the rain made little rings in the water collected beside the blue front step. It tickled the leaves. It drummed on the shed roof and whispered into the grass.

Rex remembered the clear tube Jack had helped Aunt Jo put up at Pine Park.

"Is the rain going into Jack's tube?"

"Some of it will," Aunt Jo answered. "We'll see in the morning."

Rex curled his paws under his chin and listened until the drumming became soft enough to sleep through.

By morning the rain had stopped.

The air smelled of wet dirt and pine needles, two of Rex's favorite smells. The sky was gray at one end and blue at the other.

At the corner, Gus was waiting in his green cap. A drop of water sat on the brim.

"My cap has a small lake," he announced.

He tipped it sideways. The drop fell onto his nose.

"The lake has moved."

Jack arrived holding his blue notebook beneath one arm.

"I'm going to look at the gauge," he said. "Yesterday it was empty."

"Maybe the rain filled it with soup," Gus suggested.

"If it did," Rex said, "I hope it's biscuit soup."

They walked with Aunt Jo to Pine Park. The gravel path was damp, and the bench beneath the tall pine still wore a few shining drops. Pancake Hill looked just like Pancake Hill, only darker where the soil was wet.

Near the entrance stood the clear tube. Aunt Jo had fixed it upright on its post during Dan's delivery week. No branch hung over it. Nothing had moved it.

The little tube had been empty before the rain. Now it held water.

Rex stared.

"It worked!"

"It was waiting," said Jack.

Gus bent his knees to look from a distance. "It is a very small swimming pool. For one ant who can swim."

Aunt Jo pointed to the marks on the tube. She showed Jack how to look at the water level without tipping it.

Jack looked straight at the marks and drew a line beside the third one.

He drew the post, the tube, and a small wavy line at the level of the water. On the facing page he drew the empty tube from before.

"These are different," he said, holding the pages open.

"One is hungry," said Gus. "One has eaten its breakfast."

Rex sniffed the wet grass but kept his nose away from the open top.

"Could we put a little cap on it to keep the water warm?"

"Then new rain wouldn't get in," Jack said.

"I see. The tube doesn't want a hat."

They took the short park loop. At the roadside beyond the gate, a shallow puddle reflected a piece of sky. Rex looked into it and saw his dark ear floating beside his light one.

He backed up. The ears in the puddle backed up too.

"Another puppy!"

Gus looked down. "A very flat puppy."

Rex raised one paw. The reflected puppy raised one paw.

"It does everything I do," Rex whispered. "How polite."

Aunt Jo smiled and led them along the dry edge of the path, leaving the puddle where it was.

At home, Jack slid his new drawing into his notebook. The rain was over, the gauge had something to measure, and the sky was clearing.

Rex slept that afternoon with the fresh smell of rain still drifting through his open window.
