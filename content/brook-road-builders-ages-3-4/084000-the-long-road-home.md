# The Long Road Home

On the last morning at the Moon, Jack looked out of the lander window and saw their tracks in the gray dust.

There were Rex's prints, Molly's prints, Gus's prints, and the marks from Jack's boots. Farther away, the little rover had made four thin lines on the ground.

Scout and its instruments were still working. The solar panels were open. The antenna could send messages to Earth. The new station would stay when the travelers left.

"We built something here," Jack said.

"A place where the robot can work," Molly answered.

Rex looked toward the rover. "Do you think it needs biscuits?"

"It needs power," said Max. "The engineers have planned that part."

Gus nodded. "Then give it electric biscuits."

The six travelers climbed back into their seats and fastened their harnesses. Max checked the lander's systems with the team in mission control. Dr. Ada's voice arrived through the radio, calm and familiar.

"The return cabin is waiting in lunar orbit," she said. "The plan is ready when you are."

"We're ready," Max replied after the checks.

The lander's engine started. Its feet lifted from the Moon. Soon Scout, the rover, and the gray plain were small shapes below them. The Moon became a great round world in the window again.

The lander climbed to meet the spacecraft that had been waiting in orbit. Max guided it toward the return cabin, following the team's instructions and the instruments. When the two spacecraft joined, the crew checked the connection carefully before moving back inside.

"We're home inside our space home," Gus said.

"For the trip to our other home," Aunt Jo replied.

The return spacecraft began its journey back to Earth. Day after day, the blue world grew larger. There were meals, quiet games, radio calls, and time to sleep in their softly strapped resting places.

Jack opened his blue notebook and turned slowly through the pages. He found the first excavator at Pine Park. The dump trucks. Little Bridge. The tractor, the street sweeper, the fire engine, the snowplow, the rock crusher, and the tall crane at Star Harbor.

On the last page he drew Scout's rover and a little rectangle for its solar-panel station on the Moon.

"What are you drawing next?" Molly asked.

"Home," Jack said.

The spacecraft approached Earth. The crew fastened their harnesses again. The return cabin was designed to pass through Earth's atmosphere, with a heat shield to protect it from the intense heat of coming back. High above the ocean, the cabin slowed, and its parachutes opened for the final part of the descent.

Rex watched the wide blue sea in the window.

"Are we going to land in the water?"

"Yes," Max said. "A recovery team is already waiting."

The capsule touched the sea with a splash. It rocked gently. Outside, boats approached, and trained recovery workers came to bring everyone safely aboard.

"That," Gus said, "was the biggest puddle I've ever seen."

A few days later the travelers came back to Brook Road. They passed Ms. Pam's red garage door, the turning toward Station Lane, and the path to Pine Park. Farmer Ruth waited beside the green gate. Dan, Captain Ann, Mr. Fred, and Mr. Lee were there too. Nobody had moved the bench or the rain gauge. Pancake Hill was still a small, rounded mound beneath the pine tree.

Molly and Rex exchanged their familiar one-wag greeting, this time without helmets or harnesses.

"How was the Moon?" Mr. Fred asked.

"Full of rocks," Rex said. "You'd love it."

Jack showed everyone the page with the tiny rover. Dr. Ada called from Star Harbor to say its signal was still reaching Earth. The equipment on the Moon had work to do long after the travelers were home.

At Pine Park, the town picture map still ended at the edge of the neighborhood. Jack did not draw a pretend road across the sky on it. Instead he opened his notebook to a fresh page. He drew a road from Brook Road to Star Harbor, a rocket rising from the coast, and a wide curve through space to the Moon.

"It's a different kind of path," he said.

"A path with a very big truck at the beginning," said Gus.

"And a little robot truck at the end," Rex added.

That night, Rex put his wooden dump truck beside the bed. His ears were free of the helmet. He could smell the clean blanket and the faint scent of grass coming through the open window.

The Moon shone above the quiet town. Somewhere on it, a small rover was waiting for its next instructions.

Rex closed his eyes. The road had taken them farther than any road he had known.

And it had brought them home.
