# Rex and the Three Kinds of Tracks

When Rex went outside on Thursday, the sidewalk was damp from an early shower.

Not enough rain for a great big puddle.

Just enough to make every footprint show.

Rex padded in a little circle.

Four little paw marks appeared behind him.

He padded around again.

Four more.

"Look!" Rex called. "The ground is writing about my feet."

Gus came over in his green cap.

He stood beside Rex's marks and planted one broad bear paw on the damp dirt by the flower bed.

His print was much bigger.

"Your feet write small letters," Gus said. "Mine write enormous ones."

Jack came to see.

"Can I draw them?" he asked.

Rex sat while Jack opened his blue notebook to a clean page.

He drew one small paw, one broad paw, and a question mark.

"What else makes marks?" he wondered.

"My cap makes a mark when I sit on it," said Gus.

"Does it?" Rex asked.

"It makes a very unhappy bear," Gus answered.

Down Brook Road, a deep engine sound rolled through the morning.

Aunt Jo came to the gate.

"The gravel is going into the park path today. You can see the wheel loader from the watching place."

The friends walked with her to the blue fence.

The two gravel piles from Tuesday were still there.

Beside them was a yellow machine with four large rubber tires and a bucket at the front.

"Is it a bulldozer?" Rex asked.

"Different machine," Aunt Jo said. "This is a wheel loader. Watch what it does with the gravel."

The grown-up driver rolled toward the first pile.

The front bucket went low and scooped up gravel.

Then the loader backed away carefully, turned, and carried its load toward the new path.

The bucket rose at the right place.

Rrrrrshh. Out slid the gravel.

"It has a spoon and wheels!" Gus cried.

Jack looked from the loader to the bulldozer parked farther back.

"The bulldozer pushed dirt along the ground," he said. "This one picked the gravel up and carried it."

"It brought the mountain for a little ride," Rex said.

The loader returned for another scoop.

Jack watched the tires turn in the soft dirt.

Beyond them, the excavator from Monday stood parked on its wide metal tracks.

"Those leave different marks," he said.

He drew two sets of lines in the notebook: the repeating tire pattern and the longer pattern from the excavator tracks.

Rex looked down at his own paws.

"And my feet have toes," he said.

"So does mine," said Gus.

"Your toes are the largest toes in this conversation," said Jack.

Gus was pleased.

At the little sand table beside Rex's house, they made their own marks that afternoon.

Aunt Jo brought out the wooden toy dump truck from the rug and a toy loader with four wheels.

Jack rolled the toy loader through the sand.

Two sets of narrow lines appeared.

Rex put a clean paw in one corner.

A small paw shape appeared.

Gus gently pressed his broad paw beside it.

"Three kinds of tracks," said Jack. "Little wheel tracks, little dog tracks, and bear tracks."

Gus looked again.

"I think there are four kinds."

"Four?" asked Jack.

Gus leaned over the table.

Plop.

The brim of his cap touched the sand and left a curved mark.

Rex barked one delighted bark.

Jack laughed and added a tiny green cap to the bottom of his drawing.

By late afternoon, one gravel pile at Pine Park was smaller. A long gray strip lay along the ground where the new path would run.

Aunt Jo explained that the builders would spread the gravel evenly and make the path firm another day.

"So the path isn't ready yet?" Rex asked.

"Not quite. Soon."

Rex looked at the tall pine tree and the low, wide Pancake Hill beyond the fence.

He imagined four little paws following a path around the hill.

He could almost feel the firm ground beneath them.

That night, Jack put his notebook by his bed. Gus set his cap upright on the shelf. Rex lay on his rug with his paws folded under his chin.

The damp marks outside had already begun to fade.

But the little drawings in Jack's book stayed where they were.

And down Brook Road, the gravel waited quietly for the next big machine.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Continuity:** Some gravel is now distributed as an unfinished path strip. A wheel loader, with four rubber tires and a front bucket, moved material from the delivered piles. Jack's blue notebook contains sketches of paw prints, tire patterns, tracks and a cap print. Rex's red collar and Gus's green cap remain unchanged.

**Editorial note:** Paw marks are made at the home's sand table, not in the active work area. The loader is distinguished from the excavator and bulldozer by where and how it moves material. The toy-scale marks are not a claim that all real tires or tracks leave identical patterns.

**Optional prompt:** "Which made the smallest mark—Rex's paw or Gus's paw?"

**Sources / limits:** Caterpillar's wheel-loader guide describes a loader's bucket and role in moving material; AAP's reading advice supports optional conversation. Neither evaluates the story.
