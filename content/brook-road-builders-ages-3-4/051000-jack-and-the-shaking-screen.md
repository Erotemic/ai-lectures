# Jack and the Shaking Screen

Jack had a question for Mr. Fred.

He had thought about it all the way from his house to the stone yard.

"Yesterday," he said, "the crusher made little stones. How does it know which ones belong in the park?"

Mr. Fred, the badger who managed the yard, smiled.

"The crusher doesn't know. Come and see what happens next."

Jack, Rex, and Gus stood with Aunt Jo in the office viewing room. Beyond the window, the gray crusher was working again. A belt carried rock pieces toward a long metal box.

The box was shaking.

Not jumping away. Not running down the road. Just shaking very quickly while stones moved across it.

"Is it cold?" asked Rex.

"It's a screen," said Mr. Fred.

"Maybe it has the shivers," Gus said.

Jack watched carefully.

He saw stones traveling toward the top of the box and different streams coming out underneath.

"I think it sorts them by color," he guessed. "Gray ones this way. Brown ones that way."

Mr. Fred shook his head.

"Look again."

The screen shook as stones traveled across its openings. Small pieces fell through, while bigger ones went on.

Jack leaned closer to the window.

Some gray rocks were in one stream. Other gray rocks were in another.

"It's the size," he said. "Not the color!"

"Exactly."

Stones too big for the wanted pile were sent back to be broken smaller.

Rex wagged his tail. "They get another turn on the rock cruncher."

"A very serious queue," said Gus. "Big rocks on the left, tiny rocks on the right, and no cutting in line."

At the visitor desk, Mr. Fred set out two sealed clear jars. One held bigger angular chips. The other held finer little stones.

Rex sniffed the closed lid of one jar.

"It smells like a jar," he said, disappointed.

Gus turned both jars around so the stones could catch the light.

"This one is a jar of big crumbs. This one is a jar of little crumbs."

"Stone crumbs," said Jack. "Not the kind you eat."

"I was only wondering," said Gus.

Mr. Fred gave them a tray of large wooden shapes and a cardboard sorting board with a round opening. It was a play demonstration on the visitors' table, not a piece of the real plant.

Jack tried a large wooden circle first.

It would not fit through.

He tried a smaller wooden circle.

Plop. It fell into the tray below.

"So the opening matters," Jack said.

Then he turned the board sideways.

The little circle caught on the edge instead of falling.

"And how it lands matters a bit, too," he added. "My first try wasn't perfect."

"My first tries never are," Gus said. "Last week I tried to wear a bucket as a shoe."

Rex stared at him. "Why?"

"Research."

Jack drew the screen in his notebook, making little holes across it. He drew one arrow downward for the small pieces and another arrow forward for the larger ones.

On the page before it, his drawing showed the crusher. Now the drawings connected.

"The rock goes from one machine to another," he said.

"And then another," said Mr. Fred. "The finished stone waits in piles until somebody needs it."

That afternoon, Jack placed his notebook beside Rex's wooden dump truck. He looked at the two pages again.

A big rock. A crusher. Smaller rocks. A shaking screen.

His first guess about colors had been wrong, and he was glad he had asked another question.

Rex settled beside him on the rug. Gus turned his green cap upside down and filled it with three soft blocks.

"Sorted," Gus declared. "These are the blocks that fit in my hat."

The friends laughed quietly as the afternoon light moved across the floor.
