# The Little House for a Robot

Scout was waiting beside the lander's work area.

It had arrived at the Moon before the travelers. Its little rover sat folded on the cargo platform, with four small wheels tucked beneath its body and solar panels folded like wings.

"It is a very small truck," Rex said.

"It is a robot explorer," Dr. Ada explained over the radio.

"A robot truck," Gus corrected.

Max and Aunt Jo led the second Moon outing. The grown-up crew followed a checklist for the equipment and the visitors watched from the designated area until it was their turn to help with simple, planned tasks. The rover had to be inspected before it could roll.

Jack could see a camera on a short mast. He could see a small instrument box and a place where a sample tool was attached.

"What will it do after we go home?" Jack asked.

"It can take pictures, study the ground, and send information by radio," said Dr. Ada. "When sunlight reaches its panels, it can make electricity. It also has a battery for times when it needs stored power. The team will plan when it works and when it rests."

Gus stood with his gloved paws together. "So it has a bed?"

"A sleeping mode," said Max.

"That sounds like a bed."

Aunt Jo helped place a small marker beside the rover's safe travel area. It was one of several markers the engineers had planned for the work. Jack read the number aloud. Molly checked that the marker matched the picture on her card.

Rex kept looking at the rover's wheels. They were very different from his wooden dump truck's little round wheels. These were made to grip dusty ground without needing air-filled tires like a road car.

"If Ms. Pam lived on the Moon," he said, "she would have to fix very different tires."

The adult crew unfolded the rover's panels, checked its connections, and opened the short cargo ramp. On Earth, technicians in mission control watched the pictures and measurements. The rover rolled down from Scout and moved a little way across the ground. Its wheels made shallow tracks.

"It works!" Molly said.

Then the rover stopped near a small rock. The camera showed the rock directly ahead of one wheel. The control team paused its motion and studied the image. Nothing was broken. They simply chose a clearer route.

"Can it go around?" Jack asked.

"That's what the team is planning," said Max.

The rover turned a little and rolled past the rock. Gus gave it a quiet cheer.

"It has learned the long road," he said.

The crew also checked Scout's small antenna and a station that would measure sunlight and temperatures on the Moon. The equipment was fixed securely so it could remain after the visitors departed. Its radio signal could travel back to Earth. A small shelter covered sensitive parts of the station, though it was nothing like a barn or a house with windows.

"A little house for a robot," Molly said.

"A very small farm," said Gus. "Instead of hens, it has batteries."

Rex looked around at the gray plain. There were no trees or grass or road signs. Now there was a rover with tracks, a quiet station, and a way for people on Earth to hear from the Moon.

They took a last photograph beside the equipment. Then the crew went back into the lander. There were return-flight checks to complete, and the planned time on the surface was nearly over.

Through the window Jack could still see the rover near Scout, its panels shining in the sunlight.

"It will stay here when we go?" he asked.

"Yes," said Aunt Jo. "We came to visit. The robot came to work."

Gus waved at the small rover. "Goodnight, little truck. Don't forget to park."

The robot did not wave back. It had a very different way of saying hello: a little green signal on a screen, sent all the way to Earth.
