# Molly and the Truck with All the Drawers

The red tractor at Green Gate Farm had a very strange new trick.

It could make a small click.

But it would not make its usual rumbling sound.

Farmer Ruth had tried to start it that morning. She checked the things a farmer checks, then turned it off again.

"We'll ask Pam to have a look," she said.

Molly stared at the big back wheels.

"It was rolling yesterday," she told Rex.

Rex lifted his dark ear. "Maybe it is sleeping."

"It has no pillow," Gus said, looking beneath the cab.

"And no blanket," Jack added.

Gus considered this. "No wonder it won't wake up."

They all sat with Aunt Jo at the farmyard bench. The barn was dry, with the little hay bales inside. Three hens wandered near the fence. One hen seemed to believe she was in charge of everything.

From the long farm road came a gentle rattle.

Not the big white flatbed from the garage.

This truck was shorter. It was white too, but it had square little doors down both sides.

"A truck with drawers!" Molly cried.

"A wardrobe on wheels!" Gus declared.

Ms. Pam parked near the barn and stepped down.

"It's my service truck," she said. "I bring my tools to a job when the job can't come to the garage."

She opened one side compartment.

Inside were neatly arranged tools.

She opened another.

More tools.

Gus's eyes grew round.

"Do you have sandwiches in the last one?"

"No," Pam said, "but sometimes I have my lunch in the cab."

"Then this is an excellent truck," Gus decided.

Molly's nose twitched. She could smell warm hay and the faint metal smell of the tools, but not a single sandwich.

Ms. Pam walked over to the tractor with Farmer Ruth.

"First I test the battery to find out whether it is the trouble."

She used a small testing instrument and checked the tractor's starting system.

Jack leaned forward at the bench.

"The battery is the thing that helps the engine start?" he asked Aunt Jo.

"It provides electrical power for starting," Aunt Jo said. "Pam will check whether this one is still working well."

Rex thought of his wooden toy dump truck, which had no engine at all.

"Mine starts with a nose," he said.

"Yours starts with a paw," Molly replied.

"Sometimes," Rex admitted, "it starts when Gus trips over it."

"Only by accident," said Gus.

After her checks, Pam came over.

"This old battery isn't holding a good charge any more," she said. "I've brought the right replacement."

She and Farmer Ruth worked on the tractor together while the children watched a sleepy sheep chewing behind its fence.

"Is that sheep powered by a battery?" Gus asked.

"Hay," Molly answered at once.

"I knew that," Gus said. "I was checking."

At last Farmer Ruth sat in the tractor seat again.

She turned the key.

Rrrrummm.

The red tractor started.

"There it is!" Molly called.

Gus took off his cap and bowed to the big back wheels.

"Welcome back, Mr. Tractor. Your nap was long."

"It needed a battery," Jack said, making a drawing in his notebook. "Not a blanket."

Ms. Pam shut the compartments of the service truck, one after another.

Click.

Click.

Click.

"The old battery can go back to the garage for recycling," she said.

Then she drove her little white service truck around the farm road, back toward the red door.

The farm grew quiet again. A hen settled onto a warm patch of dirt. The sheep kept chewing.

That evening Molly put her blue rope toy on the farmhouse porch.

She lay near the door and listened to the last soft noises of the barn.

The red tractor was working again, but for now it was resting under its roof.

Molly decided that even a tractor with a good battery was allowed a good night's sleep.
