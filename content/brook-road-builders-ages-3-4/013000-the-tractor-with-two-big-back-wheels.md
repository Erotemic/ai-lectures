# The Tractor with Two Big Back Wheels

Molly was waiting at the green gate with her nose through the gap.

She had something important to show Rex.

It wasn't her blue rope toy. That was on the farmhouse porch.

It wasn't a hen, although three hens were busy scratching in the yard.

It was a sound.

Rrrrrrrrum. Rrrrrrrrum.

Rex stopped walking and lifted his dark ear. Then he lifted his light ear too.

"There it is again!" he said.

Gus tipped up his green cap. "That sounds like a bear with an enormous tummy."

"My tummy doesn't sound like that," Rex said.

"Mine does," said Gus. "Sometimes."

Jack came across the Little Bridge with Aunt Jo. He had his blue notebook under his arm.

"Where's the noise coming from?" he asked.

"Behind the barn," Molly said. "Come and look."

Farmer Ruth met them by the porch. She was a grown-up goat, and she wore a broad straw hat with a bent corner.

"The tractor is getting ready for haymaking," she said. "You can watch from the bench by the barn."

They sat where she showed them.

Around the far side of the red barn came a red tractor.

It had two wheels at the front and two at the back.

Its back wheels were much bigger than its front ones.

"It has small shoes in front and big boots behind!" cried Gus.

Rex studied the huge black tires.

"Could those boots leave paw prints?"

"They leave tire prints," said Jack. He opened his notebook.

Farmer Ruth stopped the tractor. She climbed down and came over to them.

"Why are the back wheels so big?" Jack asked.

"On this tractor, those big wheels help it grip the ground while it pulls," she said. "There are other kinds of tractors too."

Gus looked at the red tractor, then at his own little feet.

"I think I will keep the feet I have. Easier to find socks."

"You don't wear socks," Molly pointed out.

"Exactly," said Gus. "So I have never lost one."

Beyond the barn lay a field of long green grass.

The grass moved when the breeze came. It looked almost like a quiet green sea.

"What grows there?" Rex asked.

"Grass for hay," said Farmer Ruth. "Later we'll cut it, let it dry, and keep it for the animals to eat."

Molly knew the path beside the field. She walked it with Farmer Ruth nearly every day.

She showed Rex where the grass brushed her whiskers without going into the field itself.

"I know what grass smells like," Rex said, taking a deep sniff.

"Then what does this one smell like?" Molly asked.

Rex sniffed again.

"Grass," he said firmly.

"What else?"

"More grass."

Molly burst out laughing.

Farmer Ruth went back to the tractor. The tractor pulled a low, empty wagon along the farm lane.

It was an ordinary wagon, with four wheels and a flat bed for carrying things.

Rrrrum. Crunch. Rrrrum. Crunch.

"Why not use the new bridge?" Gus asked.

Jack pointed back toward Pine Park. "That's our walking bridge. The wagon's going around by the farm road."

"It's a long way for a wagon," Gus said.

"The right way for a wagon," said Aunt Jo.

Farmer Ruth parked the wagon beside the barn, where it would wait for hay.

Jack drew the tractor carefully. He made the two big back wheels so round that Rex thought they looked like doughnuts.

Then Jack drew the farm lane, the field, and the wagon.

"One day," Jack said, "I want to see what goes on the wagon."

"I can show you," Molly promised.

The tractor's engine stopped, and the deep rumble faded.

Now they could hear the hens scratching.

A sheep called softly from the pen.

The wind moved the long green grass.

As they walked back over the Little Bridge, Rex turned to look at the quiet farm.

"Molly has a very big backyard," he told Gus.

"With very big boots," Gus replied.

At home, Rex put his wooden dump truck beside his rug. Its little wheels looked especially small tonight.

He rested his chin on the rug and thought about the red tractor waiting for its next job.
