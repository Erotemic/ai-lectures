# Jack and the Map of Where the Rain Went

The rain had stopped days ago, but Jack still had a question.

"Where did it all go?"

He was sitting on Rex's rug, beside Rex's wooden toy dump truck. The little truck had four good wheels again, and Rex had made it a parking space between two blocks.

"Some rain went into the tube," Rex said.

"Some went down the street," Gus added.

"Some got into my cap," Rex said.

"That was my cap," Gus replied.

Aunt Jo brought them a shallow tray and a small cup of water. She spread a towel underneath to catch drips.

Jack put one wooden block beneath one end of the tray. He set his pencil at the higher end and let it roll slowly down.

"The tray is tilted," he said.

"That pencil is trying to go for a walk," Gus said.

"The end here is lower," said Jack.

With Aunt Jo helping, Jack poured a little water onto the high end.

The drops slid toward the low end of the tray.

Rex leaned forward, nose twitching.

"A road for drops!"

"Only a tray," Jack said. "But the water goes the same way on a sloping road."

Gus stood Rex's wooden dump truck beside the tray.

"Truck, please deliver one very small puddle."

Rex moved the truck a finger's width and made a tiny noise.

"Brrrum. Delivery refused. Too splashy."

Aunt Jo emptied the tray into the sink and dried it. Jack opened his blue notebook to the drawing of Pine Park's rain gauge.

The next afternoon they returned to the park. The rain gauge still stood in the open grassy patch away from the pine. The water level was a little lower than the line Jack had drawn after the rain.

Aunt Jo helped Jack check it once more. He pointed to the original mark in his notebook and made a second, lower mark. Then, together, they emptied the tube and put it back upright in its holder.

"Now it can catch the next rain," Jack said.

"An empty cup with a job," said Gus. "Very impressive."

They walked around Pancake Hill. The path was firm again. The bench was dry beneath the pine. The grass was bright where it had drunk some of the rain.

Jack sat down with his notebook and began a new page. At the top he drew a cloud. Beneath it he drew little drops.

Some rain soaked into the soil. Some ran over the road and into drains.

Then he drew another picture: a road with a round pipe going underneath it.

"That's the farm road," said Rex. "Where Molly heard the soup noise."

"Glup, glup!" Gus said, doing his best impression.

"The pipe carried the ditch water under the road," Jack replied. He drew the green gate and a small puppy paw beside it for Molly.

Aunt Jo looked at the page. There was a pine tree, a road, a grate, a pipe, and a little yellow loader returning to work.

"Quite a lot happened after one rainy night," she said.

"And the truck with the elephant trunk cleaned under the street," Rex added.

"And the truck with spinning brooms brushed its road hair," Gus said.

"And the rain gauge did its job without moving at all," Jack said.

They closed the notebook and walked home together.

At the blue front step Rex stopped. The late sunshine warmed the boards, and the path was no longer muddy.

He looked down at his paws.

"I think they are finally clean."

Gus inspected one paw carefully.

"Yes. There is not even enough mud for a very small bulldozer."

Rex curled up on his rug that evening. Gus had gone home, and Jack had put the blue notebook safely on his desk.

The sweeper had finished sweeping. The big hose had been rolled up. The farm road carried wheels, and Little Bridge carried paws.

Outside, the rain gauge waited in the open grass of Pine Park.

Empty again.

Ready for another ordinary rainy day.
