# Gus and the Engine That Pushed Back

A rocket engine was not shaped like any engine Gus had ever seen.

It had pipes and rounded parts and a wide opening at the bottom. It was fixed to a heavy test stand far from the public viewing room.

"Which way does it push?" Gus asked.

"The hot gas goes out that way," Dr. Ada said, pointing down on the picture. "The engine gets pushed the other way."

Gus frowned. "It pushes things away so it can go closer to the Moon?"

"Something like that," said Jack.

Rex and Molly sat beside Aunt Jo near a window. They had been invited to observe a planned engine test from a safe building, with the sound coming through a speaker at a comfortable volume. No one stood beside the running engine.

Max explained that rockets carry what they need to run their engines. A jet flying through the air uses the air around it, but a rocket has to work even where there is no air.

"How does it know which way is up?" Rex asked.

"Up is where we're going at first," Max replied. "The controls help us travel along the planned path."

On a table Dr. Ada had put a little wheeled model. It had a spring inside, covered by clear plastic. When she released the model's lever, a small piece moved one way and the wheeled part moved the other. The model did not make fire or smoke. It just showed a pair of pushes.

"Two things push each other," Jack said. He drew two arrows going in opposite directions.

"Exactly," Dr. Ada said. "A real rocket uses high-speed exhaust and careful control. Your drawing is only the beginning of the idea."

The test team called over the radio. The friends watched a distant camera picture of the engine stand. A number on the screen counted down, then the engine started.

There was a brilliant light at the nozzle. Clouds of vapor and exhaust billowed in the controlled test area. Even through the speaker, the sound was a deep roar.

Gus put his paws over his cap. "That is a very big purr."

"A very loud one," said Molly.

The engine ran only for the planned test. When it stopped, the room grew quiet. People checked their screens. For a moment Rex thought everyone would cheer.

But Dr. Ada raised one feather.

"One measurement doesn't match what the engineers expected," she said. "So they will check the equipment and repeat the test."

"Is the engine bad?" Molly asked.

"We don't know yet," Dr. Ada replied. "That's why we test. We can stop and find out."

Gus had a serious question. "Do we have to push the rocket with a broom instead?"

"No," said Max, smiling. "We will wait for the engineers."

They had lunch. Jack drew the wide engine bell and the pair of arrows. Rex drew a biscuit beside it, though he could not explain why. Later a technician came to the visitor room with good news: the team had found a faulty measuring connection and repaired it. The repeat check showed the readings the engineers expected.

"So we can still go?" asked Rex.

"One more test has passed," said Dr. Ada. "There are many more."

At home Gus stood on his rug and looked at his green cap. He threw it gently upward, and it fell back into his paws. He did not try to make a rocket out of it.

"I prefer a cap that comes back," he told Jack.

Jack closed his notebook. "Me too."

Far away at Star Harbor, the engine was quiet. The grown-ups had tested it, learned something, and checked it again. That was progress too.
