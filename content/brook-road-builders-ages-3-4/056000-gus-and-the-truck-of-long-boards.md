# Gus and the Truck of Long Boards

Gus heard the truck before anyone told him it was coming.

Rrrrummm.

A low, steady sound rolled along the farm road.

"A big one," he said.

"How big?" asked Jack.

Gus stretched his paws apart. "At least this big."

His paws were not very far apart.

Rex looked down the road. "Maybe stretch your imagination instead."

They were standing with Farmer Ruth and Molly beside the red barn. The repair area around the little wagon-door shelter was marked off. Mr. Lee had already checked the old wood and was ready for the new materials.

Around the bend came a delivery truck with a long, open bed. On it lay a neat stack of wooden boards, longer than Rex's whole body, longer than Gus from nose to toes, and longer even than Jack was tall with both arms up.

"That is a truck carrying sticks for a giant!" Gus declared.

The truck stopped on the farm's vehicle road. Its driver stayed with the adult crew to arrange the unloading.

The timber bundles were held down by strong straps while the open-bed delivery truck traveled.

"They don't just lie there and hope," Aunt Jo said. "The driver secures the load before the journey."

Gus nodded. "Good. Because if one board rolled off, somebody might have to chase a very long pancake."

The truck carried timber for the shelter frame, new roofing pieces, and a small bundle of trim boards. It was not carrying a whole new barn.

"That's an important difference," Farmer Ruth said. "Most of our barn is still perfectly fine."

Jack pointed to the side shelter in his notebook. "We're fixing that little part."

"Just that," said Ruth.

A little later, a different sound came from the lane.

Beep. Beep.

Dan's familiar blue box truck drove up slowly, its tall sides and enclosed cargo body easy to recognize.

Rex's tail wagged. "Dan!"

The donkey driver waved through his window. He parked where the grown-ups had directed, away from the children and the stacked timber.

Dan brought the smaller boxes inside his blue truck, with a roof and walls around the cargo.

"Screws, brackets, and the other little things the carpenter ordered," he said, after the boxes were safely unloaded by adults.

Gus looked from the long-bed truck to Dan's enclosed truck.

"One truck carries very long things," he said. "And one carries lots of little boxes."

"Different shapes of cargo need different kinds of space," Jack said.

"I need a truck that carries one enormous sandwich," Gus replied.

"What kind of sandwich?" asked Molly.

"One with a roof," Gus said. "So it doesn't rain on the cheese."

Rex sniffed thoughtfully. "Could the truck have a biscuit compartment?"

"My blue truck has a glove compartment," Dan said.

"For gloves?" asked Rex.

"For gloves, mostly."

"Not a biscuit compartment, then," Rex said, disappointed but polite.

The adult crew moved the timber into a safe stacked place behind the temporary work barrier. Mr. Lee checked the labels and drew a line through one item on his list.

Jack stood with Gus on the visitor side of the fence. He wanted to know how long the longest board was, but the bundle was too far away to measure.

"I can measure it in Gus steps," he said.

"Excellent," said Gus.

Jack drew a line about the same length as the board in his notebook. Gus walked carefully beside that drawing on the packed ground.

"One, two, three, four, five—"

On step six he stopped to rescue his cap, which had slid over his eyes.

"Six bear steps," said Jack, drawing six small paw shapes.

"Six and a little cap emergency," Gus corrected.

Mr. Lee passed nearby and smiled at the picture. "That's a good way to compare lengths for a drawing. For cutting the real boards, I'll use proper measured marks."

Gus considered this. "Because a bear's steps can get longer when he smells lunch?"

"Exactly," said Jack.

When both delivery trucks were gone, the barn looked much the same from the porch. Red walls, tall doors, hay safely inside. Only the tidy wood stacks beyond the barrier showed that something was going to change.

Molly fetched her blue rope toy and placed it by Rex's front paws.

"The boards are here," she said.

"And the long sandwich truck has gone home," Rex said.

Gus climbed onto the porch step. He sat with his cap firmly on his head and watched the afternoon light turn the new boards golden.

"I think," he announced, "that is plenty of wood for one small roof."

"Mr. Lee thinks so too," Farmer Ruth said.

Which was a more reassuring opinion.
