# Gus and the Gravel Mountain

On Tuesday morning, Gus heard three great rumbles outside.

Rummm.

Rummm.

Rummm.

He rushed to the window in his green cap.

A truck with six big wheels was coming down Brook Road.

Its back was piled high with gray gravel.

"That's a mountain riding in a truck!" Gus cried.

The truck drove slowly past Rex's blue front step and stopped inside the fence at the new Pine Park.

Gus went to fetch his friends.

Rex was already waiting at his gate. His ears pointed in two different directions.

"Did you hear the truck?" Rex asked.

"I heard it with my whole belly," said Gus.

Jack came along holding his blue notebook against his chest.

The page from yesterday showed a yellow digger, a pine tree, an enormous spoon, and a little paw.

"I wonder where the gravel will go," Jack said.

Aunt Jo led them to the watching place outside the blue fence.

"It's for the park path," she explained. "The ground was too soft in some places."

The dump truck stood still on level ground.

A grown-up driver checked behind it and opened the tailgate.

Then the big truck bed began to rise.

Higher.

Higher.

The gravel slipped toward the open end.

Rrrrrshhhhh!

It poured into a pile behind the truck.

"The truck is pouring its mountain!" Gus shouted.

Rex's tail went swish, swish, swish.

"Will the mountain stay there forever?" he asked.

"No," Aunt Jo said. "The gravel will become part of the path."

The truck bed came down again, slow and steady, until it rested flat.

Jack looked at the pile and then at the empty truck bed.

"The truck didn't go backward to push the rocks out," he said. "The bed tilted, and the rocks slid down."

"Like a slide," said Gus.

"A slide for gravel," said Jack.

"I hope the gravel said 'wheee,'" said Rex.

"It was probably too busy going rrrrrshhhhh," Gus answered.

One more truck arrived with a smaller load. The driver stopped, raised its bed, and left another neat pile.

Now there were two gravel mountains.

Gus counted them.

"One mountain, two mountains. If I had a third mountain, I'd put my cap on it."

Rex looked at Gus's cap.

It was a small green cap. The gravel pile was enormous.

"You'd need a bigger cap," Rex said.

"Much bigger. A cap with its own front door."

Jack drew a truck in his notebook. At first he drew the back lying flat, like a very long box.

Then he drew it a second time, tipped high into the air.

He added one arrow to show which way the gravel fell.

The arrow went down.

"Why not up?" Gus asked.

Jack held his notebook so Gus could see.

"Because when the bed tips, the gravel comes down. I saw it happen."

Gus nodded gravely.

"Then it's a good thing my hat didn't fall in."

Later, at Rex's house, they played with a little wooden dump truck on the rug. It had a back that tipped.

Gus loaded it with three large wooden blocks.

Jack tilted the bed.

Thump, thump, thump went the blocks onto a folded blanket.

Rex sniffed them very carefully.

"These are not gravel," he announced.

"No," said Gus. "They're our mountains, only indoors."

They made a road between the blocks and parked the toy truck at the end.

After lunch, Aunt Jo came home and told them the gravel would be moved into place another day.

"So the mountains are still waiting?" Gus asked.

"Yes. There is more work to do."

By evening, the street was quiet. The real dump trucks had gone, but the little wooden truck remained on Rex's rug.

Gus took off his cap and tucked it under his paw.

Jack closed his notebook on the drawing of the falling gravel.

Rex leaned his head against his blanket and gave a small contented huff.

Down Brook Road, beneath the pine tree, two gray piles rested until the next workday.

They looked a little like sleeping mountains.

And tonight, sleeping was exactly what they were doing.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Continuity:** Two gravel piles have been delivered inside the fenced Pine Park site; neither is yet a finished path. Jack's notebook gains drawings of the dump bed flat and raised, with a downward arrow. Gus's green cap is still his; the wooden dump truck and large wooden blocks are at Rex's house.

**Editorial note:** The dump truck backs are raised only after trucks stop and the drivers check the delivery area. The repeated rumbling and the joke about a cap with a front door are the principal play beats, not a science quiz.

**Optional prompt:** "What was different about the back of the dump truck when the gravel came out?"

**Sources / limits:** Caterpillar loader/material-handling references establish the distinct roles of trucks and loaders in material movement; AAP shared-reading guidance supplies optional prompt context. These do not establish developmental effects of the story.
