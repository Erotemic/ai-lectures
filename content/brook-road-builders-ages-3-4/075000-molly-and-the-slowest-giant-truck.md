# Molly and the Slowest Giant Truck

The big white rocket stood inside the assembly building. Its tall body reached toward the roof. Its lower engines had been checked. Its cabin windows were covered to keep them clean.

Molly had never seen anything so tall that was going to move.

"How do they drive it to the launch pad?" she asked.

"They don't drive the rocket," said Max. "They move its platform with a crawler."

The crawler waited beneath the giant platform. It was broad and low, with many tracks instead of ordinary wheels. Its job was to carry the rocket slowly along a special wide route to the launch pad.

"A truck that carries a rocket," Rex whispered.

Gus stared. "This is the best truck in the whole world."

"It doesn't go very fast," said Dr. Ada.

"Then it has plenty of time to enjoy being a truck."

From a distant visitor deck, the friends watched the platform begin to move. At first Molly thought nothing was happening. Then she noticed that the rocket's white nose had shifted past a line on the building wall.

"It's moving," she said.

A few minutes later, the crawler had traveled a little farther. A whole team walked and rode in support vehicles, checking the route, the loads, and the equipment. The wide path had been built and tested months earlier, one layer at a time, by graders, gravel trucks, and rollers.

Jack opened his notebook to the page from that day. There was Mr. Fred's green dump truck leaving a pile of stones. There was the grader's long blade. There was the roller. All those machines had helped make this one slow journey possible.

"So the rocket is using the road we watched them build," Jack said.

"Yes," Dr. Ada answered. "The ground work mattered."

The crawler's tracks grumbled. Rex listened carefully. The sound was nothing like a rocket engine. It was a deep, patient mechanical rumble.

"If it took Molly's toy rope to the Moon," he said, "would it go this slowly?"

"It won't go to the Moon," Molly said. "It is just taking Star One to the pad."

"Good," said Gus. "I was worried it would arrive next year."

They watched as the huge platform followed the prepared route past the sea. The launch tower waited ahead, with its service connections and walkway ready. The crawler crept into position, and the workers began another long series of checks.

"Does the crawler stay under the rocket for launch?" Jack asked.

"No," said Max. "It moves away once the launcher has been set down and secured. The rocket stands on the launch structure."

Gus sighed. "So the truck doesn't get to fly."

"It has a very important job here," said Dr. Ada. "And it will be ready for another rocket later."

Aunt Jo took a photograph of the four friends from the visitor window: Rex sitting straight, Molly with her front paws close together, Jack holding his notebook, and Gus with both paws holding down his green cap. Behind them, far away, the white rocket stood against the blue sky.

The trip home was much faster than the crawler's journey. Dan's blue box truck passed them on the county highway, returning from a delivery. Rex waved at it.

That night Molly went back over Little Bridge to Green Gate Farm. Her red barn was quiet. The hay was still stacked inside, and the repaired little shelter kept the wheelbarrow dry.

The rocket was no longer inside its big building. It stood at the pad, waiting for the next work.

Molly fell asleep imagining the slowest giant truck in the world, which had carried something made to move faster than any truck could.
