# Gus and the Rock Cruncher

Gus had imagined a stone yard all morning.

In his imagination, there were mountains as tall as clouds and rocks wearing little hats.

At the real yard, the mountains were not that tall.

But the trucks were enormous.

"That's enough for me," Gus said.

Aunt Jo parked her yellow van by the office, away from the working trucks. Rex, Gus, and Jack followed her inside. Through a broad window they could see a fenced yard, a long conveyor belt, and a great gray machine.

A badger in a yellow vest came to meet them.

"I'm Mr. Fred," he said. "Welcome to the viewing room. The big machines stay on that side of the fence, and we stay here."

Gus looked through the glass.

"What does the gray one do?"

"It's a crusher."

Gus's eyes grew round.

"A rock cruncher!"

"That's one way to think about it," said Mr. Fred.

Far below the window, a haul truck slowly approached a receiving hopper. It was carrying pieces of rock much bigger than Gus's head.

A haul truck had brought the big rocks from a quarry, where they had been taken out of the ground.

"Is that the same truck that brought gravel to Pine Park?" asked Jack.

"A different kind," said Aunt Jo. "This one works here at the yard. The road dump truck delivers smaller finished material around town."

The haul truck tipped its load into the receiving area. An adult operator watched the equipment from a separate control station.

Inside the plant, the machinery rumbled.

Grrrrrrrr.

Gus pressed one paw against the window.

"It sounds like a bear getting ready for breakfast."

Rex looked at him. "Do you sound like that?"

"Only when I'm very hungry."

The crusher broke the large pieces into smaller pieces, which fell onto a moving belt.

The stones traveled away together.

"Where do they go now?" asked Jack.

"To the screens, where they're sorted," said Mr. Fred. "Some large pieces may need another turn through a crusher."

Gus watched the gray machine for a long moment.

"Does it have teeth?"

"Metal parts that press and break rock," said Mr. Fred. "Not bear teeth."

Gus looked relieved.

"Good. Because a crusher would be terribly hard to take to the dentist."

Rex was more interested in the moving belt.

"The rocks are going somewhere without any wheels underneath them."

Jack drew one large rock on the left side of his notebook page, and three smaller pieces on the right. He put a line beneath them for the conveyor.

"So the rocks don't come out already the size we need," he said.

"That's right," said Mr. Fred. "There's more work to do."

The crusher kept working. The haul truck drove away along its own yard road, ready for another load. From the quiet office, the friends could see the work without standing in the middle of it.

On the way home, Gus asked Aunt Jo whether he could make a crusher from two couch cushions.

"You can make a pretend one," said Aunt Jo. "No real rocks indoors."

At Rex's house they put three soft blocks between two cushions and pressed them gently together.

The blocks did not break.

"Excellent," Gus said. "The world's softest rock cruncher."

"It didn't crunch anything," said Jack.

"That's why it's so gentle."

Rex curled up near the cushions, his tail tapping once against the rug. Outside, the real yard was still at work, turning big rocks into smaller ones.

Gus closed his eyes and imagined one enormous rock wearing his little green cap. It looked very dignified.
