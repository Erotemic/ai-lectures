# The Truck on the Giant Scale

At the stone yard, Gus saw the biggest yellow wheel he had ever seen.

Then another.

Then a whole enormous yellow machine with a bucket on the front.

"That," he said, "is a truck wearing a shovel."

"It's a wheel loader," Aunt Jo told him.

The friends were watching from Mr. Fred's office window. Below them sat three finished-stone piles and a green dump truck with a deep metal bed.

"Not Dan's blue box truck?" asked Rex.

"No," said Jack. "Dan carries boxes. This one carries loose rocks."

"Very good," said Mr. Fred. "And we have the right stones ready for your park."

The green truck rolled into a marked loading bay. Its adult driver stopped and waited. The yellow loader approached a pile that held a mix of smaller angular stone and finer material.

The yellow wheel loader pushed its front bucket into the chosen pile, raised the bucket, and tipped stone into the waiting truck.

Clatter, clatter, clatter.

Rex's ears lifted together.

"It's raining rocks."

"Only into the truck," said Jack.

The loader went back for another bucketful. It moved on four enormous rubber tires, not on the crawler tracks of the excavator they had seen years earlier at the park.

Gus counted the loader's trips.

"One. Two. Three."

Then he looked worried.

"What if the truck gets too full?"

"That's why the crew checks the load," said Mr. Fred. "And why we have our big scale."

Through another window, the friends could see a broad flat platform set into the road near the exit.

"A bridge!" cried Rex.

"A weighing bridge," said Mr. Fred. "It's called a weighbridge."

Gus frowned. "Can Molly and I walk over it to the farm?"

"No," said Aunt Jo, smiling. "This one is for weighing trucks. Little Bridge is still the bridge for paws and feet."

Jack looked pleased. Two bridges with very different jobs would make an excellent pair of drawings.

The green truck's driver pulled slowly onto the scale and stopped. The office screen showed a number.

"It's a big number," said Gus.

"Trucks are heavy," said Rex.

"This one is a truck plus its load," said Mr. Fred.

He brought up the earlier measurement for the same empty truck.

Mr. Fred compared the weight of the loaded truck with its empty weight to find the weight of the stone alone.

Jack nodded, then hesitated.

"If I stood on a scale holding my notebook, would that number include my notebook?"

"Yes," said Aunt Jo.

"And if I put the notebook down, the number would change?"

"A little."

Gus looked down at his green cap.

"So a scale knows if I'm wearing my hat."

"A really careful one might notice," Mr. Fred said.

Rex glanced at Gus. "If you ate breakfast, it would notice that too."

"That explains a great deal," said Gus.

Mr. Fred finished the load ticket, and the green truck drove to the waiting area. Its driver would take the regular vehicle road and service lane to Pine Park the next morning, not the little walking bridge to Green Gate Farm.

Jack drew two rectangles in his notebook. One was the empty green truck. The other was the same truck with a gray mound in its bed.

Between them he drew a tiny scale.

"The rocks have been through so many machines," he said.

"And they're still rocks," Rex said, impressed.

On the way home, Gus rode beside Jack in Aunt Jo's van and held his cap on his knees.

"Tomorrow," he said, "the road gets a new pair of little stone shoes."

"Only one little patch," Jack corrected.

"One little shoe, then."

They watched the stone yard disappear behind the trees. Its trucks had jobs to finish, but the friends' day was done.
