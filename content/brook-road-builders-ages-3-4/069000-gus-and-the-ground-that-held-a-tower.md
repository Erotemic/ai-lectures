# Gus and the Ground That Held a Tower

Gus had seen piles of gravel. He had seen wooden bridge posts. He had even seen the little shelter beside Farmer Ruth's barn repaired with new bracing.

He had never seen a hole this large.

"Is this for a giant rabbit?" he asked.

"No," said Jack.

"A giant rabbit with a garage?"

"It's for the rocket's launch area," said Dr. Ada.

At Star Harbor, excavators were working far beyond a bright visitor fence. The friends watched from a covered platform. Beneath them, big trucks moved along the newly finished access road. The road had passed its checks. The ground crew now had room to bring in heavy equipment.

An excavator lowered its bucket into the earth, curled it, and raised a great scoop. It turned and emptied the scoop into a waiting haul truck.

"That's our first digger all grown up," Rex said.

He remembered the little yellow excavator that had begun Pine Park. Jack remembered it too. The first page of his notebook still showed its bucket swinging beside a patch of bare ground.

"What are they digging?" Molly asked.

Dr. Ada pointed at the plan spread across a table.

"There will be strong foundations for the launch structures. And this channel is a flame trench. When a rocket lifts off, its hot exhaust must be directed away from the equipment."

Gus stared at the deep channel.

"So the rocket has a chimney underneath it?"

"Not quite," Dr. Ada said. "But I see why you said that."

She showed them how the plan marked the large ground structures, the equipment roads, and a place for people to watch from far away. The excavators and concrete trucks were not building a tower out of thin air. They were preparing something strong below it first.

At lunch Jack tried a small experiment on the visitor table. Aunt Jo brought out a tray, a soft sponge, a firm wooden board, and two toy blocks. Gus placed a block on the sponge. It leaned as the sponge bent. On the board, the same block stood still.

"The sponge is taking a nap," said Gus.

"A soft base lets it tilt," Jack said. "But real ground is more complicated than a sponge. The engineers have to test it."

Dr. Ada nodded. "They study the soil and rock and design foundations for the loads. Your experiment helps you notice why the ground matters. It isn't a plan for building a real tower."

The friends watched a long truck arrive with steel pieces on its bed. On another side of the work area, a concrete mixer turned its drum. Rex recognized that machine from the building of Little Bridge.

"That one made the bridge feet," Rex said.

"And now it helps with the space center," said Aunt Jo.

Molly looked at Rex. "Same kind of truck. Bigger job."

The concrete went into prepared forms where the engineering team had planned it. Workers measured, checked, and waited for the material to become strong enough before the next stage. Gus was disappointed that a finished tower did not spring out of the ground by afternoon.

"If I plant a tower, will it grow?" he asked.

"Not like Farmer Ruth's hay," said Jack.

"Good," Gus said. "It might be hard to mow."

On the drive home they passed Mr. Fred's gravel truck heading back for another load. Gus watched it through the window, thinking about the enormous hole, the little sponge, and the strong ground that would one day hold a rocket.

He took off his green cap before bed and set it on the shelf.

Some things had to start at the bottom. Even things that were going all the way to the Moon.
