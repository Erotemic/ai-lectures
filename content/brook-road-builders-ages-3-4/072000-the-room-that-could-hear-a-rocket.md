# The Room That Could Hear a Rocket

There was a room at Star Harbor with no steering wheel.

Rex thought that was strange, because the room was going to help a rocket fly.

It had a long wall of screens. It had rows of desks. It had people with headsets and notebooks, each watching a different part of the space center.

"Where is the driver?" Rex asked.

"There will be a pilot inside the spacecraft," said Dr. Ada. "This is mission control. The people here watch the flight and help the crew."

Gus studied a screen that showed the launch pad. "Can it see me?"

"Not when you're in this visitor room," said Aunt Jo.

Gus waved at the screen anyway.

The friends were visiting after many weeks of building. The access road had been finished. The big assembly building had closed doors that could open for tall vehicles. The launch tower stood above the pad. Power lines, water pipes, radio antennas, and carefully marked work roads were all in place.

But there was still no crew rocket.

"Isn't the space center done?" Molly asked.

"The buildings are ready for the next work," Dr. Ada answered. "Now the rocket pieces can arrive, and we can begin testing them together."

An adult dog walked into the visitor room. He was tall, with golden fur and a blue flight jacket. He smiled at Rex and Molly.

"I'm Max," he said. "I'll be the pilot on the Moon journey."

Rex ran one short circle, stopped, and sat. "Do you know how to drive a rocket?"

"I've trained for a long time," Max said. "And I keep training. A mission is a job for many people, not one pair of paws."

He showed them a picture of the spacecraft cabin. There were six seats with special restraints. The engineers had designed and tested the roomy ship for a mixed crew of animals and people. Max and Aunt Jo would be the two adults on board. Dr. Ada would remain on Earth in mission control.

Jack pointed to the antenna shown on top of the building. "If we're far away, how will you hear us?"

"With radio," said Max. "Our voices can be turned into signals that travel through space."

"Even though space is empty?" Jack asked.

"Radio signals can travel through empty space," Dr. Ada said. "Sound from our mouths cannot."

Gus looked down at his green cap. "If I shout from the Moon, nobody here will hear me?"

"Not without a radio," said Max.

"Then I will wear the very best radio."

Dr. Ada let Jack push a harmless demonstration button. A red dot on a model of the launch tower changed to green. Across the room, another screen showed the same change.

"A message went from one place to another," she explained.

Jack drew two little boxes connected by a line. "Like our town map," he said, "but the line isn't a road."

"Exactly."

Before leaving, the friends looked through the windows toward the pad. Workers were still checking equipment. A large crane waited beside the assembly building. A crawler rested at the far end of its special track. The big machines were quiet for the evening.

"We built a road to get here," Jack said. "Then a place to make a rocket. Then a way to speak to it."

"And somewhere to park the rocket," Gus added.

Rex pressed his nose against the cool visitor glass. Past the pad lay the sea, and above the sea was the pale Moon. There was much more work to do. But Star Harbor was no longer a drawing on Dr. Ada's table.

It was a real place, waiting for a rocket.
