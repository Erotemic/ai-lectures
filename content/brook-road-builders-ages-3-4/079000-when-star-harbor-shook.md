# When Star Harbor Shook

On the morning of the launch, the sky above Star Harbor was blue.

Rex was awake before Aunt Jo called him. His red collar was in its usual place. His little wooden dump truck sat beside the bed. He gave its four wheels one last gentle push across the rug.

"I'm going farther than you today," he told it.

At the space center, the grown-up teams had been working since long before dawn. Max met the travelers at the crew building. Dr. Ada gave each friend a small card with a picture showing the path from Earth to the Moon and back.

"Remember," she said, "we can wait if anything needs another check. Getting home matters just as much as getting there."

Gus held up his green cap. "Can the Moon wait if I forget my cap?"

"The cap can travel in your bag," Aunt Jo said.

Molly touched her nose gently to Rex's. One wag each. Jack tucked his blue notebook into its closed pouch.

The six travelers put on their flight suits with help from the crew team. They rode an elevator up the launch tower, walked through the enclosed access passage, and entered the roomy cabin of Star One. Every seat had been fitted and tested for the person or animal using it.

Max checked the controls. Aunt Jo checked the four young travelers. Rex's ears lay snugly against the padding beside his head. Gus counted six seats once, then counted them again to make sure a seat had not gone wandering.

"Six," he reported.

Dr. Ada's voice came over the radio from mission control. "Star One, we are completing the final checks."

Outside, the tower stood beside the rocket. Ground equipment supplied the vehicle with what it needed, and the water systems were ready beneath the pad. The long crawler was nowhere underneath it now. Its slow, careful work had finished earlier.

Then the countdown stopped.

Rex blinked. "Did the rocket forget something?"

Max listened to the team on the radio. A ground sensor needed a second look. Nobody hurried. The crew sat comfortably and waited while engineers checked the reading. A little later, mission control said the sensor check was complete and the countdown could resume.

"Good," said Molly softly.

The final numbers came over the radio.

Three. Two. One.

Far below, the engines started. Water rushed across the launch structure to help protect it. The rocket pushed its hot exhaust downward and lifted away from Star Harbor.

The cabin trembled. Rex felt the seat pressing against his back. Gus's green cap stayed inside its bag. Jack could see blue sky in the window, but he could not see the whole rocket from his little seat.

"We're moving," Aunt Jo said.

Max watched the instruments. The team on Earth watched too. The rocket climbed above the sea, leaving a bright trail high in the sky.

After a while there was a change in the sound. A part of the rocket had finished its job and separated along the planned route. Another part continued the work of carrying their spacecraft toward orbit. The crew did not need the spent part anymore.

The shaking eased. The window grew darker blue, then black. Through it, Rex saw the curve of Earth.

"Where is the road?" he asked in a whisper.

"Behind us," said Jack. "It helped us reach the launch pad."

"And now?"

"Now we're following a path in space."

The Star One spacecraft reached its planned orbit, and the ground team confirmed the checks. Max let everyone know they could relax their shoulders while remaining fastened until instructed otherwise.

At Pine Park, the little wooden map board still showed the garage, firehouse, bridge, and farm. Farmer Ruth, Dan, Ms. Pam, Captain Ann, Mr. Fred, and Mr. Lee had watched the launch on a screen in the village hall. When the rocket rose, everyone had cheered.

Far above the clouds, Rex looked at Earth through the window. It was the biggest thing he had ever seen, and also the farthest from his front step he had ever been.

He felt Aunt Jo's hand resting beside his paw.

They were on their way.
