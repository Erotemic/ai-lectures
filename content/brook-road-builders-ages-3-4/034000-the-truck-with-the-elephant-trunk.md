# The Truck with the Elephant Trunk

Rex did not expect to meet an elephant on Brook Road.

But that is what he thought he saw.

From the waiting-room window of the Red Door Garage came a sight that made both his ears stand up.

A large green-and-white truck had parked near the road junction. It wore a round silver tank on its back. Curled along one side was a long, thick hose.

"Elephant," Rex whispered.

Gus pressed his nose to the window beside Rex's.

"Truck," he said.

"Elephant truck," Rex corrected.

Jack put his blue notebook on the waiting-room table.

"I want to know what it does."

Ms. Pam was standing outside with Aunt Jo, speaking to the town workers. She came back in to explain.

"The big truck is here to clean the storm drain," she said.

Gus looked down at the floor.

"The drain is under the street. Does the truck have a tiny worker who can fit inside?"

"No tiny worker needed," Ms. Pam said. "It has a hose."

They could see the metal street grate from the window. The rainwater had traveled there along the curb after the rainy night.

Below the grate was a catch basin where sand and little bits of rubbish could collect.

"Like the bottom of my boot," Gus said, looking at the dust on his sole.

"More like a little pocket under the road," Jack said. He drew a square, then a smaller square beneath it.

Outside, the adult workers marked the area and opened the drain for cleaning. The children stayed by the window with Aunt Jo. The workers brought the thick hose from the truck to the basin.

Rex's eyes grew wide.

"There goes the trunk!"

"A very long trunk," Gus said.

The hose bent into a smooth curve. The pump on the truck started with a low steady rumble.

The big hose drew muddy water and grit up into a tank on the truck.

It was not washing the mud into the road. It was taking it away.

"This truck eats dirt," Rex said.

"But it does not eat biscuits," Gus added solemnly.

"How do you know?" Rex asked.

"It hasn't asked for one."

Jack drew two different trucks on a fresh notebook page. One had round spinning brushes. The other had a round tank and a long hose.

"Yesterday's sweeper picked up leaves and grit from the road," he said. "This one gets mud from down inside the drain."

Ms. Pam looked at the drawings.

"Exactly. They have different jobs."

Rex looked out at the hose again. "When the truck is finished, does it take its trunk home?"

"It rolls it up on the truck," said Aunt Jo.

"An elephant who can pack its nose," Rex said, impressed.

When the work was done, the crew put the cover back and checked the area. The hose curled into place along the side of the truck. The motor quieted, and the driver waved as the truck pulled away.

The street grate looked almost the same as before. That surprised Jack.

"It looks ordinary again," he said.

"Some jobs happen where you can't see them afterward," Aunt Jo replied.

Rex sniffed the air when they stepped out onto the sidewalk. He could smell damp pavement, the garage, and a hint of the trees by the park.

"No elephant smell," he announced.

"Very good," said Gus. "Then it is definitely a truck."

Back home, Rex put the hose drawing beside his wooden dump truck.

His toy could tip a load. The broom truck could sweep the road. The elephant truck could clean beneath it.

Three very different trucks.

All of them worth dreaming about.
