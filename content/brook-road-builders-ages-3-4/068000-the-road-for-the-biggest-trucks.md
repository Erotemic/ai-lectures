# The Road for the Biggest Trucks

The first time Rex saw the road to Star Harbor, there was hardly a road at all.

There was a marked strip of ground beside a long county highway, a fence, and a row of bright orange machines. Beyond it was open land. Farther still, if Rex stood on his back paws, he could see a thin line of blue sea.

"That's a lot of room," Gus said.

"That's the idea," said Dr. Ada. "A launch site needs plenty of clear space around it."

Aunt Jo had driven Rex, Gus, Jack, and Molly out from Brook Road. They had passed Ms. Pam's red garage door, joined the county highway, and traveled much farther than the little farm road. Dan's blue delivery truck had gone ahead with lunch boxes and a crate of work gloves.

At the gate a grown-up worker checked the visitor list. Then the friends climbed the steps to a covered viewing platform. Behind the work fence, Mr. Fred's green dump truck arrived carrying gravel from the aggregate yard.

Rex recognized it at once. "Our park has some of those stones."

"It does," said Mr. Fred, who had come to explain the loads. "But this road must be built for very heavy vehicles. The engineers have made a plan for the ground beneath it."

The dump truck backed slowly to a marked spot. A worker guided the driver. The bed rose. Gravel slid down with a long rushing sound.

"A mountain delivered sideways," Gus said.

A yellow grader came next. Its long blade was slung beneath the middle of the machine.

"Why isn't the blade at the front?" Jack asked.

"It helps the driver shape the surface carefully," Mr. Fred said. "Watch the thin line of gravel it leaves."

The grader rolled on. Gravel shifted into a smooth, low ridge. A large roller followed. It passed back and forth over the prepared layers, pressing them into place.

Rex watched the roller's broad drum. It was not the first roller he had seen. A smaller one had helped finish the path around Pancake Hill. This one looked as if it could roll an entire picnic blanket flat.

"It would squash every sandwich," Rex observed.

"We will keep the sandwiches up here," said Aunt Jo.

Jack drew a line across his notebook page. First came the prepared ground. Then stone. Then leveling. Then the roller. He looked at the workers measuring the road.

"Do they need to know how much weight it can hold?"

"Yes," said Dr. Ada. "Big transporters will use this road. The design and tests are done by the road team."

Gus watched another dump truck arrive.

"What is the biggest thing that's going to ride here?"

"Parts of a rocket," Dr. Ada said. "And later, a crawler that can move a rocket slowly from its building to its launch pad."

"Slowly?" said Rex. "I thought rockets were fast."

"In the sky they are. On the ground we move them very carefully."

That was such a funny idea that Gus spent the whole picnic pretending to be a rocket creeping across a rug. He moved one foot, waited, and moved the other.

"Nearly there," he announced after three steps.

"You have reached your sandwich," Aunt Jo told him.

When they left, the new road was still being built. The grader was working on another section, and the dump trucks kept coming. No one tried to drive across the unfinished part.

Back in Brook Road, the little picture map at Pine Park had not changed. Little Bridge remained a walking bridge; the new space road branched from a distant highway instead.

That night Rex parked his small wooden dump truck beside his bed.

He had seen a road begin as lines on the ground. It would take many more truckloads before a rocket could use it.

Rex liked that thought. It meant tomorrow the trucks would still have work to do.
