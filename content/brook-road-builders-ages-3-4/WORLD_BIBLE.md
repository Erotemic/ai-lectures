# Brook Road Builders — world bible (not spoken)

## Identity, geography, and boundaries

This is an original, separate story world from Willow Bend. The protagonists are a talking dog and bear cub alongside a human boy. Machines are ordinary powered machinery, never talking or driving themselves; animal adults and other grown-up workers may drive. Adult site leadership is ordinary background to the adventures, not a repeated safety lecture.

- **Brook Road:** a quiet residential road where Rex and Aunt Jo live in a small house with a blue front step. Gus lives nearby; Jack is their neighbor. Rex's house has a rug and a wooden toy dump truck; there is a sand table near the house.
- **Old field / Pine Park:** at the end of Brook Road, formerly weeds, soft ground and a muddy patch, with **one existing tall pine tree**. The site has a work fence and safe sidewalk observation place while active; it becomes **Pine Park** after the work crew completes the project and the fence is removed.
- **Final park layout:** a firm curving gravel walking path passes the tree, circles a low wide mound called **Pancake Hill**, and comes back to the gate. A bench stands under the existing pine. A gate sign says **PINE PARK**, with a small drawing containing Jack's tree, Gus's hill and Rex's puppy pawprint. No lake, tall climbing equipment or second pine is added in Season One.

## Recurring characters

- **Rex:** a playful young brown-and-white puppy, **one dark ear and one light ear**, and a wagging tail. Loves trucks, sniffing grass and identifying things with his nose. His aunt is Jo. He lives in the house with the blue step. He talks; this is an original animal-and-human-fiction world. Do not treat Rex as a human boy or make him secretly able to drive vehicles.
- **Gus:** a small brown bear cub with a **green cap**. Loves hills, counting, absurd pictures and very large machinery. His humor is broad but not all he does. Names the gentle mound **Pancake Hill** in episode 003000. The cap is not lost, eaten, or permanently covered in sand.
- **Jack:** a **little human boy** who likes **being a scientist**. Has a **blue notebook** and a frequently sharpened pencil. He asks genuine small questions, makes observations and simple drawings, and is willing not to know. His drawings should not grant him adult-level engineering knowledge. He is a coequal friend, not a narrator's classroom assistant.
- **Aunt Jo:** Rex's adult aunt, who has an orange work vest and helps lead the Pine Park building crew. She accompanies the children to the sidewalk viewing area; grown-up drivers control all powered construction machines. She is an adult **dog**, not another puppy or a child.
- **Other animals:** bees, beetles, ants, and a mother duck with ducklings may appear naturally; keep additional named characters rare. A dog-centered series does not require a new breed or puppy name every episode.

## Machine and construction facts

1. **Excavator:** tracked machine with an articulated digging arm and bucket; it can swing its upper structure to deposit excavated material. Episode 001000 begins the earthworks.
2. **Dump truck:** transports gravel and raises its bed to tip material behind the stationary truck. Episode 002000 leaves **two gravel piles**.
3. **Bulldozer:** pushes soil along the ground using its front blade, shaping the gentle low mound. Episode 003000 establishes the name **Pancake Hill**.
4. **Wheel loader:** wheeled machine with front bucket, lifts and carries gravel from the two piles toward the route. Episode 004000 distributes some gravel but does not finish or compact the path.
5. **Road roller:** a large rotating steel drum compacts spread granular material on the path. Episode 005000 completes this stage. Adult crew finishes the bench and checks the site.
6. **Finished park:** In episode 006000 the crew has checked the park, the blue fence is gone, sign installed, and public walking begins. The heavy machines are off site. Don't later depict the park as an active construction site unless new work is explicitly introduced.

Fanciful similes (giant spoon, cookie roller, gravel mountains) are character jokes, not claims that machinery is alive or a food-production tool. Keep technical distinctions accurate and simple. References are in `metadata/sources.json`.

## Season Two — The Little Bridge: canonical expansion

### Established geography (after episode 012000)

- For simple narration, travel **east along Brook Road** past Rex's blue-step house, Gus's house, and Jack's house to **Pine Park**. The established park loop and bench under the *one original tall pine* remain unchanged.
- A **short new path branches east from the far side of the Pine Park loop** to a small pedestrian bridge across **the brook**. This bridge is *beyond* the park's original completed area and does not retroactively replace Season One's path or mound. The bridge was a separate fenced job while underway.
- Beyond the bridge to the east is **Green Gate Farm**, entered through a literal green gate. It has a red barn, a porch, a farm path, a pen, hens, woolly sheep, and a small goat. The farmhouse porch is where Molly keeps a blue rope toy. **Farmer Ruth**, a grown-up goat, manages the farm.
- Trucks and heavy machinery reach the bridge site using a **service lane north of Pine Park** and the **long farm road** to the far bank; *neither active equipment nor delivery trucks drive on the completed park walking loop or the new footbridge*. The longer farm road loops around the brook and connects outside the scene; its exact junction is not yet mapped in speech.
- The wooden pedestrian bridge has concrete end supports, a prebuilt timber deck, side rails, and gravel approach paths. The small sign says **LITTLE BRIDGE**. It is for walking, not vehicle traffic. Open only at the end of 012000 after adult checks and fence removal.

**Simple route:** Brook Road houses → Pine Park loop → short branch path → Little Bridge (over brook) → Green Gate Farm (green gate, farmhouse porch, red barn). Vehicles use the separate road route.

### Recurring neighbors

- **Molly** is a *black-and-white puppy with white front paws*, distinct from Rex's brown-and-white body and one-dark/one-light ears. She lives at the farm with Farmer Ruth. She has a **blue rope toy** kept by the farmhouse door/porch. She enjoys watching trucks and chatting with Rex across the brook. Rex and Molly meet nose-to-nose for the first time in 012000 and are now friendly; future stories can have Molly visit Pine Park with an adult.
- **Farmer Ruth** is a grown-up goat who cares for Green Gate Farm, its animals and paths. She stands with Molly at the gate or porch. Her job is farming, not being a crane operator.
- **Mr. Lee** is an adult beaver and experienced carpenter on Aunt Jo's building crew; he works on wood, decks and handrails. He isn't a child or a magical bridge builder. Both Aunt Jo and Mr. Lee remain available for ordinary future projects.
- Unnamed animals established at Green Gate Farm: **three hens, two woolly sheep and a goat with a striped collar**; other farm animals may be introduced gradually. The park previously had a duck and ducklings; avoid assigning these to the farm without an explicit journey.

## Season Three — Farm Workdays: canonical expansion

### Geography and calendar

- **Hayfield:** a modest field of long grass **south of the red barn** at Green Gate Farm. Its work entrance is from the farm lane. It was already farm land in the wider region but had not previously appeared in an episode. It is not part of Pine Park or a new construction site.
- **Farmyard bench:** beside the red barn, where Aunt Jo and Farmer Ruth can accompany the visiting children to watch field work. The working field and powered implements stay with grown-up operators.
- **Vehicle route:** Farmer Ruth's red farm tractor and wagon use the farm lane and the longer farm road. Rex, Molly, Gus and Jack may walk via the open Little Bridge with adults; neither tractor nor hay wagon crosses the footbridge.
- **Time:** The six visits occur over several warm, dry days, with drying time between mowing, tedding, raking, baling and storing. No miraculous instant hay, and no sudden change to winter. The hayfield is now cut for this harvest and may regrow later.

### New persistent machines, animals, and objects

- **Red farm tractor:** Farmer Ruth's own tractor. It has two larger rear tires, two smaller front tires, and pulls implements and a low four-wheel hay wagon. This particular shape does not describe every tractor. **The farm, not the children, owns and operates it**.
- **Hay equipment:** a tractor-drawn mower cuts grass; a tedder fluffs the cut forage to dry; a rake moves it into windrows; a small-square baler compresses it into rectangular bales tied with twine. All machine work is done by adult farm workers. Bales are carried on the wagon to the covered red barn and stored dry above bare ground.
- **Hay storage:** a raised, dry storage area inside the existing red barn contains the harvested bales at the end of episode 018000. The hens eat their own appropriate feed; the hay is for sheep/goats. There is no invented barn extension or new barn.
- **Animals:** The established three hens, two woolly sheep, and one small goat with striped collar continue at the farm. No additional named farm animal is introduced in Season Three. Farmer Ruth's straw hat is an optional new visual detail, not a plot token.
- **Molly's blue rope toy:** still kept at her farmhouse porch/door; she leaves it there before visiting Rex's park. Molly has now visited Pine Park once with Farmer Ruth, so future stories can recognize its bench, the single pine, the PINE PARK sign, and Pancake Hill as familiar to her.
- **Rex and Molly's secret wave:** one small tail wag each, begun in 015000, revisited in 018000. Their friendship has moved beyond the two sides of the brook.
- **Jack's blue notebook:** keeps every earlier page. New entries: big/small wheels and the farm lane (013), long/short cut grass (014), a tedder and two wagging tails (015), loose grass contrasted with windrows (016), lines of loose hay and rectangular bales (017), separate vehicle and foot routes (018). No notebook pages are discarded.

## Season Four - The Garage with the Red Door (019000-024000)

There is a sign reading **RED DOOR GARAGE**, a separate work yard and bay, and a small public viewing bench near a flowering shrub. ## Season Six — After the Rain (031000–036000)

### Weather and drainage changes, not a construction reset

A single moderate overnight rain takes place before 031000. It has ended by the children's first outing. **Pine Park remains open**, the curving gravel path and Pancake Hill remain stable, the tall pine and bench stand where they always have, and the completed Little Bridge across the brook stays available for walking. There is no major flood or sudden destruction. Rain drains over the course of the following days while ordinary crews perform maintenance. No individual episode claims a new storm occurred during this period.

- **Park rain gauge:** The empty clear tube Aunt Jo installed at the end of Season Five catches its **first rain**. At 031000 Jack records the water at about the **third marked line** in his blue notebook. It stays mounted upright, open to the sky, away from the tall pine. It may lose a little water during subsequent dry days; do not treat an old reading as a new storm total. In 036000 Jack and Aunt Jo inspect it again, make a second mark, **empty it**, and return it upright to its holder so it is ready for another rain.
- **Roadside drainage:** The curb on the paved stretch of Brook Road west of the Pine Park gate gently slopes toward a **grated storm inlet** near the western junction by the garage. Some rain infiltrates grassy park soil; some runs downhill over the paved road toward stormwater drainage. The children never float, throw, or place objects into the inlet. Do not imply all runoff is filtered before entering a stream.
- **Municipal street sweeper (033000):** One adult-operated **town street sweeper** has spinning side brushes and a debris-collection hopper. It removes grit and fallen leaves from the public road edge. This sweeper is separate from Ms. Pam's white mobile service truck, white flatbed tow truck, and Dan's blue box truck. Ms. Pam brings out her four orange nesting cones to mark the temporary work area; she puts them back in the garage shed afterward.
- **Vacuum drain-cleaning truck (034000):** A **green-and-white municipal vacuum truck with a silver tank and large flexible hose** removes deposited dirty water, grit, and sediment from the street **catch basin beneath the inlet** near the garage. Its hose/tank job is distinct from a street sweeper's rotating brooms. The workers close and inspect the inlet before leaving. The children observe from the garage waiting-room window and do not approach an open drain.
- **Farm road drainage (035000):** The **existing longer vehicle road to Green Gate Farm** has a short shallow ditch and **small pipe culvert beneath one low point**, first noticed this season. Fallen leaves partly slow the ditch flow. Farmer Ruth and Mr. Lee arrange an adult-led inspection/clearing and **a compact loader brings new gravel** for the softened road shoulder. - **Home observation (036000):** Jack uses a tilted shallow tray and a spoonful of water with Aunt Jo helping to observe downhill flow; it is a home-scale observation, not a literal miniature drainage system. Jack draws a compact **rain-route page** in his existing blue notebook, showing soil, curb, inlet, farm-road culvert and a loader. No separate permanent booklet or science facility is created.

## Season Seven — Firehouse Open Day (037000–042000)

### The firehouse is now a real, permanent neighbor

- **Place:** A short paved side road named **Station Lane** branches **north from Brook Road between Rex's blue-step house and Pine Park**. At its end is a brick firehouse with **two enormous red bay doors**, a clear vehicle apron, a separate designated visitor path and bench, and a small marked demonstration yard. This is **not** the Red Door Garage, which remains **west** of Rex's home near the farm-road junction. The firehouse is a new location in the storytelling, not evidence that previous landmarks have moved.
- **Recurring adult:** **Captain Ann**, an adult **fox firefighter** with a navy station shirt and yellow helmet, introduces the vehicles and later demonstrates protective gear. Other adult crew members are unnamed. Captain Ann is a neighbor the children can recognize on future visits, not a pretend rescue-hero character.
- **Apparatus:** The **shorter red pumper engine** has a water tank, pump, hose compartments, and tool holders. The **longer red aerial ladder truck** stores a retractable extending ladder above its cab/body and deploys stabilizers on ground pads when the trained crew operates the ladder. These are **distinct trucks**, with different primary roles. They remain at the station at the end of the season.
- **Visitor routine:** The visit is a scheduled, calm open day, not an emergency call. Children remain with adult caregivers along designated viewing paths. The engine compartments are shown by firefighters, the aerial ladder is raised only in a clear marked practice area by trained crew, and the water demo sprays a **painted practice target**, not a real fire. ## Authoring guardrails

- Persistent geography, name spellings and object state survive across episodes and seasons. Never erase a completed project without a depicted reason.
- Every story has its own enjoyable action, a few explicit callbacks, and a quiet ending; no manufactured fear, peril, or cliffhanger.
- **Primary listener age is three:** distinct easy-to-pronounce names, concrete action, physical sounds, visible consequences and jokes. Avoid lecturing the listener, overly long technical paragraphs, and repetitive forced morals.
- Trucks, dogs and construction equipment should be genuinely central, while the boy's scientific curiosity is a natural character trait rather than an exam. More animals can enter future episodes without replacing the established trio.
- All child character interactions with working vehicles occur from a comfortable viewing area with an adult. Adult operators perform the powered tasks. The stories are not a home-construction manual.
- No fixed word counts, narration time, video/acting roles, or generated art requirements. Single-narrator TTS with nonspoken caregiver notes and evidence records.
