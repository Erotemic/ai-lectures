# Jack and the Two Lines on the Map

Jack had drawn the farm and the bridge. He had drawn Pine Park with its tall pine and Pancake Hill.

But there was something missing from his neighborhood map.

Actually, there were several things.

"The garage," Rex said.

"The firehouse," Gus said.

"The gravel yard," Jack added. "And Dan's parcel shed."

Gus leaned over the notebook. "I think we need a bigger notebook."

"Or a smaller pencil," said Rex.

Aunt Jo suggested they walk along Brook Road and check the places one at a time. Mr. Lee could look over the drawing with them before making anything for the park entrance.

They began at the Red Door Garage. Ms. Pam the raccoon mechanic was working near her white tow truck. Its tilting bed was level and empty today. Beside the garage stood her other white vehicle, the mobile service truck with its many drawers.

Rex remembered riding in Aunt Jo's yellow van after Pam had fixed its tire. Gus remembered watching the van rise on the garage lift.

"I remember the lift," Gus said. "It made the van taller than my cap."

"Everything is taller than your cap," Rex said.

"That's why I keep it on top of my head."

Jack showed Ms. Pam his drawing.

"Here's your red door," he said. "And here's Dan's parcel shed near the junction. But I can't tell where the long farm road starts."

Ms. Pam pointed outside the garage yard, toward the road junction.

"The farm road branches here," she said. "Delivery and service trucks use it to reach Farmer Ruth's place."

Jack drew the branch where she pointed. He put a tiny blue rectangle beside the parcel shed to represent Dan's box truck.

"Good," said Pam. "My white service truck can follow that road too. No need to take it into the park."

They continued along Brook Road past Rex's blue front step, then turned into Station Lane.

The firehouse stood there with its two tall red doors. Captain Ann, the adult fox firefighter, was outside checking the red fire engine. The engine had hoses and a pump. Behind the other door rested the longer ladder truck with its folded aerial ladder.

"Hello!" Captain Ann called.

Gus raised his green cap.

"Is your ladder going up today?"

"Not today," she said. "We're checking our equipment."

"That's all right," Gus replied. "The clouds have plenty of stairs of their own."

Jack turned his notebook toward the firehouse.

He had drawn its red doors on the wrong side of Brook Road.

"Wait," he said. "Station Lane comes off the north side. I put it on the south."

He tried turning his notebook around. Now the firehouse looked right, but the farm was on the wrong side of the page.

Rex stared at it.

"Can you turn the farm around too?"

"I don't think Farmer Ruth would like that," Jack said.

Captain Ann came over to look. "When you draw a map, the directions have to stay consistent," she said. "You can turn the paper while you're working, but it helps to choose one way for the top of the page."

Jack chose north to be at the top. He redrew the little Station Lane branch in the right place and made two red door shapes beside it.

"Two doors," Rex said. "That will be easy to remember."

After a rest on a bench, they went on toward Pine Park. Across the northern service lane, they could see the route that led to Mr. Fred's aggregate yard. They did not go into the working yard. Mr. Fred, the adult badger, had arranged to meet them at the safe roadside pull-off, away from the trucks.

"What have you got there?" he asked, seeing Jack's notebook.

"A picture map for people who don't know the neighborhood," Jack said.

"Then don't forget the service lane. Trucks reach my yard from the road. They don't drive through the park's walking loop."

Jack drew the lane in the right place. Then he made a small drawing of three stones, one large and two small, to mark the gravel yard.

"Not a mountain of gravel?" Gus asked.

"Three stones will fit," Jack said.

"A mountain would fit if you used the back of the paper," Gus suggested.

Aunt Jo looked over all the little pictures. There was a red door for the garage, two red doors for the firehouse, a blue box for Dan's truck shed, three stones for the gravel yard, a pine tree for the park, a bridge over blue water, and a red barn beyond a green gate.

The solid line showed where vehicles went. The dotted line showed the park walking route across Little Bridge. Jack added a small key at the bottom, so the little pictures and the two line styles had meanings.

"It's not every tiny thing in town," Jack said.

"No," Aunt Jo agreed. "It's enough to help someone find the main places."

At Pine Park, Mr. Lee was waiting to examine the final sketch. He checked the road and path positions with Aunt Jo. He also checked that a board could stand beside the park entrance without covering the original park sign or Jack's rain gauge.

"I can build a small covered picture board," he said. "We can put it beside the entrance where walkers can see it."

"Could you put a cap on it?" Gus tried one last time.

"As a picture?" Mr. Lee asked.

"No. A real cap. For when the map feels cold."

Rex began to giggle. Soon Gus did too.

The grown-ups would make the board. Jack would help choose the pictures, and Rex and Molly would check the walking route once more.

They had done enough for one day. Aunt Jo walked the children home past the firehouse and Rex's blue step. Jack closed his notebook on a map that finally kept north at the top, the farm to the east, and the long vehicle road apart from the little bridge.

Gus took off his cap at home. It had not become a map hat. That, he decided, was probably for the best.
