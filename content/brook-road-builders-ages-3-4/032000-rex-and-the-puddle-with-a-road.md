# Rex and the Puddle with a Road

The next morning, Rex went back to see the puddle at Pine Park.

"I want to know if that other puppy is still in there," he told Gus.

"The very flat puppy?"

"The one who copies my ears."

But the puddle had shrunk. It looked more like a fat silver leaf now, lying beside the paved roadside.

Rex peered into it. His nose appeared in the water. The nose was very round.

"There he is," Rex said. "He's had a small breakfast."

Jack and Aunt Jo were walking beside them on the park path. Jack had brought his notebook, but this morning he had forgotten to sharpen his pencil.

"It still writes," he said, making a short fuzzy line.

"Maybe it writes rainy pictures," Gus said.

Near the grass beside the path, Jack pointed to a patch of dark damp earth. The soil was soft, and the grass sprang back when Aunt Jo brushed a finger across it.

"Where did all the rain go?" he wondered.

Rex sniffed the ground and made an important face.

"It went down. I can smell that it went down."

"Some of it did," Aunt Jo said.

They looked next at the paved road. There were tiny beads of water on its dark surface, and a narrow ribbon of water moving along the curb.

Some rain soaked into the grassy ground, but water on the road had nowhere to soak through.

"Like the roof," Jack said. "It ran off the roof last night."

Gus pictured the road wearing a raincoat. A very long raincoat. With pockets for all the cars.

"Does it have boots?" he asked.

"What does?" said Jack.

"The road. If it has a raincoat, it needs boots."

Rex was more interested in the ribbon of moving water. He walked along the park path, matching its direction with his nose.

"This puddle has found a road of its own."

"A very narrow road," Gus agreed. "Only one drop can drive at a time."

Aunt Jo stopped where the sidewalk turned away from the road. The children could see the water moving farther down the curb without needing to go near it.

The thin stream followed the road downhill toward a metal grate.

It slipped out of sight between the dark bars.

"Where does it go now?" Rex asked.

"Into a drain and a pipe below the street," Aunt Jo said. "The town has places for rainwater to travel."

"A secret road!" Gus exclaimed.

"For water," Jack said. "Not for trucks."

Rex looked at Gus. "A very small dump truck couldn't fit?"

"Not even a pretend one," Jack said.

They went back into Pine Park. The old pine held shining drops on its needles. The soil near the tree was damp but not covered by a wide sheet of water.

Jack drew two pictures in the notebook. On one page, arrows pointed down into grass and soil. On the other, a little arrow followed the curb toward the grate.

Gus studied them.

"I like that the arrows have jobs," he said. "One is a digger arrow and one is a traveling arrow."

Rex was still thinking about the puddle.

"Where will my flat puppy sleep when the puddle is gone?"

Aunt Jo pointed to Rex's shadow on the path, sharp and dark now that the sun was out.

"Perhaps he'll try a different kind of copy," she said.

Rex turned around. His shadow turned too.

"Oh, hello again," he whispered.

And the four friends continued around the park while the little ribbon of water quietly went its own way.
