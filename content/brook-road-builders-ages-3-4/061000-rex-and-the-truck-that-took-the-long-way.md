# Rex and the Truck That Took the Long Way

The first sound Rex heard that morning was a truck.

Not a very loud truck. Not a truck outside his window. It was farther away, down by the red-door garage.

Rummm, rummm.

Rex sat up on his blue front step. His red collar had twisted around in the night, so Aunt Jo straightened it for him.

Rummm.

"Dan," Rex said. He knew the soft rumble of Dan's blue delivery truck.

Gus came out wearing his green cap, and Jack followed with his blue notebook tucked under one arm.

"Where's it going?" Gus asked.

"We can find out," said Jack.

They walked with Aunt Jo along Brook Road. At the junction near Ms. Pam's garage, Dan the donkey was checking the back of his truck. The truck had a tall blue box behind its cab and a white stripe along the side. Its rear door rolled upward when Dan opened it.

"Morning!" Dan called. "I'm taking a sack of hen feed to Farmer Ruth. The hens don't like an empty feed bin. They are very loud about it."

"I know those hens," said Rex. "They are loud when the bin is full, too."

Dan laughed. He shut the rolling door, checked the truck, and climbed into the driver's seat.

"See you at the farm," he said.

The blue box truck turned onto the longer farm road. Its tires made a low humming sound as it went away.

Rex looked toward Pine Park.

"But Molly's farm is *that* way."

"It is," said Aunt Jo.

"Dan went the other way," said Gus. "Maybe his truck forgot where Molly lives."

"Dan knows the way," Aunt Jo said. "His truck needs the vehicle road."

The friends followed the path past the pine tree, the bench, and Pancake Hill. At the far side of the park, the little wooden bridge crossed the brook.

Rex stopped to sniff the end of the bridge. It smelled like sun-warmed wood, water, and a duck that had probably passed by earlier.

"I think I know a duck's way across," he announced.

"Can the duck drive Dan's truck?" Gus asked.

"No," said Rex. "And I hope it doesn't try."

They walked over Little Bridge with Aunt Jo. Below them, the brook made quiet swishing noises around the stones. Beyond the bridge lay Green Gate Farm, its red barn, and the little side shelter Mr. Lee had repaired. Under that dry shelter sat Farmer Ruth's green wheelbarrow, exactly where it belonged.

Molly was waiting at the farm path. Her white front paws were dusty. She gave Rex one wag, and Rex gave one back.

"You're here!" she said.

"We took the little bridge," Rex replied.

"Of course," said Molly. "You have paws."

"Dan has hooves," Gus said. "Why didn't he come this way?"

Molly looked at him. "Because his *truck* has wheels. Big ones."

A moment later the blue box truck appeared down the farm road, past the long bend near the fields. It came to a stop in Farmer Ruth's delivery area.

"There he is!" Rex barked.

Dan lowered a hand truck and eased the feed sack onto it. He rolled the load toward the dry storage place while Farmer Ruth opened the door. The hens hurried along their side of the fence, talking all at once.

"Cluck, cluck, cluck!"

"They've been waiting," said Dan.

"I think they've been practicing their speech," said Gus.

Jack opened his notebook. On one page he drew the park and the bridge. On another he drew the road curving around toward the farm.

"Our path is shorter," he said. "But it's a walking path. The truck went around where trucks can go."

He drew two little pictures beside his lines: a paw print and a wheel.

"Can you put a wheel on a paw?" Gus asked.

Rex studied his feet.

"I'd need four very small wheels," he said. "And I couldn't scratch my ear."

Molly tried to imagine a puppy on wheels. She laughed so hard she sat down.

After the feed had been put away, Farmer Ruth brought the friends cups of water and a little snack on the porch. Jack kept glancing at his drawing.

"That little sign at Pine Park tells us we're at the park," he said. "But it doesn't show how the park, the bridge, and the farm fit together."

"Your notebook does," Molly said.

"Only when I bring it."

Gus turned his cap around, thinking.

"What if the park had a map? A map big enough for a bear to see."

"And a puppy," said Rex.

"And another puppy," Molly added.

Jack drew a little square at the park gate. Inside it he put two curly lines, one for the road and one for the walking path.

"We could ask Aunt Jo," he said.

They did not have to finish their idea that day. The hens were clucking, the brook was running, and Dan's empty truck was already heading home by its own long road.

Rex lay in the porch shade beside Molly. It seemed rather nice that a little bridge and a long road could both lead to the same place.
