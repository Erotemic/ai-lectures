# Brook Road Builders — series atlas and expansion plan (not spoken)

This is a planning companion, **not** a second continuity authority. `WORLD_BIBLE.md` holds canonical facts through episode 084000. Ideas tagged *future* must not be treated as already present in a spoken episode.

## What makes this a world, not a truck catalogue

The listener should recognize places and friends when they return. Jobs finish and the result remains. A truck has a purpose in the story; it is not a character that magically acts on its own. Rex's dog senses, Gus's bear humor and Jack's scientific curiosity drive different kinds of scenes. Molly joins as a dog friend who knows the farm, without replacing the original three. The story world includes working animal adults as well as animal children and Jack, the human boy. None of the characters needs to be brave or helpful in every episode.

## The cast and the kinds of stories they produce

|---|---|---|---|

The existing animal visitors — duck with ducklings at Pine Park, sheep, hens and goat at the farm — can recur without needing individual names. If a future story truly needs another named neighbor, choose a short, familiar, aurally distinct name and give that character a reason to return.

## Established Season Four garage and roads

West of Rex's blue-step home, close to the long farm-road junction, is the **Red Door Garage**, run by **Ms. Pam**, an adult raccoon mechanic. It has a fenced adult work yard, a two-post vehicle lift in the bay, and a separate watching bench and toy-play table. She operates two distinct white trucks:

| Vehicle or equipment | First scene | Purpose and permanent location |
|---|---|---|
| White flatbed tow truck | 019000 | Tilt bed and winch recover a disabled van; based at Red Door Garage |
| Tire gauge and replacement wheel | 020000 | Pam checks vehicle tire pressure, replaces damaged tire; garage tool collection |
| Two-post vehicle lift | 021000 | Raises properly supported light vehicles for adult inspection; garage bay |
| White mobile service truck | 023000 | Takes testing tools and replacement parts to the tractor at Green Gate Farm |

The van is working after 021000, and Farmer Ruth's red tractor is working again after its battery replacement in 023000. Rex's existing wooden toy dump truck has a freed wheel after 024000. Neither the farm road nor Little Bridge moves, and the service truck uses the farm road. The park's original paved/gravel loops are still for walkers, not hauling trucks.

The story-world map now reads west to east on Brook Road: **Red Door Garage -> Rex/Gus/Jack houses -> Pine Park -> Little Bridge -> Green Gate Farm**. Farm vehicles branch along the longer road near the garage instead of using the footbridge. The exact distances have not been specified to avoid artificial travel-time constraints.

## Season Six — after the first measured rain

Pine Park's rain gauge, delivered empty in Season Five, catches a moderate overnight rainfall and is **read at the third marked line**; Jack's notebook records the first result. By the last episode the same gauge is emptied and reset, without changing its location near the park gate away from the tall pine. The existing gravel loop, Pancake Hill, bench and Little Bridge all remain intact.

A paved roadside curb slopes toward the **grated storm inlet near the Red Door Garage's western road junction**. Some rain soaks into the park's grass; other water moves along the road to this inlet. The town employs two *different* adult-driven municipal machines:

- **Street sweeper:** spinning brushes and a debris hopper collect grit and leaves from curb edges; it is not a vacuum truck for cleaning deep drainage basins.
- **Green-and-white vacuum drain-cleaning truck:** a large hose and silver tank collect dirty water and deposited sediment from the catch basin beneath the street grate; this is not Pam's white mobile repair truck or Dan's blue delivery truck.

The **long farm road** also has a modest pre-existing **ditch/pipe culvert at a low point**, observed for the first time in 035000. Adult workers clear leaves at its entrance and use a **compact front-bucket loader** to regravel a soft shoulder. No truck travels across Little Bridge. The farm's red tractor, hay, hen feed, wheelbarrow, barn and all animals continue in place. After the maintenance the road is usable.

Jack adds a **rain-route sketch** to his established blue notebook. The season ends with all work complete and ordinary calm restored; no perpetual storm, stranded vehicle, new book collection or danger storyline persists.

## Season Seven — the two red doors on Station Lane

The **Brook Road firehouse** has two red vehicle-bay doors and a marked visitor path. It lies at the end of **Station Lane, a short northward branch off Brook Road between Rex's blue-step house and Pine Park**. This is separate from **Ms. Pam's Red Door Garage west of Rex's house**. The station's vehicles can use Brook Road and the longer farm-road network; there is no fire truck route across Little Bridge or through Pine Park's pedestrian area.

**Captain Ann** is an adult fox firefighter and a newly recurring neighbor. The station's **red pumper fire engine** carries hose, water, pump and tools. Its separate **long red aerial ladder truck** can deploy stabilizers and extend a nested ladder under adult crew control. The ladder demonstration ended with the apparatus safely folded and stowed; gear and hose were returned to the station. The season has no emergency response, rescue, incident or continuing damage.

**Jack's blue notebook** now includes Station Lane with two red bays and a sketch of the controlled water route from engine tank through pump, hose and nozzle to a practice board. **Rex and Molly** visited the station together, then returned to their own homes; Molly's rope toy remains at the farm. The park rain gauge still awaits the next rainfall following the Season Six reset.

## Season Eight — Snow Trucks and winter walking routes

Several months after the firehouse open day, Brook Road receives a substantial overnight snowfall. A town **orange snowplow** with angled front blade clears roadway lanes; a **separate small red hopper/spinner spreader** treats a slick bend beside Ms. Pam's Red Door Garage. These are **municipal machines**, not the garage's tow/service trucks or the firehouse vehicles. Station Lane and the firehouse apron stay accessible. Ms. Pam’s four nesting cones are returned to her yard shed after work.

The **same red tractor** from Green Gate Farm's hay season gains an adult-operated **removable snow blade** for the long vehicle lane. The stored hay stays in the existing barn. Mr. Lee later uses a **walk-behind snowblower** on the Pine Park walking path, with snow discharged toward an empty grassy area. The Little Bridge walkway receives **hand clearing and adult inspection**, never traffic from plows, tractors or other vehicles. Rex and Molly's one-wag greeting resumes at Pancake Hill in the snow.

Jack's **same blue notebook** gains winter sketches of blade, spreader, tractor, snowblower and road-vs-footpath routes. Jack informally observes open-ground snow depth and a taller drift by the fence; the existing rain gauge is not misused for snowfall depth measurement. Rex's toy truck, Gus's green cap, Molly's rope toy, original houses, bench, storm inlet and culvert persist. The plow and spreader finish this event's work and return to municipal storage. Winter remains the ambient season at episode 048000.

## Season Nine — gravel yard and the park gate repair

After the snow of Season Eight melts naturally, a worn **small maintenance-access strip by the Pine Park gate** needs fresh material. The park's original loop and Pancake Hill remain sound. A stone processing yard lies **north of Pine Park beyond the north service lane**, which is the heavy-vehicle route; its crusher, vibrating screen, guarded conveyor system, sorted stockpiles and truck scale are new permanent settings. The supplying quarry is upstream and has not been directly visited.

**Mr. Fred**, an adult badger in a yellow vest, manages the yard and hosts adult-accompanied friends in an **enclosed viewing office**. The site has an industrial haul truck for quarry rock, a large **yellow wheel loader**, and a **green road dump truck** used for deliveries. The weighbridge records the loaded truck's gross weight; the payload can be determined by subtracting its empty weight. This is not Little Bridge.

Aunt Jo's crew repairs only the gate-side strip using a compact loader and a small roller. The restored surface and Jack's new drawing connect directly to the original Pine Park gravel and dump truck season. Rex, Gus, Jack, Molly, their fixed belongings and the whole town's older infrastructure remain unchanged. The gravel yard can recur as a source of material, not as a new playground.

## Season Ten — the small barn-side shelter repair

Green Gate Farm's **original red barn** persists with dry stored hay and Farmer Ruth's red tractor. Its existing **small side wagon-door shelter**, not the entire barn, gets a new framed roof in the **same location** after Molly hears a leak. Farmer Ruth temporarily moves her **existing green one-wheel wheelbarrow** into the main barn while the side shelter is closed, then returns it to the shelter when the adult crew completes the fix.

The repair adds no new roads. A temporary **open-bed building-materials delivery truck** brings securely strapped long lumber by the **long farm road**. Dan's **same blue box truck** brings small boxed supplies. **Mr. Lee**, the adult beaver carpenter, oversees a replacement braced timber frame. A hired **yellow telehandler** with large tires, forks and an extendable boom places a material pallet on an adult working platform; it leaves again after the job. It is not the yellow gravel-yard wheel loader or a vehicle the children drive. Adult builders install the roof with water-shedding flashing, a front gutter and a short downspout ending at a small splash-stone patch away from the wagon door. The next light shower confirms the roof works; the farm stays inhabited and the shelter remains available in all later episodes.

Jack's original notebook gains a page showing an imperfect **loose-jointed square** versus the same little craft model with a **diagonal brace**, and a before/after roof runoff drawing. Rex and Molly keep their one-wag greeting. Gus's cap, Molly's blue rope toy, the farm's hens, sheep, goat and all earlier objects remain unchanged. No new named friend is introduced.

## Season Eleven — one connected picture map, not a new road

The neighborhood now has a **public framed picture map at Pine Park's entrance**. It is a schematic with a **solid line for vehicle routes and dotted line for the public walking path over Little Bridge**, clearly labeled by a picture key. The board marks the already-established **Red Door Garage, Dan's parcel shed, Station Lane firehouse, gravel-yard service road, Pine Park, brook, Little Bridge and Green Gate Farm**. It stands **beside**, not in place of, the original entrance sign. The rain gauge, recently repaired gravel strip, tall pine, bench and Pancake Hill keep their exact earlier roles. Adult builders prepare supports and install the board; Dan's blue box truck delivers it and a compact loader brings a small amount of gravel via the northern service lane. Neither machine crosses the footbridge or enters the pedestrian park loop.

The arc starts when the puppies and friends walk the short bridge route to the farm while Dan follows the **long vehicle farm road**. Gus and Jack build a temporary cardboard town on Rex's rug using the saved carton and the wooden dump truck; Molly fixes a missing path on the map; Ms. Pam, Captain Ann and Mr. Fred check actual locations; Mr. Lee and Aunt Jo supervise the public board. A quiet picnic brings many old neighbors together; they leave the area tidy. Jack adds the picture board to his existing blue notebook; Rex and Molly resume their one-wag greeting, Gus keeps the green cap, Molly's rope toy stays home at the farm, and the original machines and animal routines remain intact.

All eleven planned seasons are now complete. Later series growth is optional and should start from the persistent town, not erase it.

## Episodic quality checks for future writers

- A clear start, a concrete action the child can picture, a humorous or surprising turn, a changed world or relationship, and a quiet local end. No late moral summary.
- Not every chapter uses the same construction-site tour structure: allow home experiments, animal-led scenes, truck rides **as passengers**, neighborhood encounters, and the consequences of completed work.
- Include recurring physical specifics (Rex's two ears and blue step; Gus's cap; Jack's blue notebook; Molly's farm porch and blue rope toy; park sign; bridge railings) selectively rather than in a ritual opening paragraph.
- Keep named characters aurally separable; no generic whimsical name generator. Don't rename canon incidentally.
- Technical facts are source checked and limited to what is explained in child language; avoid pretending a toy bridge is structurally equivalent to a real one.
- TTS narration stands alone without pictures, character voices, music or sound effects. Nonspoken caregiver prompts and evidence records remain outside it.
- Source citations ground factual or editorial claims; never say evidence proves these stories improve sleep or cognition.

## Seasons Twelve to Fourteen — Star Harbor and the Moon (complete)

These eighteen episodes are one adventure in three seasons: **The Road to Star Harbor**, **The Rocket and the Rover**, and **The Moon and the Way Home**. They expand the setting but do not change the neighborhood road/footpath topology established in Seasons One through Eleven.

```
[BROOK ROAD] -- road past Red Door Garage -- [COUNTY HIGHWAY]
      |                                      |
[Pine Park / board]                    [SPECIAL HEAVY ROAD]
      |                                      |
[Little Bridge (feet only)]              [STAR HARBOR, COAST]
      |                                  /        |        [Green Gate Farm]             [assembly building] [pad] [mission control]
                                         |           |
                                      [crawler]  [STAR ONE]
                                                     |
                                           launch -> Earth orbit
                                                     |
                                               flight to Moon
                                                     |
                     [return cabin in lunar orbit] - [Moon lander]
                                                     |
                                      [SCOUT + rover + station on Moon]
```

The sketch is conceptual, not a navigational map. The spaceport is **not** in or beside Pine Park, on the farm footpath or inside the gravel yard. Large equipment reaches it by a county highway and the new, prepared heavy-vehicle access road. The support tower stays on Earth, and the crawler does not travel on the rocket. Scout's uncrewed cargo mission launches before Star One's crew. Dr. Ada stays at ground mission control; Max and Aunt Jo are the two adult members of the **six-person** fictional crew, accompanied by Rex, Gus, Jack and Molly. Scout, the rover and small science station remain on a sunny near-side Moon site after the crew's return.

**New characters:** Dr. Ada (owl aerospace engineer, round glasses, many-pocket coat), Max (golden-furred adult dog pilot in a blue flight jacket). Do not rename or quietly expand the flight crew. Keep Rex's one dark ear, Gus's green cap, Jack's single notebook, Molly's white front paws and their distinct motivations. Existing town adults remain in their established professions. The animal/young-child crew is explicit *speculative fiction*, not a statement about actual human spaceflight capabilities.

**Future potential:** The rover can send a new photograph or data to Star Harbor as a premise for future stories. A return visit requires fresh adult planning and equipment; the Moon does not secretly have a paved road or breathable outdoor air. The first fourteen seasons now form completed canon.
