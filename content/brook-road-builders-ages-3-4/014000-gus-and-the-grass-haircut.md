# Gus and the Grass Haircut

Gus had never given a field a haircut.

He had given himself a haircut once, using a leaf as a comb.

It didn't work. The leaf fell off his head.

But today, the long grass beside Green Gate Farm was getting a real haircut.

Gus stood with Rex, Jack, Molly, and Aunt Jo in the shady place beside the red barn.

"I hope it doesn't mind," he said.

"The grass?" asked Jack.

"Yes. Mine is very particular about its hair."

He patted his green cap.

Farmer Ruth had already walked around the field to check it. Now she sat in her red tractor. A long cutting machine rode behind and to one side.

"That is the mower," Aunt Jo told them.

The tractor began to move.

Rrrrrrum.

The mower cut the tall grass and left it lying in long, soft strips.

Where the tractor had been, the tall green stalks were shorter. The cut grass rested on the ground.

"It looks like a giant comb went by," Jack said.

"A giant comb with a terrible sense of style," Gus said.

One side of the field was short. The other side was still tall.

Rex sniffed.

"I like the short side," he said.

"Why?" Molly asked.

"It smells different. Fresh and green and ... green again."

Molly sniffed too.

"I think it smells like when Farmer Ruth cuts grass by the farmhouse. Only much, much more."

The tractor turned at the end of the field and came back along another strip.

Back and forth it went, steadily, while the crew watched its path and kept everyone well away from the moving mower.

From their bench the friends could see the grass changing shape, bit by bit.

Jack opened his blue notebook.

He drew two lines across the page.

Above one line he made long, wiggly grass stalks.

Above the other he made short stalks and a flat pile beside them.

"Before and after," he said.

"Before my haircut and after my haircut," Gus announced.

"Your hair hasn't changed," Rex told him.

"Exactly. A very tidy haircut."

When Farmer Ruth finished the last strip, she parked the tractor in its working area and turned off the mower.

Soon she came over, brushing loose grass off her boots.

Gus looked across the cut field.

"Why is all the grass still lying there?" he asked.

"It has to dry before we can make hay bales," said Farmer Ruth. "Sun and air help take some of the water out. We give it time."

Rex tilted his head.

"Grass has water inside? It wasn't dripping."

"Not all water comes in drops," Jack said. He looked at a juicy green leaf near the bench.

"That's right," said Farmer Ruth. "Fresh plants hold water."

Gus studied the hayfield. "So the tractor gave it a haircut, and now the hair has to dry."

"That's one way to put it," Aunt Jo said.

"Do you want to borrow my cap?" Gus asked the field.

The field didn't answer.

A hen made a clucking sound from the yard instead.

"She says no," Molly said.

"Fair enough," Gus replied. "It's a very large head."

That afternoon the friends played beside the farmhouse porch. Molly tugged one end of her blue rope toy while Rex held the other.

Neither dog wanted to let go, so they both sat down with the rope stretched between them.

Jack watched them, then drew a tiny blue line between two puppies.

"That's not a tractor," Gus said.

"I know," said Jack. "I like drawing you too."

Later, when the sun was low, the cut field was still spread out in soft rows. The wagon still stood beside the barn.

The machines were quiet, and the two woolly sheep were settling near their pen.

Gus looked back once from the bridge.

"Good night, hayfield," he said. "I think your haircut suits you."
