# Gus and the Truck with the Spinning Brooms

Gus was certain he had seen every kind of truck.

He had seen an excavator with a long arm, a dump truck that lifted its bed, a crane that raised a bridge, and Dan's blue delivery truck with a room on its back.

Then one morning a truck arrived with brooms.

Two very big brooms.

"I was wrong," Gus said.

Rex looked up from sniffing the blue front step.

"About what?"

"I haven't seen every truck. There is a truck that brushes its own feet."

The strange truck was moving slowly along Brook Road. It had a small cab, a round brush near one front side, and a second brush tucked under its body. When the driver lowered the brushes, they began to turn.

Brrr-rrr-rrr.

"The street is getting its hair brushed!" Gus declared.

"Roads don't have hair," Jack said.

Gus pointed to the leaves gathered along the curb.

"Then what are those?"

"Leaves," said Jack.

"Very untidy road hair," said Gus.

Aunt Jo took the children to the little waiting place outside Red Door Garage. Ms. Pam was setting out four orange cones to mark the workers' area. They were the same four cones Dan had brought in a box last week.

Ms. Pam tipped her striped raccoon tail away from a cone and waved.

"Morning, friends. The town sweeper is picking up the grit the rain washed along the road."

Rex held up one paw. He had a tiny bit of dried mud between his toes.

"Can it wash my paws?"

"Too big a brush for those paws," Ms. Pam said. "Aunt Jo has a cloth your size."

The truck drew closer. Its driver guided it slowly beside the curb.

The round side brush turned against the curb and swept loose grit toward the machine.

They watched a little brown line of sand disappear from the road edge. A leaf spun once, twice, and vanished underneath the truck.

"Where did the leaf go?" Rex asked.

"Perhaps the truck is wearing it as a hat," Gus suggested.

"Its hat would be underneath," Jack said.

Ms. Pam pointed to the boxy part behind the cab.

The dirt and leaves went into a collecting bin on the truck.

"So the truck can take them away," she said.

Rex tilted his head. "It's like a dump truck that collects the little things first."

"Different job," Jack said. "The dump truck brought gravel to the park. This one picks up the mess on the street."

The sweeper passed the garage and went on toward the Pine Park gate. The friends stayed on the sidewalk with Aunt Jo while the truck worked along the roadway.

Gus waved his cap at it.

"Good job, broom truck! Don't forget the road behind your ears!"

Rex turned to Jack. "Does a road have ears now?"

"Only when Gus is looking at it," Jack said.

After the truck had gone, Ms. Pam collected the cones and nested them inside one another.

"Four cones," Gus counted, "in a tower of one."

She put them back in the garage shed. The road was tidier, but a storm drain near the junction still had its own deeper pocket of muddy grit to be checked.

Rex noticed that part only because it made him think of another kind of truck.

"A broom truck cleans the top," he said slowly. "What cleans under the street?"

"We'll find out when the drain crew visits," said Aunt Jo.

They walked home past the blue front step. Rex's paws were almost clean. Gus still had a leaf tucked into his cap.

He decided to leave it there for the afternoon.

It was his very own piece of road hair.
