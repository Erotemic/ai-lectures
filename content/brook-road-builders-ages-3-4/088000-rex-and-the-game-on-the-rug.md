# Rex and the Game on the Rug

Rex had something to ask Jack.

But the question was stuck somewhere between his ears.

Tomorrow Jack would be five. Rex knew that because Jack had told everyone, twice, and because Gus had been drawing candles on every piece of paper he could find.

Five candles. Four wheels. Five marks on Gus's pretend birthday calendar, although one of the marks looked like a potato.

Rex had another thought.

What if turning five meant Jack wouldn't want to play their old games?

They had gone to the Moon together. Jack had seen the world from a spaceship window. Perhaps he would not want to make a cardboard town on Rex's rug anymore.

Rex went home and nudged his wooden dump truck with his nose.

It made the small, familiar sound of wooden wheels crossing the floor.

Aunt Jo was in the kitchen cutting apples.

"You've been quiet," she said.

"I am thinking," Rex replied.

"That can take a while."

"Jack is going to be five."

"He is."

"Do you think he will want to play big games now?"

Aunt Jo gave Rex a slice of apple. He ate it while he thought about what a big game might be.

"Maybe he'll want to build a real rocket," Rex said. "All by himself."

"The last rocket took a great many adults," Aunt Jo reminded him.

"I know. That was the problem. It took forever."

Aunt Jo laughed. Then she took an old cardboard box from a cupboard and left it on the rug.

"You could ask him what he wants to play today."

Rex looked at the box. It was not a rocket, but it could become one.

Later, Jack arrived with his notebook and his dad. His dad stayed to talk with Aunt Jo about tomorrow's picnic.

Jack took off his shoes and sat down on the rug.

"What are we building?" he asked.

Rex blinked. "You still want to build things here?"

"Why wouldn't I?"

"Well. You are nearly five."

Jack looked down at his hands. "They're the same hands I had yesterday."

He wiggled all ten fingers.

Rex's tail began to wag.

"Can we make a rocket truck?" Rex asked.

"A truck that goes to a rocket?"

"A truck that is a rocket."

Jack thought about that. "It might need a lot of wheels."

They found two cardboard tubes, a clean box, and several large wooden blocks. They put the box on its side and stood the tubes behind it. Rex brought over his toy dump truck, and Jack used it to carry one little block at a time to the pretend launch pad.

Rex backed up too quickly and knocked a tower over.

"Oh," he said.

The cardboard tubes rolled across the rug. One stopped beneath the chair.

Jack crawled after it. He emerged with dust on one sleeve and the tube over his arm like a very long bracelet.

"We have lost an engine," he announced.

"Call the repair truck!" Rex shouted.

Jack pushed the toy dump truck over. "I am the repair truck."

They were both laughing when Gus arrived. He wore his cap backward because he was carrying the enormous birthday box in his imagination and did not want the imaginary box to hit his brim.

"What is this?" Gus asked.

"A rocket truck," said Rex.

Gus examined the cardboard tubes. "It has no biscuits."

"Not every rocket needs biscuits," said Jack.

"This one could use some."

Molly came in behind Gus and politely sniffed the tubes. Then she joined the game as a rover that could drive across a pillow pretending to be the Moon.

For a while, the whole room was full of small noises: clack, roll, bump, shuff, and a low growling sound Rex made whenever his toy truck climbed a block.

Then Jack said, "I think I might make a bigger rocket when I'm older."

Rex stopped his truck.

"But this one first?"

"This one first," Jack answered.

They kept building until the last light of afternoon reached the edge of the rug.

Before going home, Jack asked Rex if he would come early to Pine Park tomorrow. They had to decide where to put a blanket for the picnic.

"Beside Pancake Hill," Rex said at once.

"That's what I thought."

At the door, Jack bent down and scratched Rex gently behind the dark ear.

"See you tomorrow."

Rex watched him go. He looked back at the cardboard rocket truck on the rug. One tube had rolled beneath the chair again. He fished it out with his nose and stood it upright next to the little wooden dump truck. Jack had promised they could make another one.

Rex went back inside and parked his wooden dump truck next to the cardboard launch pad.

Tomorrow, he decided, would be a good day for cake.

And the day after that might be a good day for another game.
