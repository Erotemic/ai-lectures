# Jack and the Moon Beyond the Map

At Pine Park, the new picture map had a little red barn, two red firehouse doors, and a drawing of Little Bridge. It had one tall pine tree. It even had a tiny blue delivery truck.

But it did not have the Moon.

Jack was looking at the real Moon over the pine tree when he noticed that.

"There's room at the top," he told Gus.

Gus tipped his green cap back. "For a very long road?"

Rex sniffed the bottom of the map board. "If there is a road to the Moon, I hope it has biscuit stops."

Molly, who had come from Green Gate Farm with Farmer Ruth, looked up. The Moon was a pale round shape in the afternoon sky. She and Rex exchanged their one-wag greeting.

"We can see it," Molly said. "Can we go there?"

Jack thought for a moment. His blue notebook held drawings of a digger, a bridge, a tractor, a fire engine, a rock crusher, and a truck that carried other trucks. Every drawing was of something he had actually seen.

"I don't know," he said. "But I'd like to find out."

That evening Aunt Jo took the four friends to an outdoor talk at the village hall. A visitor had brought a telescope and a small gray model of the Moon. The visitor was Dr. Ada, an owl with round glasses and a coat with many pockets. She worked with a large team of people and animals building a space center called Star Harbor.

Dr. Ada put the model on a table. There were dips all across it, like little bowls.

"These are craters," she said. "We see them because the Moon has been hit by rocks from space."

Gus leaned close. "It's a very big biscuit with holes."

"It is made of rock," said Dr. Ada.

"A very old rock biscuit."

Everyone smiled. Dr. Ada let them look through the telescope. The Moon was not smooth at all. Jack saw a bright edge and dark places and a round mark with a shadow inside it.

Rex pressed his nose near the telescope, then pulled away. "Does it smell like anything?"

"Not from here," said Aunt Jo.

Dr. Ada unrolled a long drawing. On it were roads and cranes and a tower beside the sea. At the very top was a rocket.

"We are building a place to send explorers to the Moon," she said. "The big rockets are made by experienced teams. The roads and buildings that support them are just as important."

"Do you need a dump truck?" asked Rex.

"More than one."

Rex sat up straight.

"Do you need a crane?" asked Gus.

"Several."

"Do you need a notebook?" asked Jack.

Dr. Ada looked at the blue notebook in his hands. "A notebook is an excellent place to begin asking questions."

She showed them a second picture: a roomy spacecraft with seats for a small storybook crew. The engineers were designing special equipment for explorers of different shapes and sizes. A trained pilot named Max would fly the spacecraft, and grown-ups would be responsible for every important flight decision. Aunt Jo would travel with the four young friends if the preparations were completed and all the checks passed.

"Really us?" whispered Molly.

"Not tomorrow," Dr. Ada said. "There is a great deal to build and test. We cannot hurry a rocket."

On the way home, Jack opened his notebook. He drew one short road to Pine Park and, above it, a tall white rocket with a round window.

"That's not a road," Gus said, pointing up at the rocket.

"No," Jack agreed. "The road gets the rocket ready. Then the rocket goes where roads cannot."

Rex walked beside him, looking at the Moon over the roofs.

The town was quiet. The real picture map stood safely beside the park gate, showing the places they already knew. Farther away, people were working on a much larger map.

And high above them, the Moon waited without making a sound.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**Three-season arc:** The Path to Space, part 1 of 18. **Season 12, episode 1.** **Continuity before:** The Pine Park map is complete; the four friends and Aunt Jo are home with no existing space program within the established neighborhood. **After:** Dr. Ada has invited Rex, Gus, Jack, Molly and Aunt Jo to observe the Star Harbor preparation for a future storybook expedition with adult pilot Max. Nothing new has been built in Brook Road.

**Editorial evidence boundaries:** This is original science fiction: young animal characters and a young boy take an imagined Moon trip in a specially designed spacecraft with trained adult crew. It is not a real-world description of child spaceflight or a promise that this kind of vehicle exists. Adults do all actual site, machine and flight operations. Technical sources in the metadata support named broad mechanisms only, not the fictional space center, lunar mission safety, suitability for children, or outcomes for listeners.
