# Rex and the First Snowplow

Rex woke up because the world sounded different.

No truck rumbling past the house. No bicycle bell. Not even the usual scratch of a branch against the front window.

He sat up in his basket and listened.

Nothing.

Then a tiny noise came from the window.

Tick.

A soft lump of snow slid off the sill.

Rex rushed to look.

Brook Road had disappeared.

The houses were still there. The lamppost was still there. But between them lay a long, white blanket.

Even Rex's blue front step wore a white cap.

"Aunt Jo!" he called. "Somebody put a blanket on the whole road!"

Aunt Jo came in with a mug of warm tea.

"That's snow," she said. "It fell during the night."

Rex pressed his nose to the glass. His breath made a foggy circle.

A little later Gus arrived with Jack. They had walked over from their houses with an adult, carefully following the cleared front path. Gus's green cap was pulled down so far that only his nose showed.

"I brought my truck eyes," Gus announced.

"You always bring your eyes," said Jack.

"Yes, but these are for trucks."

Jack opened his blue notebook. He drew the lamppost and one crooked line where Brook Road should be.

"The snow has covered my line," he said.

Rex leaned close to the paper.

"Can you draw the road back?"

Before Jack could answer, a low sound reached them.

Rrrrrrrrrr.

Then came a soft scrape.

Shhhhhhhhh.

Round the bend came an orange truck, taller than Aunt Jo's yellow van, with flashing amber lights and a wide metal blade across its front.

"A digger!" cried Rex.

"Not a digger," said Aunt Jo. "Watch what the front does."

The town crew had fitted the big orange truck with its snowplow before the first flakes fell.

It rolled slowly along Brook Road. The blade pushed snow off the driving part of the road and toward the edge.

White snow rolled against the blade. Behind the truck, a strip of darker road appeared.

Rex's tail made one long wag.

"The road was hiding underneath!"

"It was there the whole time," said Jack.

Gus looked at the great white ridge beside the curb.

"That truck is making a snow sandwich. The road is the bread."

"Where's the cheese?" asked Rex.

Gus thought for a moment.

"Still at home, probably."

The snowplow passed Station Lane without entering its little side road. Captain Ann's red firehouse stood beyond it, quiet under a thick white roof. Other crews would clear the station entrance and its lane too.

The orange truck continued westward toward the garage, doing one part of a long morning's work.

Rex watched its red tail-lights disappear.

"Is the road all finished now?"

"Not quite," said Aunt Jo. "There are more places to clear, and they may need another pass."

Jack added a curved orange blade to his drawing. Gus gave the truck a tiny hat in the corner of the page.

"That's not how it looked," Jack said.

"It's how I feel about it," said Gus.

By afternoon the front step had been cleared and the visiting friends had returned home. Rex walked over the dry patch by the door and studied the snow on either side.

He placed his wooden dump truck on the windowsill, nose pointed toward Brook Road.

The truck didn't move.

Neither did the snow outside.

Rex settled into his basket beneath the window. Tomorrow there would be more things to discover in the white town.

Tonight the road lay quiet, and the little wooden truck kept watch.

**Editorial note:** These stories depict talking animals and an invented town. The road plow, spreader, tractor blade and snowblower are distinct adult-operated winter-maintenance machines. The children observe from warm homes or marked pedestrian places and never operate powered equipment.
