# The Bulldozer and the Pancake Hill

On Wednesday, Gus woke up thinking about mountains.

Two mountains of gravel had come to Pine Park on Tuesday.

And now Aunt Jo said the builders were going to make a hill.

Gus loved hills. He liked walking up them. He liked walking down them. Most of all, he liked sitting at the top, where his green cap caught the wind.

He raced to Rex's gate.

"Let's go see the new mountain!"

"Hill," said Rex.

"Maybe a mountain," said Gus hopefully.

Jack joined them, carrying his blue notebook and a pencil that had become very short from all the drawing.

Aunt Jo walked with the three friends to the watching place outside the blue fence.

There stood another yellow machine.

This one had a broad, flat blade across the front.

"Is that the digger?" Rex asked.

"No," said Aunt Jo. "It's a bulldozer. The excavator dug earth from one place. This machine can push earth along the ground."

Gus studied the blade.

"A giant snow shovel," he said.

"Except there's no snow," said Jack.

"Then it's a giant dirt shovel."

The grown-up driver started the bulldozer.

Brrrum. Brrrum.

The machine crept forward on wide tracks.

Its blade pushed a roll of loose earth ahead of it.

The roll grew bigger.

Then it spread into a long, low mound.

Gus rose on his toes.

"That's not a mountain," he said.

"Not yet," Rex agreed.

The bulldozer went forward again, pushing more soil.

Back it came, ready for another pass.

The grown-up driver worked carefully. The mound became smoother and wider.

Gus's eyes got rounder.

"It's a hill," he said at last.

Jack drew the bulldozer with a wide blade in his notebook.

He drew a line before it, then a line after it. The soil had moved from one place to another, but the blade hadn't carried a bucketful high in the air.

"The excavator scooped," he said. "The bulldozer pushes."

Gus nodded.

"One has a spoon, and the other has a very large hand."

Rex sat watching the blade travel slowly along the ground.

"Does it want to make the biggest hill in the world?" he asked.

Aunt Jo smiled.

"No. We're making a little hill that children can walk over. The path will curve around it."

Gus looked disappointed for one tiny moment.

Then he thought of something.

"If the hill is not very tall, I can see right over my own cap from the top."

He pushed his cap backward until it rested on the back of his head.

"And nobody will need to pack a lunch just to climb it."

At home, the three friends built a little hill of cushions on Rex's rug.

Gus fetched one square cushion and two round ones.

Rex fetched his blanket.

Jack put the square cushion down first, then the round ones on top.

"That's very steep," Rex said.

He tried walking over it and slid gently into the blanket.

Gus laughed so hard his cap tipped over one eye.

They spread the cushions flatter.

Rex walked over the little bump without sliding.

Jack drew two pictures: a steep cushion mountain and a low cushion hill.

"This one is easier to cross," he said, pointing to the low one.

Gus looked down at the flattened cushions.

"It's shaped like a pancake," he declared.

"A pancake with a blanket on it," said Rex.

"A very big pancake," said Jack.

The name stayed with them.

That afternoon, when the crew stopped work, Aunt Jo showed the friends a new drawing of the park. The path curved around the small rise.

"The hill isn't ready for visitors yet," she said. "But it has its shape."

"It has a name, too," Gus said.

"Does it?"

"Pancake Hill."

Aunt Jo looked at the wide, gentle mound.

"That fits."

By bedtime, Gus was tired from laughing and cushion building.

He hung his green cap by his bed and stretched under his quilt.

He imagined a little hill beside a tall pine tree, and a path winding around it like a ribbon.

Farther down Brook Road, the bulldozer was parked with its blade lowered.

Pancake Hill lay still behind the blue fence.

It would be there tomorrow.

For now, Gus was quite happy to stay under his quilt.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Continuity:** A low, broad earth mound has been shaped inside Pine Park; Gus named it Pancake Hill, and the park plan now calls it that. The gravel piles have not magically vanished. Jack's notebook now distinguishes excavator scooping from bulldozer pushing. Gus's cap remains green.

**Editorial note:** Bulldozer pushing is distinct from excavator digging and truck dumping. The cushion comparison is an imaginative toy-scale analogy, not an engineering demonstration. The finished walking route has yet to be built.

**Optional prompt:** "Was Pancake Hill high and pointy, or low and wide?"
