# The Crane and the Long Wooden Bridge

A long flatbed truck came along the farm service road one bright morning.

It carried something that looked like a very long wooden floor.

Rex hurried to the window.

"A floor is going for a drive!"

Gus put on his green cap. "Where do you think it's moving?"

"To a house?" asked Jack.

Aunt Jo smiled. "To the brook. That's the deck of the new footbridge. It's been put together at the builder's yard."

"The whole walking bit?" asked Rex.

"Yes. The crew will lift it onto the supports."

They went with Aunt Jo to the viewing area by Pine Park.

The flatbed truck was parked on the work lane, away from the public path. A second truck had arrived beside it.

This one had a tall folded boom and a heavy hook hanging from a cable.

"A crane truck," Jack said.

The crane truck's driver set out its wide stabilizing legs onto firm pads.

They were not paws, and they were not feet, but they helped make the machine steady for lifting.

"Look at those arms on the ground," said Gus.

Rex sat very still.

"If I had arms like that, I'd never fall over when I got excited."

Aunt Jo glanced at his tail. "You'd still find a way."

Mr. Lee and the other builders attached lifting straps to the long wooden deck.

The grown-up crane operator raised the hook slowly.

The straps straightened.

And the wooden bridge began to rise.

Not with a jump.

Not with a bang.

Just up. Up. Up.

"It's flying," Rex whispered.

"Not by itself," Jack whispered back. "The crane is holding it."

The big deck moved carefully over the ground toward the brook. Two workers guided it from safe positions while the crane did the heavy lifting.

For a moment it hung between the two shores, like a very long piece of toast in the air.

Gus held his cap firmly on his head.

"If it were toast," he said, "we would need a bigger plate."

Rex grinned. "And a bigger table."

"And a very careful breakfast," Jack added.

The operator lowered the bridge deck little by little.

It came to rest on the prepared supports at either bank.

The crew checked its position and began fastening it securely.

Across the water, Farmer Ruth and Molly watched from the green gate.

Molly's white front paws tapped the ground.

"There's a bridge!" she called.

"There's almost a bridge!" Rex answered.

"It looks like a bridge!"

"It has no rails yet!" said Jack.

"I know!" shouted Molly. "I can see!"

Mr. Lee came over after the lifting was finished.

"The big piece is in place," he told Aunt Jo. "We still need the side rails, the paths at each end, and our final checks."

Gus stared at the long deck.

"I never knew a floor could ride on a truck, fly through the air, and become a bridge before lunch."

"It took a lot of work before today," Aunt Jo said. "And there is more work left."

Jack opened his notebook and drew the flatbed with its long load. On the next page he drew the crane truck, with its legs out and the hook holding the deck.

He gave the crane hook a big curved shape, because he liked the way it hung.

Rex's tail beat a soft rhythm against the grass.

"Tomorrow, will Molly be able to cross?"

"Not quite yet," Aunt Jo answered.

"Then I will wave some more."

So he did. He waved his tail until Molly waved hers back.

The afternoon grew quiet after the trucks left.

The long wooden deck remained above the brook, resting exactly where the workers had placed it.

At home, Rex tucked his toy dump truck beside his rug and curled around it.

He dreamed of a crane hook drifting through the sky with a great big, perfectly ordinary wooden floor.
