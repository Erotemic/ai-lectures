# Molly and the Firefighter in the Big Boots

Molly liked familiar smells.

She liked the smell of hay in Farmer Ruth's barn.

She liked Rex's blue front step, which smelled a little like wood and a little like Rex.

And she liked the smell of Aunt Jo's pockets, because sometimes there were snacks.

At the firehouse, nearly everything smelled new.

Clean wheels. Rubber hose. Warm pavement.

And a coat hanging on a rack that was bigger than Molly.

"Who's going to wear that?" she asked.

"Captain Ann," said Rex.

"But Captain Ann is already wearing a shirt," Molly replied.

The fox firefighter stood beside the rack, smiling.

"This is protective gear," she told the visitors. "I'll show you how it goes on."

She pointed to a pair of enormous boots standing inside folded trousers.

Gus nudged Jack.

"Those boots could hold soup."

"Not soup," Jack said. "Feet."

"Soup in the left boot. Feet in the right."

"Definitely not," said Captain Ann.

The children giggled.

She stepped into the boots and pulled up the heavy trousers. Then she put on a coat and fastened its front.

"These boots, trousers, coat, gloves, and helmet help protect us while we work," Captain Ann said.

The broad silver-yellow stripes shone when the afternoon sun touched them.

Rex turned his head.

"Your coat has stripes like a road sign!"

"They're meant to be easy to see," said Captain Ann.

Molly watched the fox pull on the big gloves.

The hands inside them looked bigger too.

Then Captain Ann set the helmet on her head.

"There," she said, spreading her gloved fingers.

Molly sniffed the air.

"Is that really you?"

"It is," Captain Ann replied.

"You got taller," said Molly.

"The helmet adds a little height."

Gus stood on his toes to compare.

"If I wore two helmets, I might become a grown-up."

"You would become a bear wearing two helmets," Aunt Jo said.

Captain Ann slowly turned to show the coat from the side and back. She explained that firefighters wear different protective equipment for different tasks, including breathing equipment when the air isn't safe to breathe.

She showed the facepiece briefly, then left it off so everyone could hear her speak normally.

"It's still me inside all this," she said.

Molly's ears perked.

The voice was the same voice that had welcomed her at the gate.

"Captain Ann," Molly said.

"That's right."

"Could I wear a coat that big?"

"I think it would cover your paws," said Farmer Ruth.

"Then I'd have to hop," Molly said.

Rex tried to imagine Molly hopping beneath a giant coat. He laughed so hard that his collar jingled.

Captain Ann removed the gear and returned it to its special rack. The boots went down together, toes pointing out.

Jack made a quick sketch of the boots and the bright stripes.

"Does the rack have a name?" he asked.

"We call it the gear rack," said Captain Ann.

"Very practical," said Gus. "I would have called it the Big Boot Parking Lot."

Molly looked at the empty boots.

They stood neatly beside each other, waiting for their next job.

"Goodbye, Big Boot Parking Lot," she whispered.

Rex gave Molly their small tail-wag greeting.

Then the friends followed Aunt Jo and Farmer Ruth toward the open yard, where another demonstration was about to begin.

Molly knew the voice of the firefighter they had met.

And now she knew the big boots that belonged to her, too.
