# Gus and the Truck with Two Wheels

The blue delivery truck returned to Green Gate Farm with one very long carton.

Gus was certain there was a canoe inside.

"A canoe?" Farmer Ruth asked.

"For the hens," Gus explained. "They have never been boating."

"The hens would have quite a lot to say about that," Molly replied.

The hens were busy scratching the ground beside their pen. They showed no interest in boats.

Rex stood next to Gus and tried to smell through the carton. All he could smell was clean cardboard and a faint new-paint smell.

"It isn't a bone," he reported.

"That leaves a canoe," said Gus.

Dan opened the rear roll-up door of his blue box truck. Aunt Jo, Farmer Ruth, and the children watched from their places beside the barn gate.

Dan brought out a small metal frame with two wheels.

"Here's a truck without an engine," said Dan.

Gus blinked.

"That can't be a truck. It hasn't got anywhere for a driver to sit."

"This little hand truck has two wheels underneath the load."

Dan put the long carton onto the hand truck and balanced it as he moved it toward the barn. He did the wheeling himself, slowly, across the firm yard.

Jack drew the frame and its two wheels in his blue notebook.

"It can carry a box even though it doesn't have a motor," he said.

"Then my hat is a truck," Gus said. "It carries my head."

"Your hat has no wheels," Jack pointed out.

Gus looked at his hat. "That is why it's so slow."

Inside the barn, Farmer Ruth and Dan opened the carton.

There was no canoe.

There was a green metal tray, two long handles, supports, and one round wheel.

"A wheelbarrow!" Farmer Ruth said.

"A truck that arrived in pieces," Rex murmured.

While the grown-ups put the wheelbarrow together, the friends sat on a low fence bench and watched the hens wander along the yard.

When it was finished, Farmer Ruth wheeled it into view.

The green wheelbarrow had one wheel at the front and two handles at the back.

Jack looked at his drawing of Dan's hand truck, with its two little wheels.

"Two wheels on one," he said. "One wheel on the other."

"Which one is faster?" asked Rex.

"Neither is racing today," Farmer Ruth said.

"Good," Gus said. "The hens didn't enter the race either."

Farmer Ruth explained that the wheelbarrow would help her move soil and other materials around the farm. It was for farm chores, not a boat for hens or a bed for puppies.

She rolled it a short way across the yard with nothing inside. The single wheel made a neat line in a soft patch of dirt.

Molly sniffed the line. Rex sniffed beside her. The two noses moved forward together until they reached the end of it.

"It's a road for a very thin truck," Rex said.

Jack crouched to look at the mark, then drew one long line next to his two little circles.

Gus took off his green cap and held it above the wheelbarrow.

"If it ever needs a roof," he offered.

"Thank you," said Farmer Ruth. "I'll remember."

She leaned the wheelbarrow in its storage spot near the barn. Dan shut the truck door, and the blue box truck left along the farm road.

Later, back at his house, Gus set his cap carefully on its hook.

It had carried one bear all day, without even a single wheel.
