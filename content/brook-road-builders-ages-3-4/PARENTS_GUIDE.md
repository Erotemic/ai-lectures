# Parent / caregiver guide — Brook Road Builders (not spoken)

The primary intended listener is a three-year-old who enjoys **dogs, other animals, trucks, and construction machinery**. The third protagonist, Jack, is a **little boy scientist**. His curiosity is modelled as noticing, wondering, drawing, and changing his expectations when he sees something new, not as instantly explaining every physical phenomenon.

## Season One: Pine Park

The same friends and location recur for six visits. The machinery names remain consistent and clear: **excavator digs; dump truck delivers and tips; bulldozer pushes; wheel loader scoops and carries; roller compacts**. The large machines are driven by adults, seen from outside the construction fence. The children do their own small-scale playing at home and later walk through the finished park.

| Episode | One optional, simple conversation prompt |
|---|---|
| 001 | Which part of the yellow digger moved when its tracks stayed still? |
| 002 | What happened to the gravel when the truck bed went up? |
| 003 | What is Pancake Hill shaped like? |
| 004 | What kind of print would Rex's paw make in sand? |
| 005 | What did the big round roller do to the path? |
| 006 | Which machine would you like to see again? |

A child may request a favorite machine scene again, skip an episode, ask about a different truck, or want the end of the story with no prompt. No comprehension test is necessary. The written rumbles, crunches and whooshes are narrator-readable and do not require added sound effects or overstimulating sound design. Endings taper down to a quiet familiar scene.

## Season Two: The Little Bridge

The new arc includes two dogs as distinct characters: Rex (brown-and-white, opposite-colored ears) and Molly (black-and-white, white front paws). The actual bridge is built entirely by adults; the children have a toy/paper experiment at home and later visit the completed crossing. The park stays open during the new job. Deliberate pauses between episodes allow the concrete supports to gain strength before the prefabricated wooden deck is lifted into place.

| Episode | One optional, simple conversation prompt |
|---|---|
| 007 | Who was barking from the other side of the brook? |
| 008 | What kept turning on the white mixer truck? |
| 009 | What happened when the paper bridge had zigzag folds? |
| 010 | Which truck carried the long wooden bridge, and which lifted it? |
| 011 | How did the little loader help finish the path? |
| 012 | What animals did the friends meet at the farm? |

The paper-bridge experiment in the story compares one folded and one flat piece of paper with an acorn. It is not a claim that a paper model reproduces a full-scale bridge. Real lift and concrete work is not represented as a child's activity. The caregiver may revisit favorite vehicle words or simply read the final few paragraphs for a calmer ending.

## Season Three: Farm Workdays

A field south of the red barn follows a real **haymaking sequence** over several visits: Farmer Ruth's red tractor pulls a wagon and implements, the mower cuts long grass, the tedder fluffs it to dry, the rake gathers windrows, the baler makes tied rectangular bales, and adults load the wagon and store hay under cover. Several dry days pass between operations. Equipment is adult-operated, while the friends watch or play safely elsewhere. The steps explain a visible real-world change without being an equipment-operation guide.

| Episode | One optional, simple conversation prompt |
|---|---|
| 013 | Which wheels on the red tractor were bigger? |
| 014 | What looked different about the grass after the mower? |
| 015 | What did the tedder do to the loose grass? |
| 016 | Where did the hay rake put the grass? |
| 017 | What shape came out of the baler? |
| 018 | Why did the tractor and hay wagon take the farm road rather than the Little Bridge? |

The two puppies remain distinguishable: Rex has one dark and one light ear and lives by the blue step on Brook Road; Molly has white front paws and lives at Green Gate Farm. She visits Pine Park for the first time at the end, while Rex and Gus discover more of her farm. Jack's sketches are observations, not expert declarations. No listening-time requirement or comprehension check is implied.

**Farm and hay references:** Penn State Extension describes tractor functions; UNH, NC State and Maryland Extension describe cutting, drying, tedding and raking; Cornell Small Farms and University of Minnesota Extension describe baling, hauling and storage. Exact links and claims are in the source catalog and sidecars. These materials do not test this fiction's developmental or sleep effects.

## Season Four: The Garage with the Red Door

This season expands Brook Road **west** to Ms. Pam's repair garage. The first three episodes follow one repair: flatbed transport of Aunt Jo's disabled yellow van, examination and replacement of its damaged tire, and professional inspection on a two-post vehicle lift. Then Jack compares clean toy-wheel prints in clay, a separate mobile service truck brings tools to Farmer Ruth's red tractor, and Rex's existing wooden toy dump truck needs only a bit of trapped ribbon removed. The vehicles have different jobs; no child works beneath a raised van or attempts a recovery.

| Episode | One optional, concrete prompt |
|---|---|
| 019 | What part of the tow truck became a ramp? |
| 020 | How did Pam check the air pressing inside a tire? |
| 021 | Did the van become bigger, or did the lift raise it? |
| 022 | What were Jack's two different toy-wheel tracks like? |
| 023 | Why did the white service truck drive to Molly's farm? |
| 024 | What stopped Rex's wooden dump truck wheel from turning? |

Rex and Molly are the two puppies; Gus is the little bear, Jack the human boy scientist, Aunt Jo an adult dog, Farmer Ruth a goat, Mr. Lee a beaver, and Ms. Pam an adult raccoon. Neither adults nor children claim that the toy-wheel experiment reproduces real tire safety testing. The adult mechanic, not the children, operates the tow truck, lift, tire tools and battery equipment. A young listener can enjoy the ramp, striped tail, tool drawers and silly guesses without hearing a maintenance lecture.

**Source scope:** AAA describes flatbed and mobile roadside services, NHTSA provides tire-pressure and tread guidance, ALI provides lift guidance, and Bridgestone explains tread grooves on wet roads. These support the linked vehicle facts only, not effects of listening to the fiction. Exact source records and quoted narration anchors are in the sidecars.

## Season Five: The Delivery Route

Dan the adult donkey drives a blue enclosed box truck to the neighborhood's existing garage, park and farm. Each chapter follows one visible aspect of a real delivery job without asking a preschooler to follow a complicated parcel system. The objects stay where delivered: garage cones, a park rain gauge, balanced hen feed and a green wheelbarrow. The park rain gauge is empty at the end, ready for a later rain story rather than an invented same-day downpour.

| Episode | Optional conversation prompt |
|---|---|
| 025 | What did the blue truck have behind its cab? |
| 026 | How many orange cones were inside the box? |
| 027 | What will the clear tube catch when it rains? |
| 028 | Who kept trying to follow the feed boxes? |
| 029 | How many wheels did the little hand truck have? |
| 030 | Which way did Dan drive to the farm? |

**Scientific and editorial limits:** Truck bodies, liftgates and wheeled cargo helpers have specific functions. The rain gauge requires adequate open exposure, and a reading is only possible after rain. Chickens are fed a suitable balanced diet distinct from the farm hay used for sheep and goats. All deliveries and powered-equipment operation are done by adults; the animal characters and dialogue remain fiction. Manufacturer, government and extension references establish only these physical claims, not any measured benefit from this story series.

## Why there are sources

- **Machine functions:** Caterpillar's equipment descriptions were consulted to avoid confusing bulldozers, diggers, loaders, trucks and compactors. These are general equipment facts; the fictional site is not an engineering case study and the passages are not operation instructions.
- **Reading/interaction:** AAP and ZERO TO THREE provide general age-three shared-reading context for repetition and optional discussion. They do not establish a quantified or causal learning, behavior, or sleep outcome from these specific audio stories.

Source URLs, authorship, and per-passage rationale records are in `metadata/sources.json` and `metadata/<episode>.json`. These records are not read by TTS.

- **Season Two bridge facts:** FHWA and National Academies explain bridge supports and prefabrication; NRMCA describes mixing drums; ANU describes folded-paper bridge activities. Sources support the specific physical facts in the sidecars, not the invented neighbors or outcomes for listeners.
