# Molly and the Little Loader

Molly woke up early at Green Gate Farm.

She was a black-and-white puppy with white front paws and a blue rope toy that she liked to carry by one end.

She took the toy to the porch, laid it down, and looked toward the brook.

The long wooden bridge was there.

It had been there yesterday, too.

But it still had a blue work fence at each end.

"Not yet," Farmer Ruth told her.

Molly had begun to understand that *not yet* was a very busy phrase.

It meant grown-ups were still putting things together.

And sometimes it meant a new truck would arrive.

Today, a little yellow loader arrived on the farm road.

It was much smaller than the wheel loader that had once helped build Pine Park. It had a bucket at the front, and it carried small loads of gravel to make the walking path on the farm side meet the bridge.

Whirr. Scoop. Roll.

Molly sat on the porch beside Farmer Ruth and watched.

"That truck is taking tiny mouthfuls," she said.

"The path here doesn't need a giant pile," Farmer Ruth answered.

The little loader turned in the work area and carried another bucket of gravel.

On the Brook Road side, Rex, Gus, and Jack arrived with Aunt Jo.

Rex could see Molly through the rails that the carpenters had begun putting along both sides of the bridge.

"Hello, Molly!"

"Hello, Rex!"

Gus looked at the new rail posts.

"Is the bridge getting a fence?"

"Handrails," said Jack. "For the people and animals who walk across."

"Then it is getting very long arms," Gus said.

"Arms you can hold onto," said Aunt Jo.

Mr. Lee and the grown-up crew attached the rails, checked the fastenings, and worked on the gravel path leading out from Pine Park. The park's old loop stayed open. The new little branch of the path was still inside the work fence.

Rex could hear taps from the builders' tools.

Tap. Tap. Pause.

From the other side, Molly heard them too.

"I can hear the bridge getting ready!" she called.

"I can smell the fresh wood!" Rex answered.

"Can you hear my tail?" asked Molly.

She wagged it as fast as she could.

Rex lifted both ears.

"No!"

"Can you hear mine?"

He wagged his tail.

Molly lifted her ears.

"No!"

They stared at each other for a moment.

"Tails are very quiet," said Jack.

"Except when they knock over a bowl," said Aunt Jo.

Rex remembered the bowl. He decided not to discuss it.

At midday, the little loader rolled away, its bucket lowered and empty. The crew kept working on the paths and rails.

Then Mr. Lee walked slowly across the finished deck with another grown-up, checking their work. The children watched from outside the fence.

"They get to cross!" Gus said.

"They're checking it," Aunt Jo explained. "We'll wait until the bridge is opened for everyone."

Molly picked up one end of her blue rope toy and carried it back into the house.

She put it beside the door, where she would be able to find it easily another day.

Across the brook, Rex went home too.

That night he lay on his rug and practiced a very tiny tail wag.

Wiggle.

Not even the rug made a sound.

The bridge was quiet now. The trucks had gone home. The two paths almost met, and the little brook kept on traveling beneath them.

Tomorrow, or another day soon, there would be footsteps where today there had only been tools.
