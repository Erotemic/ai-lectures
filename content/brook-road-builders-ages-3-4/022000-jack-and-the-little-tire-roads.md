# Jack and the Little Tire Roads

Jack wanted to know where the little roads on a tire went.

"What little roads?" Rex asked.

Jack pointed to the tires of Aunt Jo's repaired yellow van, parked beside the house with the blue front step.

"Those dark lines. They run all around the rubber."

Gus walked in a slow circle around the van, keeping to the path.

"If I were a tiny bear," he said, "I could walk around one tire forever."

"You'd get dizzy," Molly said. She had come to visit Rex with Farmer Ruth.

"I could stop for lunch halfway," Gus replied.

Aunt Jo led them to the Red Door Garage, where Ms. Pam had promised to show Jack something he could try himself.

It wasn't a real truck wheel.

It was two little clean toy wheels, a sheet of paper, and a tray of soft modeling clay at a low table outside the work bay.

One toy wheel had wide raised bars around it.

The other had little zigzag ridges.

"Can you guess what marks they'll make?" Pam asked.

Jack drew his guesses in the blue notebook first.

"This one will make stripes," he said. "That one might make a zigzag."

Rex sniffed the clay.

"It smells like a very boring sandwich."

"Please don't eat the experiment," Jack said.

"I wasn't going to. I only said it was boring."

Jack rolled the first toy wheel slowly across the clay.

Tup. Tup. Tup.

A row of wide little bars appeared.

Then he rolled the second wheel.

A different line followed, full of crooked marks.

"Two wheels," Molly said. "Two kinds of tracks."

Gus pressed one paw beside the toy tracks.

"Three kinds," he said.

"Four," Rex said, putting his own paw beside Gus's.

Jack laughed. "We're making a map of feet."

Rex and Molly stepped back. Their pawprints were similar, but not exactly the same size.

"Mine has a bigger bump here," Rex said proudly.

"Mine goes this way," Molly answered. She pointed to a little toe mark.

Gus held his green cap over the clay.

"What kind of track does a cap leave?"

"A hat track," Jack said.

Gus pressed the edge of his cap gently into an unused corner. It made a curved line, like a smile.

"That's my happiest road," he said.

Ms. Pam brought over a large picture of a real road tire.

"Now look at these grooves," she said. "They're not just decorations."

Jack saw long channels between the raised rubber patterns.

"The grooves in many road tires help water move away from where the tire touches the road."

"Like little drains?" Jack asked.

"Something like that. Water needs a way out."

"The raised parts of the tread help the tire grip the road."

Gus looked at the toy wheel.

"So the tire isn't trying to draw beautiful pictures for the road?"

"Not really," said Pam.

"A pity," Gus replied. "Its pictures are excellent."

Jack copied the toy tracks into his notebook. This time he drew exactly what had appeared, even the one zigzag that had wobbled sideways.

"That's not what I predicted," he said.

"It's what happened," Aunt Jo said.

Jack liked that better.

They washed the clay from their paws and hands. Pam put the toy wheels back in their little box for another day.

On their way home, Rex and Molly walked along the garden edge, leaving two short trails of pawprints in soft earth.

The afternoon grew still.

At home Jack closed his notebook on the page of stripes, zigzags, paws, and one crooked hat-smile.

He could always look at the little roads again tomorrow.
