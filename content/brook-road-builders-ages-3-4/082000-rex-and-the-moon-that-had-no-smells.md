# Rex and the Moon That Had No Smells

The first thing Rex tried to do on the Moon was sniff.

His nose touched the inside of his clear helmet.

"I can't smell anything," he said over the suit radio.

"Your helmet is closed," Aunt Jo reminded him. "It keeps your air inside."

Rex looked across the gray ground. "Then I'll have to explore with my eyes."

All six travelers wore specially fitted protective suits. Max and Aunt Jo checked the suits and radios before the group stepped outside their lander. The grown-up team on Earth listened too. Nobody took off a helmet. There was no air to breathe on the Moon's surface.

Rex lifted one boot and put it down. A neat print stayed in the fine gray dust. Molly made another print beside it. Their one-wag greeting did not look quite the same through their space suits, but they managed it anyway.

Gus tried a gentle little hop. He rose a little higher than he expected and came down softly.

"My legs think they are springs," he said.

"The Moon's gravity is weaker than Earth's," Jack said. "You still come down, just differently."

Gus looked toward the sky. "Good. I wasn't planning to float away."

They walked slowly on the prepared route near the lander, following Max and Aunt Jo. The ground team had marked where to go and where to leave the ground undisturbed for science. Every step took care.

Rex stared at the dust. He could see it move when his boot touched it, but there was no breeze to blow it across the plain. The Moon had no weather like the rain and snow that visited Brook Road.

"No wind for my ears," he said.

"And no rain gauge," Jack answered.

The Sun made strong shadows. The lander's four legs cast long dark shapes across the gray surface. Jack drew one quick sketch on a small board clipped to his suit. A line for the horizon. A square for the lander. Two dots for the puppies.

Then he looked up.

Above the Moon's horizon hung Earth, blue and white against the black sky.

"There's home," he said.

Rex looked for the little green shape of Pine Park. Molly looked for the farm. Neither could find them.

"It's all in there," Aunt Jo said softly. "The farm, the park, the road, and everyone waiting for us."

Max took a picture of the six explorers with the lander behind them. Gus held up a gloved paw. Rex tried to put his ears up, forgetting again that the helmet was in the way.

"I hope the picture catches my good ear," he said.

"It has to catch your whole head," Molly told him.

They returned to the lander before they were tired. Inside, the adults checked the air system again, and everyone had a drink from a safe pouch. The suits stayed carefully stored where they could be used for the next planned outing.

Later Rex looked at the prints outside the window. They would not be washed away by tonight's rain. There was no rain coming.

He thought of muddy paw prints at Green Gate Farm and snowy ones beside Pancake Hill. Every place left a different mark.

He curled up in his resting place and looked once more at the blue Earth. The Moon had no smell for him to find, but he had found something he would remember with his eyes.
