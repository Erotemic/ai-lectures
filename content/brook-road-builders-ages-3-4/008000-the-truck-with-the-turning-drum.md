# The Truck with the Turning Drum

Gus heard the truck before he saw it.

It made a low growl as it came along the service lane beside Pine Park.

"Rrrrummm," said the truck.

"Rrrrummm," said Gus.

"That one's better," Rex told him. "You sounded like a bear with a stomach full of gravel."

Gus looked pleased.

The truck was big and white. Behind its cab was a huge round drum, tilted a little, turning slowly as the truck rolled along.

Jack had his blue notebook ready.

"Is that a washing machine?" he asked.

"A terrible washing machine," said Gus. "My green cap would come out looking like a stone."

Aunt Jo laughed. "It's a concrete mixer truck. It brings wet concrete to the bridge site."

The friends followed Aunt Jo to the safe watching spot by the new work fence. The old Pine Park path was open as usual. The machines worked beyond it, beside the brook.

Mr. Lee, the beaver carpenter, had been busy since their last visit. He and the crew had prepared wooden forms where the bridge supports would go on each side of the water.

From their viewing place, the friends could see wooden forms around the work areas. No concrete was being tipped into the brook.

The mixer truck stopped on the service lane.

The driver set the brake. The drum continued to turn.

Round and round.

Slowly. Slowly.

"Why does it keep moving?" Jack asked.

"It helps keep the wet concrete mixed while the truck carries it," Aunt Jo said.

"So it isn't washing a cap," said Gus.

"No caps," Aunt Jo agreed.

"What about a very muddy puppy?" asked Rex.

"Not a puppy either."

Rex sat down. "Good. I like my mud in small amounts."

The grown-up crew arranged a long metal chute at the back of the truck.

Then the gray concrete began to slide down into a prepared form.

Shhhhhhh. Plop.

The workers guided and smoothed it where it belonged. The drum turned as the truck delivered its load.

Jack drew a circle in his notebook. He added a curling arrow beside it.

"When the truck drove past our house, the drum was turning," he said. "And when it stopped, the drum was still turning."

"Maybe it's thinking," said Gus.

"What would a drum think about?" Rex asked.

"Round things," said Gus. "Very serious round things."

Farmer Ruth stood on her side of the brook, watching from the farm gate. Molly sat beside her, white paws neatly together.

"Hello, Rex!" Molly called.

Rex's tail began its fastest wag.

"Hello, Molly! A truck full of gray stuff is here!"

"I can see it!"

"Can you smell it?"

"No!"

"I can!" Rex announced proudly.

The crew finished their work for the day. The truck drove away by the service lane, and the workers protected the fresh concrete.

Jack looked toward the brook.

"Can the bridge go up now?"

"Not yet," Aunt Jo said. "The concrete needs time to become strong. The builders will check it before they use it."

Gus considered this.

"So today we watched a truck do something," he said, "and tomorrow we might watch something do nothing."

"That can happen on a building job," said Aunt Jo.

At home, Jack colored the drum on his drawing. Gus drew a tiny cap outside it, safely dry. Rex drew two little white paws on the opposite page.

"Those are Molly's," he said.

Aunt Jo put supper on the table.

The big mixer was already on its way to another job. Its drum might still be turning, keeping another load moving gently round and round.

But in Rex's house, the pencils were still, Gus's cap rested on a chair, and the three friends were ready for a quieter kind of evening.
