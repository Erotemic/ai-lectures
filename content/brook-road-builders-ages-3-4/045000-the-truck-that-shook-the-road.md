# The Truck That Shook the Road

There were four orange cones at the Red Door Garage.

Gus counted them through the waiting-room window.

"One. Two. Three. Four."

"They haven't multiplied," said Ms. Pam.

"Not yet," Gus replied.

The raccoon mechanic had set the cones along the edge of the yard so the visiting children could see the road crew working at the slippery bend beyond the gate. Rex, Jack, and Gus sat with Aunt Jo on the warm bench by the window.

Beyond the garage, Brook Road had been plowed. White banks lay on both sides. But one shaded turn still looked shiny and slick.

"That bit looks like a mirror," Jack said.

"A mirror for trucks?" asked Rex.

"A very long mirror," said Gus. "It would need a very long bathroom."

A small red municipal truck arrived. It didn't have the huge snowplow blade Rex had watched yesterday. Instead, it had a square container in its bed.

The truck rolled slowly toward the bend.

Krrr. Trrrr. Tick-tick-tick.

A spinner beneath the truck scattered a measured sprinkle of small grains onto the icy road.

"It's shaking!" Rex said.

"Not shaking all over," Jack replied. "Just the little spinning part."

Gus pressed his paws together.

"A giant pepper shaker!"

"The grains were road salt. Salt can help loosen ice by lowering the temperature at which water freezes," Ms. Pam explained.

"Can I put road salt on my porridge?" Gus asked.

"No," said Aunt Jo. "That's for the road crew's work, not for breakfast."

"Good," Gus replied. "I prefer raisins."

The truck made one careful pass while a worker watched the treated area. It didn't fill the road with a deep white drift or pull the shiny patch away in a bucket. It spread only what the crew had decided the road needed.

Rex tilted his head.

"So the orange snowplow has a blade, and this truck has a shaker?"

"Two different jobs," said Jack.

"And Ms. Pam has drawers," added Gus, pointing toward the garage workshop.

"Also two different jobs," said Ms. Pam.

The friends laughed.

They watched the workers check the bend. The truck turned away. The four orange cones came back through the garage gate, carried by Ms. Pam.

She stacked them: big orange cone, then another on top, then another, then one more.

"Four," said Gus.

"Still four," said Rex.

Ms. Pam put them in the shed beside her white service truck. Her flatbed tow truck was parked in the other bay. The yellow crew van stood ready to go home with Aunt Jo.

Rex looked down at his paws. A little damp snow clung between his toes from their walk along the cleared path.

Aunt Jo wiped his paws with a warm cloth before they headed home.

"Do they need a truck?" Gus asked.

"For this?" said Aunt Jo. "A cloth is enough."

Back at Rex's blue-step house, Jack drew a snowplow and a spreader on different sides of a notebook page.

Gus added an enormous pepper mill beside the spreader.

Jack looked at it.

"That is not a real truck."

"Of course not," Gus replied. "It would be terrible at corners."

The road outside rested in the cold afternoon air. The workers' job was done for now, and Rex was soon asleep with warm, dry paws.

**Editorial note:** These stories depict talking animals and an invented town. The road plow, spreader, tractor blade and snowblower are distinct adult-operated winter-maintenance machines. The children observe from warm homes or marked pedestrian places and never operate powered equipment. The linked sources support only the specific physical facts; they do not establish measured educational, developmental or sleep effects.
