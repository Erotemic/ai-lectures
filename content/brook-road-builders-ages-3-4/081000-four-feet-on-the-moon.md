# Four Feet on the Moon

The Moon filled the window.

Jack had seen it as a pale circle over Pine Park. He had seen it through Dr. Ada's telescope, with craters and shadows. Now it was a wide gray world below Star One, with bright ridges and dark hollows.

"It looks like a very large gravel yard," Rex said.

"Mr. Fred would need a very large scale," Gus replied.

The ship was in orbit around the Moon. Max pointed to a flat area on the near side, where the robotic Scout lander had arrived earlier. A little light on the display marked Scout's position. The rover was still waiting near it, its equipment checked by the engineers on Earth.

The six travelers moved into the Moon lander, which had been carried for their mission. It was roomy enough for all of them. The return cabin of Star One would remain in orbit, following its planned path until the lander came back to meet it.

"We're leaving our house up here?" Molly asked.

"Only for a little while," Aunt Jo said. "We'll come back to it before we go home."

Max checked the lander with Dr. Ada's team. The lander separated from the orbiting cabin. The children watched the gray ground move closer in the window. Max used the lander's engines and guidance systems to slow their descent.

"Where is the parachute?" Gus asked.

"There isn't one," Max said. "The Moon has almost no air. A parachute wouldn't slow us the way it does on Earth."

Rex looked at the small picture of the four landing legs.

"Four feet," he said. "Like a very tall dog."

"A dog with a rocket engine," Gus added.

Below them was a plain covered in fine dust and scattered rocks. There were no trees, no rivers, and no roads. They could see Scout standing far enough away from the planned landing spot. Max carefully guided the lander toward the cleared area selected by the mission team.

The ground rose toward them. Dust lifted beneath the descending craft.

"Almost," said Max.

The lander touched the Moon with a gentle bump.

For a moment the cabin was very still.

Then Dr. Ada's voice came through the radio. "Star One lander, we confirm you are safely on the surface."

Aunt Jo smiled. Molly looked at Rex. Each puppy gave the tiniest possible one-wag while still fastened into a seat.

"We did it," Jack whispered.

Gus looked at the display showing the four landing feet resting on the ground. "I told you it was a four-footed dog."

No one rushed to open the door. The crew first checked the lander, the air system, the suits, the radios, and the conditions outside. They had trained for this. They would go out only after the adult teams agreed that everything was ready.

Through the window, Jack could see the sun making long shadows beside the rocks. There were no clouds passing overhead. The sky above the sunlit ground was black.

"The Moon is bright," he said, "but the sky is dark."

"There is almost no atmosphere to scatter sunlight," Max said.

Rex watched a distant little shape beside Scout. It was the folded rover, waiting to be opened.

They had traveled from a park path to a county road, from a launch pad to Earth orbit, from the deep dark to the Moon. The final little distance was the space between the lander door and the ground outside.

That could wait until they had rested and checked every buckle.

For now, all six travelers looked out at the place where they had landed.
