# Molly and the Tunnel Beneath the Farm Road

Molly lived at Green Gate Farm, and she knew every ordinary noise around the red barn.

The hens made their little breakfast noises. The tractor rested in its shelter. Farmer Ruth wheeled the green wheelbarrow out to carry a small bag of feed.

But after the rain, Molly heard a new sound beside the long farm road.

Glup. Glup. Glup.

Molly tipped her head.

"Is somebody drinking soup?"

Farmer Ruth put down the wheelbarrow handles and listened.

"It's the roadside ditch," she said. "Come with me and we'll look from up here."

The ditch was a shallow channel beside the road. Rainwater had gathered there and was moving slowly past wet stones and grass.

At one place, the ditch seemed to disappear.

"Where does it go?" Molly asked.

Farmer Ruth pointed to a round opening below the road shoulder.

A culvert is a pipe that lets the little ditch carry water beneath the road.

"A tunnel for water!" Molly said.

"Just like Little Bridge?"

"Not quite," said Farmer Ruth. "Little Bridge carries paws and feet over the brook. This pipe carries the small ditch underneath the farm road."

Molly tried to imagine the pipe wearing boots. It would need one very round boot.

Then she noticed something else.

Leaves had gathered at the opening, slowing the water on its way.

Farmer Ruth asked Mr. Lee, the beaver carpenter, to inspect it with the road crew. They marked the soft edge of the road and kept vehicles on the sound part until the work was finished.

By the time Aunt Jo arrived with Rex, Gus, and Jack, the adults had cleared the leaves from the opening and checked the pipe. The little water sound had changed.

Shhhh.

"It's stopped drinking soup," Molly said.

"Now it's whispering," said Rex.

The two puppies greeted each other with their one-wag, two-wag hello.

Rex sniffed the edge of the walking path. Molly sniffed the same patch. They both looked pleased, though neither could say why.

A little yellow loader came up the long farm road. It was the same kind of compact machine that had once carried gravel when the friends' Little Bridge was being built.

"A baby bulldozer!" Gus cried.

Jack shook his head. "No. Look at the bucket."

The loader's front bucket carried a small load of fresh gravel. Its adult driver set the gravel beside the soft road shoulder, where workers could spread and firm the worn patch.

"It carries material in a bucket," Jack said. "The bulldozer pushed soil along with a blade."

Gus pictured the loader carrying a whole mountain of gravel in a spoon.

"Maybe it could carry Pancake Hill," he said.

"Then it would be the Pancake Hill Delivery Service," Rex said.

"That hill belongs at the park," Molly replied, remembering her visit there.

The crew finished shaping the road edge and checked the culvert again. Water still flowed beneath the road, and the repaired shoulder was firm.

Farmer Ruth brought everyone back to the barn porch. The hens were busy around their feeder. The red tractor was dry beneath its roof, and the green wheelbarrow rested where the family could find it.

Jack drew the road and the little pipe below it in his notebook. He drew an arrow showing the water going under, and another showing the loader's bucket coming down with gravel.

Molly peered at the picture.

"Your tunnel is very small."

"It's a picture," Jack said.

"Then I hope the water remembers to be small when it goes through."

Rex laughed so hard his ears flopped together.

After their visit, Aunt Jo took the three friends home by Little Bridge. Molly watched from the green gate until they had crossed.

Below the farm road, the water kept its quiet shushing sound.

And above it, the road was ready for ordinary wheels again.
