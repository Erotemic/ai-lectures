# Rex and the Road Made of Pebbles

After the snow melted, Rex could smell spring.

Wet grass. Pine needles. A worm turning over underground.

And something else.

He sniffed the edge of the path outside Pine Park.

Sniff, sniff.

"Mud," he announced.

"Very good, Inspector Nose," said Aunt Jo.

Rex was a small brown-and-white puppy with one dark ear and one light ear. He was proud of his nose, which had never needed a notebook to remember where Aunt Jo kept the biscuits.

Gus arrived in his green cap, with Jack walking beside him. The three friends stopped just inside the park gate.

The curving gravel path was still there. So were the bench, the tall pine, and Pancake Hill. The winter snow had gone away without taking any of them along.

But beside the gate, a short patch of ground looked worn and uneven. A little water sat in its shallow wheel tracks.

"Did somebody build a tiny pond?" asked Gus.

"Not on purpose," said Aunt Jo. "The ground softened during the thaw. Our crew will repair this little strip where the maintenance carts enter. The walking path itself is still open."

Rex tilted his head. "Could we fill it with biscuits?"

"You would eat the repair before breakfast," said Jack.

"I would check it carefully," Rex replied.

Aunt Jo showed them two stones she had brought from the crew's sample box. One was round and smooth. The other had little flat faces and corners.

"They feel different," Jack said.

Some pieces of crushed stone have rough, broken sides, not smooth sides like round river pebbles.

Gus turned the round stone in his paw. "This one looks like it's had a bath for a hundred years."

"And this one looks like it fell out of a very hard cookie," said Rex.

Aunt Jo smiled. "They can both be useful, but we need the right crushed stone mix for the patch."

"Just the very big pieces?" Jack asked.

"Different sizes together."

When the right sizes are spread and packed, the little pieces can make a firm walking surface.

"Like the path they built when Pine Park was new," said Jack.

Gus remembered the two great gravel piles from those days. He looked down at his green cap.

"I thought one of those piles might be big enough to wear this."

"It still would be," Rex said solemnly.

Jack flipped backward through his blue notebook. There was the yellow excavator, the tipping dump truck, and the first little gravel mountains. All of those drawings had been made before the park had a name on its gate.

"Where did the stones come from?" he asked.

Aunt Jo pointed past the service lane, away from the walking loop.

"There's a stone yard north of the park. The big trucks use that lane. We can visit the viewing room there with the yard manager, if you'd like."

Gus's ears lifted. "A whole yard full of stones?"

"Piles of different kinds."

"And trucks?"

"Big ones."

Gus put both paws over his green cap, just in case the news blew it away.

The friends spent the rest of their visit at the bench under the pine. Rex sniffed a patch of drying grass. Gus counted five ants carrying something that was definitely not a green cap.

Jack drew the park gate, the worn strip, and the two stones. Beside the stones he drew a question mark.

At home, Rex placed his repaired wooden dump truck on the rug. The wheels still rolled smoothly after Ms. Pam had helped fix it months ago.

"Tomorrow," Rex told it, "we find out where your big cousins get their rocks."

The wooden truck said nothing. It was excellent at listening.

Outside, the little patch waited for its repair. The rest of Pine Park was quiet beneath its familiar tree.
