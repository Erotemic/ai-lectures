# Molly and the Hill That Grew

Molly had heard all about the stone yard from Rex.

She had heard about a truck bigger than a barn door, a machine that broke rocks, and Gus's pretend crusher made of couch cushions.

But there was one part Rex had forgotten to mention.

"Do the stones have a hill?" Molly asked.

"Lots of hills!" Rex said. "Come and see."

Farmer Ruth and Aunt Jo brought the two puppies, Gus, and Jack to the yard office. Mr. Fred welcomed them to the same viewing room, where big glass windows looked over the working machines.

Molly pressed her nose toward the window.

Outside stood one gray pile, one pale pile, and a much taller pile with a long belt reaching toward its top.

"That hill is getting taller," Molly said.

"How can you tell?" asked Jack.

She pointed one white paw at a yellow post beside the pile.

"This morning the top was below that stripe. Now it's almost up to it."

Rex looked back and forth from the post to the stones.

"Maybe the hill ate breakfast."

Gus nodded. "Stone porridge."

Mr. Fred chuckled. "Watch the belt."

A long conveyor belt carried the sorted stone uphill, with no truck driving up the pile.

The belt moved over rollers inside its guarded frame. Little gray stones rode upward as if they were going to the top of a very slow staircase.

"It's a stone elevator," Gus whispered.

"An elevator without a room," Jack said.

The stones dropped off the far end of the belt and made a growing, pointed pile beneath it.

Tiny bits slid down the sides. One side grew steeper. Another side settled lower. The conveyor kept bringing more.

Molly watched a single pebble tumble a little way down the slope and stop.

"It went up on a belt," she said, "and down by itself."

"Just like me going up Pancake Hill and rolling down," said Gus.

"You don't actually roll down," Jack reminded him.

"Only in my imagination."

The tall pile looked rather different from the little rounded mound at Pine Park. Pancake Hill was covered with grass and made for gentle walking. This one was loose stone in a working yard, made for trucks and loaders.

Molly liked the difference. She knew both hills, and she knew exactly which one Rex could race her around.

The conveyor slowed. Then it stopped for a pause in the yard's work.

The hill was still.

"Did it finish growing?" Molly asked.

"For now," said Mr. Fred. "The stone will wait there until a customer needs it."

"Like the hay waiting in our barn," Molly said.

Farmer Ruth gave her a fond scratch between the ears.

"That's a good comparison. Different material, but both are stored until they're needed."

Jack drew the yellow post, the belt, and the pointed pile. He made two little lines for the pile's height, one from when Molly first looked and one from later.

Rex was busy looking at Molly.

"You noticed the stripe before I did."

"I live with a tractor," Molly said. "I'm very good at noticing things near big wheels."

At the end of the visit, Farmer Ruth took Molly home by the longer farm road. Rex went back to the blue-step house with Aunt Jo and the others.

On the rug, Gus stacked three soft pillows into a small mound.

Rex sniffed the top one.

"It smells like laundry."

"Good," said Gus. "This pile is for sleeping, not for trucks."

That night Molly slept near her blue rope toy at Green Gate Farm. Rex curled up beside his wooden dump truck. Both puppies had seen the same growing hill, and each had a favorite hill of their own.
