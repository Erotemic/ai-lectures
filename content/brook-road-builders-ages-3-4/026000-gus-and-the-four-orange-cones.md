# Gus and the Four Orange Cones

The morning delivery to Red Door Garage came in a box that looked much too small.

"Four orange cones," Ms. Pam read from Dan's delivery note.

Gus stared at the box in the back of the blue truck.

"Four?" he asked. "I think you have delivered one and a half."

"You can't have half a cone," Rex said.

"You could if a very tall goat wore it as a hat," Gus said.

Aunt Jo, Jack, and Rex were sitting with Gus on the garage's viewing bench, near the flowering shrub. Ms. Pam had asked for the cones to mark part of her work yard when vehicles were being worked on.

"I need four," Ms. Pam said. "They'll help me show which part of the yard is busy."

Dan opened the rear door of his blue box truck and moved the carton toward the back.

Dan rolled a small two-wheeled hand truck under the carton and tipped it back onto its wheels.

"Look," Jack said. "The little wheels carry the box."

"A tiny truck with no cab," said Rex.

"And no horn," said Gus. "How does it tell other trucks hello?"

"Quietly," Dan answered.

The adults moved the carton into the clear yard and set it down. Pam opened the top.

Inside was one very pointy orange shape.

"One," Gus said, as though he had been right all along.

Pam lifted it out.

And another orange shape came with it.

And another.

And another.

"Four!" Rex barked.

"That box was hiding three cones," Gus said.

"They weren't hiding," Jack said. "They were inside each other."

The four cones nested together, one inside another.

Pam stood them in a row. The tops pointed up, each the same bright orange. Rex sat at the viewing bench and tipped his dark ear toward them.

"They look like four carrots who forgot to grow leaves," he said.

Gus was delighted.

"We should send them to the farm. Farmer Ruth has a goat. Goats may know what to do with very tall carrots."

"They're not vegetables," said Molly, who had come visiting with Farmer Ruth.

"Good," Gus said. "It would take a very big soup pot."

Pam put the cones together again, one inside another, and showed how they took up less space as a stack. The cone tops made a little orange staircase.

Jack drew four separate shapes in his notebook. Then he drew the same four shapes inside one another.

"Four things," he said, "but one stack."

"Just like four friends on one bench," Gus suggested.

The bench was already quite full, especially with a bear on it.

Aunt Jo laughed and shifted to give everybody a little more room.

Pam carried the cones to her yard shed. They would be ready whenever the work area needed to be marked.

Dan closed the truck door. His blue box was a little emptier now.

On the walk home, Gus tried putting his small leaf collection into his cap. The leaves lay in one neat pile.

"My cap is a leaf delivery truck," he announced.

"Where are they going?" Jack asked.

Gus put the cap on his head.

"Right here," he said.

The leaves tickled his ears, and he took the cap off again.

That night, the four orange cones stood together in the garage shed, and Gus's green cap was empty and back on its hook.
