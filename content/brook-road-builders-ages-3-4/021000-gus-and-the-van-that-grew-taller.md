# Gus and the Van That Grew Taller

Gus had a question that was much bigger than his green cap.

"Can a van climb into a tree?"

Rex looked at the one tall pine in Pine Park.

"I have never seen one try," he said.

"What if it wanted a better view?"

"Then it would have to ask the tree," Jack said.

They were walking with Aunt Jo toward the Red Door Garage. Her yellow work van was still there, waiting for Ms. Pam to finish its repair.

The red door was open. Inside, under the bright roof lights, the van stood in a work bay.

There was a tall machine on each side.

"Those look like two enormous pencils," Jack said.

"Pencils for writing on the ceiling," Gus agreed.

Ms. Pam came out to the viewing window. "Those are the posts of my vehicle lift."

"Does the lift draw pictures?" Gus asked.

"It lifts a vehicle so I can reach the places underneath."

Jack leaned toward the window, but stayed beside Aunt Jo.

Two tall posts stood on either side, with four lifting arms reaching beneath the van.

"Four arms!" Gus whispered. "It's a very tidy spider."

"The arms hold the van," said Pam. "The pads have to touch the right strong places."

Ms. Pam checked that the lifting pads were in the right places before she raised the van.

Then the lift hummed.

A little higher.

A little higher still.

Soon the tires hung clear of the floor.

Gus put both paws on his cheeks.

"The van is growing!"

Jack watched the line of the floor behind it.

"The van's still the same size," he said. "It moved up."

"Maybe the garage floor is shrinking," Gus suggested.

"No," said Jack.

"Maybe I am shrinking."

"You're still the same Gus," said Rex.

Gus checked his cap. It fit.

"Good. I like being this Gus."

Ms. Pam looked beneath the van while it was properly supported. She checked the wheel area and the parts she needed to see. Then she fitted the prepared new wheel where the damaged one had been.

The children could not see every tool through the window, but they could see that the van was not going anywhere until Pam was satisfied with her work.

"Does it feel tall up there?" Rex asked.

"It doesn't have feelings," said Jack.

"Perhaps it likes the view," Gus said.

"Perhaps it can see my house!"

Ms. Pam finished her checks and carefully lowered the van back onto the garage floor.

Down came the body.

Down came the wheels.

Then they all rested on the ground again.

"There," Pam said when she came out. "The yellow van is ready to go."

Aunt Jo thanked her.

Gus stood on the low step outside the viewing window and imagined that he had risen as high as a van.

"What could you see?" Jack asked.

"All the way to Pancake Hill," Gus said. "And a sandwich on the other side."

Rex knew a much better way to look at the park.

"We can walk there," he said.

Aunt Jo drove the repaired yellow van home. It rolled along Brook Road with four round tires, all turning together.

When the friends passed Pine Park later, there was the same old bench, the same tall pine, and the same gentle hill.

No van had climbed the tree.

At bedtime Gus hung his green cap on its hook.

He looked at the hook.

"You may lift my hat," he told it. "But please don't lift my bed."

The hook did not answer.

Gus lay down, satisfied, and the room grew quiet.
