# Molly and the Tractor in a Snow Coat

Molly woke up to a very important smell.

Hay.

And another very important smell.

Breakfast.

And a third smell that made her sneeze.

Cold, new snow.

She stood on Farmer Ruth's farmhouse porch and watched three hens cross the snowy yard.

Their feet made little marks like arrows.

One hen turned right. One turned left. The third took three steps and decided she'd rather be back in the warm barn.

Molly wished she could ask the hens where they were going.

But the hens were busy being hens.

Then she heard the tractor.

Chug-chug-chug.

Farmer Ruth had fitted a broad snow blade to the front of her red tractor.

Molly knew that tractor. In the warm months it had pulled the hay wagon and several different machines through the field. Today the wagon and hay tools were stored away.

The red tractor wore a broad blade like a new winter coat.

"That is not a coat," said Farmer Ruth, coming out to join Molly at the porch railing.

"It's almost a coat," said Molly. "It covers the front."

"It helps clear snow from the lane."

Molly's ears lifted.

A road! The farm lane that led around to the longer vehicle road was white from end to end. Farmer Ruth's wagon could not use it comfortably yet.

Farmer Ruth climbed back into the tractor cab while her helper stayed with Molly. The tractor moved into the open yard, well away from the animals. As the tractor drove, the blade pushed a wide ribbon of snow away from the farm lane.

Molly watched from the porch with Farmer Ruth's helper.

The red tractor went forward.

The snow went sideways.

The cleared part of the lane appeared like a brown path drawn in a white picture.

One hen came to inspect the edge of the clearing. She stared at the ridge of snow and gave a short, doubtful cluck.

"She thinks the tractor has made a wall," said Molly.

"It's a snowbank," Farmer Ruth said when she returned for a moment. "She'll find her way around it."

The hens did, although the third hen tried the wrong direction first.

Molly found that extremely funny.

Near midday, Rex arrived with Aunt Jo. They had walked from Pine Park on the cleared Little Bridge and followed the shoveled footpath toward the farmhouse. The tractor had taken the separate farm lane and hadn't gone anywhere near the little wooden bridge.

Rex touched noses with Molly, then they wagged their tails once each.

"The red tractor has a big tooth!" Rex cried.

"It's a blade," said Molly. "And it can make a road appear."

They looked down the lane. The snow lay in tidy ridges at the sides, not heaped against the barn doors.

"What happened to all the hay?" Rex asked.

Molly led him to the open viewing doorway of the red barn. The dry rectangular bales were still stacked under cover, where the farm helpers could bring them to the sheep.

"There it is," she said.

"Does it need a snow coat?"

"No," replied Molly. "It's got a barn."

Rex considered that.

"A whole building is a very good coat."

Outside, the red tractor made its final pass and was parked in the equipment shed. Farmer Ruth checked the cleared lane.

Before Rex went home, he and Molly followed their own little path back to the porch, noses low, tails waving.

The snow showed two sets of tracks side by side: one from Rex, one from Molly.

In the warmth of the kitchen, Molly curled beside her blue rope toy. Beyond the window, the familiar red tractor sat quiet in its shed.

Tomorrow there could be more snow.

Tonight the farm lane was clear, and the hens were all home.
