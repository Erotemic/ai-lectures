# The Truck with a Room on Its Back

Rex was walking down Brook Road when he heard a noise he did not know.

Rrrrrrrum. Ch-ka-chunk.

He stopped so suddenly that Gus nearly walked into his tail.

"What's that?" Rex asked.

"A very large lunch box," Gus said.

A blue truck stood beside the Red Door Garage. It had a white stripe along one side and two big mirrors beside its cab.

But the most interesting part was behind the cab.

Behind its cab was a tall, square cargo box with walls and a roof.

"That's a room," said Gus.

"A room that goes driving," said Rex.

Jack opened his blue notebook. On an older page was Ms. Pam's white flatbed tow truck, carrying Aunt Jo's van. On a fresh page he drew the blue truck.

"The flatbed had an open place for a whole van," he said. "This one has a box with a roof. What's inside?"

A donkey wearing a plain blue cap stepped down from the cab. His ears stuck up through two little openings in the cap.

"Hello," he said. "I'm Dan. I make deliveries."

"I'm Rex," said Rex. "I make noises when I see trucks."

"Yes, I noticed," Dan said.

Dan waved to Aunt Jo and Ms. Pam, who were standing outside the garage. When the grown-ups had cleared the loading area, he opened the back of his truck.

The big rear door rolled upward, folding out of the way above the truck floor.

Inside stood several boxes. Some were tall. Some were wide. One was almost the same shape as Gus's green cap, except that a cap was not nearly so square.

"Does anybody live in there?" Gus asked.

"Only the parcels," said Dan. "And they have very quiet conversations."

Rex sniffed from the watching bench. He could smell cardboard and something that reminded him of dry grass.

"Maybe one parcel is a field," he said.

"A field would be hard to fit," Jack said.

"Not if you folded it," Gus replied.

Dan showed Aunt Jo a sheet of paper with three simple pictures. There was a red door, a pine tree, and a green gate.

"First, supplies for Pam's garage," he said. "Then something for Pine Park. After that, I take the longer farm road to Farmer Ruth."

Jack drew the three pictures in his notebook. Rex watched him put the green gate beyond the brook.

"Not over Little Bridge?" Rex asked.

"No," Aunt Jo said. "Little Bridge is for people, paws, and hooves on foot. Dan's truck takes the road."

"I have four hooves," Dan said, looking down at them. "But the truck has more wheels than that."

Gus counted two of the wheels he could see and then lost count when he walked around the bench.

"Perhaps it has enough to share," he said.

The delivery truck was parked for the afternoon at Dan's small covered parcel shed, just beyond the garage. Dan shut the rear door with another ch-ka-chunk.

That evening, Rex made his own box truck from a clean empty carton and his familiar wooden dump truck. He set the wooden truck inside the carton.

"Delivery," he announced.

"What are you delivering?" asked Aunt Jo.

Rex looked inside.

"A truck," he said. "I have delivered a truck to myself."

He was so pleased with the idea that his tail thumped three times against the rug.

Outside, the real blue truck rested under its shed roof, ready for the next morning's ordinary work.
