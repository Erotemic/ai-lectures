# Jack and the Water That Took a Turn

Jack liked drawings with arrows.

An arrow showed where a dump truck delivered its gravel.

An arrow showed how the hay wagon returned to Farmer Ruth's barn.

And an arrow showed where rainwater went after it rolled down Brook Road.

But now he had a question about the big red fire engine.

"Where does the water go?" he asked Captain Ann.

"Come and watch," she said.

The firefighters had set up a practice target in their yard. It was a painted board with a big yellow circle in the middle.

"Is it a cookie?" Gus asked.

"A circle," said Jack.

"A cookie-shaped circle," Gus said.

Rex looked interested.

Molly looked even more interested.

Farmer Ruth settled beside them at the visitor line.

Captain Ann showed Jack the same pump panel they'd seen behind the engine's side door.

The firefighters had connected a hose to the engine and checked the fittings. It rested in a long curve across the clear practice space.

"Does the hose make water?" Rex asked.

"No," said Captain Ann. "Water has to come from somewhere. Our engine carries some in its tank."

Jack tapped his pencil against the notebook.

"And the pump?"

"It helps move the water through the hose with enough pressure for the job."

"What about the yellow cookie?" asked Gus.

"That's our practice target. Nothing is on fire today."

An adult firefighter stood at the far end with the nozzle. Captain Ann gave a signal.

A smooth stream of water came out and splashed against the painted circle.

Shhhhhh.

The water ran down the board and into a shallow drain in the training yard.

Rex stood up.

"The cookie is getting washed!"

Molly's tail thumped once against the grass.

"It still looks like a cookie."

Gus considered this.

"Maybe it is a very clean cookie."

Jack had already begun drawing.

"From the tank, through the pump, along the hose, and out the nozzle," Captain Ann said.

Jack drew one arrow for each part.

Then he noticed something.

The hose curved around a corner, but the water still traveled through it.

"It doesn't need a perfectly straight road," he said.

"Not when it's laid out and flowing properly," Captain Ann answered.

"Like our brook," said Molly. "It turns around the meadow."

"Like a dog's tail!" said Rex, giving his a wiggle.

"Your tail doesn't carry water," Jack said.

"Not today."

Captain Ann told the firefighters when to stop. The stream slowed and ended, and the crew handled the hose, drained it, and put their equipment back in place.

Jack looked across the quiet yard. A little water still glistened on the pavement by the practice board.

"The rain came down from clouds," he said. "This water came from the truck's tank. Both ran downhill after they landed."

"Different beginnings," Aunt Jo said. "Some of the same things happen afterward."

Gus added a drawing beneath Jack's arrows: a round face with two dots for eyes.

"That's the practice cookie," he explained.

Jack tilted the page. "It has a mouth."

"It was surprised to get a bath."

The friends watched Captain Ann close the engine's final compartment door.

The firehouse yard was almost dry again by the time everyone headed for home.

Jack carried his notebook carefully. He had one drawing for where rainwater went, and another for where the engine water traveled.

Rex and Molly agreed that both were interesting.

Gus thought the cookie had been the best part.
