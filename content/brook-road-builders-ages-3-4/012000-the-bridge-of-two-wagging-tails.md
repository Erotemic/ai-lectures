# The Bridge of Two Wagging Tails

On the morning the bridge opened, Rex was awake before Aunt Jo.

He sat on the blue front step and watched Brook Road.

Gus came along wearing his green cap. Jack came with his blue notebook tucked under one arm.

"Did the trucks come back?" asked Gus.

"No," said Rex.

"Did the bridge move?" asked Jack.

"I don't think so," said Rex. "But Molly might be there."

Aunt Jo joined them. "Then let's go and see."

They walked to Pine Park, where the tall pine tree still shaded the bench and Pancake Hill was as wide and gentle as ever.

The old gravel path curved around the park just as it always had.

But now a new, short path branched off toward the brook.

The blue work fence was gone from that path.

A small sign said LITTLE BRIDGE.

Beyond it stood a wooden footbridge with rails on both sides.

Mr. Lee was there, talking with Farmer Ruth. The grown-up crew had finished its checks, and the bridge was open for walking.

"No trucks across this one," said Aunt Jo, seeing Gus study the narrow boards. "The big vehicles still use the farm road."

"Only feet?" Gus asked.

"Feet, paws, and hooves," Farmer Ruth said.

"And my green cap?" Gus asked.

"If it stays on your head," said Jack.

Gus pressed his cap down firmly.

Aunt Jo walked with them onto the bridge.

The boards were smooth and firm under Jack's shoes.

Rex's four paws made little taps.

Gus's feet went thump, thump, and he smiled at the sound.

Below them the brook moved around its pebbles.

"We're going over the water," Rex said.

"Without getting wet," said Jack.

"A very dry swim," said Gus, remembering their blue towel at home.

Halfway across, Rex stopped.

Molly stood at the far end, her white front paws together.

She had left her blue rope toy by the farmhouse door and come to meet them.

For one small moment, neither puppy spoke.

Then Rex's tail began to wag.

Molly's began too.

They walked closer and greeted each other nose to nose.

"You smell like pine trees," Molly said.

"You smell like a farm," said Rex.

"That's where I live."

"I know! I've been looking at it for days!"

They both laughed, in the doggy way that sounds a little like huffing.

Farmer Ruth opened the green gate.

Inside was Green Gate Farm. There was the red barn the children had seen across the brook, a little porch where Molly liked to sit, and a path that ran toward the fields.

Three hens scratched at the dirt.

Two woolly sheep stood by the fence, watching Gus as if his green cap were a very interesting kind of grass.

A goat with a striped collar leaned over the gate of a small pen.

"Hello," Gus told the goat. "I'm a bear."

The goat chewed slowly.

"I see," said Gus. "A quiet sort of conversation."

Jack opened his notebook.

He drew the pine tree, the brook, and the new bridge between the two banks.

He drew a little dog on each end, then moved them to the middle of the picture.

"The bridge connects the places," he said.

"It connects the dogs too," said Rex.

"It connects a bear to a goat," Gus added.

The goat was still chewing.

Molly showed Rex the farmhouse porch and the blue rope toy lying beside the door.

Rex was allowed to give one end a little tug, and Molly took the other end. They played quietly until their paws were tired.

After a visit, Aunt Jo said it was time to go home.

"Will the bridge still be there tomorrow?" asked Rex.

"Yes," said Aunt Jo. "That's the idea."

They walked back across the Little Bridge together.

Rex paused to look behind him.

Molly stood by the green gate. Her white paws were bright in the afternoon light.

"See you again!" she called.

"Now I know the way!" Rex called back.

Back on Brook Road, the three friends had supper. Jack put the new bridge drawing after his Pine Park pictures. Gus set his cap on its usual peg.

Rex curled up on his rug and rested his chin beside his wooden toy dump truck.

The truck was not allowed on the Little Bridge. It had plenty of roads for trucks, and Rex liked it just where it was.

Beyond Pine Park, the wooden bridge rested across the moving brook.

On one side slept a brown-and-white puppy with two different ears.

On the other side slept a black-and-white puppy with four paws and two bright white front paws.

Tomorrow, they could walk toward each other without having to shout.
