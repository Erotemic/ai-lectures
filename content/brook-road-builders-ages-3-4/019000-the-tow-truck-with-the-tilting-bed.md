# The Tow Truck with the Tilting Bed

The first thing Rex noticed was that Aunt Jo came home without her yellow van.

She came up Brook Road on foot, carrying her lunch box.

Rex waited on the blue front step with his wooden dump truck beside him.

"Where did you leave the van?" he asked.

"At the wide pull-off by the farm road," said Aunt Jo. "One tire went flat. I parked it, and Ms. Pam is bringing it to the garage."

"Who is Ms. Pam?"

"Come along and meet her."

Gus came too, wearing his green cap. Jack brought his blue notebook. Molly arrived with Farmer Ruth by the long farm road. She had come to see the big truck.

At the west end of Brook Road stood a low building with a bright red door.

A sign over it said RED DOOR GARAGE.

Beside the building was a small gravel viewing spot with a bench and a flowering shrub. From there the children could watch the work.

"A garage!" Gus said. "Is that where the trucks sleep?"

"Sometimes," Aunt Jo said. "Sometimes they come here because they need something fixed."

A raccoon in blue overalls stepped out of the red door. She had a striped tail and a small pencil tucked behind one ear.

"I'm Pam," she said. "I fix vehicles. And today my tow truck has a job."

They all looked toward the farm road.

A white truck was waiting beside the garage. Its cab had a little orange light on top, and behind the cab was a very long silver bed.

"It's carrying a whole road!" Gus cried.

"It is carrying its own bed," Jack said.

Rex tilted his dark ear. "Does it take naps on that bed?"

"Not that kind of bed," Pam said, smiling. "Watch."

Pam climbed into the cab and drove to the wide pull-off, just around the bend. Aunt Jo walked the children to a fenced spot on the sidewalk where they could watch the job.

With the work area clear, Ms. Pam parked her flatbed truck in front of Aunt Jo's van.

The bed tipped down until its back edge was near the road.

"It made a ramp," said Jack.

"Yes," said Pam. "Now the van can come aboard."

But the van did not start its engine.

It did not honk.

It did not drive anywhere.

The winch pulled the van slowly up the bed, even though its engine was off.

Rex sat very still. His tail, however, went thump, thump, thump against the bench.

The yellow van crept higher and higher.

"The truck is eating the van," Gus whispered.

"If it eats vans, what does it have for dessert?" Molly asked.

"A bicycle," Gus said, after careful thought.

"A bicycle is very crunchy," Rex decided.

Ms. Pam secured the van, brought the flatbed level again, and drove slowly back toward her garage.

Aunt Jo walked the children back to the garage viewing bench. The white truck came into the yard and stopped. Its long bed looked quite flat now.

"Is the yellow van smaller?" Gus asked.

"It's the same size," Jack said. "It just looks small way up on that truck."

Ms. Pam unloaded the van inside the work area and closed the gate behind her.

"Tomorrow I'll have a proper look at that tire," she said.

Rex walked to the garage sign and read it aloud with Aunt Jo.

"Red. Door. Garage."

He sniffed the air.

It smelled faintly of rubber, warm road dust, and the flowers by the bench.

"Does the garage smell like that every day?" he asked.

"Not always," Pam said. "Sometimes it smells like wet coats."

"Mine smells like dog," Rex said.

"Mine too," said Molly.

That evening, the yellow van stayed behind the red door, where it was dry and quiet.

At home Rex rolled his wooden dump truck across the rug and back to its place.

He wondered how big a flatbed would have to be to carry a truck carrying another truck.

It was a very large thought.

He let it rest beside him while he fell asleep.
