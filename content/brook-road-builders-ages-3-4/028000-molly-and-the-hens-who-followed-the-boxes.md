# Molly and the Hens Who Followed the Boxes

Molly heard the blue truck before anybody else at Green Gate Farm.

Not because she had the longest ears. Farmer Ruth the goat had rather impressive ears herself.

Molly heard it because she had been listening for Rex.

When a truck engine rumbled along the long farm road, Molly hurried to the green gate.

"Truck!" she called.

"And puppies," said Farmer Ruth.

Rex, Gus, and Jack were arriving on foot with Aunt Jo. They had come by the short path through Pine Park and across Little Bridge. The blue truck had driven the longer road around the brook.

Rex gave Molly one little tail wag.

Molly gave one little tail wag back.

Then both tails began wagging much more than one little wag.

"Your secret greeting has become a very big greeting," Gus observed.

Dan the donkey parked his box truck on firm ground near the red barn. He climbed out, checked his delivery note with Farmer Ruth, and opened the tall rear door.

There were several sturdy sacks of feed inside, stacked together on a small load platform.

"The hens' food has arrived," Farmer Ruth said.

One of the three hens looked up from the yard.

Then the second hen looked up.

Then the third.

The three hens began walking toward the gate with great purpose.

"The inspectors," Molly whispered.

"Are they checking the truck?" asked Jack.

"They check everything," said Farmer Ruth.

Dan prepared the liftgate while the grown-ups kept the yard clear. The platform at the back of the truck lowered the load from truck height to the ground.

"It's like a little elevator for boxes," Jack said.

"I would like an elevator for my cap," Gus said.

"Your cap is already up high," said Rex.

"Then I need one to bring it down for supper."

The adult workers moved the sacks into the barn. Molly and Rex watched from beside Aunt Jo, while the hens followed along their own fence line.

The smallest hen went first. She stepped quickly, as if she had remembered something important.

"Are they hungry?" asked Jack.

"They're interested," Farmer Ruth said. "The hens get their own balanced feed, and the sheep eat hay."

The dry hay from the earlier harvest still rested under cover in another part of the barn. It wasn't the same as the feed the hens received.

"I thought all farm animals ate the same dinner," Gus said.

Molly shook her head. "You wouldn't serve hay to Rex."

Rex looked offended. "I would sniff it first."

"And I wouldn't serve biscuits to a tractor," Jack added.

"Not even a little one?" asked Gus.

"Not even a little one."

Farmer Ruth put the feed away in a closed, dry container. Then she filled the hens' feeder with an appropriate amount.

The smallest hen reached it first.

The other two arrived a moment later.

All three had completely forgotten the blue truck.

"Inspection finished," said Molly.

Dan checked the next delivery on his sheet. There was another farm package still to come on a later visit.

"Tomorrow's another truck day," he told Farmer Ruth.

The sun was warm on the barn wall. Molly lay beside Rex in a patch of shade, their noses pointing toward the hens.

One hen scratched at the earth. Another shook her feathers. The third sat down as if she had finished a difficult day's work.

"They have the right dinner," Rex said.

"And you?" asked Molly.

Rex considered it.

"I think my dinner should arrive on a very small blue truck."

"Mine too," Molly said.

Their tails gave one last matching wag before Rex went home with Aunt Jo.
