# The Barn Door Stays Dry

One morning, the sky over Green Gate Farm turned the color of a soft gray blanket.

Not a stormy sky.

Not a noisy sky.

Just a sky that seemed to be thinking about a small amount of rain.

Molly stood on the farm porch with her blue rope toy. Rex stood beside her, wearing his red collar and looking very seriously at the clouds.

"Do you think the rain remembers where it went last time?" Rex asked.

Molly looked toward the red barn.

"I hope it remembers a different place."

Their friends were visiting with Aunt Jo. Gus had already tried to convince Jack that a cloud was shaped like an enormous sheep.

"It has a nose," Gus insisted.

"Clouds don't have noses," Jack said.

"Then what is that bit sticking out?"

"Another part of the cloud."

"A cloud's other nose," Gus decided.

The first drop landed on the porch step.

Plip.

Then another landed on the fence.

Plip.

Farmer Ruth looked up at the new little roof beside the wagon door. The grown-ups had completed their final inspection, but this was the first rain since the repair. Mr. Lee had come by to see how the shelter behaved in actual weather.

The friends watched from the main covered porch with the adults. They could see the shelter without walking into the wet farmyard or the work zone.

More drops came.

Softly at first.

Then with a gentle hiss on the roof.

Rex's ears lifted. One dark. One light.

"Listen," he said. "The roof is getting its bath."

Raindrops slid down the little roof, into the gutter, and down the pipe to the stones away from the wagon door.

Jack opened his blue notebook. There was his first picture of the old wet spot. There was the page with the diagonal stick in the braced square. There was the yellow telehandler with its stretching arm.

Now he drew the new roof and a line of tiny blue drops.

"It has a road," he said.

"A rain road," Rex said.

"One without trucks," Molly added.

"A road so tiny that even my hat wouldn't fit," Gus observed.

The rain passed through quickly. The cloud drifted toward the far field and left the light bright again.

Farmer Ruth and Mr. Lee walked around to check the shelter. The four friends waited on the porch with Aunt Jo until the adults returned.

Ruth smiled.

"Would you like to see? It's ready."

The friends followed her by the usual path after she had taken down the temporary barrier.

The small roof was red, just like the barn. Its edge was straight, and the gutter ran along the front. Water had fallen onto the little patch of stones, not beside the door.

The old wet place under the little shelter stayed dry.

Molly put her white front paws on the ground where the puddle used to be.

"No plip," she said.

"No plip," Rex agreed.

Gus waited.

They all waited.

Nothing dripped onto the doorway.

"The barn has stopped having hiccups," Gus announced.

A hen stepped into the dry shelter and stared at them. It gave one confident cluck, as if it had built the roof herself.

"She thinks she is the inspector," Molly said.

"She gave it a good cluck," Rex replied.

Farmer Ruth went into the main barn and brought out the same green wheelbarrow that Dan had delivered months before. It still had one front wheel, two handles, and its green tub. She rolled it under the little roof to its familiar resting place.

"Back home," she told it.

Gus frowned thoughtfully. "I thought the wheelbarrow's home was the barn."

"It has two homes," Jack said. "The big barn when the shelter was closed, and the little roof when it's open."

"Very busy for a wheelbarrow," said Gus.

Rex sniffed the air beneath the new roof. Fresh wood. Damp grass beyond the edge. A little clean stone where the rain had landed.

No old wet smell.

Jack showed Farmer Ruth his latest drawing. On one page the old roof had a drip going the wrong way. On the next, the repaired roof had arrows leading to the gutter and the stones.

"I thought we'd need a whole new barn when Molly first heard the dripping," he said.

"I'm glad we didn't," said Farmer Ruth. "The rest was doing its job. We fixed the part that needed fixing."

The red tractor remained in its bay. The stacked hay was still dry. Across the field, two sheep walked toward their usual grassy patch, entirely uninterested in roofing.

Molly found her blue rope toy on the porch. She carried it to Rex, set it down, and gave him one tail wag.

He gave her one back.

Later, when Rex, Gus, and Jack returned to Brook Road with Aunt Jo, the sun was low over the farm. No truck stood in the yard. The telehandler had gone back to its work base. Mr. Lee had taken his tools home.

From the path by Little Bridge, the friends could see the red barn beyond the green field. Its little side roof was dry and quiet.

Jack closed his notebook. Gus pulled his green cap snugly over his ears. Rex glanced back at Molly on the far porch.

She lifted one white paw in goodbye.

The day had begun with rain.

It ended with a roof doing its simple, good job, and the farm settling peacefully toward evening.
