# American Revolution: continuation plan

**Published manuscripts:** Six substantial, individually playable lectures, starting with the imperial crisis of 1763 and ending with the constitutional founding. A full-length Washington biography and a social-history portrait series are included; every lecture has structured date, geography and source metadata.

## Listening order

1. `001000` — The Empire After Victory: Britain and the Colonies, 1763–1774
2. `002000` — When Protest Became Independence, 1775–1776
3. `003000` — The War Across an Ocean: Saratoga to Yorktown
4. `004000` — George Washington: The Life, the War, and the Republic
5. `005000` — Whose Revolution, Whose Freedom?
6. `006000` — The Republic They Did Not Agree On: Confederation to Bill of Rights

## Additional lectures that would materially deepen the track

- **Benjamin Franklin: printer, scientist and diplomat.** His long life, imperial loyalties, difficult family division, French diplomacy and criticism of slavery merit a full biography rather than a brief portrait.
- **Alexander Hamilton and the fight over the first American state.** Caribbean childhood, service to Washington, political writing, treasury, party conflict, personal contradictions and the collapse of friendship with Aaron Burr.
- **Lafayette, Rochambeau and de Grasse.** Explain the French contribution through the people, finances, logistics and naval decisions that made victory possible.
- **Benedict Arnold and political loyalty.** Military talent, promotion grievances, divided allegiances, André affair and the choices people remembered as betrayal.
- **The war on Native homelands.** The Haudenosaunee split, Sullivan–Clinton expedition, Ohio Valley conflict and treaty-making, anchored in Indigenous political history rather than a patriot/loyalist binary.
- **The Revolution in the South as a civil war.** Backcountry conflict, Black and Indigenous decisions, Nathanael Greene, Cornwallis and local reprisals.
- **Ordinary life through war.** Food shortages, smallpox inoculation, family separation, women's labor and camp support; use first-person documents without invented scenes.
- **The road from British North America to the 1790s party system.** Hamilton's and Jefferson's conflicting models, federal coercive power, western land and the democratic tensions of the early republic.

Priority is given to well-documented human stories and explanatory depth, not a predetermined number of lectures. Periods, geographical paths and source claims should remain specific to each contribution.
