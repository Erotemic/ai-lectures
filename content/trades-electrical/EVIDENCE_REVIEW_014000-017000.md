# Electrical systems: source and safety review for lectures 014000–017000

**Review date:** 2026-11-15. **Document status:** source-audited *educational drafts*; **not** professionally or jurisdictionally reviewed installation, diagnostic or energized-work instructions.

## Coverage and method

Four newly authored manuscripts cover arc-flash risk, isolation and stored energy, human factors, and the interpretation of diagrams. Claim records reside in the corresponding `metadata/<id>.json` files; the same claim IDs appear in each manuscript's non-spoken source map. `metadata/sources.json` gives the original author or sponsoring technical committee, publication identity, exact scoped location where available, and access limitations.

Verification consisted of inspecting official public regulatory text and publication scopes, reading relevant HSE government guidance, and visually inspecting relevant pages of HSE HSG85 (2013, pp. 21–22), HSG48 (1999, pp. 13–15), and DOE-HDBK-1016/1-93 (Module 1 pp. 1, 8 and the 1993 front matter). Source maps distinguish a full public regulation, selected PDF pages, and a publisher catalog entry. Two URLs hosted by IEEE or two webpages authored by HSE are not treated as independent authorship merely because the URLs differ.

## Claim-specific cautions likely to matter in future editorial work

1. **Model applicability matters as much as model credibility.** IEEE 1584-2018's published scope describes specified 208 V–15 kV three-phase AC equipment and does not supply PPE recommendations or a blanket method for single-phase home equipment. Do not publish a residential incident-energy calculation merely by substituting panel voltage into a three-phase model. A competent reviewer must confirm the actual model, system and protective-device inputs.
2. **An operator command is not isolation evidence.** OSHA §1910.333(b)(2)(ii)(B) excludes ordinary push buttons, selector switches and interlocks as sole isolation means in its covered circumstances. HSE HSG85 separately discusses identification, disconnection, secure isolation and proving dead; its pp. 21–22 were inspected. Both are independently authored institutional lines about workplace electrical safety, but they are not identical national rules.
3. **Testing and stored energy belong to qualified field practice.** HSG85 p. 22 does **not** recommend relying on a non-contact volt stick as the sole dead test, and OSHA §1910.333(b)(2)(iv) specifies a qualified-person verification requirement. Do not turn this into a nonqualified homeowner's instrument sequence.
4. **Human-factors explanations are attributable, not generic folklore.** The job/individual/organization framing and slips/lapses/mistakes taxonomy are linked to HSE's HSG48, with pages and edition identified. Avoid presenting an HSE summary and HSG48 as two independent validations of the same claim. The NIOSH hierarchy is similarly attributed to the agency's published framework, not an invented individual originator.
5. **Electrical-symbol catalogs establish scope, not the meaning of every glyph.** IEC 60617 is maintained by IEC TC 3 and is a database; its published catalog record establishes the areas covered. IEC 61082-1 addresses document presentation. An old IEEE/ANSI 315 print is historically relevant, but IEEE lists its standard inactive-reserved. A drawing legend and actual revision govern reading a particular plan.
6. **A nominally low voltage is not zero arc hazard.** OSHA emphasizes serious low-voltage arc-flash exposure. The exposure at an individual installation still cannot be inferred from voltage alone; neither OSHA's overview nor IEEE's catalog abstract can certify the risk of a specific panel.
7. **Worked examples are explicitly fictional.** The 4 kW × time calculation is a constant-power arithmetic model, not an IEEE 1584 implementation. The desk-only block diagrams intentionally omit buildable installation detail. Neither yields installation instructions, PPE ratings or verified isolation states.

## Publication and change-control gates

- An independent licensed/qualified electrician or electrical engineer should review arc-risk mechanisms, breaker/arc interaction and the assumptions of the isolation discussion before promoting to practical guidance. Electrical field work is subject to actual authorization and competent supervision appropriate to jurisdiction and task.
- Have a human-factors specialist or competent safety reviewer examine the real-world claims about procedure quality, interruptions, independent review and error taxonomies if the lecture will be used for training on a real site.
- Obtain the complete current normative text, governing jurisdiction/edition/amendments, and product-specific documentation before describing a clause as legally required in a particular building or promoting a diagram to installation instruction. NFPA 70E is a workplace standard; NEC, IEC 60364 and locally enacted rules have separate scopes.
- Recheck standards editions, active status and agency documents on future publication dates. The public NFPA listing indicated 2027 as the current NFPA 70E edition on the check date; this is not a finding about what a particular state has adopted.
- Narration length and word count are descriptive only. There is **no ten-minute target or word-count quota**. Proofreading must prioritize clarity, completeness and safety, not an arbitrary runtime.

**Verification limitations:** This review checked cited public text and selected standard abstracts, not the full proprietary standards, independent arc-flash calculations, particular equipment, an installation site, or the professional competence of any person. Machine-readable metadata and a successful lecture inventory cannot establish physical safety or legal permission.
