# Lie Groups, Lie Algebras, and Representation Theory

Six complete audio-first lectures bridge linear algebra, abstract algebra and differential geometry. The route starts from explicit matrix symmetries, derives Lie brackets and group actions, then builds continuous representations through examples of SU(2) and SU(3). It complements, rather than replaces, finite-group representation theory in `abstract-algebra:013000` and vector-field geometry in `topology-geometry:008000`.

## Listening order

- `lie-groups-representation-theory:001000` — **When Symmetry Moves Continuously**: Derives the infinitesimal generators of rotation, special linear and special unitary matrix groups and explains the local/global distinction.
- `lie-groups-representation-theory:002000` — **Why Tiny Rotations Do Not Commute**: Calculates commutators of infinitesimal transformations, derives the leading group-commutator and BCH terms, and explains why topology constrains integration.
- `lie-groups-representation-theory:003000` — **A Sphere as a Space of Symmetries**: Models the sphere as SO(3)/SO(2), computes orbit-stabilizer tangent dimensions, and derives the adjoint and isotropy representations.
- `lie-groups-representation-theory:004000` — **One Symmetry, Many Linear Worlds**: Connects continuous group representations to Lie-algebra operators, integer circle weights, Haar averaging, irreducibility and Schur’s lemma.
- `lie-groups-representation-theory:005000` — **The Ladder Inside SU(2)**: Constructs the polynomial representations of SU(2), proves the sl-two highest-weight ladder, decomposes tensor products, and distinguishes descent to SO(3).
- `lie-groups-representation-theory:006000` — **Roots, Weights, and the Triangle of SU(3)**: Computes the A2 root system of sl-three, Weyl permutations, fundamental weights, highest-weight dimensions and the 3-tensor-3 decomposition.

## Learning routes

The default route contains all six lectures. `geometry-of-symmetry` focuses on matrix groups and homogeneous spaces; `irreducibles-and-weights` develops the continuous representation theory; and `highest-weight-path` is a shorter option for listeners who know the geometry already.

Relevant older lectures: `linear-algebra:001000`, `linear-algebra:006000`, `abstract-algebra:001000`, `abstract-algebra:008000`, `abstract-algebra:013000`, and `topology-geometry:008000`.

## Mathematical boundaries

- Matrix Lie groups are used to avoid demanding a complete manifold theory at the outset. The broadest Lie-group theorems require explicit connectedness, compactness, or simply-connectedness hypotheses.
- Differentiating a group representation produces a Lie-algebra representation; integrating in the reverse direction can require global compatibility.
- Complete reducibility is proved for finite-dimensional complex representations of compact groups, not claimed for all noncompact groups.
- The SU(2) module classification is derived through the highest-weight ladder; the SU(3) highest-weight and Weyl dimension results are stated as theorems with their standard hypotheses, while the root and tensor-product computations are worked out.
- Every manuscript has a single spoken region and a separate exact mathematical companion. No binary media are included.
