## Foundation-first continuation

The new route is a worked alternative to the short-topic consolidation in `001000` and `004000`. These originals remain unchanged. A listener needs only basic single-variable calculus for the first three new lectures; the fourth adds familiar trigonometry and introduces second-order initial data. The new route builds explicit solution checks and counterexamples before higher-dimensional dynamics and PDE theory.

## Fourier and periodic-forcing foundation bridge

The `051000`–`054000` route starts from Fourier coefficients and orthogonality, explains projection and convergence, then solves first- and second-order linear ODEs under periodic forcing. It supplies worked calculations, initial-data adjustments and failure cases before introducing higher PDE modes or transfer-function formalisms. The earlier manuscripts and survey remain unchanged.


## Laplace transform foundation bridge

The `055000`–`058000` route derives the one-sided transform directly from its integral, then shows how initial values appear as boundary terms, how switches and impulses affect solutions, and why the causal convolution integral becomes multiplication in the transform domain. Each derivation is checked against the original time-domain ODE. The route builds on first-order ODEs and can be followed independently of the Fourier route, although the two connect through harmonic transfer functions. There is no PDE prerequisite.

## Two-dimensional linear systems foundation bridge

The `059000`–`062000` route derives constant-coefficient two-dimensional systems from scalar ODEs, constructs eigenvector modes and phase portraits, obtains real solutions from complex eigenvalues, and completes the method with generalized eigenvectors and matrix exponentials. The system is solved both in a homogeneous and a forced example. These are worked foundations: no nonlinear stability theorem or PDE prerequisite is assumed. The original consolidated survey and other foundation routes are not modified.

## Numerical time-stepping foundation bridge

The `063000`–`066000` route begins with tangent-line integration and explicit midpoint/Heun calculations, constructs classical RK4, proves a first-order global Euler estimate under Lipschitz hypotheses, and shows the stability failure of forward Euler on a rapidly decaying mode. The sequence distinguishes local defects, fixed-time convergence, and absolute stability. The earlier 29 manuscripts and all pre-existing listening routes remain intact. No advanced PDE prerequisites are needed.

## Elementary boundary-value and Fourier-mode foundation bridge

The `067000`–`070000` route distinguishes endpoint constraints from initial data, solves compatible and incompatible Neumann examples, derives fixed/mixed/free eigenfunctions and resonant forcing, then constructs the heat and wave equations from conservation and force balance. It calculates an exact parabolic heat-profile sine series and a plucked-string triangular sine series, checks decay and energy conservation, and requires no advanced PDE or function-space background. The original overview and advanced PDE manuscripts remain unchanged.


## Fourier transform and method-of-lines foundation bridge

The `071000`–`074000` route derives the continuous-frequency transform as a long-period limit of Fourier series, solves the infinite-line heat equation through its Gaussian kernel and convolution, constructs first and second finite differences from Taylor expansions, and turns a bounded-interval heat equation into a tridiagonal ODE system. It checks explicit and implicit Euler against exact eigenmodes, showing how spatial accuracy and time stability are separate questions. The first two lectures can be heard without numerical linear algebra; the final two require only basic matrices and numerical ODE time steps. Previous 37 manuscripts are unchanged.


## Conservation laws and numerical transport foundation bridge

The `075000`–`078000` route derives transport from characteristics and integral conservation, constructs the donor-cell update with a precise CFL condition, quantifies numerical diffusion by a modified equation, compares Lax–Wendroff and monotone upwind methods on a sharp pulse, and builds finite-volume flux balances with a fully worked five-cell inlet example. Smooth-wave and discontinuous-data accuracy are distinguished; shock theory remains a preview rather than a prerequisite. Earlier 41 manuscripts and their metadata are unchanged.


## Sampling, DFT and FFT foundation bridge

The `079000`–`082000` route supplies a concrete sequence from an eight-samples-per-second aliasing calculation, through an exact four-point DFT and its inverse, to spectrum interpretation with phase/window/leakage distinctions and a radix-two FFT derivation. Sampling theorem hypotheses, amplitude normalization, and periodic versus nonperiodic boundary conditions are made explicit. This route connects the Fourier series, continuous Fourier transform, and numerical PDE introductions. All 45 earlier manuscripts and per-lecture metadata are preserved.

It builds directly on the earlier numerical time-stepping and oscillator lectures; all 49 prior manuscripts and per-lecture metadata remain unchanged.

## Time–frequency analysis and spectral-noise foundation bridge

The `087000`–`090000` route constructs an exact two-window STFT, proves the Gaussian time–frequency uncertainty bound, interprets chirps, impulses and Fourier phase using a circular-shift counterexample, and derives one-sided/two-sided white-noise PSD normalization before introducing Welch's averaged periodogram. It distinguishes true temporal resolution from frame-hop spacing, Fourier-grid interpolation from information, and random noise fluctuations from persistent spectral lines. The route follows the sampling/DFT foundations and assumes no advanced PDE theory. All 53 earlier manuscripts and their metadata are preserved.

## Nonlinear conservation, Riemann problems, and MUSCL foundation bridge

The `091000`–`094000` route starts from characteristic steepening in Burgers' equation, derives weak shocks and rarefaction fans, checks the quadratic entropy inequality, and computes the exact Godunov interface flux. A complete periodic four-cell update demonstrates conservative flux cancellation. The closing lecture reconstructs limited piecewise-linear cell states, works a MUSCL–Hancock half-step, and distinguishes total variation, conservation, and time-integration accuracy. This route follows `075000`–`078000`; no advanced shock-stability theory is required. All 57 preceding manuscripts and their individual metadata are retained verbatim.


## Boundary grids, discrete energy, and convergence foundations

The `095000`–`098000` route develops the numerical mathematics of fixed and insulated rod boundaries, derives a nonnegative discrete heat maximum principle and dissipative energy laws, proves a staggered discrete energy identity for the centered wave equation, and combines consistency plus stability into fixed-time convergence bounds. It includes a mass-conserving ghost-node example, a hand-calculated string step, and two measured second-order refinement tables. No functional-analysis results are assumed. All 61 prior manuscripts and their individual metadata are unchanged.
