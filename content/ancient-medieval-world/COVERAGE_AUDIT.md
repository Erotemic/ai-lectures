# Chronological coverage audit — November 2026

## Current collections and the missing bridge

| Existing collection | Current role in chronology | Limit for a continuous history |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep Time (`draft-stages/tts-deep-time`) | Physical and biological deep history, human evolution, farming transitions and methods of prehistoric evidence; 36 indexed lectures at time of this audit | Does not sustain an account of later states, empires, belief systems, and linked trade networks |
| World History (`draft-stages/tts-world-history`) | 110 indexed lectures on individual ancient and later people, literary works, archeological methods, and other historical episodes at time of audit | Many focused subjects, but not a sequential global course covering every period |
| The Making of the Modern World (`draft-stages/tts-modern-world`) | 94 indexed lectures beginning around 1500 CE and continuing into early COVID-19 at time of audit | Starts after many institutions, economic systems, beliefs, and population histories already took shape |
| **This pack**, `ancient-medieval-world` | Indexed 001000–090000 with eight inserted sub-thousand IDs. The first 50 form a chronological bridge through roughly 1500 CE. Forty-eight further lectures deepen histories in Central Asia, the Americas, Africa, and Southeast Asia | **Six introductory units and forty-eight targeted deep dives are written**; additional region-specific and source-level gaps remain |

The temporal boundary between Deep Time and political history is not a universal date: agriculture, writing, urbanism, and state formation developed at different times in different parts of the world. Our approximate bridge is **3500 BCE–1500 CE**, not a claim that all regions entered the same historical stage together.

## Targeted coverage added in this installment

- **Central Asia:** 051000–052000 distinguish steppe pastoral empires from Sogdian urban and family histories. The Sogdian lecture names the ancient-language letters, their findspot, the modern translator Nicholas Sims-Williams and the limits of preserving one lost packet. Other Central Asian languages and periods still need treatment.
- **Indigenous North America:** 053000–054000 cover Cahokia's urban and agricultural networks and Chaco's regional great-house system. They explicitly distinguish archaeology, working hypotheses, and descendant community histories. Many North American regions remain unaddressed.
- **Indigenous Caribbean:** 055000 treats maritime movement, ceremonial landscapes, Indigenous arts, pre-contact genetics and the biases of early colonial writing. This is a case study, not a survey of every island.
- **African social and regional history:** 056000 tests the centralized-state assumption using Djenné-Jeno's excavated urban complex; 057000 considers Lake Chad and Kanem–Bornu using regional archaeological questions as well as the Arabic geographers. Neither substitutes for a full history of women, kinship, or rural eastern African polities.
- **Southeast Asian countryside:** 058000 investigates Angkor's dispersed settlement and agriculture through lidar, Khmer inscriptions, population models and excavation rather than monuments alone. Comparative work beyond the Khmer heartland remains needed.

The initial fifty-lecture chronological sequence is unchanged. The additional regional lectures are deep dives, not new chronological eras and not proof that each geographic region is now covered comprehensively.

## Second group of regional and documentary deep dives (059000–066000)

- **Great Lakes and Northeast:** 059000 distinguishes indigenous copperworking, changing lakeshores, fishery networks, and regionally different transitions toward maize and village life. 060000 separates physical longhouses, Haudenosaunee constitutional knowledge, Wendat archaeology, and the disputed academic chronology of confederation. These are cases, not a comprehensive map of living nations or their complete precolonial histories.
- **Northwest Coast:** 061000 connects Kwakwaka'wakw transmitted clam-garden knowledge with archaeological dating and recent fish-trap research; the age of one studied garden cannot be generalized to every community. Relations of access and authority require Indigenous historical interpretation.
- **Nubian families and women:** 062000 uses identified Old Nubian texts, modern translations, and Maryam's Arabic court dossier to examine legal capacity and property. The most visible actors are disproportionately those documented by institutions; legal capacity cannot be extrapolated equally to every person.
- **Swahili households:** 063000 examines Songo Mnara's rooms, open spaces, plants, coins, and foodways through specialist site archaeology, distinct from the broader port-and-trade narrative in 036000.
- **South and Southeast Asian local systems:** 064000 investigates Bagan's donations, inscriptions, labor and landscape; 065000 examines Java's religious monuments, sima land/tax-status texts and Trowulan settlement remains. Archaeological and literary sources are kept distinct.
- **Central Asian documentary life:** 066000 follows secular and religious texts in Turfan, separating manuscript languages, difficult dates, and the provenance of modern collections. This does not replace region-wide histories of other Central Asian societies.

The new content does not claim equal research depth across periods: some evidence is based on public abstracts of specialist books and articles, while other references give accessible primary translations or full peer-reviewed methods and field results. Source records identify these differences. For future deep dives, prioritize material where direct field reports or primary-language editions can be read and checked in full.


## Third group of evidence-based regional histories (067000–074000)

- **Southeastern Woodlands and the Plains:** 067000 studies Moundville on the Black Warrior River from a fortified population center to a ceremonial place, with the University of Alabama's descendant-collaborative interpretation. 068000 studies pre-1500 Middle Missouri farming and bison-hunting economies and explicitly distinguishes early archaeological sites from historically documented Mandan, Hidatsa and Arikara villages of later centuries.
- **Arctic:** 069000 treats Inuit ancestral movement, diverse coastal adaptations, changing ice conditions, and the distinction between archaeological category names and living community identities. Its date ranges vary across sites.
- **Africa and household economy:** 070000 examines rock-cut architecture, land grants and field labor in medieval Ethiopian highlands. 071000 uses named fragments from the Cairo Geniza to study property, marriage, travel and credit. The latter is specifically a selective Jewish documentary corpus within medieval Egypt, not a population census.
- **Southeast Asia:** 072000 separates Dvaravati Buddhist art from evidence of a unified political state, while 073000 treats Cham river valleys and inscription-supported political claims beyond a single fixed map of Champa. These subjects particularly need future domestic archaeology and locally edited epigraphy.
- **Inner Asian manuscript worlds:** 074000 uses identifiable Chinese and Tibetan Dunhuang documents for scribal labor, local associations and coercive transactions, making both source contents and the colonial-era dispersion of Cave 17 manuscripts explicit.

**Status does not equal verified exhaustive coverage.** Some source records are based on scholarly abstracts rather than full papers; individual dated primary documents and accessible institutional manuscript catalogs receive priority where available. None of the eight lectures changes the dates of the chronological bridge ending around 1500 CE.

## Fourth group of regional and documentary histories (075000-082000)

- **South Asia beyond the survey dynasties:** 075000 reads actual western Deccan cave donation inscriptions alongside scholarly comparisons of rulers and local patrons. 076000 uses the 919/921 Uttaramerur rules to analyze restricted committee membership and the limits of labels such as democracy. 077000 reads a named Nalanda copper plate and the archaeology of Nalanda and Somapura to connect patronage to village revenue and monastic labor.
- **Great Basin and Southwest:** 078000 treats wetland artifacts and Fremont material patterns as histories of flexible subsistence rather than a failed path to cities. 079000 connects ancient irrigation systems to the continuing Oodham historical account and separates archaeological category from community identity. 080000 follows thirteenth-century Mesa Verde movement to Pueblo communities, distinguishing local Sand Canyon violence from region-wide causation.
- **Korean provincial records:** 081000 uses the Silla village registers to examine age, households, fields, animals, and trees. Scholarly disagreements between proposed dates including 695, 755 and 815 CE are represented explicitly.
- **Rural western Central Asian documents:** 082000 uses the Oxford inventory of Bactrian parchments, Nicholas Sims-Williams's editorial work and scholarship on slavery in contracts. The location of collection and the relationship of an edited document to an excavated archive are kept separate.

Sources include accessible primary-source descriptions, scholar-edited inventories, community-authored histories and peer-reviewed abstract-only specialist work. Evidence metadata records which access level applies. Existing 001000-074000 manuscripts and paragraph metadata are not revised by this installment. The new IDs do not extend the chronological end date of roughly 1500 CE.

## Fifth group of regional and documentary histories (083000-090000)

- **Tamil-speaking worlds:** 083000 contrasts genre conventions in the Sangam corpus with potsherd inscriptions and site archaeology. 084000 centers the 1088 Barus guild inscription while keeping Sumatra’s forest-product workers, port residents, and other maritime groups visible.
- **Western North America:** 085000 treats Indigenous California’s varied oak stewardship and Chumash maritime tools without presenting one group as representative of the whole region. 086000 pairs long-term occupation and fisheries at Celilo with Indigenous testimony and the evidence of camas-root grounds, distinguishing precolonial institutions from nineteenth-century documentation and the 1957 flooding.
- **Korean peninsula and adjoining northeast Asia:** 087000 reads Balhae through royal women’s epitaphs and provincial archaeology, separating an ancient plural polity from modern nationalist categories. 088000 examines the Goryeo Tripitaka Koreana as a material and scholarly project, without pretending the surviving blocks constitute a ledger of craft labor.
- **Central Asian archives:** 089000 analyzes Mount Mugh’s excavated documents, including the named Sogdian marriage agreement, while 090000 studies the edited May 1011 Bamiyan debt acknowledgment and acquired Afghan Genizah materials. Their different provenience allows a direct lesson in documentary bias.

Some specialist scholarship was available only as public abstracts or excerpts, indicated in `metadata/sources.json`. The Sangam UNESCO legacy PDF sometimes redirects, so its access is described as excerpt-level; it should not be advertised as a newly consulted complete critical edition. No changes are made to 001000-082000 manuscripts or their paragraph evidence files. The chronological endpoint remains approximately 1500 CE.

## Cross-cutting deficits to investigate

**History of ordinary people.** Household organization, farmers' and craftspeople's work, bondage and unfreedom, gender and family, migration and displaced populations should be part of political history rather than appended after rulers and battles.

**Evidence and attribution.** Identify whose voices survive and why: funerary inscriptions, tax documents, missionary and traveler accounts, excavated settlement patterns, oral traditions, literary works, translated chronicles. Distinguish original evidence, later reconstruction, and modern historical debates. Where available, add original discoverers, document editors, translators, and field archaeologists to source records rather than attributing findings only to a museum or book publisher.

**Regional balance without quotas.** Add the regions required to explain consequential historical developments, including Africa, South and Southeast Asia, East Asia, Central Asia, the Americas, and the Pacific. Do not treat Europe as the default setting or assume contact where none is established.

**Chronological intersections.** Readers should understand which developments occurred simultaneously and which were causally connected. Parallel chronology is not the same as a single unified global economy.

**Existing material should be reused conceptually.** Where the World History collection already contains a full lecture on Gilgamesh, Fu Hao, Hatshepsut, Mansa Musa, or early archaeological evidence, a bridging lecture should give the necessary historical setting and refer readers to the specialist work rather than duplicate the whole account.

## Status and next action

Ninety lectures are indexed and contain paragraph-level evidence mapping: the original fifty-lecture chronological bridge plus forty focused regional and documentary deep dives. The planned **introductory chronological bridge** from approximately 3500 BCE to 1500 CE is written, but it is not comprehensive world history. Prioritize the remaining regional and social-history gaps, original-language evidence, specialist archaeology, and explicit links to existing specialist tracks. A future coverage audit should distinguish manuscript existence, evidentiary quality, and depth of teaching instead of treating the presence of a topic as proof that it has been mastered.
