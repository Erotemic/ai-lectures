# Stargate SG-1: proposed lecture map

**Planning status:** Ten complete manuscripts are indexed in `track.json`: `001000`, `005000`–`009000`, and `017000`–`020000`. All other IDs below remain proposals, not manuscripts or catalog records. `001000`–`004000` form the planned essentials route, with only `001000` published so far; the remaining essays are independently listenable. The final retrospective is a deliberate ending, not a requirement to cover every episode. IDs increase by 1,000 for manuscript stability; writing and listening order can differ.

## Published manuscripts (four batches)

- **`001000` — From a Film to a Universe: SG-1 Opens the Gate.** A complete, accessible film-to-pilot essay; indexed in the Essentials and Every Lecture collections.
- **`005000` — Jack O'Neill Does Not Take the Universe Seriously.** A complete, spoiler-labeled fan essay about Anderson's performance, leadership, grief, humor, and command; featured at the beginning of the main fan route.
- **`006000` — Daniel Jackson Keeps Asking the Wrong Question.** A character essay about the interpretive and ethical work of his curiosity, with *Scorched Earth*, Chaka, Sha’re, ascension and the Ori.
- **`007000` — Samantha Carter Makes the Impossible Practical.** A character essay about science as action, power, family, selfhood, and the lasting cost of technical success, from *Solitudes* to *Unending*.
- **`008000` — Teal'c's Freedom Is Not a Single Defection.** An extended story of Bra'tac, Teal'c's family, Jaffa resistance, physical dependence, and the move toward elected government on Dakara.
- **`009000` — Hammond, Fraiser, and the People Who Keep the Gate Open.** A study of command, medical care, Cassandra, recurring technical staff and institutional ethics.
- **`017000` — The Fifth Race: When the Galaxy Gets Older.** A close reading of the Ancient archive, Jack’s language loss, the eight-chevron gate, the Asgard, and a possibility of interspecies fellowship.
- **`018000` — Window of Opportunity: The Joke That Ends in Grief.** Close analysis of editing, physical comedy, loop mechanics, credited production decisions, and the encounter with Malikai.
- **`019000` — Heroes: Who Gets to Tell the Story of a Death?** A close reading of the documentary framing, Janet's death and the responsibility of recording.
- **`020000` — Lost City: When a Weekly Adventure Became a Blockbuster.** The Ancient quest, planetary defense, feature-film origins and human costs of an enormous finale.

These are complete transcripts with source notes, selected claim-level anchors and measured spoken lengths. The tables below preserve the original ID map; rows for published manuscripts are already complete, and the other entries remain briefs.

## C. Villains, freedom, and dangerous alliances

| Proposed ID | Working title | Primary question and scene anchors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `011000` | **Apophis, Ba'al, and the Art of Playing God** | Why are the Goa'uld such entertaining villains, and what makes their godhood a system of terror rather than a costume? Performance, intimidation, dynastic rivalry, technology and the economics of obedience; choose sharply observed scenes over a System Lords census. |
| `012000` | **The Tok'ra, the Jaffa, and the Cost of an Ally** | Shared enemies do not create shared ethics. | `013000` | **The Replicators: An Enemy That Cannot Stop Eating** | The shift from theatrical gods to indifferent self-replication. How do the Replicators force new ways to think about engineering, intelligence, escalation and enemyhood? Read *Small Victories*, *Menace* and later human-form stories without flattening them into a present-day AI warning. |
| `014000` | **The Nox, The Other Side, and the Cost of Helping** | The show's best ethical discomfort: what if Earth is wrong about whom to rescue or what kind of power to use? Compare pacifism, intervention and the slow revelation of political facts. Give opponents intelligible reasons where the episodes provide them, but do not manufacture a false moral balance. |
| `015000` | **Who Is Allowed to Know About the Stargate?** | A secret war involving other planets is also a constitutional and international problem. Examine Hammond, civilian officials, Kinsey, the NID, rival states and the machinery of disclosure; use *Disclosure* and *Inauguration* as promising anchors. |
| `016000` | **The Ori: What Happens When the Gods Are Not Frauds?** | The late-series threat rearranges the old problem of worship, power and evidence. Analyze the Ori's coercive religion, the Arthurian adventure frame, Adria and the burden on the changed team. Distinguish the story's treatment of manipulation from any sweeping claim about real-world faiths. |

## E. The craft and history of a ten-season show

| Proposed ID | Working title | Primary question and evidence to gather |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `021000` | **The Stargate Is a Machine for Plot** | Why does the gate's physical grammar make better stories than unrestricted teleportation? The one-way journey, dialing, power, iris, uncertainty and terrifying edge cases. Use *Solitudes*, *A Matter of Time* and an alternate-future story to show rules enabling suspense; verify each technical claim against the episodes. |
| `022000` | **When Earth Stops Being the Underdog** | The road from soldiers with an iris and small arms to ships and inherited technology. How does competence change the series' power balance, and when do technological victories create a harder moral burden? Compare early desperation with later fleet-era decisions. |
| `023000` | **Wormhole X-Treme, 200, and the Show That Could Laugh at Itself** | The self-parody of *Wormhole X-Treme!* and *200*, plus smaller performance gags. | `024000` | **Making a Galaxy out of Vancouver** | Practical sets, gate-room geography, costume and creature design, Canadian production, recurring guest casts, visual effects and the changing economics of television. Research credited craftspeople and verified production accounts; don't invent budget numbers or behind-the-scenes anecdotes. |
| `025000` | **Ascension and the Price of Refusing to Intervene** | Daniel's ascent and the Ancients' rules of noninterference complicate a show built on helping strangers. Examine *Meridian*, *Full Circle* and later Ancient/Anubis choices, drawing a clear distinction between a story-world doctrine and the lecturer's moral analysis. |
| `026000` | **After the Last Dial: Two Films and a Bigger Franchise** | What *The Ark of Truth* actually resolves, what *Continuum* revisits, and how *Atlantis* makes the earlier show's change of scale visible. ## F. Historical conclusion

**`027000` — *A Decade Through the Gate: Stargate SG-1 from 1994 to 2008*.** An extended, self-contained concluding essay, long enough to trace the film-to-television adaptation, Showtime-to-Sci Fi years, the core quartet's growth, the accumulation of Earth power and mythological history, *Lost City*, the mid-series and late-series handoffs, the Ori period, the 2007 finale and the 2008 films. It must also confront the show's weaker stories, repeated cultural shortcuts, shifting plausibility and the problem of keeping exploration alive after acquiring overwhelming power. This is not a season-by-season episode guide: it should narrate how the *kind of show* SG-1 could be changed across ten years and why its central relationships endure. Write it near the end to avoid repeating the other essays.

## Editorial sequencing rather than a production quota

**Completed:** Four batches have produced the film-to-series opening (`001000`), five character and SGC personnel essays (`005000`–`009000`), and four episode close readings (`017000`–`020000`). The fan route begins with these self-contained essays; the Essentials route remains a separate introduction.

**Next areas:** The remaining accessible foundations (`002000`–`004000`), or focused essays on the Goa’uld, the Jaffa, intervention, and the SGC’s changing political position (`010000`–`016000`). Rotate subjects according to the strongest manuscript, not ID order.

**Middle passes:** Rotate between characters, ethically difficult episodes, the villains, comedy and technical production. Do not spend an entire batch reciting four seasons of plot. The film-follow-up and long capstone belong late in the track, after more specific essays have had room to develop their own arguments.

**Writing briefs requiring special attention:**

- In the completed `019000`, the question of who controls the film footage connects the documentary form to the way military and medical labor becomes visible. An excellent episode permits an excellent lecture, but the essay should make its own argument, supported by episode viewing and production accounts.
- In `024000`, research crew interviews and credits before pronouncing any production anecdote fact. Manufacturing a familiar-looking alien village from practical constraints is an interesting production question, not a license to invent a financial explanation.
