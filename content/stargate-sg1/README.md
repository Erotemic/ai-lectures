## The promise

An audio-essay series for people who enjoy *Stargate SG-1* and want to spend more time thinking about why it works. The primary pleasures are the characters' conversations and choices, the excitement of the next address, excellent self-contained episodes, the patient construction of a galaxy, the show's willingness to be funny, and the political and moral consequences of Earth's growing power. Readers who know only the premise can join without a complete rewatch.

This is **not** a synopsis of 214 television episodes. Plot summaries supply the minimum necessary setup for an argument about a character, an episode, a creative choice, a production constraint, or the series' development. A lecture should have something to say, tell an enjoyable story, consider alternatives when they improve the reading, and finish its thought.

The 1997–2007 television series is the center. The 1994 film supplies its origins. *The Ark of Truth* and *Continuum* (both 2008) supply necessary closure or counterpoint. *Atlantis*, *Universe*, and other franchise continuations enter only when they illuminate SG-1; none requires a parallel comprehensive franchise survey. The planned extended capstone ends this finite series.

## Editorial personality

**What to lean into:** Jack's humor as a tool of leadership rather than proof he lacks seriousness; Daniel's curiosity and scruples; Carter's intelligence as dramatic action, not technobabble; Teal'c's many-year political and familial struggle; Hammond and Fraiser as adults making costly decisions. Enjoy the show's confidence in friendship, practical work, and hope, while recognizing the damage that can follow from intervention, secrecy, mistaken certainty, and a new weapon.

**What to examine critically:** The series' deployment of real-world myths and religions, its occasional treatment of other cultures as puzzles awaiting rescue, its optimistic assumptions about the Stargate program, its gender and military premises, and the enormous shift from vulnerable explorers to a powerful Earth. Criticism should grow out of specific episodes and decisions, not imported slogans. Equally, the show should be allowed to be adventurous, silly, exciting, and emotionally direct.

**What not to do:** An episode ranking without argument, a lore encyclopedia, episode-by-episode recap, repeat lectures about the same joke, fan theories presented as production facts, claims that an actor or writer intended something without evidence, or a policy that every essay must be the same length. A scene is worth lingering over when its writing, acting, camera placement, staging, or emotional turn rewards that attention.

## Sources and production context

Treat **on-screen events** as primary story evidence; separately record **broadcast dates, credits, script history, and production claims** from reliable records, contemporary interviews, and production testimony. Avoid relying on fan discussion alone for historical attribution; use it for documented reception and rival readings. Later creator recollections are testimony, not certainty about every production decision.

Starting references (research leads, not a substitute for claim-level verification when writing):

- [Amazon MGM's franchise overview](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/entertainment/stargate-franchise-amazon-mgm-studios-prime-video): series, film, and 2008 follow-up films.
- [GateWorld's SG-1 episode guide](https://www.gateworld.net/sg1/): chronology, episode credits, contemporary reception, and links to production notes.
- [Richard Dean Anderson's SG-1 series information](https://rdanderson.com/stargate/seriesinfo/index.htm): main cast, creative personnel, Showtime/Sci Fi transition.
- [GateWorld's “Window of Opportunity” record](https://www.gateworld.net/sg1/s4/window-of-opportunity/): episode credits and premise.
- [GateWorld's “Heroes, Part 2” production record](https://www.gateworld.net/sg1/s7/heroes-part-2/): credits and sourced production testimony. Attribute disputed decisions precisely.
- [Interview with Brad Wright, 2006](https://www.gateworld.net/news/2006/07/wright-on-target/): franchise and production context.

Completed lectures use the repository's spoken markers, `metadata/NNNNNN.json` claim anchors, `metadata/sources.json`, measured words and durations, and carefully authored subject tags. Use original air dates as **work chronology** and do not confuse the fictional setting with filming in British Columbia. Proper nouns and pronunciation should be checked for listening clarity. Do not include television footage, scores, screenshots, publicity photos, or other binary assets in content overlays.
