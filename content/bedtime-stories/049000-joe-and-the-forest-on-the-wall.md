# 049000 — Joe and the Forest on the Wall

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original, single-narrator bedtime fiction. **Story shape:** miniature moss ecosystem.

# Joe and the Forest on the Wall

Joe the puppy had a favorite place to sniff in the backyard.

It was beside the old brick wall, where a patch of green grew in the crack between two stones.

Joe sniffed it every morning.

It smelled like damp earth.

He sniffed it every afternoon.

It still smelled like damp earth.

One day Dad came outside and found Joe with his nose pressed against the wall.

“Have you found something?” Dad asked.

“Green,” Joe said.

Dad knelt beside him.

“That green patch is moss,” Dad said. “Shall we look closer?”

Joe pushed his nose closer. His whiskers touched the wall.

“I can't get any closer,” he said.

Dad laughed. He went inside and came back with a magnifying glass.

It was round, with a handle, like a little window on a stick.

Dad held the glass in front of the green patch.

“Look through here,” he said.

Joe looked.

Then he looked again.

Through the glass, the tiny green patch became a forest of little stems.

Each stem had its own tiny leaves. Some stood straight. Some leaned over. Some had droplets of water resting among them.

Joe stepped back in surprise.

“That was on the wall?”

“It still is,” Dad said.

Joe looked without the glass. He saw one fuzzy green patch, no bigger than his paw.

He looked through the glass again.

Now he could see the little leaves.

“It's a forest,” he whispered.

“A very small one,” Dad said.

Joe thought about the real woods beyond the garden. He had walked there with Dad, under tall trees whose branches were high above his head.

In those woods, Joe was the small creature.

Here, next to the brick wall, Joe was enormous.

He imagined taking a walk among the moss stems.

His front paw would be bigger than a whole row of trees.

His tail could sweep across the forest before he even noticed.

He took one careful step backward.

Then something moved.

A small gray woodlouse came out from behind one of the green stems.

It had lots of little legs, and it traveled slowly across the damp stone.

Joe watched through the glass.

The woodlouse stopped beside a bead of water. It walked around the bead and disappeared underneath the moss.

“It has a road!” Joe said.

“Perhaps it has several,” Dad replied.

Joe bent down to sniff where the woodlouse had gone.

Dad steadied the magnifying glass, and they waited.

The woodlouse did not come back.

Joe waited another moment.

Still nothing.

“Maybe it's having lunch,” Joe decided.

“Maybe,” said Dad.

Dad put the glass down on the bench. Joe examined the old wall again.

A small spiderweb shone between two bricks. A dried leaf was tucked under a stone. Above the moss, a very small seed had lodged in a crack.

How many things had been there yesterday, while Joe was busy sniffing?

Joe took a slow walk along the rest of the wall.

He found a feather caught against a flowerpot and three pebbles near the drainpipe.

He also found his lost blue ball behind a watering can.

“My ball!” Joe said. “I wondered where that went!”

He brought the ball to Dad, and they played fetch until the shadows grew long.

Before going indoors, Joe returned to the moss.

Without the magnifying glass, it looked like a small patch of green again.

But Joe knew about the little stems, the water droplets, and the gray visitor with many feet.

He gave the patch plenty of room as he passed.

After supper, Joe curled up on his round dog bed. His tail made one last soft thump against the rug.

He dreamed of a forest so small that a single drop of water could be a pond, and of a woodlouse going home along a secret little path.

The forest was still there on the wall, even when nobody was looking.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Editorial intention:** Joe’s large-puppy viewpoint gives the tiny moss a comic sense of scale, while the reveal of miniature stems and a small creature provides a concrete moment of discovery.

**Optional prompt:** “What looked different when Dad held up the magnifying glass?” It is fine if the child simply listens.

**Background sources:** [National Park Service, Acadia National Park, *Moss*](https://www.nps.gov/acad/learn/nature/moss.htm); [American Academy of Pediatrics, HealthyChildren.org, *How to Share Books with Children 2 and 3 Years Old*](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-2-and-3-Year-Olds.aspx). The first supports a natural phenomenon; the second supports optional shared noticing or discussion. Neither evaluates the original plot or predicts a bedtime or developmental outcome.

**Narrator note:** Keep all dialogue in one clear narrator register; no character voice acting, music, sound effects, or timed narration target is required.
