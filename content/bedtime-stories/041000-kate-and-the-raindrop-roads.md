# Kate and the Raindrop Roads

Kate the hedgehog had planned to play in the garden that afternoon.

She had taken her little basket to the back door. She had even put a biscuit inside, in case she got hungry.

But outside, rain was falling.

Not a fierce rain. Just steady little drops that made the grass shine and the garden path look dark.

Aunt Sue helped Kate hang up her coat again.

“We can go out another time,” she said.

Kate put her biscuit on a plate and took it to the window.

She had been looking at the garden. Now she looked at the glass.

Kate watched the rain make little roads down the glass.

One drop near the top of the window was big and round. Another beside it was very small.

Kate called the big one Round Drop.

She called the little one Little Drop.

Both were on the outside of the window. Kate was warm and dry on the inside.

“Which way will you go?” she asked them.

Round Drop moved first.

It slipped a short way down the glass and stopped.

Little Drop did not move at all.

Round Drop started again.

Down a little.

Stop.

Down a little.

Stop.

Kate followed it with one finger, held close to the inside of the glass.

“It is making a wiggly road,” she said.

Aunt Sue came over with her tea.

“Where does the road go?” she asked.

“We don't know yet.”

Round Drop slid sideways around a tiny speck on the window and joined another drop.

Now it was bigger.

It slipped down twice as far as before.

“Oh!” said Kate. “That road has a fast part.”

Little Drop was still waiting near the top.

Kate watched it for a long moment.

“Maybe that one likes the view.”

She could see the wet apple leaves beyond the window. She could see the wooden fence. She could see a sparrow waiting beneath the fence rail with its feathers puffed up.

Then the small drop met another drop, and together they began to move.

“It's going!” Kate whispered.

Little Drop crept down one narrow line, turned, and slipped into a long wet streak left by another drop.

For a few moments it moved very quickly.

Round Drop was still farther down, but Kate had lost track of which part of the water had belonged to which drop.

“They've all mixed together,” she said.

The window was full of little roads now.

Some curved.

Some went almost straight down.

Some stopped halfway and waited for more rain.

Kate had been watching two drops. Now she could not count all the ones she could see.

Aunt Sue set her tea on the table.

The sparrow shook itself outside and hopped farther beneath the rail.

The rain grew lighter.

Little spaces appeared between the drops, and the garden looked clearer through the glass.

Kate ate her biscuit in small bites. She watched the last long streak slide toward the bottom of the window.

“I think the roads are closing,” she said.

Aunt Sue smiled. “They may open again next time it rains.”

Kate put her empty plate in the kitchen. She washed her paws and changed into her pajamas.

When she returned to the window, only a few drops remained.

The garden path was wet. The leaves were still. The sparrow had gone home.

Kate could not see Round Drop or Little Drop anymore.

But she remembered where their roads had been.

Outside, the rain stopped.

Inside, Aunt Sue drew the curtains.

And Kate went to bed with the soft sound of the last drops falling from the apple leaves into the grass.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** The story invites noticing movement without asking the child to solve a science problem. Raindrops following different paths and joining are ordinary observations; the roads and race are Kate’s imaginative descriptions.

**Optional prompt:** “Did the two drops travel down the same path?” The child may also simply listen; nothing needs to be answered.

**Sources and limits:** [National Association for the Education of Young Children, *Rocking and Rolling: Fostering Curiosity in Infants and Toddlers*](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/yc/dec2020/fostering-curiosity-infants) and [American Academy of Pediatrics, HealthyChildren.org, *How to Share Books with Children 2 and 3 Years Old*](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-2-and-3-Year-Olds.aspx) provide background on shared reading, play, or observing change. These are parent-facing references about reading and observation, not sources for the original plot. No benefit from the specific story has been experimentally evaluated; invented scenes are fiction.

**Single-voice note:** Keep any character speech in the same narrator register. The narrated story needs no music, sound effects, or separate acting voices.
