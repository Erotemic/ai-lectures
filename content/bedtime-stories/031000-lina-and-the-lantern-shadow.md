# 031000 — Lina and the Lantern Shadow

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original fiction for a single narrator. **Story shape:** A moth discovers a playful shadow near a softly lit porch.

# Lina and the Lantern Shadow

Lina was a little moth with pale wings and two soft feelers.

She lived beneath the leaves of a climbing vine.

Her favorite time was the hour when the daylight faded and the garden became blue and silver.

One evening, Lina saw a new light on the porch.

It came from a round paper lantern with a little electric lamp inside.

The lantern hung beneath the porch roof.

It glowed as softly as a peach.

Lina flew over to have a look.

Below the lantern stood a large flowerpot.

Behind the flowerpot was a plain wooden wall.

Lina landed on the edge of the pot.

And there, on the wall, was an enormous moth.

Its wings looked as wide as two dinner plates.

Lina blinked.

The enormous moth blinked too.

Well, its shape changed a little when Lina moved.

Lina lifted her left wing.

The enormous moth lifted a left wing.

Lina lowered it.

The enormous moth lowered it too.

Lina tried both wings at once.

The giant moth spread enormous wings across the wall.

That was a splendid trick.

She took a step closer to the lantern.

The dark wings on the wall grew even larger.

She took a step away.

The wings grew smaller.

Lina moved from one side of the flowerpot to the other.

The big moth went with her.

“Good evening,” Lina said softly.

The shape on the wall did not answer.

But it waved a wing when she waved hers.

Lina thought carefully about that.

She looked at the lamp.

She looked at her own little body.

She looked at the wall.

Then she moved one feeler.

The shadow moved one feeler too.

It was not another moth.

It was her own shape, made big by the light.

She had never seen herself so enormous.

For a little while, Lina played a quiet shadow game.

She stood tall.

The shadow stood tall.

She crouched low.

The shadow crouched low.

She made herself look like a teapot, although she did not know whether a teapot should have wings.

The shadow looked like a very mysterious teapot indeed.

Then a breeze passed through the garden.

The lantern swayed a tiny bit on its string.

The shadow slid gently along the wooden wall.

Lina did not chase it.

She simply watched the soft shape travel back and forth.

The breeze settled.

The lantern stopped moving.

Lina spread her small wings and flew down to the lavender bush beneath the porch.

From there, she could still see the paper lantern glowing.

But the enormous moth was gone from the wall.

Lina did not mind.

She knew where it had come from.

A beetle walked quietly under the lavender leaves.

A thin cloud crossed the moon.

Somewhere beyond the fence, a cricket began its evening song.

Lina tucked her wings together.

The lantern glowed above the porch.

The wooden wall was plain again.

And in the lavender, the small moth rested, just the right size for herself.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** A small observation puzzle about light and shadow grows naturally into a playful sequence of matching movements. The shadow is not a threatening figure. The story makes a physical claim in simplified terms: the light, moth, and wall together produce the shadow; its apparent size changes with position. The fictional moth's teapot pose is deliberately silly. The paper lantern contains a small electric lamp, not a flame, and no craft or insect-handling activity is implied.

**Optional prompt:** “What moved when Lina moved her wing?” A child's answer need not be the scientific explanation.

**Sources and limits:** [NAEYC, *Fostering Curiosity in Infants and Toddlers* (2020)](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/yc/dec2020/fostering-curiosity-infants) supports the practice of noticing changes with young children; [AAP, *Playful Reading Tips* (2023)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/playful-reading-tips-ways-to-engage-your-child-in-the-magic-of-books.aspx) supports open discussion and prediction while reading together. For the **physical shadow idea**, [NASA Science, *Exploring Earth: Bear’s Shadow* (2022)](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/exploring-earth-bears-shadow/) describes family experiments moving a light around an object to create shadows. Those three sources do not evaluate this fictional moth story or establish any learning or sleep effect.

**Single-voice note:** The repeated wing movements are narrated, not acted out. No large or frightening character voice is needed for the shadow.
