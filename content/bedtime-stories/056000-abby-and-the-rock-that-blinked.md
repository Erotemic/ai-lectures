# Abby and the Rock That Blinked

Abby the octopus lived beneath a round rock in a quiet part of the sea.

Outside her door she had arranged an underwater garden with pebbles, shells, and two long green ribbons of seaweed.

One morning her friend Deb the crab came to visit.

Deb was carrying a new shell.

It was pale pink on one side and brown on the other, with a little chipped place near its edge.

“I found something for your garden,” Deb called.

No one answered.

Deb walked around the three pebbles.

“Abby?”

No answer.

She looked under the waving seaweed ribbons.

Still no Abby.

Beside the garden sat a round, brownish rock that Deb could not remember seeing before.

“That's odd,” she said. “I thought there was a flat stone there.”

She leaned toward it.

The rock had a tiny bump on top.

And another bump on the side.

Then the rock blinked.

Deb leaped backward so quickly that she nearly dropped the shell.

“Rocks are not supposed to do that!”

The rock gave a little laugh.

It was Abby.

Abby could change the colors and little bumps of her skin, so she blended into the rocks around her.

She had been sitting quite still beside her garden, watching a little fish go by.

“I was here the whole time,” she said.

“Well,” said Deb, “you were a very convincing rock.”

Abby looked pleased.

“Shall we play a game?”

Deb set the new shell safely beside the white pebble.

“Only if you promise not to turn into the entire sea.”

“I don't think I can do that,” said Abby. “The sea is rather large.”

They played a game of finding.

First Abby slipped beside a patch of sandy bottom. Her skin grew paler and speckled.

Deb looked behind the seaweed.

No Abby.

She looked behind the curly shell.

No Abby.

She stared at the pale patch of sand.

An eye opened.

“There you are!”

Next Abby moved beside a dark rock. Her skin changed again, and little bumps made her outline harder to see.

Deb went right past.

Abby wriggled one arm.

“Found you,” Deb said, turning around.

“You only found my arm.”

“An arm is part of an octopus,” said Deb. “I think that counts.”

They tried a third hiding place.

Abby tucked herself beside the striped pebble.

But this time Deb found her at once.

“How?” Abby asked.

“Because your eye was following me.”

Abby laughed so hard she floated a little way above the bottom.

Deb looked at the gift she had brought.

The pink-and-brown shell was sitting alone by the white pebble.

“I nearly forgot my present,” she said.

Abby held it in one arm and examined the chipped edge.

“It's wonderful. It looks different from every side.”

They chose a place for it where the seaweed ribbons made a little arch.

The pink side faced the garden path. The brown side faced Abby's doorway.

“Will it be a hiding place?” Deb asked.

“No,” said Abby. “It's too small to hide me. But it's a very good place for something beautiful to sit.”

A small fish passed overhead and looked down at the garden.

It saw the striped pebble, the pink-and-brown shell, and the green ribbons moving softly in the water.

It also saw one round rock with an eye.

The fish stopped.

Abby winked.

The fish hurried on, probably with a very interesting story to tell.

By evening Deb had gone home, the garden was quiet, and the ribbons leaned gently with the current.

Abby changed back to her familiar color and settled beneath the round roof of her little house.

She closed one eye, then the other.

For once, there was no need to hide from anything.

The sea rocked her softly toward sleep.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Editorial intention:** The comic mystery comes from real octopus camouflage, while Abby and Deb remain familiar talking animals in an invented scene.

**Optional prompt:** “Which part of the rock moved when Deb looked closely?” A child may simply listen without answering.

**Background sources:** [Smithsonian Ocean, *How Octopuses and Squids Change Color*](https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/how-octopuses-and-squids-change-color); [American Academy of Pediatrics, *Young Children Learn a Lot When They Play*](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/young-children-learn-a-lot-when-they-play.aspx). The first supports a specific science detail; the second supports only optional shared reading or conversation. Neither source evaluates this story or promises a bedtime or developmental effect.

**Narrator note:** Single calm narrator; no voice acting, music, sound effects or timed length requirement.
