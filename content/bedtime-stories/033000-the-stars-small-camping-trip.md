# 033000 — The Stars' Small Camping Trip

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original, single-narrator bedtime fantasy. **Story shape:** Three pretend stars prepare a campsite in the night sky, with a gentle cumulative goodnight.

# The Stars' Small Camping Trip

Once upon a very quiet evening, three little stars decided to go camping.

Not on the ground, where people set up tents.

High above the rooftops, in a make-believe corner of the night sky.

The first star was called Dot.

Dot was small and bright and liked to be prepared.

The second star was called Pop.

Pop liked to see what was around the next cloud.

The third star was called Glow.

Glow was sleepy before the trip had even begun.

They packed a tiny tent made of dark blue night.

They packed three little pillows shaped like clouds.

And Dot brought a small silver spoon, although nobody was quite sure what it was for.

They floated through the sky until they found a wide, peaceful patch between two soft clouds.

“This will do,” said Dot.

Pop looked up.

There was sky above them.

Pop looked down.

There was sky below them too.

That seemed a very good place for a star to camp.

They opened their blue tent.

One corner drifted toward a cloud.

Another corner drifted the other way.

Glow held the middle while Dot and Pop tucked in the sides.

At last the tent hung comfortably between the clouds.

Dot placed the three cloud pillows inside.

Pop set the little silver spoon beside the entrance.

Glow lay down on a pillow immediately.

But camping had one more important part.

Supper.

The stars had brought a bowl of pretend moonberry soup.

It was the color of the evening sky and tasted, in this make-believe world, a little like warm blueberries.

Dot tried to stir it.

The spoon was too small for the bowl.

Pop tried next.

The spoon was still too small.

Glow opened one sleepy eye.

“Maybe it's a tasting spoon,” Glow suggested.

And that turned out to be exactly right.

They each took a small taste.

It was very good soup.

Then they watched a slow white cloud go by.

It looked like a sheep.

A moment later, it looked like a pillow.

After that, it looked like a sheep carrying a pillow.

Pop found that very funny.

Dot smiled.

Glow was almost asleep.

The sky grew darker and softer around them.

Far below, a little town was settling for the night.

Windows glowed warmly and then, one by one, grew dark.

In one home, a lamp went out beside a storybook.

In another, a stuffed bear was tucked under a blanket.

On a quiet street, a cat curled up on a doorstep and made itself into a round, warm shape.

The stars watched without making a sound.

Dot folded the empty soup cloth.

Pop put away the silver spoon.

Glow pulled the edge of the blue tent a little closer.

For a while, the three stars rested together.

Their tiny lights shone through the tent, faint and steady.

Dot thought about the sheep-shaped cloud.

Pop thought about the spoon that had finally found its job.

Glow was thinking of nothing very much at all.

The night breeze moved the cloud pillows only a little.

The town below was quiet now.

The three campers were quiet too.

Dot said goodnight to Pop.

Pop said goodnight to Glow.

Glow gave a very small, sleepy goodnight to both of them.

The blue tent hung peacefully between the clouds.

And from the ground, the three little stars looked like three gentle points of light, resting together in the wide night sky.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** This is deliberately fantastic, with no scientific explanation hidden inside it: stars do not really camp, eat soup, or sleep in tents. The structure moves from a small comic problem to repeated goodnight scenes. It can be enjoyed purely as a sleepy fantasy. Three named characters remain easy to distinguish through narration alone.

**Optional prompt:** “What did Dot bring that turned out to be useful?” If the child is settling to sleep, skip the prompt.

**Sources and limits:** [AAP, *How to Share Books with Children 2 and 3 Years Old* (2021)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-2-and-3-Year-Olds.aspx) recommends shared storytelling and following a child's comments; [AAP, *Playful Reading Tips* (2023)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/playful-reading-tips-ways-to-engage-your-child-in-the-magic-of-books.aspx) discusses imagination and prediction during shared reading. These sources did not evaluate this story, are not astronomy references, and do not establish that its final pages induce sleep.

**Single-voice note:** Keep Dot, Pop, and Glow in the same narrator voice. Their names and small repeated actions do the work that three separate acted voices might otherwise do.
