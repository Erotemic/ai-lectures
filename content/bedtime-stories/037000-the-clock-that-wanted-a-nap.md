# 037000 — The Clock That Wanted a Nap

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original, single-narrator household fantasy. **Story shape:** A clock tries to find a quiet place to rest without ever leaving its wall.

<!-- BEGIN SPOKEN LECTURE -->

# The Clock That Wanted a Nap

There was a little clock on the wall of Lisa's kitchen.

It was round and cream-colored, with a blue circle around the edge.

It had two black hands and twelve neat numbers.

And all day long, it went tick, tock, tick, tock.

In this make-believe story, the little clock had a secret wish.

It wanted a nap.

Not a big nap.

Just a small, cozy, afternoon sort of nap.

But the kitchen was a busy place.

A spoon clinked into a bowl.

A cupboard opened and closed.

Lisa's slippers made soft slapping sounds across the floor.

And the clock kept going.

Tick, tock.

Tick, tock.

It watched the tea towel hanging by the sink.

The tea towel looked wonderfully relaxed.

It wasn't going anywhere.

It wasn't counting anything.

It just hung there, with one corner folded over the other.

The clock wished it could curl up like a tea towel.

But clocks, even pretend sleepy ones, don't have corners to fold.

Then it noticed the small kitchen rug.

The rug was flat and soft.

A very good place for somebody to lie down.

But the clock was high on the wall, and the rug was far below.

The clock imagined stretching out across the rug like a great big round pancake.

That was a funny thought.

A clock-shaped pancake would have twelve little raisins around the edge.

It would also be much too large for Lisa's breakfast plate.

Tick, tock.

Tick, tock.

After supper, the kitchen grew quieter.

Lisa helped Grandpa put the napkins away.

One napkin went into the drawer.

The next napkin went into the drawer.

The last napkin was folded into a tiny triangle, because Lisa liked triangles.

Then Grandpa turned off the bright kitchen light.

A soft lamp in the next room made the doorway glow.

The clock watched the shadows settle.

It listened to Lisa and Grandpa walking down the hall.

One set of footsteps was slow and heavy.

The other was quick and light.

At the bedroom door, the quick footsteps stopped.

Lisa had remembered something.

She padded back into the kitchen and looked up at the clock.

“Goodnight, clock,” she whispered.

Then she went back to Grandpa, and the footsteps disappeared down the hall.

The little clock felt very pleased.

Nobody had ever wished it a goodnight before.

The kitchen was still now.

The tea towel hung without moving.

The rug lay smooth and flat.

The cupboard doors were shut.

The little clock was still on the wall, doing its ordinary clock work.

Tick, tock.

Tick, tock.

But in its make-believe mind, it was dreaming of the softest things in the kitchen.

A folded napkin.

A warm rug.

A tea towel resting on its hook.

And a great big pancake with twelve tiny raisins.

The hands went gently around the face.

The house grew quiet.

And the little clock had the nicest sort of nap a clock could imagine, without moving from its place at all.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** A familiar household sound becomes a gentle piece of nonsense. The clock's feelings and imagined nap are explicitly fantastical; the story doesn't claim clocks sleep. Repeated “tick, tock” lines can give the steady narrator a rhythm without relying on sound effects. There is no moral to extract.

**Optional prompt:** “What did the clock dream about?” A caregiver can also skip the question and leave the clock dreaming.

**Sources and limits:** [AAP, *How to Share Books with Children 2 and 3 Years Old*](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-2-and-3-Year-Olds.aspx) discusses children's enjoyment of repeated words and story-sharing routines. [AAP, *Playful Reading Tips*](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/playful-reading-tips-ways-to-engage-your-child-in-the-magic-of-books.aspx) offers imaginative reading suggestions. The invented clock story is not a studied sleep aid, and the sources do not predict how a TTS rendering will sound.

**Single-voice note:** The repeated sound words should be read naturally in the same voice, not performed as mechanical sound effects.
