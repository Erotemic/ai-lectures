# 028000 — Della and the Daisy Sneeze

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original fiction for a single narrator. **Story shape:** A friendly dragon's improbable sneeze creates a very silly garden party.

<!-- BEGIN SPOKEN LECTURE -->

# Della and the Daisy Sneeze

Della was a small green dragon with round feet and a long, bendy tail.

She lived beside a hill with three trees and a very crooked fence.

Every morning, Della liked to sit on a flat stone and watch the clouds.

But one morning, something felt funny in her nose.

She wrinkled it.

She rubbed it.

Then she gave one little sneeze.

And out came a daisy.

Not a puff of smoke.

Not a spark.

One white daisy with a yellow middle.

It landed between her toes.

Della looked down.

She looked up.

She looked down again.

She had never sneezed a flower before.

She put the daisy in a cup of water on her windowsill.

Then she went to visit her friend Bea, who was setting up a picnic beneath the three trees.

Bea was a round brown bear. She had brought a blue blanket, a basket of pears, and four tiny cakes.

Della helped smooth the blanket.

She put a pear in each corner so the breeze would not blow it away.

Then her nose tickled again.

She turned away from the picnic and gave a second little sneeze.

Three daisies floated down.

One settled on the blanket.

One landed on Bea's head.

And one fell neatly into an empty cup.

Bea looked at the flower on her head.

Della looked at the flower in the cup.

For a moment, neither of them said anything.

Then Bea began to giggle.

Della giggled too.

They put the three daisies together on the blanket.

Soon their friend Pip arrived carrying a plate of sliced apples.

Pip was very small, so he carried the plate with both paws.

Della gave him a place beside the basket.

She reached for an apple slice.

Her nose made the tiniest twitch.

“Oh dear,” she said.

Pip carefully covered the apples with a napkin.

Della turned toward the open grass.

This time, five daisies drifted into the air.

They came down slowly, turning like little white umbrellas.

One rested on a stone.

Two landed near the fence.

And two settled by Della's enormous green feet.

Bea counted them.

Pip counted them again, because counting flowers twice seemed like a good thing to do.

Della was puzzled. She did not feel sick, and her nose had stopped tickling. But now there were daisies all over the picnic place.

Bea made a ring of flowers around the basket.

Pip placed one beside each little cake.

Della chose a daisy to wear tucked behind one ear.

It kept falling out.

She tried her other ear.

It fell out again.

At last she balanced the flower on the end of her tail.

That worked for exactly three steps.

Then the flower slid down her tail and landed on Pip's apple plate.

Everyone laughed.

They ate the pears and apples and the four little cakes.

When the picnic was finished, they gathered the flowers into a little bunch for Bea to take home.

Della carried the empty basket.

Pip carried the blue blanket.

Bea carried the flowers.

As they walked along the crooked fence, Della felt one final, very small tickle.

She stopped and turned toward the grass.

A single daisy floated out and landed in the basket.

Bea smiled.

There was room for one more.

The sun began to sink behind the hill.

Della went home, washed her feet, and climbed into her soft bed.

The first daisy was still in its cup on the windowsill.

Outside, the three trees stood quietly in the evening air.

Della's nose felt perfectly comfortable now.

She watched the daisy for a little while.

Then she closed her eyes.

And not another flower sneezed all night.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** Pure nonsense and friendly surprise. The premise is intentionally impossible: dragons are imaginary, and real sneezes do not produce flowers. The small counting sequence and predictable repetitions give a listener familiar things to notice without turning the picnic into a lesson. No one is embarrassed or punished for a bodily mishap.

**Optional prompt:** “Where did the daisies land?” Or invent a different, silly thing for Della to sneeze, if the child wants to play. No discussion is required at bedtime.

**Sources and limits:** [American Academy of Pediatrics, *Young Children Learn a Lot When They Play* (2022)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/young-children-learn-a-lot-when-they-play.aspx) gives general guidance about imaginative play; [CDC, *Milestones by 3 Years*, “More Parenting Tips and Activities”](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html) includes counting and story conversation. These sources did **not** study this story, endorse a flower-sneeze activity, or demonstrate a benefit from listening to it.

**Single-voice note:** Della, Bea, and Pip are distinguished by names and actions. No character imitation or theatrical sneeze is needed; the narrator can read the daisy arrivals in a steady, amused rhythm.
