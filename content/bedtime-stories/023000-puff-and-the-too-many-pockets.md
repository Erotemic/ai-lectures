# 023000 — Puff and the Too-Many Pockets

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original, single-narrator fiction. **Story shape:** In an invented sky, a cloud tries to find homes for a collection of silly things.

<!-- BEGIN SPOKEN LECTURE -->

# Puff and the Too-Many Pockets

High above a town with red roofs was a little cloud called Puff.

In this particular sky, clouds could have pockets.

Puff had seven.

One on the left.

One on the right.

Two near the middle.

And three little ones that Puff always forgot about until something tickled.

Puff loved finding things that had blown into the sky.

In the first pocket was a yellow feather.

In the second was a tiny paper crown.

In the third was a scrap of blue ribbon.

In the fourth was a cloud-shaped cookie, which was a funny thing for a cloud to own.

Puff had never eaten it.

He mostly liked its shape.

The fifth pocket held a button with four holes.

The sixth held a warm patch of sunlight.

And the seventh was empty.

At least, Puff thought it was empty.

One day a breeze hurried past and dropped a little silver bell into the seventh pocket.

The pocket gave a tiny bounce.

Puff looked down.

There was the bell, shining beside the edge of the pocket.

Now all seven pockets were full.

Puff puffed himself up with pride.

But a cloud with seven full pockets does not glide in a very tidy shape.

He dipped to one side.

Then he dipped to the other.

The ribbon slipped out and waved behind him like a tail.

The paper crown leaned sideways.

The feather tickled.

Puff tried to stand very straight in the sky.

Clouds, however, are not especially good at standing.

A small breeze came by and asked why he was tilted.

Puff explained about the pockets.

The breeze asked if Puff could find somewhere to put a few of his treasures so he wouldn't have to carry them all.

Puff looked at his things.

He really did like them.

Then he noticed a rainbow stretching across the far side of the valley.

It had seven soft bands of color and no pockets at all.

Puff had an idea.

He hung the blue ribbon over the near end of the rainbow, where it fluttered gently.

He rested the paper crown on a small hilltop, which looked very grand with a crown.

He laid the feather on a broad branch, where it might make a fine pillow for someone very small.

The cookie he kept.

A cloud-shaped cookie seemed like something a cloud ought to keep.

Next he found a little gap between two soft patches of cloud and tucked the four-holed button there, like a window.

The patch of sunlight drifted into the open sky and brightened one corner of a sleepy field.

That left only the bell and the cookie.

Puff was feeling lighter already.

But just as he settled comfortably above the red roofs, something tickled him again.

It came from the pocket that had once held the feather.

Puff reached inside.

There was a small, soft yawn.

He had apparently collected it without noticing.

Puff considered putting the yawn on the rainbow.

No. It might fall off.

He considered placing it on the crowned hill.

No. The hill was still awake.

He considered keeping it in his pocket.

Then Puff yawned himself.

The yawn grew larger, floated out of his pocket, and drifted over the town.

One little window went dark.

Then another.

Then a third.

Puff watched the town grow quiet beneath him.

He tucked the cloud-shaped cookie into a comfortable corner of his only full pocket.

The bell rested beside it.

His other pockets hung loose and empty.

That felt pleasant.

A slow breeze carried him toward the last light of evening.

The rainbow became pale.

The crowned hill settled into shadow.

And Puff, with just one pocketful of treasures, drifted gently into sleep.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** A cumulative counting-and-collection joke in an explicitly impossible sky. The cloud's pockets, rainbow shelf, and stored yawn are fantasy, not weather facts. The ending closes the comedy rather than delivering a lesson about possessions or tidiness.

**Optional prompt:** “What funny thing would you put in a cloud's pocket?” Nothing needs to be counted or classified at bedtime.

**Sources and limits:** [CDC, *Milestones by 3 Years* (2026), “Parenting Tips and Activities”](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html) suggests stories, rhymes and counting games as caregiver activities. [American Academy of Pediatrics, *Young Children Learn a Lot When They Play* (2022)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/young-children-learn-a-lot-when-they-play.aspx) describes imaginative play. Neither supports the literal cloud story or proves that listening increases numerical ability or improves sleep.

**Single-voice note:** Seven pockets are described in short groups to avoid a rapid count. Do not attempt a special character voice for the breeze.
