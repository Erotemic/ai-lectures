# Children’s Bedtime Stories (Ages 3–4)

**Category:** Children's Bedtime Stories  
**Intended age range:** **3–4 years**; stories are primarily written for **a child aged three**.  
**Format:** Original standalone audio-first fiction, written for **one calm narrator**.  
**Content status:** Pilot collection for evaluating narrated story quality; no audio or voice samples included.

This is a separate **Agent E** source pack at `content/bedtime-stories/`.
It does not change any other agent's work, shared server files, or global registries.
The runtime title includes the age range so that the audience is visible in the
course browser; each story sidecar repeats genre and age-range metadata.

## Parents' rationale and citations (004000–057000)

[**A quiet guide for parents and caregivers**](PARENTS_GUIDE.md) explains the
earlier developmental themes and newer play-first stories, gives optional
conversation prompts, and links to age-relevant child-development guidance.
Each story from 004000 onward has an unspoken
**For parents and caregivers** section with its own citations, and the
corresponding metadata sidecar contains `rationale_citations` mapping exact
story passages to source IDs and scope notes. Lighthearted stories have no
assigned moral or learning outcome; citations describe related play guidance,
not benefits attributed to a specific plot. `metadata/sources.json` stores
reference titles, institutional authorship, publication dates, sections, and
limitations. These references support **editorial approaches**, not claims
that our stories have been tested or cause measured learning outcomes.

The original three pilot stories (001000–003000) remain unchanged and are
original fiction without claimed developmental intervention effects.

The **fifty-seven** stories require no character-specific synthesized voices: dialogue
is reported by the narrator, or is absent. The stories are not required to
have a particular duration. They stand alone even though one includes a small
cross-story reference.

## Narration and attribution

The exact TTS text is between the `BEGIN SPOKEN LECTURE` and `END SPOKEN
LECTURE` markers. Author companions below the end marker give reading notes
and explain what the story is trying to accomplish. They are **not** spoken.

All fifty-seven manuscripts are newly authored original fiction. They do not adapt
published plots or reuse external story text. `metadata/sources.json` holds
parent-facing developmental and play references for 004000–039000 only. Original
fictional descriptions and bedtime choices should not be misrepresented as
scientific findings about childhood development.

## Playfulness in this second batch

Stories 010000–021000 include playful and imaginative narratives, small social moments, and a few adjustments when a plan does not work. No explicit moral is required of any story. Fun is enough of a reason for a story to exist. The child does not need to answer a prompt, identify a virtue, or demonstrate a skill. As elsewhere in this pack, no fixed duration is imposed.

## Suggested pilot evaluation

Use a single consistent narrator and listen to the *actual* TTS output. Review
pronunciation, pauses, clarity of cause-and-effect, whether a three-year-old
can follow the character and situation, whether the story's emotional arc
remains comfortable near bedtime, and whether the ending feels complete.
Ask caregivers for feedback without treating subjective preferences as
universal outcomes. Avoid requiring images, character voices, background
music, or sound effects to understand the story.
