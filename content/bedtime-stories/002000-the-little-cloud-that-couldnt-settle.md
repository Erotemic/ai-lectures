# 002000 — The Little Cloud That Couldn't Settle

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (written primarily for age three). **Form:** Original, single-narrator bedtime fiction. **Story shape:** A gentle cumulative journey with a repeated, changing refrain.

# The Little Cloud That Couldn't Settle

High above a green hill floated a small cloud named Puff.

Puff was round on top and a little lumpy underneath. Sometimes Puff looked like a sheep. Sometimes Puff looked like a scoop of mashed potatoes.

And sometimes, when the wind was playful, Puff looked like a sheep made of mashed potatoes.

All day long, Puff had floated across the sky.

Now evening was coming.

Far below, the birds tucked their heads beneath their wings. The flowers leaned toward the ground. Warm lights came on in the windows of the little houses.

Everything seemed to be getting ready to rest.

Puff wanted to rest, too.

So Puff tried to stay in one place.

Puff made a very round shape and held it as carefully as possible.

But a small breeze came by.

And Puff floated a little farther.

Puff tried making a flat shape, like a pancake resting on a plate.

The breeze came by again.

And Puff floated on.

Perhaps, Puff thought, a cloud needed to find a special resting place.

Below was a field of long grass.

Puff drifted down the sky until the field filled the whole view beneath.

The grass was moving, just a little. One blade bent. Then another. Then another. All of them leaned together in the evening wind.

Puff watched for a while.

The grass did not fight the breeze. It moved with it, slowly and softly.

Puff tried moving that way, too.

A little slower. A little softer.

But the breeze still carried Puff onward.

Next came a pond.

Three ducks were making their way toward the reeds. One duck left a small line in the water. Then the line grew wide and faint. Soon the pond looked smooth again.

Puff watched the ducks fold their wings.

The water did not stop being water when it moved. It made tiny ripples, then became quiet.

Puff let the edges of the cloud grow soft.

A little slower. A little softer. A little quieter.

Still, Puff floated on.

Beyond the pond was an orchard.

The apple trees stood close together. Their leaves made a gentle whisper whenever the breeze passed through.

One leaf turned over and showed its pale underside.

Another leaf twirled once, then settled against a branch.

In the grass below, a fallen apple rested without rolling anywhere at all.

Puff liked that apple. It seemed very comfortable.

Puff tried to make an apple shape.

It was not easy for a cloud to be an apple. Puff came out looking more like a pear with fluffy ears.

But that was all right.

The breeze carried Puff toward the little houses on the far side of the hill.

A little slower. A little softer. A little quieter.

The sky was turning from pale gold to blue.

Puff could see one yellow window, then another. In a small cottage, a rabbit was pulling a blue blanket up to his chin. Nearby, a little wooden boat was resting on a shelf.

Soon the cottage light went out.

Puff looked down at the quiet orchard, the smooth pond, and the long grass leaning with the breeze.

The trees had moved.

The water had moved.

The grass had moved.

And all of them were resting now.

Puff understood.

A cloud didn't need to stay in one spot to be quiet.

Puff could float gently.

The breeze was soft, and the night was soft, and Puff was soft, too.

A small star appeared in the blue sky.

Puff drifted past it, slowly enough to notice its light.

A little slower.

A little softer.

A little quieter.

And across the evening sky, Puff floated on, peaceful and light.

## Author's companion — not spoken

**Experiment:** A character with a simple frustration learns through three repeated but different observations. The refrain gains one phrase at each stop, then returns at the end. The cloud's changing shape allows quiet humor without any additional character's dialogue.

**Single-voice note:** All speech is indirect. Allow the refrain to become increasingly spacious. The single cameo of Moss and the blue blanket is optional continuity; a listener does not need to know the earlier story.

**Originality:** Newly written fantasy fiction. Cloud behavior is personification, not an explanation of meteorology.
