# 046000 — Lisa and the Colors in the Sunbeam

# Lisa and the Colors in the Sunbeam

On a sunny afternoon, Lisa went to Grandpa's house to help him look for a missing button.

The button was small and brown, and it had fallen off his gardening coat.

Grandpa looked beside the chair. Lisa looked under the table. The cat looked at them both, then went back to sleep.

“Buttons are good at hiding,” Grandpa said.

“Maybe it went on an adventure,” said Lisa.

She peered behind a stack of books. No button.

But on the wall beside the books, something was glowing.

It was a little band of colors, all in a row.

Lisa leaned closer.

“Grandpa, did somebody paint the wall?”

Grandpa turned around. He had a patch of colors on his shirt too.

Across Grandpa’s shirt lay a bright red stripe, and under it were yellow, green, and blue.

He looked down at himself.

“Well,” he said, “I didn't put those on this morning.”

Lisa held her hand in front of his shirt. The colors moved onto her fingers.

“Oh!” she said. “They like me better.”

Grandpa turned toward the window.

There, on the sill, stood a little piece of clear glass. It was long and thin, with three flat sides. It looked a bit like a tiny tent made of ice.

Grandpa picked it up.

“This is a prism,” he said. “It takes the sunlight coming through the window and spreads it into colors.”

Lisa looked at the prism.

“It's clear.”

“Yes.”

“Where were all those colors hiding?”

“In the sunlight.”

Grandpa tipped the prism very slightly.

The bright band climbed from his shirt to the wall.

Lisa gasped.

Grandpa tilted the glass again. Now the colors slid down to the bookcase and stretched across the back of a wooden chair.

“It's going for a walk!” Lisa said.

She fetched her stuffed rabbit and put him in the chair.

“Rabbit,” she told him, “your ear has turned green.”

Grandpa shifted the prism again. The colors jumped to Rabbit's foot.

“Now his foot is red!”

They tried the colors on an open picture book, on Grandpa's gardening coat, and on the top of the table. Whenever the sunlight was in the right place and Grandpa held the prism at the right angle, the colors appeared.

When he put the prism in his pocket, they vanished.

Lisa checked under the table.

“No colors down here,” she reported.

Grandpa took the prism out again. He held it in the sunny window, and the colored band returned to the wall.

“I thought I had lost them,” Lisa said.

She watched the line of colors for a long moment.

“Can we put one in the button box?”

“I don't think a bit of sunlight would stay in a button box,” said Grandpa. “But we can draw it.”

Lisa fetched her crayons. She drew a long red line, a yellow line, a green line, and a blue one. She added a rabbit with one red foot and a green ear.

Then the room grew a little dimmer.

The colors on the wall faded.

Lisa looked at the window. A wide gray cloud had drifted in front of the sun.

“Grandpa, did the cloud take our rainbow?”

“Let's watch,” he said.

They sat on the rug beside the sleepy cat. Nobody moved the prism. Nobody looked for the button.

They waited while the cloud crossed the sky.

Then the cloud slipped away, and the colors came back.

“There!” whispered Lisa.

For a while they watched the small bright band on the wall. It did not have to go anywhere. It was lovely just where it was.

Later, when Lisa went to hang up Grandpa's gardening coat, she found the brown button caught in the fold of its pocket.

“The button had the adventure after all,” Grandpa said.

He put it safely in the button box.

By bedtime, the sun had moved away from the window, and the wall was plain again.

Lisa took her drawing home. She put the rabbit picture beside her pillow and looked once more at the colors she had made.

They were still there, even though the sunlight had gone.

“Goodnight, little rainbow,” she said, and closed her eyes.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Editorial intention:** The changing patches of color create a concrete mystery, while a clear prism provides one satisfying cause. The story makes room for delight without demanding that a child recite the colors.

**Optional prompt:** “Where did the colors appear when Grandpa moved the little glass prism?” It is fine if the child simply listens.

**Background sources:** [NASA Science / Space Telescope Science Institute, *Sunlight Spread Out into a Spectrum*](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/sunlight-spread-out-into-a-spectrum/); [National Association for the Education of Young Children, *Rocking and Rolling: Fostering Curiosity in Infants and Toddlers*](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/yc/dec2020/fostering-curiosity-infants). The first supports a natural phenomenon; the second supports optional shared noticing or discussion. Neither evaluates the original plot or predicts a bedtime or developmental outcome.

**Narrator note:** Keep all dialogue in one clear narrator register; no character voice acting, music, sound effects, or timed narration target is required.
