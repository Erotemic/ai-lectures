# 026000 — The Night Train to Pillow Mountain

**Collection:** Children's Bedtime Stories. **Age range:** Ages 3–4 (primarily age three). **Form:** Original, single-narrator fiction. **Story shape:** A child's pretend train takes a quiet journey through tiny made-up places before returning to bed.

<!-- BEGIN SPOKEN LECTURE -->

# The Night Train to Pillow Mountain

Ari was tucked into bed, with a blanket up to the chin and a small lamp glowing beside the door.

There was a pillow under Ari's head.

There was another pillow beside the bed.

And on that second pillow sat a little wooden train.

It had a round engine, three cars, and a chimney painted blue.

Dad had brought it in after tidying the toys.

Ari looked at the train.

Then at the pillow.

Then back at the train.

Perhaps, Ari thought, this is not an ordinary pillow.

Perhaps it is a mountain.

And perhaps the little train is waiting for a passenger.

In Ari's imagination, the wooden train grew just big enough for one child to sit inside.

The engine gave a very small puff.

Not a noisy puff.

A puff like someone blowing gently on warm soup.

The conductor was a stuffed bear in a blue coat.

The bear had one ticket.

It was shaped like a sleepy leaf.

Ari climbed aboard the pretend train and handed over the ticket.

The bear checked it carefully.

There was no number on the ticket.

There was only a picture of a pillow.

That was exactly the right ticket for a trip to Pillow Mountain.

The train rolled away from the bedside station.

The first stop was Button Bridge.

The bridge was made from four very large buttons placed in a row.

The train rolled over the first button.

Then the second.

Then the third.

And when it reached the fourth, the conductor lifted a tiny paw and waved to a snail waiting beside the track.

The snail was wearing a scarf twice as long as its body.

It waved back with the end of the scarf.

The train moved on.

The next stop was Blanket Meadow.

Here the grass was made of soft threads, and the little flowers were round stitches in the cloth.

A small sheep stood near the tracks.

It was looking for a place to lie down.

Ari pointed to a patch of blue blanket beneath a stitched yellow flower.

The sheep curled up there at once.

The conductor looked pleased.

The train continued, more slowly now.

Ahead was the Tunnel of Hush.

There were no lamps inside the tunnel.

But the walls were made of pale wool, and the whole tunnel glowed softly in Ari's imagination.

Inside, the wheels made almost no sound at all.

Ari could hear the tiniest rustle of the ticket.

The bear folded the ticket and placed it in a pocket.

Then the train came out beneath a sky full of little silver dots.

Pillow Mountain stood ahead.

It had one round peak and one long, comfortable slope.

At the top was a flag made from the corner of a pillowcase.

The train rolled up the slope without hurrying.

At last it came to a stop.

Ari climbed out.

The air was warm.

The mountain felt soft beneath Ari's feet.

Far below, Button Bridge had become four tiny circles.

Blanket Meadow looked like one blue square.

The train waited quietly at the station.

Ari looked at the conductor.

The bear was very still.

Of course the bear was still.

It was a stuffed bear, sitting on a wooden train beside a real pillow.

Ari was still tucked safely in bed.

Dad was still nearby in the house.

The little lamp still glowed beside the door.

The journey had been a fine one.

Ari reached out, placed the wooden train on the pillow beside the bed, and tucked the sleepy leaf ticket into the story where it belonged.

Then Ari settled down on the real pillow.

Button Bridge rested.

Blanket Meadow rested.

Pillow Mountain rested.

And the little wooden train waited quietly for another day.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** The child remains in a familiar bedroom while ordinary objects become a miniature journey. The narration explicitly returns to the actual room. The ticket, conductor and mountain are imagined. There is **no educational objective or fixed sleep outcome** attached to the train ride.

**Optional prompt:** “Where should the tiny train stop next?” The story is complete even without another stop or a response.

**Sources and limits:** [CDC, *Milestones by 3 Years* (2026), “Parenting Tips and Activities”](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html) recommends stories and pretend-play role-playing about new experiences. [American Academy of Pediatrics, *Young Children Learn a Lot When They Play* (2022)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/young-children-learn-a-lot-when-they-play.aspx) recommends allowing room for imagination. They provide broad caregiver context, not proof that this train story improves sleep, flexibility or spatial reasoning.

**Single-voice note:** Make the transition into and out of Ari's imagination completely clear without adopting a special bear voice. The invented train sounds are optional syllables in narration, not required effects.
