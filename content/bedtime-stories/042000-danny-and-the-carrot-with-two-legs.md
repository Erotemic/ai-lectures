# Danny and the Carrot with Two Legs

Danny the rabbit had helped Aunt Sue plant carrots in the vegetable patch.

He remembered putting the tiny seeds into the soil. He remembered watering the rows. He remembered waiting and waiting while green tops grew higher.

One afternoon, Aunt Sue brought out a little basket.

“Some of our carrots are ready,” she said.

Danny hurried over.

The first carrot came out of the ground with a small tug.

It was short and round at the top, with a thin orange end.

The second carrot was longer. It had a patch of soil stuck to one side.

The third carrot did not want to come out.

Danny held the green top. Aunt Sue loosened the soil around it with her small garden fork. Then Danny pulled gently.

Out came a carrot unlike either of the others.

It had a fat orange top.

And below that, instead of one long end, it had two.

The carrot had one thick top and two long orange roots below it.

Danny stared.

“Aunt Sue,” he said, “this carrot is wearing trousers.”

Aunt Sue looked at the carrot.

“Very orange trousers,” she agreed.

Danny held it upright.

The two roots hung down like two legs.

He moved it a little to the left.

The carrot took one pretend step.

He moved it to the right.

The carrot took another pretend step.

Danny made it march all the way to the basket.

“Left carrot leg, right carrot leg,” he said.

The normal carrots lay quietly in the basket, not marching at all.

At the kitchen sink, Aunt Sue washed the soil from their harvest. She set the funny carrot on a clean towel.

Danny looked at its two long roots.

“Now it needs boots.”

He found two small paper squares in his craft box. Aunt Sue helped him fold them into little pockets. Danny slid one over the end of each root.

The carrot wore one blue paper boot and one red one.

It looked like someone going to a party who had dressed in the dark.

Danny laughed so hard he nearly dropped it.

Aunt Sue set the carrot in a cup to keep it upright.

It leaned to one side.

Danny straightened it.

It leaned to the other.

“Maybe it is tired from all that marching,” Aunt Sue said.

Danny considered this. “Maybe its boots are too heavy.”

He took the paper boots off again. The carrot still leaned.

That made it funnier.

Soon the kitchen table held three ordinary carrots, two folded paper boots, and one carrot with its legs in the air.

Danny decided to make a picture before supper.

He took a piece of yellow paper and a big orange crayon.

First he drew the fat top.

Then he drew the green leaves.

Then he drew one long root, and another long root beside it.

He added the blue boot and the red boot, because the real carrot had worn them for a little while.

He showed Aunt Sue.

“That's the carrot before it went to bed.”

Aunt Sue put the washed carrots away to be cooked on another day. The paper boots went back in Danny's craft box.

Danny pinned his drawing to the kitchen board, down low where he could see it.

He had drawn the carrot exactly as it was, with two long legs and no feet at all.

Well, except for the boots he had added.

At bedtime, Danny washed his own feet and put on two matching socks.

He looked down at them.

“Lucky,” he said. “Neither of my socks is a carrot.”

Then he climbed into bed, pulled up his blanket, and let both his legs rest after a day of very important marching.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**Why this one exists:** The forked carrot is an ordinary real-world possibility, used here for a nonsensical game about carrot trousers. The story does not prescribe a harvest activity or make claims about gardening outcomes.

**Optional prompt:** “What made Danny think the carrot was wearing trousers?” The child may also simply listen; nothing needs to be answered.

These are parent-facing references about reading and observation, not sources for the original plot. No benefit from the specific story has been experimentally evaluated; invented scenes are fiction.

**Single-voice note:** Keep any character speech in the same narrator register. The narrated story needs no music, sound effects, or separate acting voices.
