## Selection and teaching objective

Marcel Proust's *À la recherche du temps perdu* (*In Search of Lost Time*) earns a major section through its sustained influence on literary treatments of memory, time, consciousness, social identity, and narrative architecture. It is selected for critical and literary-historical importance, **not** to fill a geographic or demographic representation quota. The manuscript sequence teaches the **complete narrative orientation** first, in seven volume-by-volume retellings, then seven independently coherent close studies of the novel's techniques and history. Read in numerical order. These transcripts are **original critical prose**, not a replacement transcription or unauthorized translation of Proust.

## Sources and evidence hierarchy

- **Primary reference** — Proust, *À la recherche du temps perdu*, publicly accessible French editions as indexed by the Société des Amis de Marcel Proust. These establish fictional events and language, subject to textual states and translation variation. The reader is directed to the original rather than to an adaptation.
- **Main scholarly cross-check** — Adam Watt, *The Cambridge Introduction to Marcel Proust*, Chapter 4, pp. 44–103, with sections allocated to each of the seven volumes (44–52, 53–60, 61–69, 70–77, 78–85, 86–94, 95–103). This is the principal independent narrative/interpretive cross-check for the seven plot lectures. The full chapter was available for review; the PDF's first page was visually checked.
- **Text and manuscript history** — Société des Amis de Marcel Proust, *Note on the Text*, and Bibliothèque nationale de France manuscript exhibition and Gallica guide. Distinguish the society's period French transcription from later critical editions and the 1922 corrected typescript discovered in 1986.
- **Publication and translation record** — Proust Ink chronology, Society works, University at Buffalo translation catalog and Cambridge research on the genesis of the novel. Use these for original dates, publication sequence, and differences in English titles.
- **Specific historical corroboration** — United States Holocaust Memorial Museum's *Alfred Dreyfus and the Dreyfus Affair* for the historical conviction and exoneration; the Dreyfus Affair's influence on fictional characters remains an interpretation of Proust's text, not a literal historical case record.
- **Secondary interpretive context** — Joshua Landy's Oxford book abstract for memory versus impression; Gabrielle Townsend's Cambridge chapter abstract for the fictional artists. Neither **abstract-only** record should be presented as having been read in full. General references and encyclopedia summaries are orientation only, not independent confirmation of disputed matters.

## Factual and interpretive guardrails

1. **Narrator vs author:** The first-person narrator is not automatically Marcel Proust; the novel is not a historical diary of the author's personal actions. Interpret similarities rather than silently identifying them.
2. **Seven books, one developing novel:** Four volumes were published in Proust's lifetime (1913, 1919, 1920–21, 1921–22), and **three** afterward (1923, 1925, 1927). Proust died in November 1922. The Society's overview webpage contains an inconsistent statement about “last four”; bibliographic entries and textual scholarship support **three**. Do not copy that overview error.
3. **Posthumous textual divergence:** Proust left the last three books without the same level of final print preparation. The 1922 corrected typescript of *Albertine disparue*, rediscovered in 1986 and published as an edited text in 1987, diverges significantly from the familiar posthumous volume. Some Venetian and ending transitions are edition-dependent. Do not make one edition's passage an uncontested authorial last intention.
4. **Title conventions:** *À l'ombre des jeunes filles en fleurs* is translated as *In the Shadow of Young Girls in Flower* and *Within a Budding Grove*; *La Prisonnière* as *The Prisoner* or *The Captive*; *Albertine disparue* as *Albertine Gone* or *The Fugitive* (among other historical options); the whole series is also called *Remembrance of Things Past*. Different English titles do not signify extra volumes.
5. **The narrator's mother:** Proust's mother died in 1905; the **fictional narrator's mother does not die in the novel**. The narrator's grandmother does die and must not be conflated with the mother. The seventh-volume Venice journey includes the narrator's mother.
6. **Albertine:** A narrator's speculation is not neutral evidence of Albertine's private history. His emotional suffering does not absolve his surveillance, restrictive arrangements or coercive choices; equally, her actions cannot be flattened into the narrator's allegations.
7. **Sexuality and period categories:** Proust's language and classificatory theories arose in a period of criminalization, moral policing and medical speculation. Describe what the fiction says and the pressures it depicts without treating every period label as a timeless scientific category.
8. **Dreyfus:** The real Alfred Dreyfus was wrongly convicted amid antisemitic agitation. Fictional salon reactions are evidence of how the novel represents society, not contemporaneous transcripts of historical conversations.
9. **The First World War:** The transformation of Paris and Robert de Saint-Loup's death are narrative events embedded in an actual historical conflict. Do not infer that Proust witnessed each fictional episode personally.
10. **Narrative time:** The novel does not simply recreate an exact chronological archive, and the narrator's ultimate artistic theory does not erase the novel's many contradictory voices and difficult behavior.
11. **Claiming influence:** Joyce, Woolf and other modernists also developed formal innovations independently and sometimes concurrently. Claims of direct borrowing require documented reception, not shared subject matter alone.
12. **No duration policy:** Length is a by-product of what the subject requires. The sections deliberately vary in form and focus; no fixed target, minimum or maximum runtime should be retroactively imposed.
