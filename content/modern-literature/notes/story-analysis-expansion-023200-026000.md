## Editorial rationale

The three works cover different modes of familiar twentieth-century literature: wartime institutional farce, social-realist and documentary epic, and speculative dystopia. The stories are independently comprehensible and reach their endings without requiring the following analysis; the critical recordings ask different questions about narrative form and moral reasoning. Runtime and word counts appear only as descriptive metadata. No fixed target, minimum or maximum governs manuscript length.

The *Grapes* analysis acknowledges research from federal camp manager Tom Collins and Sanora Babb's independent literary fieldwork without asserting a demonstrated exact chain of appropriation for every field note. The *Brave New World* analysis recognizes earlier reproductive-planning satire, particularly Rose Macaulay's *What Not*, while framing its effect on Huxley as a sourced editorial observation. The *Catch-22* analysis connects its 1961 publication and extended development to archival evidence rather than presenting it as a direct report on every military practice of the Second World War.
