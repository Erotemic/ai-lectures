## Listener-first decisions

- Each story episode is a single continuous spoiler-inclusive lecture. The two roles (narration and interpretation) remain distinct; no fixed duration or word quota is used.
- Handmaid's Tale avoids replacing Offred's unknown outcome with the plot of a screen adaptation. It identifies the voice, the reproductive hierarchy, the dangers of consent under coercion, and the ending's professional archive.
- Fahrenheit 451 resists the simple equation that reading alone automatically makes people ethical. Clarisse and Faber are different forms of attention, while Beatty knows books and abuses them.
- Bulgakov has several relatively independent scenes and nested narrators. Storytellers may organize their contents into one understandable sequence without claiming a definitive edition where surviving witnesses differ.
- Publication and historical claims receive source records. For scholarship whose public access is restricted to an abstract, only the abstract's actually visible propositions are attributed.
