# Kim and the Little Round Window

Kim had brought three pebbles to visit a new friend.

She carried them in her familiar violet pouch. One was round and gray, with a thin white stripe. The other two were little stones she knew just as well.

Sue the mouse lived with Grandpa Ed in the stone cottage along the orchard path, before the old mill.

It had a round window.

Kim wanted to see that window.

She came with her grown-up on a warm afternoon. Grandpa Ed welcomed them at the door.

Sue was sitting beside a cardboard building.

"It's a castle bakery," Sue explained. "Ben helped me make it. It has a carrot flag."

Kim looked at the paper roof, the drawn windows, and the little door.

"Where are the biscuits?" she asked.

"All eaten by a mountain."

Kim thought about that.

"A very hungry mountain."

"Extremely hungry," said Sue.

The two friends sat near the round window. A soft patch of sunlight lay across the floor.

Kim liked the way the light stopped at the edge of the rug.

Sue liked how the curtain made a long shadow with a frilly end.

"It looks like a sleepy caterpillar," Sue said.

"Or a scarf," said Kim.

"A scarf for a caterpillar."

"That might take some knitting."

Sue giggled.

Kim opened her violet pouch.

"I brought my pebbles to show you."

She tipped them carefully onto a folded cloth on the rug.

One.

Two.

Three.

Sue leaned close.

"That one has a little road," she said, pointing to the gray pebble with the white stripe.

"It's my favorite," said Kim. "I keep it with the others."

"Can we make them into mountains for our castle bakery?" Sue asked.

Kim looked at the pebbles. She looked at the cardboard building.

"We can pretend they are mountains right here on the cloth," she said. "I don't want to put them under the boxes."

"That's fine," said Sue.

She moved the castle bakery a little closer, without touching the stones.

They imagined three mountains lined up outside the bakery.

The first mountain wanted a pretend oat biscuit.

The second mountain wanted a pretend raisin biscuit.

The third mountain wanted to know why mountains were always expected to eat biscuits.

"Maybe it wants soup," said Kim.

"Mountain soup!" said Sue. "Made from clouds!"

"Cloud soup would be very wet."

"And quite fluffy."

The two of them laughed.

After a while, the sunlight had moved farther across the rug. Its long, frilly shadow was no longer in the same spot.

Kim gathered her pebbles back into the violet pouch.

One.

Two.

Three.

"Would you like to build something else?" Sue asked.

Kim looked out the round window at the orchard tree.

"Not just now. I think I'd like to sit for a bit."

Sue nodded.

She took the green cushion with its yellow star from beside her bed and placed it on the rug.

"I'll sit on this," she said. "You can have the other cushion."

Kim settled down beside her.

They listened to the leaves outside.

After a little while Sue hummed a tune. It was a tune without words, and it wandered around as if it were looking for a place to land.

Kim listened.

"That's a funny tune," she said.

"I made it up while Grandpa was unpacking."

"Do you know where it ends?"

"Not yet," said Sue.

So they sat quietly while Sue tried a few different endings.

One ending sounded like a bouncing ball.

One sounded like a bird going to bed.

The last ending was so soft that Kim almost missed it.

"That one," Kim said.

"That one?"

"Yes. The quiet one."

Sue smiled.

At the doorway, Kim's grown-up said it was time to go home toward Round Pond.

The pouch stayed in Kim's paw, with all three pebbles inside. Sue's green cushion stayed in the cottage, and the cardboard castle bakery stood beside the round window.

"Come again," Sue said. "Perhaps the mountains will be hungry."

"Perhaps," said Kim. "Or perhaps we'll just sit."

"I'll save you a spot by the window."

When Kim had gone, Sue sat by the round window once more.

The patch of sunlight was nearly gone from the rug.

But she remembered the soft end of the little tune.

And she hummed it once more before supper.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Kim and Sue meet at Round Window Cottage. With permission, Kim temporarily arranges three pebbles on a cloth as pretend mountains, then returns all three to her violet pouch. Sue keeps her cushion; the castle bakery stays inside the cottage. Kim and Sue discover a shared taste for a soft song ending. Kim goes home near Round Pond with her grown-up.

**Why this episode exists:** The friends enjoy both make-believe and an unhurried pause. Kim can decline another activity and Sue can enjoy a different sort of quiet without being reduced to copies of one temperament.

**Optional prompt:** "Did the mountains want biscuits or soup?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
