# Nori and the Little Round Window

Nori had brought three pebbles to visit a new friend.

She carried them in her familiar violet pouch. One was round and gray, with a thin white stripe. The other two were little stones she knew just as well.

Mira the mouse lived with Grandpa Rowan in the stone cottage along the orchard path, before the old mill.

It had a round window.

Nori wanted to see that window.

She came with her grown-up on a warm afternoon. Grandpa Rowan welcomed them at the door.

Mira was sitting beside a cardboard building.

"It's a castle bakery," Mira explained. "Bo helped me make it. It has a carrot flag."

Nori looked at the paper roof, the drawn windows, and the little door.

"Where are the biscuits?" she asked.

"All eaten by a mountain."

Nori thought about that.

"A very hungry mountain."

"Extremely hungry," said Mira.

The two friends sat near the round window. A soft patch of sunlight lay across the floor.

Nori liked the way the light stopped at the edge of the rug.

Mira liked how the curtain made a long shadow with a frilly end.

"It looks like a sleepy caterpillar," Mira said.

"Or a scarf," said Nori.

"A scarf for a caterpillar."

"That might take some knitting."

Mira giggled.

Nori opened her violet pouch.

"I brought my pebbles to show you."

She tipped them carefully onto a folded cloth on the rug.

One.

Two.

Three.

Mira leaned close.

"That one has a little road," she said, pointing to the gray pebble with the white stripe.

"It's my favorite," said Nori. "I keep it with the others."

"Can we make them into mountains for our castle bakery?" Mira asked.

Nori looked at the pebbles. She looked at the cardboard building.

"We can pretend they are mountains right here on the cloth," she said. "I don't want to put them under the boxes."

"That's fine," said Mira.

She moved the castle bakery a little closer, without touching the stones.

They imagined three mountains lined up outside the bakery.

The first mountain wanted a pretend oat biscuit.

The second mountain wanted a pretend raisin biscuit.

The third mountain wanted to know why mountains were always expected to eat biscuits.

"Maybe it wants soup," said Nori.

"Mountain soup!" said Mira. "Made from clouds!"

"Cloud soup would be very wet."

"And quite fluffy."

The two of them laughed.

After a while, the sunlight had moved farther across the rug. Its long, frilly shadow was no longer in the same spot.

Nori gathered her pebbles back into the violet pouch.

One.

Two.

Three.

"Would you like to build something else?" Mira asked.

Nori looked out the round window at the orchard tree.

"Not just now. I think I'd like to sit for a bit."

Mira nodded.

She took the green cushion with its yellow star from beside her bed and placed it on the rug.

"I'll sit on this," she said. "You can have the other cushion."

Nori settled down beside her.

They listened to the leaves outside.

After a little while Mira hummed a tune. It was a tune without words, and it wandered around as if it were looking for a place to land.

Nori listened.

"That's a funny tune," she said.

"I made it up while Grandpa was unpacking."

"Do you know where it ends?"

"Not yet," said Mira.

So they sat quietly while Mira tried a few different endings.

One ending sounded like a bouncing ball.

One sounded like a bird going to bed.

The last ending was so soft that Nori almost missed it.

"That one," Nori said.

"That one?"

"Yes. The quiet one."

Mira smiled.

At the doorway, Nori's grown-up said it was time to go home toward Round Pond.

The pouch stayed in Nori's paw, with all three pebbles inside. Mira's green cushion stayed in the cottage, and the cardboard castle bakery stood beside the round window.

"Come again," Mira said. "Perhaps the mountains will be hungry."

"Perhaps," said Nori. "Or perhaps we'll just sit."

"Either is good."

When Nori had gone, Mira sat by the round window once more.

The patch of sunlight was nearly gone from the rug.

But she remembered the soft end of the little tune.

And she hummed it once more before supper.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Nori and Mira meet at Round Window Cottage. With permission, Nori temporarily arranges three pebbles on a cloth as pretend mountains, then returns all three to her violet pouch. Mira keeps her cushion; the castle bakery stays inside the cottage. Nori and Mira discover a shared taste for a soft song ending. Nori goes home near Round Pond with her grown-up.

**Why this episode exists:** The friends enjoy both make-believe and an unhurried pause. Nori can decline another activity and Mira can enjoy a different sort of quiet without being reduced to copies of one temperament.

**Optional prompt:** "Did the mountains want biscuits or soup?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
