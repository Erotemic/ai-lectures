# The Bus with a Book in Its Belly

On Saturday morning, Ben held a clean cloth bag on his lap.

Inside was his blue Book of Little Sounds.

He sat beside his grown-up on the green bus. Amy and Grandma May sat ahead, Sue and Grandpa Ed beside the aisle. Wendy and Aunt Kay had joined them at the stop beyond Far Meadow. Kim came with her grown-up, and Todd and Aunt Pat had made the longer journey from Reed Meadow, past Round Pond and across Pebble Bridge.

It was a full little group, and everyone had a seat.

The bus door went psssh.

"That's a good sound," Todd said.

"It isn't in the book," Ben answered.

"Yet," Sue said.

"No more pages," Ben reminded her. "It's finished."

"Then the bus will have to make its own book."

Ben looked around. The green bus had windows, seats, and a long aisle. It did not appear to have a pencil.

"Nobody gave it a pencil," he said.

Sue considered it. "We could give it one."

For a while everyone watched the road and the fields go by. Far Meadow slipped behind them. The bus traveled west along the paved road toward Market Street, not across the creek and not through the orchard.

At a bend, the cloth bag slid a little on Ben's knees.

He caught it with both paws.

"The book's going somewhere," he said.

"That's what buses are for," said Amy.

"Yes, but it doesn't know where."

"You know," Kim said from the next seat. "And the grown-ups know."

Ben looked down at the bag.

"Market Street," he told the book. "Library. Table by the window. Not a bread shop."

"Good thing," Sue said. "A baker might put it in a loaf."

"A very square loaf."

The bus passed the last buildings before the market square, slowed, and gave another soft psssh at its stop. The grown-ups led everyone onto the pavement.

Market Street smelled faintly of bread from the bakery. A pigeon strutted beside the bench as if it were checking tickets.

Ben held up the bag.

"We all have tickets," he told the pigeon. "This one is for a book."

The pigeon kept walking.

"I don't think it wants a ride," said Wendy.

At the library, the large window showed its painted books. Inside, the low wooden table had been cleared. The handwritten sign about homemade books stood on it beside a little vase with one leaf.

The librarian greeted them and knelt so Ben could see her face.

"Is that the book you told us about?"

Ben nodded. He pulled the notebook gently out of the bag.

The librarian looked at the blue cover and the careful six pages. She did not try to add a seventh.

"It's yours," she said. "Would you like me to keep it safe here until the sharing table tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow?" Todd asked.

"The little table will be open again Sunday afternoon," she explained. "The grown-ups arranged that so everyone could visit together."

Ben thought about leaving the book in a place he knew only from a few bus rides. Then he remembered that Kim's borrowed library book had gone home and returned safely, and that the librarians kept many books.

"Will it be by the window?" he asked.

"On the staff shelf overnight. Tomorrow I'll put it here."

"That's a good shelf," Ben said. "Does it have a bedtime?"

"The library closes in the evening," the librarian said.

"Perfect."

Ben let her take the notebook, carefully, without the traveling bag. He folded the bag and kept it himself.

The friends visited the nearby flower stall with their grown-ups, then returned to the bus stop in time for their ride home. Nobody had to hurry or guess at a departure time; the adults had checked the route.

That night the little blue book slept, if books can be said to sleep, on a safe library shelf.

At home in Willow Bend, Ben folded his red scarf and laid the empty cloth bag beside it.

He knew exactly where the book was.

Tomorrow he could see it again.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** All six friends and their grown-ups take the established green bus west from its stop beyond Far Meadow. Ben hands the blue six-page book to the librarian, who stores it on a staff shelf Saturday night for the Sunday reading table; its cloth bag returns home with Ben. No extra page or bus ticket is added, no alternate route is invented, and all children return home.

**Why this episode exists:** The familiar bus and library are now used for a concrete shared errand; the original book leaves its usual shelf temporarily with clear custody and an ordinary next-day plan.

**Optional prompt:** "Where did the librarian keep the book overnight?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Education Resources — Natural Sounds](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/sound/education-resources.htm); [Read Early and Often](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/read-early-and-often/). These are editorial context for shared reading, active play, or observation; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories or verify the fictional events.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
