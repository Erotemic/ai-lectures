# Todd and the Almost Ocean

The morning after the rain, Todd went walking in his yellow boots.

One boot had a blue patch. The other did not.

He wore both anyway. It would have been rather silly to wear only one.

Todd lived beside Reed Meadow with Aunt Pat. That morning they took the familiar dry path past Round Pond.

Near the pond, they met Grandpa Ed and Sue, the young mouse who had moved into the little stone cottage beyond the orchard.

Sue had heard about Todd's boots from Ben.

"You have two!" she said.

Todd looked down.

"Most days, yes."

Sue laughed.

Grandpa Ed said they were exploring Willow Bend, one familiar path at a time.

"Then you've come to the right place," Todd said. "I know a puddle."

Aunt Pat smiled. "We can look from the dry side of the path."

Just ahead, in a shallow dip beside the firm walking path, rainwater had gathered.

It was not nearly as big as Round Pond.

It was not even as big as a picnic blanket.

But it had a small cloud floating in it.

Not a real cloud, of course.

A picture of a cloud.

Sue peered down at the water.

"It's a sky puddle," she said.

Todd examined the same puddle from a different angle.

"It is the Almost Ocean."

"That's an enormous name," Sue said.

"For a very important puddle."

They stood safely on the firm path and looked for things that could belong to an ocean.

There was a brown leaf near one edge.

"A boat," Sue said.

There was a little stone on the bank.

"An island," said Todd.

There was a long blade of grass.

"A lighthouse," said Sue.

Todd squinted at the grass.

"That is a very skinny lighthouse."

"It only has to shine one tiny light."

A breeze moved the water.

The reflected cloud broke into wriggly pieces.

"The sky is having a dance," Sue said.

Todd leaned slightly to one side.

"Then the Almost Ocean needs music."

He made a quiet little bup sound.

"Bup," Sue answered.

They listened to their two small sounds and laughed.

"We should put the Almost Ocean on a map," Todd said.

Sue imagined a drawing of a giant sea, complete with a leaf boat and a grass lighthouse.

"What if tomorrow it's just a little wet patch?" she asked.

Todd thought about that.

"Then tomorrow," he said, "it can have a different name."

Grandpa Ed suggested they continue to Round Pond.

They followed the same dry path they had been walking on, with the grown-ups close by.

Round Pond was wide and quiet beneath its willow. Sue had seen pictures of it on Amy's yellow map, but this was her first visit.

"This one really is a pond," Sue said.

"It's called Round Pond," Todd told her.

"Who chose the name?"

Todd looked at the water and considered.

"Possibly someone who liked round things."

Sue smiled. "Then I would like that person."

They sat for a little while on the grassy rise where Aunt Pat liked to rest.

Todd showed Sue the reeds that marked the path toward his own home in Reed Meadow. He did not take her into the wet hollow.

"And if you follow the path back to the bridge," he said, "you'll find Village Green."

"And beyond the Green, the orchard," said Grandpa Ed.

"And beyond the orchard," Sue said, "a round window waiting for me."

When it was time to part, Todd and Aunt Pat went east toward Reed Meadow. Sue and Grandpa Ed walked west over the one familiar Pebble Bridge, through Village Green, and down the orchard path to their cottage.

The Almost Ocean was still beside the path.

Perhaps tomorrow it would be smaller.

Perhaps tomorrow it would be gone.

For today, it was a perfectly good ocean for a leaf and a very skinny lighthouse.

That night, Sue told Grandpa Ed about a frog with two yellow boots and a puddle with an enormous name.

Grandpa Ed listened until the story grew quiet.

Then Sue settled on her green cushion beside the round window, happy that there was still so much of the valley to discover.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Sue meets Todd beside the adult-visible dry path near Round Pond, after Grandpa Ed and Sue walk from orchard through Village Green and across Pebble Bridge. A temporary rain puddle is jokingly called the Almost Ocean; it is not marked as permanent geography. Sue sees Round Pond for the first time and Todd shows the route back. They part with adults along the established east/west directions. Boots and belongings unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** A short route to a real pond becomes a pretend sea adventure without altering the map or suggesting unsupervised water play. A child-generated question keeps the future of the puddle open.

**Optional prompt:** "What did they pretend the little puddle was called?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
