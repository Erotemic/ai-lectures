# Rill and the Almost Ocean

The morning after the rain, Rill went walking in his yellow boots.

One boot had a blue patch. The other did not.

He wore both anyway. It would have been rather silly to wear only one.

Rill lived beside Reed Meadow with Aunt Cress. That morning they took the familiar dry path past Round Pond.

Near the pond, they met Grandpa Rowan and Mira, the young mouse who had moved into the little stone cottage beyond the orchard.

Mira had heard about Rill's boots from Bo.

"You have two!" she said.

Rill looked down.

"Most days, yes."

Mira laughed.

Grandpa Rowan said they were exploring Willow Bend, one familiar path at a time.

"Then you've come to the right place," Rill said. "I know a puddle."

Aunt Cress smiled. "We can look from the dry side of the path."

Just ahead, in a shallow dip beside the firm walking path, rainwater had gathered.

It was not nearly as big as Round Pond.

It was not even as big as a picnic blanket.

But it had a small cloud floating in it.

Not a real cloud, of course.

A picture of a cloud.

Mira peered down at the water.

"It's a sky puddle," she said.

Rill examined the same puddle from a different angle.

"It is the Almost Ocean."

"That's an enormous name," Mira said.

"For a very important puddle."

They stood safely on the firm path and looked for things that could belong to an ocean.

There was a brown leaf near one edge.

"A boat," Mira said.

There was a little stone on the bank.

"An island," said Rill.

There was a long blade of grass.

"A lighthouse," said Mira.

Rill squinted at the grass.

"That is a very skinny lighthouse."

"It only has to shine one tiny light."

A breeze moved the water.

The reflected cloud broke into wriggly pieces.

"The sky is having a dance," Mira said.

Rill leaned slightly to one side.

"Then the Almost Ocean needs music."

He made a quiet little bup sound.

"Bup," Mira answered.

They listened to their two small sounds and laughed.

"We should put the Almost Ocean on a map," Rill said.

Mira imagined a drawing of a giant sea, complete with a leaf boat and a grass lighthouse.

"What if tomorrow it's just a little wet patch?" she asked.

Rill thought about that.

"Then tomorrow," he said, "it can have a different name."

Grandpa Rowan suggested they continue to Round Pond.

They followed the same dry path they had been walking on, with the grown-ups close by.

Round Pond was wide and quiet beneath its willow. Mira had seen pictures of it on Tess's yellow map, but this was her first visit.

"This one really is a pond," Mira said.

"It's called Round Pond," Rill told her.

"Who chose the name?"

Rill looked at the water and considered.

"Possibly someone who liked round things."

Mira smiled. "Then I would like that person."

They sat for a little while on the grassy rise where Aunt Cress liked to rest.

Rill showed Mira the reeds that marked the path toward his own home in Reed Meadow. He did not take her into the wet hollow.

"And if you follow the path back to the bridge," he said, "you'll find Village Green."

"And beyond the Green, the orchard," said Grandpa Rowan.

"And beyond the orchard," Mira said, "a round window waiting for me."

When it was time to part, Rill and Aunt Cress went east toward Reed Meadow. Mira and Grandpa Rowan walked west over the one familiar Pebble Bridge, through Village Green, and down the orchard path to their cottage.

The Almost Ocean was still beside the path.

Perhaps tomorrow it would be smaller.

Perhaps tomorrow it would be gone.

For today, it was a perfectly good ocean for a leaf and a very skinny lighthouse.

That night, Mira told Grandpa Rowan about a frog with two yellow boots and a puddle with an enormous name.

Grandpa Rowan listened until the story grew quiet.

Then Mira settled on her green cushion beside the round window, happy that there was still so much of the valley to discover.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Mira meets Rill beside the adult-visible dry path near Round Pond, after Grandpa Rowan and Mira walk from orchard through Village Green and across Pebble Bridge. A temporary rain puddle is jokingly called the Almost Ocean; it is not marked as permanent geography. Mira sees Round Pond for the first time and Rill shows the route back. They part with adults along the established east/west directions. Boots and belongings unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** A short route to a real pond becomes a pretend sea adventure without altering the map or suggesting unsupervised water play. A child-generated question keeps the future of the puddle open.

**Optional prompt:** "What did they pretend the little puddle was called?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
