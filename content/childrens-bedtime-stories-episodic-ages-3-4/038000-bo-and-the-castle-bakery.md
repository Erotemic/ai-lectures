# Ben and the Castle Bakery

Ben arrived at the little stone cottage carrying a very serious plan.

The cottage stood along the orchard path, before the old mill fence. Sue the mouse had moved in with Grandpa Ed only a few days ago.

Ben wore his red scarf. Grandma May had come with him and Amy, but Amy was helping Grandpa Ed sort the books at the kitchen table.

Ben's plan was for Sue.

"I hear you have boxes," he said.

Sue looked around the room.

There were boxes beneath the table. Boxes beside the door. A box that had somehow ended up wearing a tea towel.

"We do," she said.

"Then we can build something."

Grandpa Ed found two empty, clean cardboard boxes and set them on the rug.

"These are yours to play with," he said. "I'll keep the heavy boxes by the wall."

Ben turned one box upside down.

"A castle," he said.

Sue turned it right side up.

"A bakery."

Ben turned it sideways.

"A castle bakery."

"Why would a castle need a bakery?" Sue asked.

Ben looked at her in surprise.

"For the royal biscuits."

Sue considered this.

"Fair enough."

They made a doorway from a wide opening that was already cut in the box. They drew little windows with crayons. Ben drew a flag shaped like a carrot.

"Why a carrot?" Sue asked.

"It is the king of vegetables," Ben said.

"Not in my bakery."

"Who is the king in your bakery?"

"The spoon."

She fetched the one spoon that Grandpa Ed had packed in a box of its own.

Ben stood it by the doorway like a guard.

"A very shiny ruler," he said.

Sue pointed to the top of the castle bakery.

"It needs a roof."

Ben looked around. His eyes landed on Sue's green cushion, the one with a yellow star.

"That would fit."

Sue picked up the cushion and held it close.

"I don't want to use this for a roof," she said. "It's where I sit."

"Oh," said Ben.

He looked at the castle bakery. Then he looked at Sue.

"What if we use the packing paper?"

Sue smiled. "That would be an excellent paper roof."

They folded a broad piece of brown paper over the top.

The paper leaned left.

They folded it again.

Now it leaned right.

Ben crouched beside it.

"I know a lantern that would be proud of this roof."

Sue had heard of the tilted lantern, but she had not seen it yet.

"Does everything in Willow Bend lean?" she asked.

"Only the very interesting things," Ben said.

At last the roof stayed on.

They stood back to admire their work.

A castle. A bakery. A carrot flag. A spoon at the door.

Something was still missing.

"Customers," Sue said.

Ben folded a tiny napkin into a point and placed it beside the entrance.

"A hungry mountain," he announced.

"Mountains aren't hungry," Sue said.

"This one is. It hasn't eaten since Tuesday."

"What does a mountain order?"

"A biscuit the size of a hill."

Sue leaned down to the paper mountain.

"I'm sorry," she said, "but the bakery has only pretend biscuits today."

Ben made the mountain bow.

"Then I shall have two."

They laughed so much that Grandpa Ed came from the table to see what was happening.

"What have we here?" he asked.

"A castle bakery," said Ben.

"With a very polite hungry mountain," said Sue.

Grandpa Ed admired the carrot flag. Then he put the important spoon back on the kitchen table before supper.

The children left their little cardboard building beside the round window, where it would be safe until another day.

Ben went home with his grown-up before dusk, carrying his red scarf and a story about a mountain that ate biscuits.

Sue curled up on her green cushion.

The cardboard roof still leaned a little.

The castle bakery was closed.

Even the pretend mountain had finished its supper.

And in the stone cottage, the last boxes waited quietly for tomorrow.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Sue and Ben build a modest cardboard castle bakery with drawn windows, carrot flag, packing-paper roof, and an important spoon-guard. The paper roof leans. The castle bakery remains beside Sue's round window for the next visit; the spoon returns to the kitchen table. Sue's cushion has not been loaned as a roof and stays with her. Ben goes home before dusk.

**Why this episode exists:** The story is mainly comedy about a pretend business. Sue's refusal to use her own cushion is respected without depicting either child as bad, and they find another roof.

**Optional prompt:** "What did the hungry mountain order at the bakery?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
