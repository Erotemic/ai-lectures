# Bo and the Castle Bakery

Bo arrived at the little stone cottage carrying a very serious plan.

The cottage stood along the orchard path, before the old mill fence. Mira the mouse had moved in with Grandpa Rowan only a few days ago.

Bo wore his red scarf. Grandma Fern had come with him and Tess, but Tess was helping Grandpa Rowan sort the books at the kitchen table.

Bo's plan was for Mira.

"I hear you have boxes," he said.

Mira looked around the room.

There were boxes beneath the table. Boxes beside the door. A box that had somehow ended up wearing a tea towel.

"We do," she said.

"Then we can build something."

Grandpa Rowan found two empty, clean cardboard boxes and set them on the rug.

"These are yours to play with," he said. "I'll keep the heavy boxes by the wall."

Bo turned one box upside down.

"A castle," he said.

Mira turned it right side up.

"A bakery."

Bo turned it sideways.

"A castle bakery."

"Why would a castle need a bakery?" Mira asked.

Bo looked at her in surprise.

"For the royal biscuits."

Mira considered this.

"Fair enough."

They made a doorway from a wide opening that was already cut in the box. They drew little windows with crayons. Bo drew a flag shaped like a carrot.

"Why a carrot?" Mira asked.

"It is the king of vegetables," Bo said.

"Not in my bakery."

"Who is the king in your bakery?"

"The spoon."

She fetched the one spoon that Grandpa Rowan had packed in a box of its own.

Bo stood it by the doorway like a guard.

"A very shiny ruler," he said.

Mira pointed to the top of the castle bakery.

"It needs a roof."

Bo looked around. His eyes landed on Mira's green cushion, the one with a yellow star.

"That would fit."

Mira picked up the cushion and held it close.

"I don't want to use this for a roof," she said. "It's where I sit."

"Oh," said Bo.

He looked at the castle bakery. Then he looked at Mira.

"What if we use the packing paper?"

Mira smiled. "That would be an excellent paper roof."

They folded a broad piece of brown paper over the top.

The paper leaned left.

They folded it again.

Now it leaned right.

Bo crouched beside it.

"I know a lantern that would be proud of this roof."

Mira had heard of the tilted lantern, but she had not seen it yet.

"Does everything in Willow Bend lean?" she asked.

"Only the very interesting things," Bo said.

At last the roof stayed on.

They stood back to admire their work.

A castle. A bakery. A carrot flag. A spoon at the door.

Something was still missing.

"Customers," Mira said.

Bo folded a tiny napkin into a point and placed it beside the entrance.

"A hungry mountain," he announced.

"Mountains aren't hungry," Mira said.

"This one is. It hasn't eaten since Tuesday."

"What does a mountain order?"

"A biscuit the size of a hill."

Mira leaned down to the paper mountain.

"I'm sorry," she said, "but the bakery has only pretend biscuits today."

Bo made the mountain bow.

"Then I shall have two."

They laughed so much that Grandpa Rowan came from the table to see what was happening.

"What have we here?" he asked.

"A castle bakery," said Bo.

"With a very polite hungry mountain," said Mira.

Grandpa Rowan admired the carrot flag. Then he put the important spoon back on the kitchen table before supper.

The children left their little cardboard building beside the round window, where it would be safe until another day.

Bo went home with his grown-up before dusk, carrying his red scarf and a story about a mountain that ate biscuits.

Mira curled up on her green cushion.

The cardboard roof still leaned a little.

The castle bakery was closed.

Even the pretend mountain had finished its supper.

And in the stone cottage, the last boxes waited quietly for tomorrow.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Mira and Bo build a modest cardboard castle bakery with drawn windows, carrot flag, packing-paper roof, and an important spoon-guard. The paper roof leans. The castle bakery remains beside Mira's round window for the next visit; the spoon returns to the kitchen table. Mira's cushion has not been loaned as a roof and stays with her. Bo goes home before dusk.

**Why this episode exists:** The story is mainly comedy about a pretend business. Mira's refusal to use her own cushion is respected without depicting either child as bad, and they find another roof.

**Optional prompt:** "What did the hungry mountain order at the bakery?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
