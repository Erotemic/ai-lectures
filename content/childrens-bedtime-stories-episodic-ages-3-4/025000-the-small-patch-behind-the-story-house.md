# The Small Patch Behind the Story House

There was a place in Willow Bend that Tess had walked past many times without drawing on her map.

It was not the fountain on Village Green.

It was not Pebble Bridge, which made a different sound beneath every pair of feet.

It was not even the Story House door, which squeaked as though it were trying to remember a tune.

It was a small patch of earth behind the Story House.

On a late spring afternoon, Tess noticed it because a fallen leaf had landed there, right in the middle, like a little brown boat waiting for a puddle.

The patch was no bigger than Grandma Fern's old kitchen rug.

One side touched the Story House's warm southern wall. The other sides were edged with smooth stones. A worn clay pot sat upside down nearby.

"What's growing here?" Tess asked.

Grandma Fern leaned down beside her.

"At the moment? Mostly questions."

Tess looked carefully.

There was dirt. A few bits of grass. The fallen leaf.

She found one small ant walking over a stone.

"And an ant," she said.

"And an ant," Grandma agreed.

Bo came around the corner with his red scarf swinging behind him.

He had been at the front of the Story House, admiring his old orange lantern. It still leaned a little, silver spots and all, by the window.

"I heard someone say questions were growing," he announced. "Are they tall questions?"

"One of them is very small," Tess said. "Could this be a garden?"

Bo examined the patch.

He spread his arms as wide as they would go.

"It could be a garden for giants."

"A very small giant," said Tess.

"The smallest giant in the world," said Bo. "He would grow one enormous carrot for his dinner."

Grandma Fern smiled. "The Story House belongs to everyone in the village. We should ask the other grown-ups who care for it before we change the patch."

So they went inside and asked. The grown-ups said a little garden could grow there, provided the paths stayed clear and the children helped look after it with an adult.

Grandma Fern brought out a small watering can, still empty.

Tess brought a pencil and her folded yellow map.

Bo brought something too.

A spoon.

"For the giant," he explained.

"Or for your pocket," said Grandma.

Bo put it back in his pocket.

They walked around the patch once. Then they walked around it again, much more slowly.

"Where would somebody stand to look at the plants?" Tess asked.

Grandma Fern showed them the dry path along the wall.

"Here is a good place. We won't need to step into the little bed."

Tess drew a tiny square behind the Story House on her map.

The square was so small that it looked like a green freckle.

Bo leaned in.

"You forgot the giant."

"We haven't seen him," said Tess.

"He's very good at hiding."

Tess thought for a moment. Then she drew one small question mark in the margin, where Bo could point to it and laugh.

They had not planted anything yet. Grandma Fern would bring seeds and prepare the soil another day.

But the patch had already changed.

It was a place the friends had stopped to notice. A place they had asked about. A place they could visit again.

Before leaving, Bo set the fallen leaf neatly on the stone border. It looked rather like a flag for the world's smallest giant.

When they walked back around the Story House, the evening sun lay across Village Green.

Tess could see the tilted lantern through the window. Beneath it, on the low shelf, were Bo's blue Book of Little Sounds and the brown envelope holding their pretend Button Town map.

All those familiar things were still where they belonged.

And behind the building was one small square on Tess's real map, waiting for something green.

That night, at the Blue Door Cottage, Tess folded her map beside her bed.

She did not draw a flower on the new square.

Not yet.

Outside, the little patch of earth rested quietly beside the warm wall.

The ant had gone somewhere else.

The leaf stayed where Bo had placed it.

And the garden that was not quite a garden waited for another day.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Tess and Grandma Fern notice a stone-bordered patch behind the sunny southern Story House wall. Grandma checks with other caretakers and obtains permission to garden without blocking the path. Tess adds a tiny square behind Story House on her real yellow map; the patch has no seeds yet. A fallen leaf sits on the border; old clay pot nearby.

**Why this episode exists:** Noticing a familiar place differently and asking open-ended questions make a satisfying plot; the giant-carrot joke is imaginative play. No study of this audio story is claimed.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Tess find behind the Story House?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
