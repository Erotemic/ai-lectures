# The Round Window on the Yellow Map

On the day Tess finally drew the little stone cottage on her map, the friends met at Village Green.

It was an ordinary afternoon.

No lantern parade. No ribbon picnic. No special celebration.

The Story House door squeaked when Grandma Fern opened it.

Inside, Bo's orange lantern still hung a little crooked by the window. On the low shelf were the blue Book of Little Sounds, the brown envelope holding the pretend map of Button Town, and the folder with their garden picture.

Tess carried her folded yellow map.

Mira had come from the orchard with Grandpa Rowan. She wore no hat, but she had plenty of ideas about hats after visiting Lila in Far Meadow.

Bo was already there with his red scarf.

Nori arrived from Round Pond with her grown-up, and Lila came over Bell Hill with Aunt Mallow.

A little later Rill and Aunt Cress joined them from Reed Meadow, by the dry path, over Pebble Bridge.

Six friends in one room.

And one very squeaky door.

"I think it said hello to all of us," Bo whispered.

Tess opened her map on the low table.

"There's a place missing," she said.

Mira leaned forward.

The map had Village Green, the fountain, Pebble Bridge, Round Pond, the orchard, the two-trunk Doorway Tree, Far Meadow beyond Bell Hill, and Reed Meadow farther east.

It also showed the old mill, with its wheel behind the fence.

But between the Doorway Tree and the mill, there was only a small pencil mark.

"That's where we met," Mira said.

"Yes," Tess replied. "I'd like to draw your cottage. What should it look like?"

Bo raised his paw.

"A castle bakery!"

"It is not a castle bakery," said Mira.

"Not on the outside," Bo agreed.

Rill pointed at the pencil mark.

"A yellow boot would be easy to recognize."

"But I don't have yellow boots," Mira said.

"You could borrow one of mine."

"Then you'd have one boot."

Rill looked down at his two yellow boots, one with a blue patch.

"That would be a problem."

Lila suggested a blue ribbon. Nori suggested three little circles, but only if everyone remembered they would be drawings, not her real pebbles.

Mira listened to all the ideas.

She looked toward the Story House window. It was a perfectly good window, but it was not round.

"I think," she said, "I'd like my house to have its own window."

Tess smiled.

She drew a little stone-colored roof beside the orchard path, before the old mill fence.

Under it she drew one round window.

Then she handed Mira the yellow pencil.

Mira added a tiny yellow star inside the window.

"That's my cushion," she explained. "It's what I see when I come home."

The friends looked at the picture.

It was small. It didn't show every box in the cottage, or the important spoon, or the cardboard castle bakery.

But it showed enough.

Bo looked at the map.

"Now there are six places where friends can go home," he said.

"Many more than six," said Nori. "There are grown-ups and neighbors too."

"And the dandelion audience," Lila added.

"The Almost Ocean," Rill said.

"That one might not last," Mira replied.

They all laughed.

Grandma Fern folded the map carefully and gave it back to Tess.

Outside, they checked the little garden behind the Story House. The plants had more leaves than before, and the small stepping stones were still where the children had left them.

There was no need to decide whether the garden was finished.

The visit had been a good one.

As the afternoon grew softer, the friends began going home with their grown-ups by the paths they knew.

Mira and Grandpa Rowan walked south through the orchard, past the Doorway Tree and toward the cottage near the old mill. Tess and Grandma Fern followed the westward path to Blue Door Cottage. The others went toward their own familiar neighborhoods.

At the little stone cottage, Grandpa Rowan opened the door.

The cardboard castle bakery was still beside the round window. The important spoon was back in the kitchen drawer.

Mira put the green cushion with the yellow star in its place below the window.

She was still learning where all the cups belonged.

Sometimes she still thought of the square window at her old home.

But now she knew the orchard path, the Story House squeak, the Almost Ocean, and the way Lila's hat waved in the wind.

She had found friends who knew where her home was, too.

At bedtime, Mira sat for a moment by the round window.

The orchard was quiet.

The old mill wheel stood still beyond its fence.

And on Tess's folded yellow map, in a little space between the tree and the mill, there was a round window with a yellow star.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** With Mira's input, Tess draws stone cottage between Doorway Tree and fenced mill on accurate real yellow map, symbolized by a round window and small yellow star representing Mira's green cushion. Tess keeps map and returns to Blue Door Cottage with Grandma. Everyone safely returns by established paths with grown-ups. Mira's cushion and castle bakery remain at Round Window Cottage; the spoon is in kitchen drawer; the blue cart remains with Grandpa. The Story House shelf remains three collections, no new project. Mira is established recurring friend without pressure to abandon memories of the previous home; season closes fully.

**Why this episode exists:** A calm shared map update gives Mira the final say in representing her home. The story keeps conflicting suggestions playful and lets remembering a previous home coexist with the pleasures of a new neighborhood.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Mira ask Tess to draw on the map?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
