# The Round Window on the Yellow Map

On the day Amy finally drew the little stone cottage on her map, the friends met at Village Green.

It was an ordinary afternoon.

No lantern parade. No ribbon picnic. No special celebration.

The Story House door squeaked when Grandma May opened it.

Inside, Ben's orange lantern still hung a little crooked by the window. On the low shelf were the blue Book of Little Sounds, the brown envelope holding the pretend map of Button Town, and the folder with their garden picture.

Amy carried her folded yellow map.

Sue had come from the orchard with Grandpa Ed. She wore no hat, but she had plenty of ideas about hats after visiting Wendy in Far Meadow.

Ben was already there with his red scarf.

Kim arrived from Round Pond with her grown-up, and Wendy came over Bell Hill with Aunt Kay.

A little later Todd and Aunt Pat joined them from Reed Meadow, by the dry path, over Pebble Bridge.

Six friends in one room.

And one very squeaky door.

"I think it said hello to all of us," Ben whispered.

Amy opened her map on the low table.

"There's a place missing," she said.

Sue leaned forward.

The map had Village Green, the fountain, Pebble Bridge, Round Pond, the orchard, the two-trunk Doorway Tree, Far Meadow beyond Bell Hill, and Reed Meadow farther east.

It also showed the old mill, with its wheel behind the fence.

But between the Doorway Tree and the mill, there was only a small pencil mark.

"That's where we met," Sue said.

"Yes," Amy replied. "I'd like to draw your cottage. What should it look like?"

Ben raised his paw.

"A castle bakery!"

"It is not a castle bakery," said Sue.

"Not on the outside," Ben agreed.

Todd pointed at the pencil mark.

"A yellow boot would be easy to recognize."

"But I don't have yellow boots," Sue said.

"You could borrow one of mine."

"Then you'd have one boot."

Todd looked down at his two yellow boots, one with a blue patch.

"That would be a problem."

Wendy suggested a blue ribbon. Kim suggested three little circles, but only if everyone remembered they would be drawings, not her real pebbles.

Sue listened to all the ideas.

She looked toward the Story House window. It was a perfectly good window, but it was not round.

"I think," she said, "I'd like my house to have its own window."

Amy smiled.

She drew a little stone-colored roof beside the orchard path, before the old mill fence.

Under it she drew one round window.

Then she handed Sue the yellow pencil.

Sue added a tiny yellow star inside the window.

"That's my cushion," she explained. "It's what I see when I come home."

The friends looked at the picture.

It was small. It didn't show every box in the cottage, or the important spoon, or the cardboard castle bakery.

But it showed enough.

Ben looked at the map.

"Now all six of our homes are on the map," he said.

"And there's the pond," said Kim.

"And the dandelion's meadow," Wendy added.

"And my Almost Ocean," Todd said.

"That one might not last," Sue replied.

They all laughed.

Grandma May folded the map carefully and gave it back to Amy.

Outside, they checked the little garden behind the Story House. The plants had more leaves than before, and the small stepping stones were still where the children had left them.

There was no need to decide whether the garden was finished.

The visit had been a good one.

As the afternoon grew softer, the friends began going home with their grown-ups by the paths they knew.

Sue and Grandpa Ed walked south through the orchard, past the Doorway Tree and toward the cottage near the old mill. Amy and Grandma May followed the westward path to Blue Door Cottage. The others went toward their own familiar neighborhoods.

At the little stone cottage, Grandpa Ed opened the door.

The cardboard castle bakery was still beside the round window. The important spoon was back in the kitchen drawer.

Sue put the green cushion with the yellow star in its place below the window.

She was still learning where all the cups belonged.

Sometimes she still thought of the square window at her old home.

But now she knew the orchard path, the Story House squeak, the Almost Ocean, and the way Wendy's hat waved in the wind.

She had found friends who knew where her home was, too.

At bedtime, Sue sat for a moment by the round window.

The orchard was quiet.

The old mill wheel stood still beyond its fence.

And on Amy's folded yellow map, in a little space between the tree and the mill, there was a round window with a yellow star.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** With Sue's input, Amy draws stone cottage between Doorway Tree and fenced mill on accurate real yellow map, symbolized by a round window and small yellow star representing Sue's green cushion. Amy keeps map and returns to Blue Door Cottage with Grandma. Everyone safely returns by established paths with grown-ups. Sue's cushion and castle bakery remain at Round Window Cottage; the spoon is in kitchen drawer; the blue cart remains with Grandpa. The Story House shelf remains three collections, no new project. Sue is established recurring friend without pressure to abandon memories of the previous home; season closes fully.

**Why this episode exists:** A calm shared map update gives Sue the final say in representing her home. The story keeps conflicting suggestions playful and lets remembering a previous home coexist with the pleasures of a new neighborhood.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Sue ask Amy to draw on the map?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
