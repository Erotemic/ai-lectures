# Bo's Moon Hat Department

Bo decided that the Moon needed a hat.

Not because the Moon had asked for one.

The Moon was much too far away to ask.

But Bo had heard Tess describe a thin silver curve in the evening sky, and it had given him an idea.

"What if it gets chilly?" he asked.

They were sitting at the long table in the Story House, with Grandma Fern sorting scraps of paper nearby.

Tess had come from Blue Door Cottage carrying her folded yellow map. She left the map closed on the table, because this wasn't a map-making day.

"I don't think the Moon gets chilly the way we do," she said.

"Then we shall make a pretend hat," Bo announced. "A very fine pretend hat."

He drew a big white circle on a piece of paper.

"That's the Moon."

Tess looked at it.

"It's round."

"Yes. Very round. My best roundness."

Then Bo made a tiny paper hat and placed it on the circle.

The hat fell off.

"Too small," he said.

He made another hat.

This one covered the entire paper Moon, including the part where its face might have been, if paper Moons had faces.

"Too big," said Tess.

Bo made a third hat, with two flaps, a yellow pom-pom, and one long strip that hung down like a rabbit's ear.

Grandma Fern picked it up.

"Who might actually wear this?"

Bo tried it on his own head.

The long strip tickled his nose.

"Ah," he said. "A hat for a bear who sneezes at the Moon."

He sneezed.

The paper circle slipped off the table and sailed into Grandma's empty basket.

Tess leaned over the basket.

"The Moon has landed," she said.

"Very gently," said Grandma Fern.

Bo made a fourth hat. He put a little paper spoon on top.

"For soup," he explained.

"What sort of soup?" asked Tess.

"Cloud soup. With one noodle."

For a while the Story House table became the busiest imaginary hat shop in Willow Bend.

There were hats for a round paper Moon.

Hats for the same Moon turned sideways.

A hat for an apple.

A hat for Grandma Fern's pencil.

And a very serious hat for the empty basket, which refused to tell them whether it liked the fit.

The basket was, after all, a basket.

When it was time to tidy up, Grandma helped them fold the paper pieces into Bo's own little bag. The finished blue Book of Little Sounds and the brown Button Town envelope stayed undisturbed on the low Story House shelf. Bo's tilted orange lantern still hung beside the window.

"Will the Moon be thin again tonight?" Bo asked.

"We can look," said Tess. "It looked like a smile a few evenings ago."

The friends went home with their grown-ups.

Later, after supper, Bo stood by his own window with the red scarf folded across the foot of his bed.

The sky was darkening, and above the rooftops he could see the Moon.

It still had a curved edge.

But its bright part was wider than the skinny silver smile Tess had described.

Not by a great big bear-sized amount.

Just wider.

"Huh," said Bo.

He held the little paper spoon-hat against the window. Of course it was too small to fit anything in the real sky.

Bo did not mind.

"A fine pretend hat," he told the Moon. "But I think you can manage without it."

He hung the paper hat on the bedpost, beside the tiny bag of scraps.

Outside, a few clouds moved slowly past the bright curve.

Inside, the room was quiet enough to hear the soft rustle of Bo's blanket.

His pretend hat shop was closed for the night.

The Moon needed no soup, no spoon, and no pom-pom.

Bo, however, did need sleep.

And soon he was getting plenty of it.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Several evenings after 031000, Bo and Tess make paper pretend Moon hats at Story House with Grandma. Hat scraps, the paper Moon circle and a tiny spoon-hat go home in Bo's bag. One tiny paper hat hangs at his bedpost. The real Moon is a wider waxing crescent that night. Story House collections unchanged; orange lantern tilted at window.

**Why this episode exists:** The funny shop is primarily imaginative play. Bo's real view provides a simple continuity marker for lunar phase change without requiring children to know phase names.

**Optional prompt:** "What was the silly paper spoon for?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
