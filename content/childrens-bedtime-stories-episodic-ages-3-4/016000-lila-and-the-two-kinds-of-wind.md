# Wendy and the Two Kinds of Wind

In Far Meadow, the wind had a very familiar game.

It liked to find the blue ribbon on Wendy's straw hat and make it flutter.

Wendy knew the game well.

She held the brim of her hat whenever a big breeze came by.

That morning, Aunt Kay told her they were going to visit Grandma May in Willow Bend.

"And after lunch," Aunt Kay said, "we might go farther east and see the reeds you told me about."

Wendy had heard of Reed Meadow from Amy, but she had never seen it.

She looked out the window of her cream-colored cottage. Their orange, blue, and violet picnic banner still hung inside, where the grown-ups had put it after the picnic.

"Will Reed Meadow have a big tree like ours?" Wendy asked.

"We can find out," Aunt Kay said.

They walked up and over Bell Hill, past the familiar resting bench, and down toward the blue-door cottage. Grandma May welcomed them in.

Amy had her yellow map. Ben had his red scarf and his little blue notebook. Kim was coming from Round Pond to meet them on the eastern path.

Ben showed Wendy the pictures in his book.

Reeds that went shhh.

Boots that went tok and tuk.

And a little round bup from a frog called Todd.

"A frog in yellow boots?" Wendy asked.

"With a blue patch on one boot," Amy said.

Wendy looked down at her own blue ribbon.

"We may have good taste in blue things."

The grown-ups accompanied the children through Village Green, across Pebble Bridge, and toward Round Pond. Kim was waiting by the willow.

She knew the path beyond the pond well now. She led them along the dry curve above Reed Meadow's wet hollow.

The wind arrived just as they reached the reeds.

Shhhhh.

Wendy stopped.

Back in Far Meadow, the broad leaves of the Windbreak Tree moved in the breeze. They made a soft fluttering sound.

Here the slender reeds brushed together and made a long whisper.

"That wind sounds different," she said.

"It's the reeds making that sound," Ben told her.

Kim looked pleased with that answer.

"They're much thinner than the leaves on your tree."

They listened again.

Shhhhh.

From nearby came another sound.

Bup.

Todd hopped onto the dry bank in his yellow boots, with Aunt Pat behind him.

"Hello," Todd said.

"Hello," Wendy replied. "I'm Wendy."

Todd looked at the blue ribbon on her hat.

"Your hat has a little flag."

"It does not always stay in the right place," Wendy said.

At that moment the ribbon fluttered up and brushed her ear.

"There," she added. "It's waving at you."

Todd waved back, just in case.

The friends sat on the grass. Aunt Kay and Grandma May talked with Aunt Pat where they could watch the children.

Wendy told Todd about Windbreak Tree and the Sitting Stone in Far Meadow. Todd told her about the curve of reeds and the yellow boots that always seemed to gather a little mud.

Then Ben opened the fourth page of his blue book.

"We need a picture of two kinds of wind," he said.

Wendy drew a broad branch with one sideways limb, like the Windbreak Tree near her home.

Kim drew tall, thin reeds beside it.

Amy helped Ben make two little swirls in the air above them.

"Which one goes shhh?" Wendy asked.

"This one, over the reeds," replied Ben.

Grandma May wrote flutter under the tree and shhh under the reeds.

Wendy examined the page.

"Now I can remember both places," she said.

"Even when you're at home?" Todd asked.

"Especially then," Wendy said.

The afternoon was nearly over. Aunt Kay and Wendy had a long walk back over Bell Hill, so they said goodbye while there was plenty of daylight.

Wendy held her straw hat as they went.

At home, the ribbon picnic banner was waiting in the cottage window. The Windbreak Tree made its familiar fluttering sound outside.

Wendy listened.

Flutter.

Then she remembered the reeds on the far side of the valley.

Shhh.

She settled into bed with her hat on its peg and the blue ribbon still neatly tied.

The two winds could be different.

And both places could feel like somewhere she knew.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Wendy and Todd have met; Wendy knows Reed Meadow east of Round Pond; fourth sound-book page contrasts fluttering Windbreak Tree leaves and shushing reeds; hat’s blue ribbon remains attached; banner remains at cottage.

**Why this episode exists:** Two familiar places sound different without one being better. The borrowed sound imagery lets earlier places return naturally; not a developmental claim. This is original fiction; no developmental or sleep outcome from listening is claimed.

**Optional prompt:** "Which sound belonged to Far Meadow, and which to Reed Meadow?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
