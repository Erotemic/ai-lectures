# Lila's Nearly Round Biscuit

At the cream-colored cottage in Far Meadow, Lila was helping Aunt Mallow make biscuits.

Aunt Mallow had already mixed the dough.

Lila's job was to choose the shapes.

She made one round biscuit.

She made another round biscuit.

And then she made a biscuit that looked like a very surprised potato.

"That one is for Bo," Lila said.

"Why Bo?" asked Aunt Mallow.

"He appreciates important potatoes."

They laughed.

Lila's straw hat was hanging safely by the kitchen door, its broad blue ribbon still tied around it. In the window, the ribbon picnic banner from their earlier adventure hung where they had left it.

Outside, the light was fading over the meadow.

Lila could see the Windbreak Tree and the path leading back toward Bell Hill.

And above the tree, the Moon was becoming bright enough to notice.

It was more than half bright now.

Not quite round.

"I want a biscuit like that," Lila said.

Aunt Mallow looked out.

"Like the Moon?"

"Just like it."

They made a round biscuit first.

Then Lila asked her aunt to take a little curve away from one side.

Now it was not quite round.

"Almost," Lila said.

Aunt Mallow held it on a small plate beside the window.

Lila looked at the biscuit.

She looked at the Moon.

She looked back at the biscuit.

"The Moon is much farther away," she said.

"And much too big for our oven," said Aunt Mallow.

Lila nodded.

"That explains why it hasn't come inside for a biscuit."

They made a second Moon biscuit.

The edge bent too far and became a crooked hook.

"A moon with a long nose," said Lila.

They made a third one.

It looked like a soft slipper.

"A moon for Rill's foot," she decided.

Rill had two yellow boots, and one had a blue patch. But this slipper-shaped biscuit would not fit either boot.

Aunt Mallow slid the baking tray into the oven. Lila watched from her stool while her aunt handled the hot parts.

There was nothing for Lila to do but wait.

She looked out again.

A small cloud had moved in front of the Moon.

Now the bright shape was partly hidden.

"It's changed!" she said.

Then the cloud moved on.

The bright, nearly round Moon appeared again.

"There you are," Lila said.

The cloud had covered part of the view, but it had not taken a bite from the Moon.

Soon the kitchen smelled of warm biscuits.

Aunt Mallow took them out, set them on a rack, and waited until they were ready to eat.

Lila arranged the finished shapes on a plate.

One moon.

One long-nosed moon.

One moon that looked like a slipper.

And Bo's very important potato.

"Which is your favorite?" Aunt Mallow asked.

Lila considered this difficult question.

She picked the crooked slipper.

It was the least like the real Moon.

It was, however, a very good biscuit.

After supper she carried her little plate to the table, leaving some biscuits for tomorrow.

She did not need to carry any to Willow Bend tonight. The road over Bell Hill was a daytime road for visits with Aunt Mallow, and bedtime was already near.

Lila put on her soft nightclothes.

Her straw hat remained on its hook by the door.

The picnic banner stirred slightly in the open window before Aunt Mallow closed it.

Outside, the Moon shone over Far Meadow.

Inside, the last biscuit crumbs were brushed away.

Lila looked at the Moon one more time.

"Goodnight, almost-round biscuit," she said.

Then she climbed into bed, glad that the Moon could stay in the sky and the biscuits could stay in the kitchen.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Some evenings after first quarter, a waxing gibbous Moon is visible near dusk. Aunt Mallow bakes biscuits with Lila, who chooses crooked Moon, slipper and potato shapes. Some biscuits remain at the cottage for tomorrow; no snack delivery or inter-meadow travel occurs at night. Hat ribbon and banner remain unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** This is chiefly a silly kitchen story. The real Moon appears more than half bright but short of full; the cloud temporarily covering it is not described as a lunar phase.

**Optional prompt:** "Which biscuit looked least like the Moon?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
