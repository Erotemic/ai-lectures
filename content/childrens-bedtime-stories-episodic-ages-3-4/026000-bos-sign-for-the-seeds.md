# Bo's Sign for the Seeds

The next time Bo visited the Story House, he brought a sign.

It was a very large sign.

It was so large that he had to turn sideways to fit through the squeaky door.

Tess was already inside with Grandma Fern. On the table stood two paper packets of seeds, a little pot of pencils, and a smaller piece of cardboard.

Bo unfolded his sign.

It said, in letters Grandma Fern had helped him write:

WELCOME TO THE GARDEN OF ENORMOUS AND EXTREMELY IMPORTANT PLANTS.

Beneath the words he had drawn a carrot as tall as the Story House.

"That's going to be a big carrot," Tess said.

"It's only a picture," Bo replied. "The real carrot might be even bigger."

Grandma Fern looked at the seed packets.

"Today we have radishes and sunflowers. No carrots."

Bo turned the sign over.

"Then the carrot will have to visit another garden."

Outside, the small patch behind the Story House was ready. Grandma Fern had loosened the earth, and the two kinds of seeds would have room in their own rows.

Tess and Bo stood on the dry path beside it.

Grandma Fern opened the first packet.

"These are radish seeds."

Bo stared at the little brown specks in her hand.

"Those? They're not radishes. They're crumbs."

"They are seeds," said Grandma. "The radishes come later, if the plants grow well."

Bo considered that.

"Could I give them a sign that says, 'PLEASE BECOME RADISHES'?"

"You could," said Tess. "But I think they already know what sort of seeds they are."

Grandma made shallow spaces in the prepared soil. The children helped place the little radish seeds where she showed them, with room between each one. Grandma covered them lightly.

Then she opened the sunflower packet.

These seeds were much larger.

"Ah," said Bo. "Now those look like something a bird might pack for a picnic."

"A bird might," Grandma agreed. "We'll put these in the back row, where they will have room later on."

The children helped with those seeds too.

When all the seeds were covered, nothing green was visible.

There was only dark earth, a few small stones along the edges, and Bo's extremely large sign leaning against the wall.

Bo looked from the packet to the earth and back again.

"Do we have to tell them where they are?"

"The seeds don't need a street address," Grandma Fern said. "But the signs could help us remember which row is which."

Tess drew a round red radish with two little leaves on one piece of cardboard.

Bo drew a sunflower with a face like a surprised biscuit on another.

Grandma helped them fasten the labels at the ends of the rows, well away from where the seeds would grow.

Bo held up his enormous sign again.

"It doesn't fit, does it?"

The whole patch was almost hidden behind it.

A visitor could have walked past and thought the Story House was opening a shop that sold only signs.

Tess looked at the smaller cardboard on the table.

"What if we make one that fits the garden?"

Bo was quiet for a moment. Then he nodded.

They drew a small green square, a sun, and two very small leaves. Grandma wrote a short name beneath the pictures.

LITTLE GROWING GARDEN.

"It doesn't mention enormous carrots," Bo said.

"There aren't any enormous carrots," said Tess.

"Yet," said Bo, and they both laughed.

Grandma watered the newly planted rows gently. The earth darkened as the water sank in.

The sign was left on a little peg beside the dry path. Bo's giant sign went home with him, rolled up under one arm like a very important sleeping scroll.

On her next visit to Round Pond, Tess told Nori about the two rows.

Nori listened, turning the white-striped gray pebble inside her violet pouch.

"Are they growing?" Nori asked.

"We can't see anything yet," said Tess.

Nori looked pleased rather than disappointed.

"Then I can come and look closely."

Tess liked that idea.

At bedtime, Bo set the enormous sign beside his bed.

He had made a sign for a garden, and the garden had made him a new one.

Outside, under the spring sky, the small watered patch looked much the same as it had in the morning.

Only now, underneath the earth, there were seeds.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** In mild late-spring conditions, Grandma Fern supervises planting radishes in the front row and sunflowers in the back, with space and two picture labels. Small sign on path-side peg reads LITTLE GROWING GARDEN. Grown-up waters gently. Bo takes oversized carrot sign home; garden has seeds but no visible seedlings.

**Why this episode exists:** The giant sign is mostly a joke. The label and sowing details are ordinary horticultural context; external gardening guidance supports species identification and moisture practices, not this particular garden result.

**Optional prompt:** "Why did Bo make a smaller sign?" Optional, especially at bedtime.

**References and evidence limits:** [Marissa Schuh and Jill MacKenzie, University of Minnesota Extension, *Growing radishes in home gardens*](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-radishes); [University of Minnesota Extension, *Starting seeds indoors*](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/starting-seeds-indoors). These sources support relevant horticultural facts or provide broad adult-guidance context, not evidence that this particular fiction produces a measurable developmental or sleep benefit.

**Single-voice production:** Names and actions identify speakers. No impressions, music or sound effects required. Omit all text outside spoken markers.
