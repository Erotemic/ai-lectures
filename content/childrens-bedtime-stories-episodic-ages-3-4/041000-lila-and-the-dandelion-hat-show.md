# Lila and the Dandelion Hat Show

Lila was waiting by the Windbreak Tree when Mira reached Far Meadow.

Mira had come with Grandpa Rowan along the familiar road through Village Green, west past Blue Door Cottage, and over Bell Hill. They had rested at the bench halfway up.

Now they were on the far side of the hill, where the grass moved in long waves.

Aunt Mallow stood beside Lila with a small basket of oat biscuits.

Lila wore her straw hat with its wide blue ribbon.

"Welcome!" she called.

The breeze lifted the ribbon so that it waved above her head.

Mira stopped and looked at it.

"Your hat is saying hello," she said.

"It does that a lot," Lila replied.

They followed Aunt Mallow to the Sitting Stone, near the Windbreak Tree. It was broad and flat, just right for setting down a basket.

Mira noticed something else near the stone.

A single dandelion stood in the grass.

Its fluffy head looked like a tiny white hat.

"That dandelion is dressed for a party," Mira said.

Lila smiled.

"Then perhaps we should have a hat show."

"For the dandelion?"

"Of course. It's our audience."

Grandpa Rowan helped them find two broad leaves that had already fallen to the ground.

Lila placed one leaf on the Sitting Stone.

"The first hat," she announced, "is called The Very Serious Salad."

Mira set the second leaf beside it.

"And this is The Sleepy Pancake."

The dandelion did not clap.

"A very quiet audience," Lila observed.

"Perhaps it is thinking," Mira said.

The wind came around the tree and lifted the corner of The Sleepy Pancake.

It flipped over on the stone.

"Oh!" Lila said. "It's changed its mind about being a hat."

"Now it's a blanket."

"A very flat blanket."

"For a very flat giant."

The two friends laughed.

They tried another hat made from a fallen twig and a leaf.

It leaned.

Mira had seen a leaning cardboard roof at home. She had heard all about a leaning orange lantern in Willow Bend.

"I think leaning things are very popular around here," she said.

"They are not easy to stack," Lila answered.

When the little hat show was finished, Aunt Mallow offered biscuits. Everyone sat in the shelter of the Windbreak Tree, where the breeze felt softer.

Mira looked across the meadow.

She could see the cream-colored cottage where Lila and Aunt Mallow lived. From its window, the colored picnic banner made by the friends rested safely indoors.

"I like your meadow," Mira said.

"What did your old home look like?" Lila asked.

Mira thought for a moment.

"There was a square window beside my bed. And a doorstep that had a warm spot in the afternoon."

"Do you miss it?"

"Sometimes," Mira said. "But our new window is round. I like looking through it too."

Lila nodded. She didn't try to decide which window was better.

Instead she held up the last oat biscuit.

"Is this one round or square?"

Mira inspected it carefully.

"It is shaped like a biscuit," she said.

"That's a very good shape."

They shared the afternoon with the dandelion and the quiet breeze.

Later, Mira and Grandpa Rowan walked back over Bell Hill while the sun was still high. Lila and Aunt Mallow waved from the meadow.

Mira had brought no hat home, and the dandelion still wore its own fluffy head.

But she had a new story to tell at supper.

She had visited a meadow where a hat could say hello, a leaf could become a sleepy pancake, and one dandelion could attend an entire fashion show without making a sound.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Mira meets Lila on a supervised trip over Bell Hill via existing halfway bench. They stage a pretend leaf-hat show at the established Sitting Stone for a dandelion, using fallen materials, leaving the flower in place. Mira briefly mentions missing her former square window but also liking the new round one. Grandpa Rowan and Mira return over Bell Hill in daylight. Lila's ribbon and picnic banner remain unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** The main attraction is a ridiculous fashion show. An optional conversation about Mira's former home makes room for enjoying a new place and remembering an old one without requiring her to choose a winner.

**Optional prompt:** "Who was the audience at the hat show?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
