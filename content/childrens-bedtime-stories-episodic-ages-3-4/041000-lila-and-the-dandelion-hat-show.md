# Wendy and the Dandelion Hat Show

Wendy was waiting by the Windbreak Tree when Sue reached Far Meadow.

Sue had come with Grandpa Ed along the familiar road through Village Green, west past Blue Door Cottage, and over Bell Hill. They had rested at the bench halfway up.

Now they were on the far side of the hill, where the grass moved in long waves.

Aunt Kay stood beside Wendy with a small basket of oat biscuits.

Wendy wore her straw hat with its wide blue ribbon.

"Welcome!" she called.

The breeze lifted the ribbon so that it waved above her head.

Sue stopped and looked at it.

"Your hat is saying hello," she said.

"It does that a lot," Wendy replied.

They followed Aunt Kay to the Sitting Stone, near the Windbreak Tree. It was broad and flat, just right for setting down a basket.

Sue noticed something else near the stone.

A single dandelion stood in the grass.

Its fluffy head looked like a tiny white hat.

"That dandelion is dressed for a party," Sue said.

Wendy smiled.

"Then perhaps we should have a hat show."

"For the dandelion?"

"Of course. It's our audience."

Grandpa Ed helped them find two broad leaves that had already fallen to the ground.

Wendy placed one leaf on the Sitting Stone.

"The first hat," she announced, "is called The Very Serious Salad."

Sue set the second leaf beside it.

"And this is The Sleepy Pancake."

The dandelion did not clap.

"A very quiet audience," Wendy observed.

"Perhaps it is thinking," Sue said.

The wind came around the tree and lifted the corner of The Sleepy Pancake.

It flipped over on the stone.

"Oh!" Wendy said. "It's changed its mind about being a hat."

"Now it's a blanket."

"A very flat blanket."

"For a very flat giant."

The two friends laughed.

They tried another hat made from a fallen twig and a leaf.

It leaned.

Sue had seen a leaning cardboard roof at home. She had heard all about a leaning orange lantern in Willow Bend.

"I think leaning things are very popular around here," she said.

"Look, this one's nearly falling over," Wendy answered.

When the little hat show was finished, Aunt Kay offered biscuits. Everyone sat in the shelter of the Windbreak Tree, where the breeze felt softer.

Sue looked across the meadow.

She could see the cream-colored cottage where Wendy and Aunt Kay lived. From its window, the colored picnic banner made by the friends rested safely indoors.

"I like your meadow," Sue said.

"What did your old home look like?" Wendy asked.

Sue thought for a moment.

"There was a square window beside my bed. And a doorstep that had a warm spot in the afternoon."

"Do you miss it?"

"Sometimes," Sue said. "But our new window is round. I like looking through it too."

Wendy nodded. She didn't try to decide which window was better.

Instead she held up the last oat biscuit.

"Is this one round or square?"

Sue inspected it carefully.

"It is shaped like a biscuit," she said.

"That's a very good shape."

They shared the afternoon with the dandelion and the quiet breeze.

Later, Sue and Grandpa Ed walked back over Bell Hill while the sun was still high. Wendy and Aunt Kay waved from the meadow.

Sue had brought no hat home, and the dandelion still wore its own fluffy head.

But she had a new story to tell at supper.

She had visited a meadow where a hat could say hello, a leaf could become a sleepy pancake, and one dandelion could attend an entire fashion show without making a sound.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Sue meets Wendy on a supervised trip over Bell Hill via existing halfway bench. They stage a pretend leaf-hat show at the established Sitting Stone for a dandelion, using fallen materials, leaving the flower in place. Sue briefly mentions missing her former square window but also liking the new round one. Grandpa Ed and Sue return over Bell Hill in daylight. Wendy's ribbon and picnic banner remain unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** The main attraction is a ridiculous fashion show. An optional conversation about Sue's former home makes room for enjoying a new place and remembering an old one without requiring her to choose a winner.

**Optional prompt:** "Who was the audience at the hat show?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.
