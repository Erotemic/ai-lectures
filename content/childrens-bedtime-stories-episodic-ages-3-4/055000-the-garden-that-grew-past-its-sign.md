# The Garden That Grew Past Its Sign

Amy was carrying her yellow map through Village Green when Ben came running toward her.

"Come and see," he said. "The garden has done something enormous."

"Enormous like a hill?" asked Amy.

"Not quite. Enormous like a garden that has forgotten it is small."

It had been many weeks since Amy and her friends drew their first picture of the garden behind the Story House. There had been bus rides, walks, warm afternoons, and days when nobody had been able to visit together. Grandma May and the other grown-ups had cared for the patch all the while.

Amy followed Ben around the side of the Story House. The door opened without its old squeak. When Grandma May shut it behind them, the latch made its familiar little click.

Then Amy saw the garden.

Two tall green stems stood where there had once been two tiny sunflower shoots. Broad leaves reached into the sunlight. At the top of one stalk was a round, tightly closed bud, green as a pea. The other stalk was a little shorter.

The sign that said LITTLE GROWING GARDEN was almost hidden behind a leaf.

"That is not little," Amy said.

"I know," Ben told her. "I checked it twice."

Amy looked at her yellow map. On the paper, the garden was still only a square with two tiny green marks.

"The plants have grown," she said, "but my map hasn't."

"You can't water paper," Ben pointed out.

Grandma May said, "You could draw how tall they are now."

Amy looked toward the front row. There were no big radish leaves there anymore, only tidy soil and the old picture label with its red radish drawing.

"Where did the radishes go?"

"We picked them when they were ready, weeks ago," Grandma May explained. "You helped me wash some for lunch, remember? There were round ones and one long, skinny one. The neighbors enjoyed the rest."

Amy remembered the little red circles on the kitchen table and how Ben had called the skinny radish a mouse without ears.

"Did they all go to lunch?" Ben asked.

"Some went to other kitchens," said Grandma May. "And some were too small to eat."

Ben bent down to look at the empty row, but he stayed on the dry path beside the two flat stepping stones Kim had once helped place.

"The radishes have left," he announced. "The sunflowers have taken the upper floor."

Amy imagined a house with red radishes downstairs and a sunflower upstairs, leaning out of a window to ask where its slippers were.

"I hope the upstairs neighbors are quiet," she said.

They stood for a moment beneath the broad leaves. A little breeze moved them. One leaf shaded Ben's nose, then moved away again.

"Good morning," said Ben to the leaf.

It moved again.

Grandma May pointed to the closed round bud. "There's something there that we haven't seen yet."

"A sunflower?" Amy asked.

"The beginning of its flower head. It's not open yet."

Ben looked at the green bud very seriously.

"Maybe it forgot where it put the opening."

"Perhaps it's taking its time," Grandma May said.

Amy drew a taller green mark on a scrap of paper, not on her map. A proper map showed where things were, not necessarily how tall they had become. She decided to keep that difference.

Later that afternoon she opened the LITTLE GROWING GARDEN folder in the Story House with Grandma May. Inside was the picture the friends had made when the plants were still small. She set her new scrap beside it.

The first picture showed two short green shoots. The new scrap showed tall stems and a closed bud.

"They're both right," she said.

"Right for different days," Grandma May agreed.

Amy left the scrap inside the folder, next to the first picture. It wasn't a finished second garden picture, only her little reminder that things had changed.

By bedtime, her yellow map was back beside her pillow. The square for the garden was still small. But Amy knew what was growing inside that square, just behind the Story House.

She pictured the leaves moving gently above Ben's nose, and the closed green bud holding its place at the top.

The garden was not finished growing.

It was simply having another day.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** Two sunflower stems have grown tall; one has a round green bud and the other is shorter. The radishes were harvested by caretakers at their proper time in preceding weeks, some washed and eaten, leaving the front row empty with its red label. The sign is partly hidden by a leaf but has not yet moved. Amy puts a small interim tall-plant sketch into the original LITTLE GROWING GARDEN folder beside the first picture. Map remains unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** The time passage is genuine: radishes are harvested when ready, while sunflowers can still be developing. A comic observation about the small name carries the scene.

**Optional prompt:** "What did the big leaf hide?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Growing radishes in home gardens](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-radishes); [Sunflowers (Helianthus): Home](https://libguides.nybg.org/Sunflowers). These are horticultural facts or general play/conversation guidance; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories. The named characters, places and events are fiction.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
