# A Map for Two Kinds of Places

Amy's yellow map was getting full.

It had Village Green, Pebble Bridge, Round Pond, and the path east to Reed Meadow.

It had the hill to the west, the Sitting Stone in Far Meadow, and the little green circle for Clover Nook.

It had the orchard to the south, the old mill, and the new bend around the Doorway Tree.

All of those places were real places the friends had visited.

But lately, the friends had been visiting another place too.

Button Town.

Button Town was a game they played beside the two-trunk tree. It had a bakery for ants, a tiny post office, a lake the size of a teacup, and a spot where bells were not needed.

Amy wanted to remember it all.

On a calm afternoon, Grandma May brought a large sheet of plain paper to the Story House.

Ben was already there, wearing his red scarf. His tilted orange lantern with silver spots still hung by the window.

On the low shelf stood the completed blue Book of Little Sounds, just where Ben had left it after the friends finished its sixth page.

Kim arrived from Round Pond carrying her violet pouch.

Wendy came over Bell Hill with Aunt Kay. Her straw hat still had its blue ribbon.

Todd came from the east with Aunt Pat, his two yellow boots making small steps across Pebble Bridge. One boot wore its blue patch.

All five friends had played in Button Town now.

Amy spread her yellow map on the table.

"This map shows where we walk," she said.

Then Grandma May spread out the plain paper beside it.

"And what would you like this one to show?" she asked.

"Where we pretend," Amy said.

Ben leaned over the blank sheet.

"We need a very tall doorway."

He drew two wobbly brown trunks.

Wendy added a small round bakery. Its roof looked rather like a hat.

"That bakery makes bread for ants," she explained.

"And invisible socks," Ben added.

"That's the shop next door," Amy reminded him.

Kim drew a little basket for the post office.

"It had very quiet wheels," she said.

"Slipper wheels," Ben agreed.

Todd took the blue pencil and drew the smallest lake anyone had ever put on a map.

It was one blue circle.

"Lake Teacup," he said.

"The real puddle might have dried," Amy said, "but there's room for a lake here."

Todd drew one small boat on the circle. Inside it was a sleepy beetle with a pillow.

Grandma May looked at the drawing.

"A very comfortable captain."

Kim found an empty corner on the paper, close to the imaginary tree.

She drew a green patch and a tiny pillow.

"The Little Quiet Place," she said.

Ben examined it carefully.

"Should I draw the bell beside it?"

Kim shook her head.

"Maybe a little farther away."

Ben drew his imaginary bell on the other side of Button Town, next to an enormous shop selling socks for clouds.

Wendy gave the shop a striped awning.

Todd gave it a tiny front step.

Amy drew one winding street connecting everything.

"That street looks like a noodle," Ben said.

"A noodle road," said Wendy.

"A noodle road with no noodles on it," said Todd.

They all liked the sound of that.

When the drawing was finished, it looked nothing like the real orchard.

It did not need to.

Amy laid the two maps side by side: the yellow one showing the places they knew, and the new one showing Button Town as they had imagined it together.

"This one helps us find our way," Amy said, touching the yellow map.

"And this one helps us find our game," said Ben.

Grandma May brought the flat brown envelope from the Blue Door Cottage. Inside were the little drawings of the lantern and the Blue Door Cottage. The card for Todd had already gone home with him. Amy took the small blue-circle ticket from her yellow map and added it to the envelope, along with the Lake Teacup drawing and the new folded Button Town map.

The grown-ups helped put the envelope on the low Story House shelf, beside the blue Book of Little Sounds.

Both the book and the envelope could be borrowed again with permission.

Ben looked up at his tilted orange lantern, still hanging above the shelf.

"I wonder whether Button Town needs a lantern."

"Maybe another day," said Amy.

For now, the map was finished, the pretend post office was closed, and all the visitors had somewhere warm to go.

Wendy and Aunt Kay took the familiar way west over Bell Hill. Todd and Aunt Pat went east across Pebble Bridge. Kim went home by Round Pond.

Amy and Grandma May walked to the Blue Door Cottage.

Before bed, Amy folded her real yellow map and set it in its familiar place.

The tiny bend around the Doorway Tree was still there.

Outside, the valley settled into evening.

The orchard was an orchard. The tree was a tree. The Story House kept the friends' paper pictures safe.

And Button Town, with its slipper-wheeled post office and its one-blue-circle lake, was ready to be imagined again whenever the friends wanted it.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** At Story House all five children draw a complete separate Button Town pretend map with Doorway Tree, bakery, post office, Lake Teacup and the Little Quiet Place. Grandma May places that map, the visitor ticket and remaining paper letters in the brown envelope on the same low shelf beside Ben’s blue book. Todd already has his delivered card. Amy keeps the accurate real yellow map at home. All friends return home safely; no event is pending.

**Why this episode exists:** The final scene preserves separate, intentionally useful representations of physical geography and shared make-believe. It rewards revisiting the existing world with comedy and closure; neither shared reading nor pretend-play references establish causal benefits from this audio fiction. It is original fiction; no developmental or sleep outcome from listening is claimed.

**Optional prompt:** "Which map would Amy use to find the real orchard path?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
