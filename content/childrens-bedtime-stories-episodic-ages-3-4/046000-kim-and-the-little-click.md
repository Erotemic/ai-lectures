# Kim and the Little Click

Kim knew the Story House by its sounds.

There used to be a squeak when someone opened the door. The window sometimes rattled in the wind. A foot on the wooden floor made a gentle tap.

And when Ben laughed, it was difficult to hear anything else for a moment.

On an afternoon when the sky was bright after rain, Kim came from Round Pond with her grown-up. Todd and Aunt Pat met them near Pebble Bridge.

Todd wore both his yellow boots. One still had the blue patch on its side.

"Do you want to hear my new noise?" Todd asked.

Kim thought about that. "Is it a loud one?"

"Not very."

Todd lifted one foot and set it down on the damp path.

Shup.

He lifted the other.

Shup.

"That's your boots," Kim said.

"I know," Todd replied. "They have been practicing."

They walked west across Pebble Bridge with their grown-ups and arrived at Village Green. Grandma May was standing in the open Story House doorway.

The door made no squeak at all.

Kim looked at it. She had heard about Grandpa Ed repairing the hinge, but she hadn't visited since.

"It's very quiet now," she said.

"And easier to open," said Grandma May.

Todd looked at his boots. "I could lend it a shup."

"Then it could go click-shup!" Kim said.

They went inside to look at the garden picture on the low shelf. The little plants in the garden behind the house had more leaves than they had when the picture was made.

Kim still had her violet pouch. Inside were exactly three pebbles, including the round gray one with a white stripe. She liked knowing where they were, and today she left them tucked safely in the pouch.

Todd spotted the tilted orange lantern by the window.

"That has a good shape," he said.

"Ben made it," Kim answered. "He wanted it straight at first."

Todd tilted his head to match the lantern.

Then he tilted the other way.

"I think the lantern has won."

Kim smiled. She watched Grandma May move a chair near the table. Its legs made the softest scrape across the floor.

A breeze brushed the window curtain.

Fff.

Outside, a leaf moved against the garden fence.

Tap.

The Story House was still full of little sounds. They were simply harder to notice when the door used to announce everyone with one great creak.

Grandma May went to bring a basket in from the step. Kim stood beside her, with her grown-up nearby, as Grandma gently shut the door.

Click.

Kim lifted her ears.

"There," she said.

Grandma May turned toward her. "There what?"

"The door still has a sound. It's just a very small one."

Todd listened while Grandma May opened and closed the door once more.

Click.

"That's a much tidier noise than my boots," he said.

To prove it, he took one step on the dry floor.

Nothing.

"Oh," Todd said. "They only practice outdoors."

Kim laughed so hard that she had to sit for a moment on the low bench.

After a little while, she and Todd walked home with their grown-ups. At Pebble Bridge they parted, Kim toward Round Pond and Todd along the dry path to Reed Meadow.

That evening Kim set her violet pouch by her bed. The three pebbles were still inside.

She remembered the old squeak. She remembered the new click. She remembered Todd's boots saying shup only when they were damp.

There were enough sounds for one day.

Outside, the pond grew still, and Kim settled down to sleep.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Kim has personally heard the new latch click, Todd jokes that his boots say shup only when damp. Kim retains all three pebbles, Todd retains both yellow boots, and no finished Book of Little Sounds page or other shelf item is changed. They return east with their grown-ups, then part at Round Pond.

**Why this episode exists:** Kim gets an attentive, funny experience that does not reduce her to shyness; Todd's boot joke belongs to his established personality. The sound-book remains complete and untouched.

**Optional prompt:** "Which sound came from the door, and which came from Todd's boots?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Building Social and Emotional Skills at Home](https://www.naeyc.org/our-work/families/building-social-emotional-skills-at-home); [Education Resources — Natural Sounds](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/sound/education-resources.htm). These provide broader editorial or caregiver context, not evidence of benefits caused by these fictional episodes.

**Single-voice production:** Narration only between the spoken markers. No character voices, effects or music are required.
