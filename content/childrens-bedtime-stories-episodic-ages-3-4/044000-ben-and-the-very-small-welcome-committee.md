# Ben and the Very Small Welcome Committee

Ben had a problem with the Story House door.

It opened beautifully now. Grandpa Ed had helped fix the stiff hinge, and everybody could come and go without pushing hard.

But when a visitor arrived, the door no longer made its long squeak.

Ben missed knowing that someone was there.

"Maybe," he told Grandma May, "we need a welcome committee."

"What sort?" Grandma May asked.

Ben looked around Village Green.

There was a bench, a fountain, and a particularly round pebble sitting in the dust by his foot.

"A very small one," he said.

He picked up the pebble and set it on the bench.

"This is the chairman."

Amy had come over from Blue Door Cottage to visit the Story House garden. She peered at the pebble.

"What does the chairman do?"

"Welcomes people."

"It's facing the wrong way."

Ben turned the pebble around.

Then he found a leaf with a long stem and placed it beside the chairman.

"This is the assistant chairman."

A breeze lifted the leaf and carried it halfway across the Green.

"Your assistant has gone on a trip," Amy observed.

"Important business," Ben said.

They retrieved the leaf from the grass. It had not gone far.

Ben arranged the two committee members carefully on the bench.

The pebble sat quietly.

The leaf sat quietly.

The door sat quietly.

A beetle walked past all three of them without stopping.

"The committee is not working," Ben whispered.

Grandma May came out with a little basket. "What would you like a visitor to know?"

"That we're happy they came," said Ben.

"You could tell them," Amy suggested.

Ben considered this. It seemed far too simple for a committee.

Then Sue and Grandpa Ed appeared on the orchard path.

Ben straightened his red scarf. He stood beside the silent door, raised one paw, and gave a grand, slow wave.

His scarf slipped over his nose.

He could not see Sue.

"Welcome!" he called into the scarf.

"Thank you!" Sue called back.

Ben tugged the scarf down.

"I was testing a new kind of wave."

"The invisible one?" said Sue.

"Yes. Very advanced."

Sue tried it too, lifting her sleeve just enough to hide one eye. Amy raised both ears and wiggled them. Grandpa Ed gave a tiny wave with two fingers.

Before long, everyone had a different wave.

Ben liked the one where he wiggled his whole paw as though he were shaking water off it.

He named it the wibble-wave.

They went inside. The tilted orange lantern still watched from beside the window, and the low shelf was just as they had left it.

Grandma May set out a few picture books to look through. When Ben was finished, he carried his pebble back to the edge of the path and placed the leaf under its tree.

"What happened to the welcome committee?" Amy asked.

"It retired," said Ben. "Excellent service."

At the end of the afternoon, the door opened quietly for Sue and Grandpa Ed, and quietly again for Amy and Grandma May.

Ben stood by the doorway and tried one last wibble-wave.

This time his scarf stayed out of his face.

He went home quite pleased with himself.

The Story House was quiet behind him, and the village had several new ways to say hello.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Ben invents a pretend welcome committee of a found pebble and leaf, then returns them to the path/tree after the afternoon. Ben coins the wibble-wave, which Sue, Amy and Grandpa Ed have seen. No sign, bench prop or new shelf item remains. Story House door still opens quietly.

**Why this episode exists:** A deliberately absurd attempt to organize two inanimate committee members gives Ben a distinct comic failure and a satisfying local resolution.

**Optional prompt:** "Who were the two committee members?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2058); [Message in a Backpack: Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321). These provide broader editorial or caregiver context, not evidence of benefits caused by these fictional episodes.

**Single-voice production:** Narration only between the spoken markers. No character voices, effects or music are required.
