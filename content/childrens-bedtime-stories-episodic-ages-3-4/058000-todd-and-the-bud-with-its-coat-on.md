# Todd and the Bud with Its Coat On

Todd had brought a drawing to Village Green.

He held it carefully between both paws, though the paper kept trying to fold in the middle. His yellow boots made little shup sounds on the damp path. The boot with the blue patch made exactly the same sound as the other boot, which Todd thought was a missed opportunity.

Aunt Pat walked with him past the Story House. Grandma May was already behind the house, looking at the garden.

"I brought my old picture," Todd said.

"The one you drew when the first radishes came up?" asked Grandma May.

"Yes. It has three sprouts and one enormous boot."

"I remember that boot," said Grandma May. "It was almost as big as the garden."

Todd laid the drawing on the dry viewing stone and looked up.

The sunflowers were taller now. One had a round green bud held above its leaves. The second had a smaller bud, still tight and green.

Todd blinked.

"My drawing has very short plants."

"It was right when you made it," Aunt Pat said.

"Then I need a much bigger piece of paper."

Todd held the old sheet up beside the tall stem. The paper reached only a little way up.

"Perhaps a paper as tall as the mill," he said.

"That might be awkward to carry home," Grandma May replied.

Todd looked closely at the round bud.

"What is that?"

"The sunflower's flower head, still closed," Grandma May said. "The yellow parts haven't opened out yet."

"It's wearing a green coat."

"It does look that way."

"And the other one has a smaller coat."

Aunt Pat nodded. "They haven't grown at exactly the same speed."

Todd imagined two sunflowers getting ready to go outside. One wore a big green coat with a hundred buttons. The other wore a smaller coat and could not find its shoes.

He looked down at his boots.

"You can't borrow my boots," he told the plants. "They're too muddy."

The plants did not seem troubled by this.

While the grown-ups checked the soil, Todd watched a little breeze brush the taller stem. Its leaves trembled, but the bud stayed closed.

"Perhaps it needs a knock," Todd said.

"Not on the plant," Aunt Pat reminded him gently.

"I know," said Todd. He knocked very softly on his own knee instead.

Tap, tap.

"Hello, flower. No hurry."

He waited.

The bud did not open.

Todd considered knocking on the other knee, but decided that the plant probably couldn't hear knees anyway.

Aunt Pat helped him turn over his drawing. The back was blank, except for a faint smudge from his boot.

"You could draw what you see today," she suggested.

"On this little paper?"

"You can draw just the part you're looking at."

Todd took his pencil. He drew a round green circle with little points around it. He added a thick stem below, then a tiny pair of boots at the bottom.

"Do sunflowers wear boots?" Grandma May asked.

"Only in my drawing."

A few days later, Todd returned on another walk with Aunt Pat. The tall bud had grown plumper. At one edge, between the green folds, he could just see a narrow pale yellow line.

Todd leaned forward from the dry path, hands behind his back.

"There's something yellow in there!"

"Yes," said Aunt Pat. "The flower is getting ready to open."

Todd looked at his own bright boots, then at the tiny hint of yellow.

"It's going to be yellow, like my boots!" he said.

He didn't try to help the petals along. He drew one thin yellow stroke on his little picture, then tucked both drawings into his bag.

Back at his home near Reed Meadow, Todd showed Aunt Pat's kitchen table the two versions: three new sprouts on one side of the paper, and a green bud with a yellow edge on the other.

"One garden," he said. "Two different days."

Aunt Pat put the pictures where they could dry flat.

At bedtime Todd set his patched boot beside the unpatched one. They stood together, toes pointed toward the door.

Somewhere beyond Pebble Bridge, the sunflower was still growing at its own pace.

Todd was quite happy to leave it to that.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** Across two visits with Aunt Pat, Todd compares the old drawing with present buds and makes a second drawing on the back of the same sheet. Several days later the larger bud shows a thin pale-yellow edge within its folds; it is not yet open. Todd takes his drawings home to Reed Meadow. No garden plant is handled or forced open.

**Why this episode exists:** The flower does not open on command, and comparing a past drawing with a present observation makes the time progression audible without a lesson speech.

**Optional prompt:** "What color did Todd finally see inside the green bud?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Sunflowers (Helianthus): Home](https://libguides.nybg.org/Sunflowers). These are horticultural facts or general play/conversation guidance; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories. The named characters, places and events are fiction.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
