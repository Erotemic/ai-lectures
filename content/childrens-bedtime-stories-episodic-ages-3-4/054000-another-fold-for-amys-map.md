# 054000 - Another Fold for Amy’s Map

# Another Fold for Amy's Map

Amy's yellow map had been folded so many times that its creases were starting to feel soft.

It showed Blue Door Cottage, the Village Green, the Story House with its old squeak curl and new blue click, Pebble Bridge, Round Pond, Reed Meadow, the orchard, the Doorway Tree, and Bell Hill.

It showed Far Meadow too.

But it did not show Market Street.

Amy had drawn a tiny green bus on a loose scrap of paper. She had kept the scrap beside her bed, waiting until she knew where the next town belonged.

"I don't have to put every shop on it," she told Grandma May. "But I want the bus to go somewhere."

Grandma May smiled. "Perhaps today's visit will help you decide."

The six friends had arranged to spend the morning on Market Street with their grown-ups. It was not a parade or a celebration. They simply wanted to see the place together.

Some came from the east. Todd and Aunt Pat walked from Reed Meadow by Round Pond and across Pebble Bridge. Kim came with her grown-up, carrying a borrowed picture book to return.

Sue and Grandpa Ed came up the orchard path to meet them near the Village Green. Ben wore his red scarf, with his grown-up beside him. Amy and Grandma May joined the group by Blue Door Cottage.

They stopped to rest on Bell Hill before continuing to Wendy's cream cottage in Far Meadow, where Wendy and Aunt Kay were waiting. Then everyone walked to the green bus stop on the paved road beyond the meadow.

The bus came humming around the bend.

Psssh.

"It's saying hello," Wendy said.

"Very softly," said Todd.

"For such a big bus," said Ben.

The grown-ups helped everyone find seats. Ben held his scarf close. Sue looked for the clock through the window. Kim kept the borrowed book safely against her lap.

At Market Street they stayed together on the broad pavement. Amy took out her loose scrap and drew the green bus stop beside a very small square.

"What's the square?" asked Kim.

"Market Street, until I draw it better."

Ben pointed toward the pigeons beside the bench.

"My parade is here!"

A pigeon was standing still with one foot lifted.

"The drummer is taking a break," Ben whispered.

Todd looked toward the bakery.

"That's where I saw a bear through my bread."

"And I saw a frog!" Ben said.

"In yellow boots!"

Sue pointed at the shop clock.

"Mine has pockets. That one still doesn't."

Wendy tipped her hat toward the flower stall. One purple flower hung over the rim of its bucket as though it wanted to hear the conversation.

"It's still wearing its big skirt," she said.

Kim held up her borrowed book. "I'm going to take this back before we forget."

With her grown-up, she went to the library's return desk. The librarian checked in *The Loud Blue Sock*, and Kim watched it go onto a cart to be put away.

"Goodbye, sock," she whispered. "Don't drive too fast."

When Kim came out again, the others were looking at the painted books in the big library window.

"They look different from inside," she told them.

"Backwards," Sue remembered.

"And here they're looking at us," Kim said.

They found a bench and ate the food the grown-ups had brought. Nobody tried to feed the pigeons, who were perfectly busy with their own pigeon business.

Amy unfolded her yellow map on her lap. Wendy pointed to Far Meadow. Kim found Round Pond. Todd put one yellow boot beside the tiny garden square, careful not to cover it.

"Where's Market Street?" Ben asked.

Amy opened the loose scrap beside the western edge of the map.

"Here," she said. "Past Wendy's meadow, after the bus stop."

Grandma May looked closely. "The bus follows the road, not your walking path over Bell Hill. We can mark the two kinds of travel differently."

Amy thought of her real map and the separate pretend map of Button Town. This time she wasn't inventing a road. She was recording one she had ridden along.

"Maybe a green line for the bus," she said.

"And a circle for the clock," said Sue.

"And a bird," said Ben.

"And a book," said Kim.

"And a bread window," said Todd.

"And a flower with a skirt," added Wendy.

Amy looked down at the small scrap.

"We're going to need a bigger scrap."

They laughed, and soon it was time to catch the bus back.

In the afternoon, after everyone had returned home with their grown-ups, Grandma May laid Amy's map on the cottage table. She brought out a fresh piece of yellow paper.

Together they made a fold-out flap at the map's western edge. Amy drew the paved-road bus stop beyond Far Meadow, then a green line that ended at Market Street. The line was clearly marked BUS, not a walking shortcut.

She drew the shop clock, the bakery, the library, and a bench with a pigeon on it. Wendy's flowers became three colored dots.

There was no room for every bird, every shop, or every crooked fence post.

Amy didn't mind.

The map showed enough to find her way back with a grown-up. And it still had space for things she had not seen.

That evening she folded the new flap over the old map. It fitted a little awkwardly, like a paper coat with one extra sleeve.

Grandma May tucked her into bed. Amy put the map beside her pillow, with the new folded edge facing up.

She could remember the green bus, the pigeons, the shop clock, and the big window full of books.

And she could remember her own front door, waiting close by at Blue Door Cottage.

The paper made one soft rustle as she set it down.

Tomorrow the map would still be there, with room for the rest of the world.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** All six children visit Market Street with their grown-ups. Kim and her grown-up return The Loud Blue Sock to the public library. Each child remembers a real market landmark. At home Grandma May helps Amy attach a new yellow fold-out flap to the west edge of her physical map, distinguishing the green bus line on the paved road from the walkable path over Bell Hill. Market Street’s bakery, shop clock, library, pigeon bench and flower stall become real map marks. The map goes beside Amy’s bed; the three Story House collections and repaired door, garden, lantern, pouch, hat, boots, and Sue’s bakery stay as before. There is no pending event.

**Why this episode exists:** The world grows in a documented, geographically coherent way; the earlier borrowed book is returned on the page, and the ending rests at home rather than launching a cliffhanger.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Amy add to the edge of her yellow map?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Message in a Backpack: Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321); [The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2058). These give general reading, play or conversational context; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories.

**Single-voice production:** Narration only between the spoken markers. No character voices, effects or music are required.
