# Ben and the Sign That Went Missing

The next morning Ben marched into Village Green wearing his red scarf and a very important expression.

"I have come to read the sign," he announced.

"Which sign?" asked Sue, who was walking with Grandpa Ed from Round Window Cottage.

"The garden sign. I read it every time I visit."

Sue tilted her head. "What does it say?"

"LITTLE GROWING GARDEN. At least it used to."

They went around the Story House together. Grandpa Ed opened the newly repaired door first to let Grandma May know they were there. The door opened quietly, and when he shut it, the latch made its small click.

Behind the house, the two sunflowers swayed above the stone-edged patch.

Ben pointed at the sign.

Or he tried to.

He could see its wooden peg and one corner of the board. A broad sunflower leaf hung directly in front of the words.

"It's gone," Ben whispered.

"The board is still there," Sue said.

"The words have disappeared."

"I think they're on the other side of that leaf."

Ben went one step to the left along the dry path. Now he could read GARDEN, but not the rest. He stepped right. He could read LITTLE, but not GARDEN.

"It is a sign with moving words," he said.

Sue looked up. "Or a leaf with moving elbows."

The wind shifted. For one moment the whole sign appeared.

LITTLE GROWING GARDEN.

Then the leaf covered it again.

"It's playing peekaboo," Sue said.

Ben gave his wibble-wave, the silly greeting he had invented when the Story House door became quiet. The leaf did not give it back. It merely wobbled in the breeze.

"Very shy sign," Ben said.

"It's not shy," said Sue. "It's busy growing behind a sunflower."

They tried reading it with their heads tipped sideways. They tried standing on the two dry viewing stones, which were only a tiny bit higher than the path. They tried crouching, though Ben's scarf kept falling over his nose.

Grandpa Ed watched from beside the stone border.

"We could move the sign," he suggested. "It doesn't have roots like the sunflower."

Ben looked suddenly concerned. "Would the sign mind?"

"You can ask it," said Sue.

Ben knelt on the path, facing the hidden board.

"Dear sign," he said, "would you like to stand somewhere people can actually read you?"

There was no answer.

"It is considering," he whispered.

Grandpa Ed and Grandma May chose a clear place a little farther along the same dry approach, where the board wouldn't cover the growing plants or narrow the path. The grown-ups lifted the sign and its peg together.

Ben and Sue watched from the viewing stones.

When Grandpa Ed had settled the sign securely at the new spot, Ben read every word aloud.

"LITTLE. GROWING. GARDEN."

"It still says little," Sue said, looking up at the tall leaves.

"It's the garden's name," Ben replied. "A name can stay even when someone gets taller."

Sue thought about that.

"My castle bakery is still called a bakery, even though nobody has baked a real loaf in it."

"And I am still Ben," Ben said, "even when I stand on this very tall stone."

The stone was not tall at all.

That made Sue laugh so hard she sat down on the grass beside the path.

A breeze came through the garden. The tallest leaf turned toward the place where the sign had stood before. Then it turned away again.

"It looks like the sunflower is checking where its sign went," Sue said.

"We'll tell it," Ben replied.

He looked up at the plant. "Your sign has moved two steps. You don't have to go looking for it."

On the way home, Sue told Grandpa Ed that the sunflowers were much better at growing than at reading. Ben told his grown-up that he had rescued a sign without moving a single flower.

Behind the Story House the sign now stood where anyone on the dry path could see it, and the sunflower leaves had room to sway.

At bedtime Ben folded his red scarf across the end of his bed. He thought of the board standing in its new place.

LITTLE GROWING GARDEN.

The words were still the same. It was just easier to see them now.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** With Grandma May present, Grandpa Ed relocates the existing garden sign and peg a short distance along the same dry approach, outside the sunflower leaves and without narrowing the path or touching the plants. Sue and Ben visit together and joke about the sign. No other permanent props or plants change.

**Why this episode exists:** A garden object changes through a practical, small adult action; Ben and Sue supply the story through imaginative observations, not a moral.

**Optional prompt:** "Why did the sign look like its words had vanished?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2058); [Message in a Backpack: Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321). These are horticultural facts or general play/conversation guidance; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories. The named characters, places and events are fiction.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
