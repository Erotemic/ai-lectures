# Kim and Button Town's Quietest Place

One afternoon, Amy unfolded her yellow map on the table at the Blue Door Cottage.

Inside its folds was the tiny blue-circle ticket from Wendy's first visit to Button Town.

Beside it lay the brown envelope from the pretend post office and a little drawing marked Lake Teacup.

Ben had brought a new idea.

"Every town should have a bell," he announced.

"Why?" Amy asked.

"So everybody knows when it is time for something very important."

"Like what?"

"Like putting on your socks."

Amy laughed.

Kim had arrived from Round Pond. She looked at Ben thoughtfully.

"I already know when it's time for socks," she said. "My toes tell me."

Ben admitted that toes were useful.

Grandma May took the friends to the orchard, and Aunt Kay brought Wendy down from Far Meadow to meet them there.

Wendy wore her familiar straw hat, with its blue ribbon safely tied on.

The Doorway Tree spread its branches over the path.

"Welcome to Button Town!" said Ben.

He held up a pretend bell between two empty paws.

"Ding! Time to wear your socks."

Wendy looked down at her hooves.

"I don't have any socks."

"Then your first pair is free," said Ben.

"Invisible?"

"Naturally."

Wendy pretended to put on four invisible socks, one for each hoof.

She walked like someone wearing very slippery socks. Then she remembered that imaginary socks could be whatever she liked.

She made them snug.

Amy showed Wendy the spot where Ben had pretended to deliver letters.

Then they visited the place where a shallow puddle had been after the rain. The ground was drier now, and Lake Teacup had become a patch of ordinary earth.

"Oh," Ben said. "Our lake has gone visiting."

"It may come back with rain," Amy said.

"And it can stay on our pretend map," said Wendy.

Kim stood beside a little grassy patch on the south side of the Doorway Tree, open to the path and easy for Grandma May to see.

It was quiet there.

Not silent. The leaves still rustled, and a bird sang somewhere beyond the orchard.

But it was away from Ben's imaginary bell.

"I like this part," Kim said.

Ben began to ring his pretend bell again.

"Ding! The town needs a—"

"Could this part be a place without bells?" Kim asked.

Ben stopped, his paws still in the air.

"A place with no bells at all?"

"Just this little bit," Kim said. "I could still hear the leaves."

Amy looked around at the patch of grass.

"We could call it the Little Quiet Place."

"What happens there?" Wendy asked.

Kim considered.

"You can sit. Or look. Or not say anything for a while."

Ben scratched one ear.

"Can you wear invisible socks there?"

"Of course," Kim said.

"Even the slippery ones?"

"Especially the snug ones," said Wendy.

Ben decided to open an imaginary shop at the edge of the Little Quiet Place.

It sold invisible pillows.

He offered one to Amy.

It was so soft she had to pretend to catch it before it floated away.

He offered another to Wendy.

Wendy tucked hers beneath her big straw hat.

Then Ben offered one to Kim.

Kim took the pillow in her paws, pretended to fluff it once, and settled down in the grass.

For a little while, they sat together.

The leaves made their ordinary soft rustling sound.

Ben's pretend bell had nothing urgent to announce.

Before leaving, Amy drew a small green patch on a separate Button Town sketch. Beside it, she made a little pillow shape.

"Now we can remember where the quiet place belongs," she said.

They walked back north with the adults, carrying all their paper things with them.

At home, Kim counted the three pebbles in her violet pouch. The round gray one still had its white stripe.

She set the pouch beside her bed and smiled at the thought of a town where even invisible pillows had somewhere to rest.

And in the orchard, the grassy patch waited quietly beneath the wide branches.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** The real puddle area is now mostly dry; the pretend lake remains a known feature. Kim names a visible grassy spot south of the Doorway Tree the Little Quiet Place. Its pillow icon enters a separate Button Town sketch kept with the other papers at Blue Door Cottage. No signs are left outside.

**Why this episode exists:** Kim asks for a quieter part of a shared game while others continue the silly town. The compromise is shown through invented pillow shop and imagined bell; related social/play guidance is contextual, not a prescription. It is original fiction; no developmental or sleep outcome from listening is claimed.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Ben sell near the Little Quiet Place?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
