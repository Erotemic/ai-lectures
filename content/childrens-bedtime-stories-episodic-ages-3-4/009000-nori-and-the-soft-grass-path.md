# Kim and the Soft-Grass Path

There were two ways to reach the Sitting Stone in Far Meadow.

Amy knew the first way.

It went across Bell Hill, along the wide meadow path, and straight toward the great Windbreak Tree.

The path was easy to see. It had short grass and little patches of pale earth.

But one morning, Wendy the lamb had something else to show her friends.

"There's another way," she said.

Wendy stood near her cream-colored cottage, with Aunt Kay close by. Her straw hat had a blue ribbon that fluttered when the breeze passed.

Amy, Ben, and Kim had come with Grandma May for a little morning walk.

Ben wore his red scarf. Amy carried her yellow map. Kim had her violet pouch tucked safely at her side.

"Another way where?" Ben asked.

"To the same stone," Wendy said. "But around the back of the tree."

Ben looked at the Sitting Stone, which lay in a sunny patch of grass.

"Does the stone know it has two ways?"

"I don't think it minds," said Wendy.

Just then, some neighbors walked along the wide path, talking about baskets and sandwiches and a picnic they might have someday.

Ben waved to them.

Kim watched them pass.

"I'd like to try the softer path," she said.

"Me too," said Amy.

"Me three," Ben said.

"Then let's have a look," said Grandma May.

Wendy led them around the Windbreak Tree. Aunt Kay came along as well.

Behind its wide trunk, the air was quieter. The tall grass made a little wall on one side, and a few low clover plants spread across the open ground.

It was a small, sheltered patch in plain view of the adults beside the tree.

"I call it Clover Nook," Wendy said.

Kim stopped.

She could hear a bee moving among the flowers farther away.

She could hear the grasses brushing softly.

And, very faintly, she could hear Ben's scarf moving against his coat.

"Sff. Sff," she whispered.

Ben held his scarf very still.

"Is it telling you something?" he asked.

"It says you are wearing a scarf," Kim said.

Ben nodded. "It got that right!"

They all sat in the clover for a little while.

Amy opened the yellow map.

"Should I draw this place?" she asked.

Wendy looked at Kim.

"Do you want it on the map?"

Kim thought.

"A small green circle," she said. "Not a giant one."

Amy drew a small green circle behind the tree.

"Like this?"

"Yes," said Kim. "That's just right."

Ben examined the circle.

"It looks like a pea," he said.

"Then it is a very comfortable pea," said Amy.

After a while, Wendy showed them how the soft-grass path came out beside the Sitting Stone.

Ben took three extra-large steps and sat down on it.

"We have arrived," he announced.

"In the same place," Amy said.

"By a different way," Kim added.

Aunt Kay opened her basket and offered everyone a little drink of water.

The friends watched a cloud cross the bright meadow. Its shadow slid over the grass, past the Sitting Stone, and away.

Then the grown-ups said it was time to head back.

Wendy waved from the path to her cottage.

"Come again," she called.

"We will," said Amy.

Kim chose the soft-grass path for the first part of the walk. Ben took the wide path beside Grandma May, and they met again where the paths joined. Everyone could see one another the whole time.

Nobody had to choose the same way to arrive at the same place.

That evening, Amy placed her yellow map beside the bed.

Far Meadow had a leaning tree, a Sitting Stone with a tiny spoon beside it, and now a small green circle.

It was a good little circle.

Kim, at home near Round Pond, put her pouch beside her blanket. She counted her three pebbles, including the round gray one with the white stripe.

One.

Two.

Three.

Outside her window, the leaves hardly moved at all.

The quiet of Clover Nook seemed to have followed her home.

And Kim fell asleep thinking of the soft grass under the tree.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Clover Nook named; Amy adds its small green circle to the map; Kim is comfortable choosing a quieter path.

**Why this episode exists:** A small ordinary choice stays valid without making anyone wrong. The sheltered place gives the ongoing world a different pace and a concrete location to revisit. No developmental or sleep improvement from listening is asserted.

**Optional prompt:** "Would you visit the Sitting Stone or Clover Nook first?" This is optional, especially at bedtime.
