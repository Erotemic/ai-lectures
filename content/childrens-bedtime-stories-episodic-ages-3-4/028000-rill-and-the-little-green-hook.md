# Todd and the Little Green Hook

Several mornings after Kim's visits, Amy met Todd at Pebble Bridge.

He was coming from Reed Meadow with Aunt Pat.

His yellow boots were clean at the toes and muddy at the heels. The left one still had its blue patch.

"I have come to inspect the garden," Todd said.

"I didn't know you were a garden inspector," Amy replied.

"Neither did I until breakfast."

He gave a little bup, as though a frog could put a full stop at the end of a sentence.

Grandma May joined them near Village Green, and together they went around to the sunny patch behind the Story House.

Ben's small sign stood beside the dry path.

The flat stepping stones were where Kim had helped place them.

And in the first row, something green was showing.

It was not very tall.

It was not even as tall as the word LITTLE on the sign.

It looked like a small, bent hook, with its tip still close to the earth.

Todd leaned forward, staying on the stepping stone.

"A tiny green question mark," he whispered.

"I thought it might be a worm with a leaf for a hat," Amy said.

Grandma crouched down to look.

"That's a radish seedling beginning to come up."

"A radish?" Todd said. "Where is the round red part?"

"Still growing below the ground," Grandma answered. "The first green parts have only just appeared."

Todd looked again.

There were two more tiny green shapes nearby. One leaned to the left. One leaned to the right. The first was still curled like a hook.

"Three small guests," said Todd.

He held up one boot and wiggled it in greeting.

"Welcome to the garden. Please leave your boots at home."

Amy giggled.

Aunt Pat brought Todd a small piece of paper and a blue pencil from her bag. Todd made three green marks on it.

The first looked like a hook.

The second looked like a sleepy bird.

The third looked like a squashed letter V.

He proudly showed the page to Grandma May.

"It's an extremely accurate portrait," he said.

"I can tell which is which," Grandma said.

Todd held the picture up beside the real row and looked back and forth.

Then he added a fourth thing to the paper: a tiny yellow boot, standing on its own.

"That's me looking," he said.

The back row still looked empty.

"Did the sunflower seeds go on holiday?" Todd asked.

"They're still underneath," said Amy. "At least, that's where we planted them."

"They might be dreaming of being tall," Todd said.

"Perhaps," Grandma answered. "We'll know more when we see what happens."

The friends stayed long enough to watch a cloud's shadow cross the patch, dimming the sun for a moment before the light came back.

No leaf suddenly leaped up while they watched.

The three little green shapes remained little.

Todd's paper looked quite a bit taller than any of them.

He folded it carefully. Aunt Pat helped him slip it into her bag for the journey home.

At the front of the Story House, Ben's tilted lantern shone orange in the afternoon sun, though its electric light was turned off.

Through the window, Amy could see the blue Book of Little Sounds and the brown Button Town envelope, still resting side by side.

Todd noticed them too.

"I've got a picture to add to my own collection now," he said.

"What's it called?" asked Amy.

Todd considered his three marks and the yellow boot.

"The Day the Dirt Grew Three Hats."

That sounded about right.

The grown-ups accompanied them back toward Pebble Bridge. Todd and Aunt Pat took the path east to Reed Meadow, and Amy went home with Grandma May.

Amy planned to tell Kim the good news on their next visit: something green had finally appeared.

By then the garden was quiet again, with three small seedlings under the evening sky.

The little hooks did not need anyone to watch them all night.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** A few mornings later, about a week or more after sowing, three small radish seedlings have emerged; sunflower row has not visibly emerged yet. Todd visits with Aunt Pat, makes a paper sketch of three sprouts and a yellow boot, takes sketch home. Todd and Amy leave with adults; Kim has not yet been told directly on-screen.

**Why this episode exists:** The plot contrasts the joke of a green hat with a real seedling. UMN Extension describes radish seedlings and emergence; the paper portrait is fictional humor, not measured instruction.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Todd think the tiny green shoots looked like?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
