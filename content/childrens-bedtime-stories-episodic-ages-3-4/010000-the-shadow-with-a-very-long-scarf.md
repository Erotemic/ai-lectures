# The Shadow with a Very Long Scarf

The next time Wendy came over Bell Hill, she wasn't wearing her straw hat.

She carried it under one arm.

The blue ribbon around the hat had been tugging at the wind all morning, and Wendy had decided that the wind could play by itself for a while.

Aunt Kay walked with her down the gentle hill toward Willow Bend.

At the resting bench, Amy and Ben were waiting with Grandma May. Kim sat nearby, turning her round gray pebble so its white stripe caught the light.

"Welcome to our side of the hill," said Amy.

"Hello!" said Wendy.

Ben pointed to the bench.

"This is our bench," he explained. "It is good at sitting."

Wendy looked at the patch of shade beneath it.

"And it has a little shadow," she said.

Ben stood beside the bench and looked down.

"I have one too."

His shadow rested near his feet, like a little black ribbon.

Then Ben lifted both paws.

The shadow lifted two long paws.

Ben bent one knee.

The shadow bent one knee.

"I have brought a very quiet friend," he announced.

Amy held out her ears.

Her shadow had long pointed ears too.

"Mine is a rabbit," she said.

"Mine is a bear," Ben replied.

"Mine is a bear with a little scarf."

He was wearing his usual red scarf, but its shadow looked much longer than the scarf itself.

Kim watched their shadows move across the path.

"Do they always look like that?" she wondered.

"Let's look again later," Grandma May suggested. "The sun will be in a different place then."

So they went down to Willow Bend.

Wendy saw the crooked apple tree beside Amy's blue door.

She saw the fountain in the Village Green, which made a small, friendly splash.

And she saw the Story House, with its squeaky door.

Eee.

"That's a good squeak," Wendy said.

Ben showed her the orange paper lantern he had made for the Little-Light Walk.

It was still hanging near the Story House window, and it still leaned to one side.

"It isn't meant to be straight?" Wendy asked.

"It has decided it likes leaning," Ben said.

Wendy looked at it for a moment.

"It does look comfortable."

They had a little lunch on the Village Green with the grown-ups. Then they helped Grandma May carry a stack of picture books into the Story House.

Wendy liked the book with the great big goose on the cover.

Ben liked the one with a turnip wearing shoes.

Kim liked the one with a tiny house tucked into a tree.

Amy liked all three, though not all at once.

When the afternoon grew late enough for the light to turn honey-colored, the friends climbed back toward Bell Hill.

At the resting bench, Ben stopped.

"Look!"

The shadow of the bench stretched far across the grass.

Earlier it had been tucked close beneath the wooden seat. Now it reached toward the path.

Ben stood where he had stood before.

His shadow had grown long.

"I didn't grow," he said.

"Neither did your scarf," Amy said.

Grandma May pointed to the sun, low in the sky, without asking anyone to look directly at it.

"The light reaches us from a different angle in the afternoon," she said. "That makes the shadows look different."

Ben tried holding his paws like the branches of a tree.

His shadow became a little forest.

Amy stood beside him, and their shadows met at the edges.

Kim made a small shadow with her pouch.

Wendy held up her straw hat.

Its round shadow landed beside Ben's long one.

"Your friend has a hat now," she said.

Ben was delighted.

"A very well-dressed shadow!"

Aunt Kay said it was time for Wendy to go home across Bell Hill. Before she left, Wendy looked back at the Village Green.

"Next time you come to Far Meadow, we should have a picnic," she said. "The wind could come too."

"It usually does," said Amy.

"A ribbon picnic," Wendy decided. "So we can see which way the breeze goes."

Ben tugged gently at his red scarf.

"My scarf would like to attend."

Everyone agreed that sounded very nice.

As Wendy and Aunt Kay went over the hill, the shadows followed them along the path.

At home, Amy drew one long, crooked line beside the Bell Hill bench on her map.

"That's the afternoon shadow," she told Grandma May.

Then she put the pencil down.

The afternoon was nearly gone, and the room was growing quiet.

Amy folded her map. Grandma May drew the green curtains.

The long shadows stayed outside with the fading light.

Inside the blue-door cottage, it was time for a little rest.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Wendy has visited Village Green and seen the tilted lantern; Amy draws a long shadow by Bell Hill bench; Wendy suggests a ribbon picnic.

**Why this episode exists:** One concrete science observation is embedded in the familiar village tour. Ben has fun with his scarf shadow; the physical explanation is not treated as a test of preschool science knowledge. No developmental or sleep improvement from listening is asserted.

**Optional prompt:** "What shape did Ben make with his paws?" This is optional, especially at bedtime.

**References and evidence limits:** [UCAR Center for Science Education, *Sunshine and Shadows*](https://scied.ucar.edu/activity/sunshine-shadows) explains that an object blocking sunlight casts a shadow, and that the length varies with the angle of sunlight. [NAEYC, *Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking*](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321) offers general guidance for optional curiosity questions. This story is not a validated preschool teaching intervention.

**Single-voice production:** Narrate consistently; names and actions identify characters. Parent notes and citations are not for TTS. No acting voices, sound effects, or music are required.
