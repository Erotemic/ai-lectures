# Ben and the Book's Traveling Coat

On the low shelf inside the Story House lived a blue book with six pages.

It did not have a bedroom. It did not have slippers. And it had never, as far as Ben knew, been on a bus.

"That's going to change," Ben told it.

The book made no reply.

Amy, Kim and Grandma May were there with him. Through the window, the orange paper lantern still hung at its familiar slant, its little silver spot catching the afternoon light. The Story House door, repaired by Grandpa Ed, had opened with its small click.

Kim explained the note at the Market Street library.

"They said they would like to see a book someone made," she told Ben. "But only if you want to take yours."

Ben pulled the blue book toward him.

"Does it need a library card?"

"We'll ask the librarian," Grandma May said. "For now, it needs its author to agree."

"I agree," Ben said. "But it also needs a traveling coat."

"Books don't usually wear coats," Amy pointed out.

"Neither do clocks," said Ben, "but Sue's clock has a sandwich."

That was difficult to argue with.

Grandma May found a clean cloth bag, the kind used for carrying a folded sweater. It was just the right size for the blue book. Before putting it inside, they checked the pages.

On the first page, tall reeds bent over a narrow path.

"Shhh," Kim said. "That's my way to Todd's meadow."

"It's the wind's way," Ben corrected kindly. "You're allowed to use it."

The second page showed feet on Pebble Bridge, making tok and tuk, and the enormous ears of Ben's paper listening hat.

"Your hat was not very good at listening," said Amy.

"It was very good at being a hat," Ben said. "For a hat made from one piece of paper."

The third page showed Todd's round bup, with a long buuuuup beside it.

"That's a lot of u," Amy said.

"He worked hard on that u," Ben replied.

On the fourth page were the windbreak tree in Far Meadow and the crescent of reeds in Reed Meadow: flutter and shhh.

The fifth held a watering can beneath the Story House roof. Plink, with pauses between the drops.

The last page showed Amy's yellow map opening with a soft fffft.

Amy had the real yellow map in her bag now. Its western fold-out showed the green bus route and Market Street. She opened it.

Fffft.

"Still working," Ben said.

He looked at the book again.

All six pages were filled. None had come loose. Nothing needed replacing. The notebook still had the faint crease on its blue cover where he'd once pressed too hard with a paw.

"We could make it a grand purple coat," Ben suggested, looking at the cloth bag.

"It's a plain bag," said Kim.

"A plain bag can be grand on the inside."

Ben set the book into the bag, then took it out again.

"What if it gets lonely?"

Amy held up her map. "A book can't get lonely. You might miss it, though."

Ben thought about this. He had left it on the Story House shelf for other people to read, and he liked knowing where it was. Taking it to Market Street would mean letting it spend a little time somewhere else.

"I think I'd like to go with it," he decided.

"We can do that," Grandma May said. "The librarian has agreed to look after it between visits, and we'll bring it home afterward."

Ben put the notebook back inside the cloth bag. He folded the opening neatly over the top.

Then he tried the bag on his head.

It slid down to his eyebrows.

"A fine coat," Amy said. "Terrible hat."

"Not even a window," Ben said from inside it.

They laughed, and Ben removed the bag before Grandma May helped him carry it home.

That night he set the bag with the blue book beside his bed, where he would remember it in the morning. His red scarf lay folded over a chair.

The six pages were ready for the library.

Ben himself was ready for a very ordinary sleep.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** Ben willingly agrees to lend the original book for a short library visit and keep it as his. The six original pages remain intact; the book is taken home with Ben in a clean soft cloth bag for the planned trip, the original shelf temporarily empty. No book cover or extra page is fabricated. His red scarf and tilted orange lantern remain his established belongings.

**Why this episode exists:** A book already familiar to the listener gets a specific, gentle new role; revisiting its six pages rewards continuity without resetting the first story.

**Optional prompt:** "What did Ben try wearing on his head?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Read Early and Often](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/read-early-and-often/); [The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2058). These are editorial context for shared reading, active play, or observation; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories or verify the fictional events.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
