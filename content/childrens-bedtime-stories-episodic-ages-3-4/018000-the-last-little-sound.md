# The Last Little Sound

On a clear afternoon, Ben carried his little blue notebook to the Story House.

He carried it carefully, with both paws.

He had filled five pages with pictures of sounds from Willow Bend and the places beyond it.

The sixth page was still blank.

"Today," Ben told Grandma May, "I'd like to show everyone the book."

Grandma May helped him put it on a low table by the window.

Amy came from the blue-door cottage with her folded yellow map. Kim arrived from Round Pond with her violet pouch. The round gray pebble with the white stripe was inside, just where it belonged.

Aunt Kay and Wendy walked over Bell Hill from Far Meadow. Wendy wore her straw hat, and its blue ribbon made one little wave as she entered.

From the east came Todd and Aunt Pat, along the known path past Round Pond and across Pebble Bridge. Todd's yellow boots made small steps on the wooden boards.

The grown-ups were nearby, and the Story House was warm and bright.

Ben looked around.

"I think we have enough people for a very small book reading."

"It's an excellent size," Kim said.

They found places on cushions and chairs. Kim chose a cushion a little to the side, where she could see the pages without sitting in the middle.

Grandma May sat beside Ben and helped hold the book open.

On the first page were tall, thin reeds.

"Shhh," Ben said.

"That's my meadow," Todd said, recognizing the shape.

"And the path beside it," Kim added.

On the second page were three tiny boots, one with a very large paper hat.

"Tok and tuk," said Ben.

Amy remembered the different boards on Pebble Bridge.

"And your listening hat," she said.

"It was better at crinkling than listening," Ben admitted.

On the third page was a round shape with lines coming out of it.

Todd leaned forward.

"Is that my bup?"

"Your very important bup," said Ben.

"It's a fine-looking bup," Wendy told him.

Todd sat a little taller in his yellow boots.

The fourth page held a broad tree branch and a curve of reeds.

"Flutter in Far Meadow," Wendy said.

"Shhh in Reed Meadow," said Kim.

Aunt Kay smiled. She had walked through both places now and could remember their different sounds too.

On the fifth page was a watering can, with raindrops above it.

"Plink," Amy said.

"The rain was making music on a can," Ben explained.

Then Grandma May turned to the final page.

It was empty.

Everyone looked at it for a moment.

"Did you forget a sound?" Todd asked.

"Not exactly," Ben said. "I saved one page because I didn't know what to put there."

The Story House was quiet.

Outside, a few leaves brushed the wall.

Somebody shifted on a cushion.

Wendy's ribbon fluttered once when she turned her head.

Then Amy reached into her bag and unfolded the yellow map.

Fffft.

A soft paper sound.

Ben looked at the map, then at his blue book.

"That's it," he said.

"The map?" Amy asked.

"The sound of opening something you want to look at."

Amy unfolded the map the rest of the way.

The blue-door cottage, Village Green, Story House, Pebble Bridge and Round Pond were all there.

To the west were Bell Hill and Far Meadow, with Wendy's cottage and the Windbreak Tree.

To the east were Reed Meadow, the dry grassy path, and the little yellow boot Amy had drawn for Todd.

Ben turned his notebook to the blank page. He drew two folded corners and a yellow rectangle between them.

Grandma May wrote a little word beneath it.

Fffft.

"That's a very soft final sound," Todd said.

"It's a good one for a little book," Kim replied.

Ben looked at his six finished pages.

The book held no real breeze, no bridge boards, no frogs, and no rain.

Just pictures and a few words.

But the friends knew the sounds they meant.

Afterward, Ben asked Grandma May whether the book could live on the low Story House shelf for a while.

"Of course," she said. "And you may borrow it again whenever you'd like."

He set it beside the other books, where everyone could find it.

Then the visitors began to head home.

Aunt Kay and Wendy went west over Bell Hill, to the cream-colored cottage with its orange, blue, and violet banner in the window.

Todd and Aunt Pat went east past Round Pond, toward the dry path above the reeds.

Kim went home with her grown-up. Ben went to put away his red scarf.

Amy followed Grandma May to the blue-door cottage.

That evening, Amy folded her yellow map once more and put it beside her bed.

In the Story House, the blue book waited on its low shelf.

It had six finished pages.

There were still plenty of sounds in the valley that nobody had drawn.

But nobody needed to find them tonight.

The doors closed gently. The paths grew quiet.

And from Bell Hill to Reed Meadow, the whole valley was ready for sleep.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Sixth page depicts the sound of unfolding Amy’s map; completed Book of Little Sounds rests on the low Story House shelf with Ben’s permission to borrow it again; everyone returns home; lantern and banner remain in established places.

**Why this episode exists:** The concluding moment is small and familiar, rather than another festival. Familiar sounds and characters return, then the book stays in the community space until borrowed again. No cliffhanger. This is original fiction; no developmental or sleep outcome from listening is claimed.

**Optional prompt:** "Which picture in Ben’s book would you want to see again?" Optional, especially at bedtime.

**References and evidence limits:** [ZERO TO THREE, *Read Early and Often*](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/read-early-and-often/); [American Academy of Pediatrics (HealthyChildren.org), *How to Share Books with Children 2 and 3 Years Old*](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-2-and-3-Year-Olds.aspx). Sources supply general guidance for play, reading or listening, and where applicable a physical or natural-history point; they do not evaluate this story or establish that it improves the listener's development or sleep.
