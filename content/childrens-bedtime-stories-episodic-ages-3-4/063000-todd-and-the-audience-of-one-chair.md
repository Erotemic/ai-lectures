# Todd and the Audience of One Chair

The afternoon before the library visit, Todd came to the Story House with Aunt Pat.

His two yellow boots were as bright as ever. The right one still had its blue patch.

Ben had brought the little blue sound book in its clean cloth bag. Sue and Grandpa Ed were visiting, too. Kim sat near the window where she could see the low table without being in the middle of the room.

"We can show the pages on Saturday," Ben said. "But how do you show a sound?"

Todd knew the answer immediately.

"BUP!"

The little wooden chair in front of him did not move.

"I don't think it heard me," he said.

"It hasn't got ears," Sue explained.

"Neither has a potato," Ben said, "and people tell potatoes all sorts of things."

Sue looked at him.

"Who tells potatoes things?"

"Gardeners. 'Please grow.' 'Please don't turn green.' 'Please make enough chips for a bear.'"

Todd faced the chair again.

"Would you like to see page three?"

The chair said nothing.

"That is a very polite audience," he decided.

They practiced with the chair for a little while. Ben held the book open. Todd made his quick bup and his long buuuuup. Sue bowed to the chair so deeply that her ears nearly touched her knees.

Then Wendy and Aunt Kay arrived from Far Meadow. Wendy carried a straw hat with its blue ribbon, not a pretend audience member.

"What are you doing?" she asked.

"Giving a book to a chair," said Sue.

"Is the chair enjoying it?"

"It hasn't complained," Todd answered.

Wendy sat down in the chair.

"Now the chair has a person in it," she said. "Try again."

Todd looked at the page. The round sound he'd drawn was easy to see. He could almost hear the reeds behind his home.

"Bup," he said, much more softly.

Wendy smiled. "That sounds like you."

Kim had been listening from the side. She opened her violet pouch, checked her three pebbles, and closed it again.

"I don't want to say anything in front of everyone at the library," she told them.

"You can just look," said Grandma May, who had come in to help. "Or you can point to a page. Or you can choose not to."

"I might point," Kim said.

"Would you like me to hold the book then?" Ben asked.

Kim nodded.

They tried that, too. Ben opened to the first page. Kim pointed at the reeds. Wendy whispered shhh, because the trees outside were moving a little in the breeze.

No one asked Kim to speak louder. She had shown exactly what she wanted to show.

Next Sue pretended that the library table was a bakery counter. She opened her paws.

"One book sandwich, please."

Ben offered the blue notebook.

"What's inside?" Sue asked.

"Reeds, rain and a frog," said Ben.

Todd made a small bup into his sleeve. The chair remained quite impressed.

By the time the grown-ups began gathering everyone's things, they had worked out a simple plan. Ben could hold the book. Todd could make the bup if he wanted to. Wendy could show the wind page. Sue could make a joke. Kim could point, or just listen. Amy would come on the library day and open her map if anyone asked where the sounds came from.

Nobody had to do the same thing.

Aunt Pat walked Todd home across Pebble Bridge and past Round Pond, along the dry path to Reed Meadow. Wendy went home over Bell Hill with Aunt Kay. Sue and Grandpa Ed took the orchard path.

Ben took the book home with his grown-up, still inside its cloth bag.

At bedtime, Todd told Aunt Pat about the chair.

"Best audience I've ever had," he said.

"Why was that?"

"It stayed until the end."

Aunt Pat pulled his blanket up, and the two yellow boots waited neatly beside the bed.

The chair stayed in the Story House, where it was very good at waiting too.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** The friends agree on an optional, flexible way to share the book: Ben holds it, Todd may bup, Wendy may show wind, Sue may joke, Kim may point or simply listen, and Amy may open her real yellow map. No one must perform. The Story House chair and props stay put; Ben takes the book home after rehearsal. Todd, Kim, Wendy and Sue retain their established things.

**Why this episode exists:** The chair is a genuine comic focus while different social preferences are preserved without turning Kim into an object lesson or requiring a public performance.

**Optional prompt:** "Who was the very polite audience?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2058); [DAP: Creating a Caring, Equitable Community of Learners](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/creating-community/). These are editorial context for shared reading, active play, or observation; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories or verify the fictional events.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
