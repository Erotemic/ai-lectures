# Kim and the Library Window

Kim had been to the Story House many times. She knew the low shelf with Ben's blue sound book, the brown envelope of Button Town pictures, and the folder with the garden drawing.

But she had never seen a window full of books.

"A whole window?" she asked Amy.

"A whole big window," Amy said. "On Market Street."

"Do the books look outside?"

"I suppose they could."

Kim wanted to find out.

So on a bright morning, Kim and her grown-up met Sue and Grandpa Ed at the Village Green. The four of them walked west, past Blue Door Cottage, over Bell Hill, and beyond Far Meadow to the green-bus stop.

Sue brought no bakery boxes. Kim brought her violet pouch with exactly three pebbles. Her favorite round gray pebble, with the white stripe, was still tucked safely inside.

The bus took them to Market Street.

The window was just as wide as Amy had said. On the glass, someone had painted a red book, a yellow book, and a blue book, each one tilted a different way.

Behind the glass stood a row of real books, facing outward.

"They are looking outside," Kim said.

Sue leaned close enough to see the pictures without touching the glass.

"Maybe they're waiting for someone."

Inside the library, the shelves made long, straight paths. There were low chairs for small visitors, a table with paper and crayons, and a little basket for returned books.

A librarian smiled and showed the children where they could sit.

Kim walked down a row of shelves with her grown-up. She did not rush. A book with a beetle on its cover caught her eye, and she stopped.

"This beetle is wearing shoes," she said.

Sue came to look.

It was true. The beetle had four shoes, and only two of them matched.

"It might have dressed in the dark," Sue suggested.

A little farther along, Sue found a picture book with a sock on its cover.

The sock was blue. It had two wheels and a tiny bell.

"Look, it has wheels!" Sue said. "Grandpa Ed, it could deliver our bread."

"Does the sock deliver bread?" Kim asked.

"Let's open it and see."

The book was called *The Loud Blue Sock*. In the pictures, the sock rolled across a kitchen floor, under a chair, and into a cupboard where a spoon was taking a nap.

Sue looked up at Grandpa Ed.

"Our spoon is not allowed to take naps in the cupboard. It has to work at the bakery."

Grandpa Ed smiled. "It spends quite a lot of time in my kitchen drawer."

Kim laughed. Then she turned the page and discovered that the blue sock had stopped beside a very quiet pair of slippers.

"This one is funny," she said. "Can I take it home?"

Her grown-up explained that library books could be borrowed and brought back. They asked the librarian, who helped the grown-up check out the book.

Kim carried it carefully while the grown-up held the library card.

Near the door was a wide slot marked BOOK RETURNS.

"If we put it in there now, would it come back out?" Sue asked.

"Not right away," the librarian said. "That is where returned books go for us to put away."

"Then let's not return it yet," said Kim. "I haven't finished the story."

Before they left, Kim looked once more at the painted books on the big window. From inside, the pictures were backwards.

"Now they're looking at us," she said.

On the bus home, she kept *The Loud Blue Sock* on her lap. Sue sat beside Grandpa Ed and told him that the paper beetle in her castle bakery would need at least four shoes.

Kim did not tell her how many the beetle should have. Sue was already busy counting.

At home, Kim set the borrowed book beside her violet pouch, where it would be safe until the grown-up helped her take it back.

She opened to the page with the slippers, then closed the cover gently.

Three pebbles in one pouch. One blue sock in one book. A library full of stories on the other side of a hill.

Kim climbed into bed, pleased that there would still be more pages to look at tomorrow.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** Kim and her unnamed grown-up visit Market Street by green bus alongside Sue and Grandpa Ed. A librarian helps Kim’s grown-up borrow a fictional picture book titled The Loud Blue Sock. Kim takes the book to her home by Round Pond and keeps it safe beside her violet pouch; it remains checked out, not yet returned. Sue and Grandpa Ed return to their cottage. No library books are added to Story House collections.

**Why this episode exists:** Kim pursues her curiosity actively and enjoys a shared joke with Sue rather than only observing. An ordinary public library differs from the familiar Story House without replacing it.

**Optional prompt:** "What did the blue sock in Kim’s borrowed book have?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [How to Share Books with Children 2 and 3 Years Old](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-2-and-3-Year-Olds.aspx); [Message in a Backpack: Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321). These give general reading, play or conversational context; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories.

**Single-voice production:** Narration only between the spoken markers. No character voices, effects or music are required.
