# Five Windows and One Moon

On the evening the Moon looked round, the friends were all at Village Green.

They had not gathered for a festival or a lantern walk.

It had simply been a good afternoon to visit the Story House.

Tess had come with Grandma Fern from Blue Door Cottage.

Bo wore his red scarf.

Nori had walked from Round Pond with her grown-up, carrying the violet pouch that held her three pebbles.

Lila and Aunt Mallow had crossed Bell Hill from Far Meadow. Lila's straw hat still wore its blue ribbon.

Rill and Aunt Cress had come by the dry path from Reed Meadow, past Round Pond and over Pebble Bridge. Rill wore two yellow boots, including the one with the blue patch.

Everyone knew the way now.

The Story House door gave its old friendly squeak.

Bo's tilted orange lantern hung in its place by the window.

On the low shelf were the blue Book of Little Sounds, the brown Button Town envelope, and the folder with the picture of their little growing garden.

The garden itself was outside behind the southern wall. Its leaves were a little bigger now, though the sunflowers were still far from being tall yellow flowers.

Inside, the friends played a game called What Does It Look Like?

Bo held up a piece of white paper with a round hole cut out of it.

"A button," said Lila.

"A wheel," said Rill.

"The top of a biscuit tin," said Nori.

"A Moon," said Tess.

"An excellent round thing," Bo declared. "All answers accepted."

He tipped the paper sideways.

"Now what?"

"Still a hole," said Nori.

"A sideways hole," said Rill.

"A hole that should wear a hat," said Bo.

Tess laughed.

"You already made hats for the Moon."

"A whole department," Bo reminded her. "Closed at bedtime."

Lila told them about her almost-round biscuit that had turned into a slipper.

Rill was delighted.

"A biscuit for my boots!"

"Not really," Lila said. "It was much too small."

Nori said that the pond had made a wiggly picture of the Moon. She described how the little ripples changed the reflection while the Moon in the sky stayed the same.

Rill showed them how a nearby object could hide something far away. He held his paw in front of the round hole in Bo's paper.

"Now you see it," said Rill.

He lowered his paw.

"Now you see it again."

"A very short magic show," said Bo.

"Not magic," said Tess, smiling.

It was time to tidy up before going home.

The white paper went into Bo's own bag. The three older collections remained on the shelf where they belonged.

Grandma Fern opened the Story House door.

The sunset had left a pale orange band above the westward path to Bell Hill.

Beyond the east side of Village Green, over the direction of Pebble Bridge, a large round Moon was rising.

Nobody had to search for a skinny silver smile.

The whole bright face was visible.

"It got rounder!" Bo said.

Tess looked from the Moon to the friends.

"It was round all along," she said. "We're seeing more of its bright side now."

"And tonight," said Lila, "it looks like the biscuit before anyone makes it into a slipper."

That was an excellent description.

The friends stood together for a little while.

There was enough time for one last look.

Then the grown-ups began the separate journeys home.

Lila and Aunt Mallow headed west, toward Bell Hill and the cream-colored cottage.

Rill and Aunt Cress walked east across Pebble Bridge toward Reed Meadow.

Nori and her grown-up continued past the bridge toward Round Pond.

Tess and Grandma Fern turned toward Blue Door Cottage.

Bo went home with his grown-up, humming a tune about hats that never quite fit.

They did not all have the same window.

Some windows looked past grass. Some looked past trees. One looked toward the fountain, and another toward the reeds.

But above the valley was the same round Moon.

At Blue Door Cottage, Tess folded her yellow map beside her bed. The Moon was still not a place she could put on it.

At Far Meadow, Lila's picnic banner hung quietly inside the cottage window.

At Round Pond, Nori's three pebbles rested safely in their pouch.

At Reed Meadow, Rill's two yellow boots stood together, one patched and one plain.

And at Bo's house, the smallest pretend hat in Willow Bend waited on the bedpost.

The friends had looked at the Moon on different evenings.

They had noticed different things.

Now each had a familiar place to rest.

Outside, the Moon shone over Bell Hill, over the orchard, over Round Pond, and over the little growing garden.

Inside, the valley grew quiet.

There was nothing left to finish tonight.

Goodnight, Willow Bend.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** At Village Green the five friends meet during an ordinary afternoon, share Moon jokes, and briefly observe a full Moon rising on the eastern side of the sky at sunset. They return to their established homes with grown-ups. Story House shelf still holds blue Book of Little Sounds, brown Button Town envelope, and LITTLE GROWING GARDEN folder; Bo takes cutout paper home. Real map still has no Moon location, and garden leaves are a little bigger without sunflower blooms or harvest. The season is fully resolved.

**Why this episode exists:** This is a gentle ensemble ending, not a lesson about agreement. NASA supports the full-Moon timing and appearance; different windows and rituals are fictional reflections of the established valley.

**Optional prompt:** "Which window or place would you have liked to look from?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
