# Five Windows and One Moon

On the evening the Moon looked round, the friends were all at Village Green.

They had not gathered for a festival or a lantern walk.

It had simply been a good afternoon to visit the Story House.

Amy had come with Grandma May from Blue Door Cottage.

Ben wore his red scarf.

Kim had walked from Round Pond with her grown-up, carrying the violet pouch that held her three pebbles.

Wendy and Aunt Kay had crossed Bell Hill from Far Meadow. Wendy's straw hat still wore its blue ribbon.

Todd and Aunt Pat had come by the dry path from Reed Meadow, past Round Pond and over Pebble Bridge. Todd wore two yellow boots, including the one with the blue patch.

Everyone knew the way now.

The Story House door gave its old friendly squeak.

Ben's tilted orange lantern hung in its place by the window.

On the low shelf were the blue Book of Little Sounds, the brown Button Town envelope, and the folder with the picture of their little growing garden.

The garden itself was outside behind the southern wall. Its leaves were a little bigger now, though the sunflowers were still far from being tall yellow flowers.

Inside, the friends played a game called What Does It Look Like?

Ben held up a piece of white paper with a round hole cut out of it.

"A button," said Wendy.

"A wheel," said Todd.

"The top of a biscuit tin," said Kim.

"A Moon," said Amy.

"An excellent round thing," Ben declared. "All answers accepted."

He tipped the paper sideways.

"Now what?"

"Still a hole," said Kim.

"A sideways hole," said Todd.

"A hole that should wear a hat," said Ben.

Amy laughed.

"You already made hats for the Moon."

"A whole department," Ben reminded her. "Closed at bedtime."

Wendy told them about her almost-round biscuit that had turned into a slipper.

Todd was delighted.

"A biscuit for my boots!"

"Not really," Wendy said. "It was much too small."

Kim said that the pond had made a wiggly picture of the Moon. She described how the little ripples changed the reflection while the Moon in the sky stayed the same.

Todd showed them how a nearby object could hide something far away. He held his paw in front of the round hole in Ben's paper.

"Now you can't see it," said Todd.

He lowered his paw.

"Now you can."

"A very short magic show," said Ben.

"Not magic," said Amy, smiling.

It was time to tidy up before going home.

The white paper went into Ben's own bag. The three older collections remained on the shelf where they belonged.

Grandma May opened the Story House door.

The sunset had left a pale orange band above the westward path to Bell Hill.

Beyond the east side of Village Green, over the direction of Pebble Bridge, a large round Moon was rising.

Nobody had to search for a skinny silver smile.

The whole bright face was visible.

"It got rounder!" Ben said.

Amy looked from the Moon to the friends.

"It was round all along," she said. "We're seeing more of its bright side now."

"And tonight," said Wendy, "it looks like the biscuit before anyone makes it into a slipper."

That was an excellent description.

The friends stood together for a little while.

There was enough time for one last look.

Then the grown-ups began the separate journeys home.

Wendy and Aunt Kay headed west, toward Bell Hill and the cream-colored cottage.

Todd and Aunt Pat walked east across Pebble Bridge toward Reed Meadow.

Kim and her grown-up continued past the bridge toward Round Pond.

Amy and Grandma May turned toward Blue Door Cottage.

Ben went home with his grown-up, humming a tune about hats that never quite fit.

They did not all have the same window.

Some windows looked past grass. Some looked past trees. One looked toward the fountain, and another toward the reeds.

But above the valley was the same round Moon.

At Blue Door Cottage, Amy folded her yellow map beside her bed. The Moon was still not a place she could put on it.

At Far Meadow, Wendy's picnic banner hung quietly inside the cottage window.

At Round Pond, Kim's three pebbles rested safely in their pouch.

At Reed Meadow, Todd's two yellow boots stood together, one patched and one plain.

And at Ben's house, the smallest pretend hat in Willow Bend waited on the bedpost.

The friends had looked at the Moon on different evenings.

They had noticed different things.

Now each had a familiar place to rest.

Outside, the Moon shone over Bell Hill, over the orchard, over Round Pond, and over the little growing garden.

Inside, the valley grew quiet.

There was nothing left to finish tonight.

Goodnight, Willow Bend.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** At Village Green the five friends meet during an ordinary afternoon, share Moon jokes, and briefly observe a full Moon rising on the eastern side of the sky at sunset. They return to their established homes with grown-ups. Story House shelf still holds blue Book of Little Sounds, brown Button Town envelope, and LITTLE GROWING GARDEN folder; Ben takes cutout paper home. Real map still has no Moon location, and garden leaves are a little bigger without sunflower blooms or harvest. The season is fully resolved.

**Why this episode exists:** This is a gentle ensemble ending, not a lesson about agreement. NASA supports the full-Moon timing and appearance; different windows and rituals are fictional reflections of the established valley.

**Optional prompt:** "Which window or place would you have liked to look from?" Optional, especially at bedtime.
