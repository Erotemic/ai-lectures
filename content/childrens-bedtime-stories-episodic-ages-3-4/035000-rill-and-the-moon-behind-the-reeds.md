# Todd and the Moon Behind the Reeds

Todd was wearing his yellow boots when he went out to look at the Moon.

One boot had a little blue patch.

The other did not.

"They are different boots," Todd told Aunt Pat, "but they are excellent friends."

Aunt Pat helped him straighten the patched one.

Then they followed the dry path beside Reed Meadow as the evening grew soft and blue.

The tall reeds made a quiet shushing sound.

The crescent of reeds bordered the low wet hollow, but the path stayed on the grassy rise above it.

Todd liked that path.

It went past his favorite listening place and back toward the cottage where Aunt Pat kept smooth leaves on her windowsill.

Tonight something bright had appeared beyond the reeds.

The Moon.

It was nearly round now, though one thin dark edge was still visible.

"Oh, hello," Todd said.

He gave his small friendly bup.

Then he moved one step sideways.

A tall reed crossed in front of the bright Moon.

And from where Todd stood, the Moon seemed to vanish.

He blinked.

"Aunt Pat."

"Yes?"

"I have misplaced the Moon."

Aunt Pat looked up at the sky, then at the tall reed between them and it.

"Try one step the other way," she said.

Todd stepped back.

There it was.

The bright Moon had returned.

Todd stepped sideways again.

The reed hid it.

Back again.

The Moon appeared.

"A very good trick," Todd said.

He tried it three times.

Step. Hidden.

Step. There.

Step. Hidden.

Aunt Pat stood beside him.

"Is the Moon walking behind the reeds?" Todd asked.

"No," said Aunt Pat. "We're changing where we stand. The reed is close, and the Moon is very far away."

Todd considered this.

Then he looked at his own yellow boots.

"So my boots are helping me do the trick."

"Your feet are certainly doing some of the work."

Todd made a long, pleased buuuup.

The sound floated over the grass.

He tried a different trick.

He held one paw up in front of his face.

His paw was small, but close enough to cover the Moon from one eye.

He lowered it.

The Moon appeared again.

"My paw is now a Moon curtain," he announced.

Aunt Pat smiled.

"A very small curtain."

"With no string and no window."

They sat together on a dry bench beside the path.

Todd rested his boots on the grass.

The reeds moved in the breeze, sometimes crossing the Moon's bright face and sometimes bending away.

There was no more mystery to solve.

It was simply pleasant to watch.

Todd could hear a tiny insect ticking in the grass.

He could hear leaves touching one another.

He could hear Aunt Pat hum a little tune that had no words.

The Moon shone almost round above the far meadow edge.

Tomorrow, Todd thought, he might tell Amy about the Moon curtain. Amy liked finding out how things worked, and Ben would probably make a paper curtain with an enormous hat attached.

But tomorrow was tomorrow.

Aunt Pat stood and offered her paw.

"Home time," she said.

They followed the same dry path back to their cottage.

The Moon sometimes disappeared behind the tall reeds as they walked, but Todd no longer needed to stop and search for it.

At home, he set his yellow boots neatly together.

The patched boot stood beside the plain one.

Two excellent friends, waiting for another day.

The Moon was still in the sky, whether the window showed it or not.

And Todd, without any more tricks to perform, went quietly to sleep.

## For parents and caregivers - not spoken

**What carries forward:** On an evening shortly before full Moon, Todd and Aunt Pat observe from the dry rise above the wet hollow; the near-full Moon is alternately occluded by a reed as Todd shifts position. He makes a paw curtain and returns home. Boots unchanged and paired by bed; no crossing of wet hollow, no new picture or book.

**Why this episode exists:** The playful disappearance is caused by changing the line of sight, not the Moon suddenly moving away. Repetition offers a satisfying simple game without instructions to go out alone.

**Optional prompt:** "Why did the Moon show up again when Todd took a step?" Optional, especially at bedtime.

**References and evidence limits:** [NASA Science, *Moon Facts*](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/); [Janis Strasser, National Association for the Education of Young Children, *Message in a Backpack: Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking*](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321). These sources support narrowly identified physical facts or general caregiver context; they do not demonstrate developmental, educational, emotional, or sleep benefits from this fiction.
