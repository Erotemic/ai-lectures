# Kim and the Empty Row

Kim had come to see the garden after the radishes were gone.

She brought her violet pouch with the three pebbles inside, and she came with her grown-up along the familiar path from Round Pond, across Pebble Bridge, to Village Green.

Behind the Story House, the sign stood in its new place beside the dry path. Two tall sunflower stems bent a little in the breeze.

"Those weren't tall last time," Kim said.

"They've had a lot of mornings," her grown-up said.

Kim looked down at the front of the garden.

"And those aren't there at all."

"The radishes?"

Kim nodded.

The old picture label still showed a round red radish with leaves. But the row beneath it was bare, except for small bits of soil and a tiny mark where a grown-up had taken out a weed.

"I liked looking at their funny leaves," Kim said.

"You can look at the sunflowers now."

"I can," said Kim. "But I can also look at this."

She sat on one of the two dry viewing stones, keeping both feet out of the planted patch.

From there she could see something the tall leaves made difficult to notice. A thin shadow stretched across the empty front row. When the wind moved a sunflower leaf, the shadow moved too.

It looked like a long arm waving.

Kim waved one little paw back.

A second shadow appeared beside the first. It was wider, with a pointy tip.

"That's a hat," Kim told her grown-up.

"Whose hat?"

"A very tall worm's."

Her grown-up sat nearby. Together they watched the shadows for a while.

A small beetle walked over the bare soil. It stopped beneath the edge of a leaf's shadow, turned around, then walked the other way.

"Maybe it's searching for a hat too," Kim said.

"Maybe," her grown-up answered. "Or maybe it's just a beetle going somewhere."

Kim preferred not to decide for the beetle.

She reached into her pouch and felt the three pebbles. One was round and gray with a white stripe. She held that one in her paw for a moment, then returned it to the pouch with the other two.

"I won't put it in the row," she said. "It already has a home."

The beetle disappeared under a fallen leaf at the edge of the patch.

Kim stood, brushed her skirt, and went to look at the plant label.

"The picture says radishes," she said, "but there aren't any here now."

Grandma May had come around the Story House with a little watering can. She listened while Kim explained the empty row.

"The label shows what grew here earlier," Grandma May said. "When the weather is right, we can decide what to plant here next. For now the row can rest."

"Like a bench?" Kim asked.

"A little like that."

Kim imagined a very tired row of soil putting up its feet. It wore no hat. It had no feet either, of course, but that didn't stop the picture in her mind.

"Then I won't wake it," she said.

They walked back to the dry stones. The breeze had gone still. The long shadow lay quietly across the bare row, not waving at anyone now.

Kim picked up a fallen yellow leaf from the path, turned it over to see the little lines on its back, then laid it down again where she'd found it.

Her grown-up took her hand for the walk home. They crossed the same Pebble Bridge, listened to its wooden boards, and went toward Round Pond.

At home Kim placed her violet pouch on its usual hook. She had all three pebbles. She had seen a beetle, two moving shadows, and a row with nothing growing in it just now.

She had enjoyed seeing all three.

Outside her window, the evening breeze made a leaf turn slowly in the fading light.

Kim pulled up her blanket and let the quiet row rest in her thoughts.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** Kim notices a beetle and shifting sunflower-leaf shadows in the bare front row but puts nothing in the soil. Her pouch still contains all three pebbles, including the striped one. Grandma May says no new planting has been decided yet. The existing stepping stones and moved sign are unchanged.

**Why this episode exists:** An unplanted space can still be the center of a funny observation; Kim actively investigates without needing to be cast as shy.

**Optional prompt:** "What did the shadows look like to Kim?" Optional; do not interrupt a child settling to sleep.

**References and evidence limits:** [Message in a Backpack: Asking Questions to Stretch Your Child's Thinking](https://www.naeyc.org/node/5321); [The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2058). These are horticultural facts or general play/conversation guidance; they do not demonstrate benefits from listening to these stories. The named characters, places and events are fiction.

**Single-voice production:** Only material between the spoken markers is narrated. No effects, music or acting required.
