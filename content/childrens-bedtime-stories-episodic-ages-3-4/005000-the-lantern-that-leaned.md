# The Lantern That Leaned

In Willow Bend, the Little-Light Walk was nearly here.

At the end of the week, the neighbors would carry their small shining lanterns from the Village Green, across Pebble Bridge, past Round Pond, and back again before going up Bell Hill.

Amy had been drawing the places on her yellow map.

Ben had been helping, mostly by suggesting that every tree should wear a hat.

Kim had helped draw a willow and three circles on the pond.

And Grandma May had helped with nearly everything else.

Today they were at the Story House, preparing the lanterns.

The Story House smelled of paper and old books. Its door made the familiar sound.

Eee.

Inside, the tables were covered with paper stars, ribbons, and lanterns that held little electric lights.

The grown-ups would take care of the lights and hang the lanterns where they belonged.

The children were making paper decorations for them.

Amy chose a blue paper star. It had five points, though one point was shorter than the others.

Ben chose a round orange paper lantern.

Kim chose a silver circle that reminded her of the ripples at Round Pond.

Grandma May tied a loop of cord to the top of Ben's lantern. Then she held it from a low hook so everyone could see how it hung.

It leaned to the left.

A little.

Then a little more.

Soon it looked as though it were peeking around an invisible door.

Ben put his paws on his hips.

“My lantern is doing something different.”

“It certainly is,” said Amy.

“I wanted it to stand up straight.”

Grandma May set the lantern gently on the table.

“The paper on one side is a little heavier,” she explained. “We could move the loop.”

She moved the loop.

The lantern leaned to the right instead.

Ben stared at it.

“Oh,” he said. “Now it is peeking around the other side of the door.”

Amy laughed.

Kim watched the lantern for a moment.

“Can I try an idea?” she asked.

Ben nodded.

Kim placed a silver paper circle on the side that had been looking up. Grandma May helped attach it.

Now the lantern had an orange face with one bright silver spot.

When Grandma lifted it again, it still leaned.

But only a little.

It looked like a round head tilting to listen.

Ben looked at the lantern.

Then he tilted his own head to match.

Amy tilted hers, too.

Kim tilted hers last.

Grandma May looked at three tilted heads and one tilted lantern.

“Are you all going to walk around like that?” she asked.

Ben giggled.

“I think it should stay like that.”

“Then we'll let it,” Grandma said.

She hung the lantern by the Story House window, where its little electric light could shine safely after dark.

When they stepped outside, Ben looked toward Bell Hill. It rose on the other side of the Green, beyond Amy's blue-door cottage.

“Will everyone walk all the way up?” he asked.

“Whoever wants to,” Grandma May said. “There's a bench partway up, too, for anyone who needs to rest or just wants to watch.”

Kim's ears gave a small twitch.

“I might like the bench.”

Amy took out the yellow map.

She drew the hill with three green bumps. Then she added the bench halfway up: two short lines and four tiny legs.

Ben leaned over her shoulder.

“Another long beetle,” he observed.

“It is a bench,” said Amy.

“A hill beetle,” said Ben.

Amy decided not to argue with the map's best beetle expert.

Below the hill she drew a little orange circle beside the Story House.

“That is your lantern,” she told Ben.

“Is it leaning?”

She added a tiny line to one side.

“Very politely.”

That afternoon, Grandma May brought out the apple slices she had prepared from the orchard fruit. The children sat on the grass and shared them while the sun moved slowly across the Green.

The walk was tomorrow evening.

Everything wasn't perfectly straight. The map had crooked trees. The pond had lumpy rings. Ben's lantern leaned a little to the side.

But each thing was right where it belonged.

At home, Amy slipped the map beneath the little blue bowl beside her bed so it wouldn't unfold while she slept.

Outside, the Story House window glowed faintly with Ben's tilted lantern.

It seemed to be listening to the hush of the village.

And by the time the stars came out, the whole Story House had grown still.

## For parents and caregivers — not spoken

**What carries forward:** The walk is tomorrow evening. The route is Village Green → Pebble Bridge → Round Pond → back to Village Green → Bell Hill, with a resting bench partway up the hill. Ben's orange lantern with a silver circle remains slightly tilted and hangs at the Story House window; it does **not** suddenly become straight in episode six. The lanterns use small electric lights handled or hung by adults; no flames appear. Amy's map now includes Bell Hill and its bench. Kim may choose to watch from the bench instead of completing the hill climb.

**Why this episode exists:** A comic physical imperfection becomes a character detail rather than a failure to be punished or magically erased. The resting bench makes a legitimate different choice available to Kim; participation is not framed as mandatory. The story's central pleasure is the tilted-head joke and a familiar place being prepared for a special evening.

**Optional prompt:** “Could you tilt your head like Ben's lantern?” No prompt is necessary if the child is ready to sleep.

**References and limits:** [AAP, *Social Development in Preschoolers* (2024)](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-In-Preschoolers.aspx) covers emerging cooperation and support from adults; [Harvard Center on the Developing Child, *Activities Guide* (2014)](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/handouts-tools/activities-guide-enhancing-and-practicing-executive-function-skills/) provides age-specific contexts for flexible problem solving through supported play. Neither shows that this story improves flexibility, nor prescribes a perfect lantern or a particular choice for Kim.

**Single-voice production:** A single narrator can emphasize the comic repeated tilt with normal sentence rhythm. No distinct character voice or lantern sound effect is needed.
