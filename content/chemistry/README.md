# Chemistry: From Atoms to Reactions

**Status:** six substantive audio-first foundation lectures (001000–006000). This is a new independent Science and Nature pack, not an addition to Earth Systems.

Chemistry connects the physical ideas introduced elsewhere in the science library to the experimental properties of materials, solutions and reactions. The first lecture teaches chemical identity from observation and separation. The remaining lectures explain the periodic organization of atoms, bonding and shape, solutions, reaction quantities, and the meaning of energy and chemical direction. Each narration is standalone enough for a general adult listener; the sequence is recommended because later concepts build on earlier ones.

All source manuscripts contain exactly one spoken region and an unspoken scientific companion with worked examples, evidence links and questions. The `metadata/` directory contains source-linked claim anchors, scientific topic paths, historical dates and geographic tags **only where supported by the content**. Scientific processes are not assigned arbitrary chronological or geographic coverage. The `navigation.json` guided route covers all six, with two smaller topical routes. No audio, imagery, binaries, or server code are included.

## Editorial direction

- Explain concepts at listener speed before relying on notation. Retain the equations in the companion rather than assuming the listener is looking at a page.
- Chemical equations account for species and quantities; they do not, on their own, specify microscopic mechanisms, conditions or reaction rates.
- Distinguish evidence from models: Lewis structures, orbital sketches and valence rules are helpful approximations with stated limits.
- Use IUPAC terminology, SI standards, and verifiable thermochemical data for consequential quantitative definitions; avoid treating introductory examples as universally valid laws.
- Do not pad a lecture to hit a duration target. Develop the reasoning needed for a coherent subject.
