# World literary classics — importance-first gap audit

**Prepared:** 2026-11-16. **Baseline inspected:** the supplied ai-lectures snapshot, plus the first eight `content/literature-survey` ancient narrative expansions through `069000`. **Decision:** elevate missing major literary monuments above further low-priority filling of an already strong Sumerian, Greek, Hittite, or Egyptian queue. This is a prioritized **working audit**, not a claim of completeness across all literary history.

## Selection principles

1. **Cultural, historical and literary importance** comes first: influence on later texts and artistic forms, long-standing reception, endurance as an object of interpretation, and the ability to teach major literary techniques or intellectual problems. Geographic rotation helps discover missed major works, but no region receives a commission merely to fill a quota.
2. Check the entire relevant library before writing: `content/literature-survey`, `draft-stages/tts-world-history`, `draft-stages/tts-pre-1900-literature`, `draft-stages/tts-modern-literature`, and the historical surveys under `content/ancient-medieval-world`. This audit's *absent* means **no dedicated literary lecture identified in those inspected manifests at this checkpoint**, not that the work is unknown to every other document.
3. **Narrative-first, source-first.** When a work is a poem, dialogue, scripture, genealogy, historical biography, or anthology rather than an epic, make its form audible instead of converting it into a fictional adventure. Do not impose time or word-count targets.
4. **Date the witness rather than the tale.** Place early-medieval and later works in an explicitly labeled adjacent-world-classics portion of the route, not under claims of ancient manuscript survival. Name oral narrators, performers, communities and publications. A modern recording of an older tradition is not an ancient manuscript.
5. Use **specific witnesses** and claim locators. Translations are reading aids, not evidence that all versions agree. Cite primary text and suitable scholarly context; Wikipedia links are useful orientation, not the factual foundation.

## Recommended work order and stopping rule

1. **Immediate next installment:** a *Shiji* narrative sequence, Tamil *Silappatikaram*, and early Arabic *Muʿallaqāt*. They expose three large omissions from ancient/classical textual traditions, with identifiable editions available for research.
2. **Then medieval world monuments:** start a paced *Tale of Genji* treatment, further *Nights* stories, and the *Poetic Edda*, interleaved by major importance rather than batching one region to completion.
3. **Parallel oral-literature research:** prepare source dossiers for *Sundiata*, *I La Galigo*, *Manas*, Andean and Pacific traditions, and begin authoring only where narrated public versions and cultural permissions can support the treatment.
4. **Parallel scriptural and philosophical reading:** the Gospel of Mark, Qurʾanic narrative sūras, and select ethical dialogues, each addressed as the source's actual form, not converted into a generic hero's journey.

A work becomes **covered** only when a usable, source-linked manuscript is in `track.json` and reachable from navigation. *Selected* coverage must not be described as *complete*. The priority queue should be refreshed against later repository snapshots; no fixed quota or lecture duration is required.
