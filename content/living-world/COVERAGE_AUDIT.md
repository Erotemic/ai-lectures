## Deliberate omissions and recommended *separate* foundations

1. **Cells, genes, and development.** Membranes, energy conversion, replication, transcription and translation, Mendelian and non-Mendelian inheritance, cell differentiation, embryogenesis, gene regulation, and developmental evolution. The current track introduces these when necessary but does not teach them as a systematic sequence.
2. **Systems physiology.** Nutrition and digestion, circulation, respiration, osmoregulation, endocrine systems, immune responses and homeostasis across representative animals. Mechanisms appear throughout the nature essays, but not as an integrated anatomy/physiology course.
3. **Population and community models.** Birth and death rates, exponential versus density-limited population growth, competition, predator-prey cycles, metapopulations, demographic stochasticity and quantitative conservation decisions. The present series emphasizes observed mechanisms over formal modeling.
4. **Classification and tree thinking.** Phylogenetic reconstruction, deep branches of life, the limits of 'kingdom'-based intuition, and comparative evolutionary history. The existing Deep Time series offers historical coverage, but taxonomy and inference could merit their own foundations.

These gaps do not prevent the natural-history series from being complete on its own terms. It explores what present-day organisms and ecosystems do, how biologists know, and why the apparent stability of living systems is sustained by continual change.
