# The Living World: How Nature Works

This is a **complete** sixteen-lecture natural-history audio track. It is meant to complement, not duplicate, `tts-deep-time` (the history of the universe and of life) and `tts-physical-world` (foundational physical laws). Its organizing question is how contemporary organisms and ecosystems work, and how experiments reveal those mechanisms.

## Listening and editorial design

- Each essay is a complete audio lecture, not an abbreviated list of facts or a planned stub. No prior installments are required.
- Explain enough biology for an intelligent newcomer without hiding technical mechanisms from science enthusiasts. A mechanism, a concrete organism or experiment, a rival explanation, and the limits of evidence should matter more than a quota of examples.
- Speak naturally. Keep bibliographic details, source links, technical distinctions, and suggested readings outside the spoken section when they are more useful as a reference than as narration.
- Source records live in `metadata/sources.json`; lecture-specific evidence anchors live in `metadata/ID.json`. Every anchored passage is present verbatim inside the spoken region. Each companion has accessible Wikipedia orientation links alongside research and agency sources.
- No fixed minimum, maximum, or target duration. All topic and length decisions are editorial.
- Content is text only. No downloaded images, TTS artifacts, server changes, or global registries.

## Boundaries and connections

The first twelve manuscripts retain their source-critical treatment of forests, sensory biology, soil carbon, evolution, insects, plants, fungi, microbes, lakes, oceans, environmental limits and migration. They are preserved verbatim, along with their lecture-specific evidence maps.

The final four essays develop additional mechanisms: fig-wasp sanctions and fish client choice; host detection versus egg rejection; reproductive allocation and sex change; biological legacies after disturbance; restoration as renewed process rather than exact return; and the interplay of metabolism, inheritance and ecological feedback. The final **How a Living World Endures** is a longer capstone, not an index of earlier lectures.

Five listening collections are available. The main guided route ends with the capstone; deep-dive routes organize ecology and evolution, hidden interactions, habitats and journeys, and relationships and renewal. Each lecture remains independently understandable.

## Status and complementary subjects

They can be taught in future courses without turning this track into an unending index.
