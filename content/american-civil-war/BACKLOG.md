# American Civil War: continuation plan

**Published manuscripts:** Eight substantial, individually playable lectures from the causes of the war through Reconstruction in 1877. They include a dedicated Lincoln portrait, a campaign-level treatment of the western theater, and a lecture centered on civilian lives, Black military service and self-emancipation.

## Listening order

1. `001000` — The Republic Built Around Slavery, 1820–1850
2. `002000` — The Decade That Broke the Union, 1850–1861
3. `003000` — Armies and the Meaning of Union, 1861–1862
4. `004000` — Rivers, Railroads and the War for the West
5. `005000` — Emancipation and the End of the War, 1863–1865
6. `006000` — Abraham Lincoln: The Making of a War for Freedom
7. `007000` — Living Through the Civil War: Camps, Hospitals and Households
8. `008000` — Freedom After the Surrender: Reconstruction, 1865–1877

## Additional lectures that would materially deepen the track

- **Frederick Douglass: a life spent redefining American citizenship.** Escape, writing, publication, movement-building, confrontation with Lincoln, recruitment, emancipation and suffrage. This is a first-priority biography.
- **Harriet Tubman beyond the legend.** From Maryland enslavement and escape to the Underground Railroad, intelligence, Combahee River Raid and later decades; distinguish documented actions from embellished retellings.
- **Ulysses S. Grant: the general and the president.** Failures in civilian life, Fort Donelson, Vicksburg, sustained coordinated campaigning, Appomattox and the fight against white supremacist terrorism during his presidency.
- **Robert E. Lee and the Confederate command.** Family, slavery, military decisions, invasions, manpower and the Confederate cause, without a Lost Cause narrative.
- **Gettysburg as a campaign, and what happened afterward.** Operational decisions, terrain, army command, casualties, memory and the limits of battle-centered explanations.
- **Black Reconstruction: democratic politics under attack.** Schools, churches, conventions, elected officials, Black voters, federal enforcement, political violence and retreat, with primary-source evidence.
- **Clara Barton and Civil War medical work.** Family life, battlefield supplies, locating missing soldiers and her postwar work, correcting an overly tidy origin myth.
- **Jefferson Davis and Confederate governance.** Constitutional claims, cotton diplomacy, military decisions, wartime dissent and internal political fracture.
- **The international and material war.** European neutrality, blockade, commerce raiding, industrial production, food systems and the collapse of Confederate logistics.

The track is complete enough to listen to as a narrative, but the portraits and wartime experiences can be expanded where the historical record offers real material.
