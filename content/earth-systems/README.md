# Earth as a System: Air, Water, Ocean, and Climate

**Status:** in progress. Thirty-six complete narrated lectures (001000-036000). The first four establish the physical foundation; carbon, frozen water and landscape chapters connect reservoirs; three lectures develop ENSO; five follow the mechanisms and impacts of extreme weather; four connect the nitrogen, phosphorus and oxygen cycles through soils, waters and microbes; six reconstruct past climate, six develop how Earth-system models work, and five follow the changing coast.

The course explains the coupled Earth system by following energy, water, carbon, geological material and information in ocean-atmosphere feedbacks. Each manuscript has a single spoken region and a non-spoken scientific companion containing source notes, definitions and questions.

## Editorial conventions

- Explain budgets, feedbacks and physical mechanisms in spoken language; exact definitions and specialist references belong in scientific companions.
- Separate observations from analysis estimates, numerical model results and inferences. Do not treat one SST index as a complete coupled ENSO diagnosis.
- State the time window and region of climate anomalies. Teleconnections are conditional seasonal tendencies, not local daily-weather guarantees.
- Distinguish ONI and NOAA CPC's 2026 shift to RONI; historical operational changes may be dated, whereas general physical processes must not receive invented chronological coverage.
- Explain storm intensity, event probability, exposed populations and realized damage as distinct quantities; a wind category, moisture flux, drought index or event attribution statement is never a complete local impact forecast.
- Keep source-specific and place-specific tags on the lectures where those sources and places actually occur. Discovery paths include geographic case tags without implying every region has the same impact.
- All content is text-only source material; audio and images are not bundled.

## The Changing Coast (032000–036000)

Five independent but connected lectures follow astronomical tide forcing, wave-driven sand transport, barrier island change, mixing within estuaries and delta sediment/elevation balances. Sources and topic-specific, geographic and historical publication/event tags are encoded in each lecture sidecar. Listening routes: `the-changing-coast`, `coastal-sediment-and-land`, and `coastal-water-exchange`. The earlier 31 lectures and their spoken text are unchanged.

## How We Know the Climate Past (020000–025000)

A six-chapter methodological sequence: ice cores and air ages; crossdated tree rings and radiocarbon calibration; marine and lake sediments; cave calcite and U-series dates; combining chronological constraints; and a multi-proxy synthesis using deglaciation, the Younger Dryas and PAGES 2k. Each chapter is a complete standalone spoken manuscript with claim-linked evidence and hierarchical topic, method, place and historical-study tags.


## How Earth System Models Work (026000–031000)

The course extends from observing climate to testing numerical physical hypotheses. The six lectures form a complete, optional methods unit with no hard prerequisites. They distinguish computational grids and conservative fluxes, unresolved physical parameterizations, coupled reservoirs, observation-conditioned analyses, model experiments and critical uncertainty assessment.

| ID | Lecture | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 026000 | A Planet in Finite Boxes: The Laws Behind an Earth System Model | How conservation laws, computational grids, dynamical cores, time integration and effective resolution turn atmospheric and ocean physics into a numerical model, with a worked energy-budget example. |
| 027000 | The Weather Inside a Grid Cell: Clouds, Turbulence, and Tuning | A mechanistic account of how global models approximate convective clouds, radiation, turbulent transport and land-surface effects below grid scale, including scientific tuning and independent validation. |
| 028000 | When Models Share a Boundary: Coupling the Ocean, Air, Ice, and Living Earth | How atmosphere, ocean, land, sea ice and the carbon cycle exchange heat, freshwater, salt, momentum and tracers across grids and time steps; coupling drift, conservative remapping and spin-up. |
| 029000 | A Forecast Begins with a Guess: Observations, Data Assimilation, and Reanalysis | How observations and physics-based backgrounds are combined through uncertainty-weighted analysis, observation operators, 4D-Var, ensemble assimilation and historical reanalysis, with a worked numeric example. |
| 030000 | A Second Earth for Experiments: Controls, Forcings, and Climate Projections | How counterfactual climate experiments use control runs, step and ramp forcings, historical hindcasts and future emissions- or concentration-driven pathways; what CMIP6 and CMIP7 protocols mean. |
| 031000 | When a Climate Model Is Wrong: Testing Skill, Uncertainty, and Trust | How model evaluation separates weather trajectories from climate statistics and tests budgets, independent skill, physical mechanisms, ensemble dependence, internal variability, model response and scenarios. |
