## People, names, and material facts

- Jane's maternal aunt by marriage is **Mrs. Reed**; her paternal uncle **John Eyre of Madeira** writes to her and later leaves the inheritance. **St. John, Diana, and Mary Rivers** are Jane's cousins, not Rochester's relatives. "St. John" is traditionally pronounced like "Sinjin" in British English; that may guide TTS pronunciation, but the text keeps the usual spelling.
- **Adèle Varens** is Rochester's ward, raised under his guardianship; the novel does not resolve paternity with documentary certainty. **Céline Varens** is Adèle's mother.
- **Bertha Antoinetta Mason** is Rochester's existing wife. **Richard Mason** is her brother. Rochester's account of the Caribbean marriage is his speech within Jane's memoir, not neutral evidence of every claim about Bertha or her family.
- Jane leaves Rochester when she learns the wedding cannot lawfully proceed. She later inherits **£20,000** and voluntarily shares it equally with three Rivers cousins, giving each **£5,000**.
- **Helen Burns** dies of consumption during a broader infectious disease emergency at Lowood. Her individual death should not be collapsed into the outbreak's diagnosis.
- Rochester's later blindness and loss of a hand occur during the fire that destroys Thornfield; they do not justify casting his injuries as a divine punishment or Bertha's death as an ethically cost-free solution.
- Jane recounts events as an adult. Her narrative gives powerful access to *her* perceptions but no independently narrated autobiography for Bertha.

## Documentary and textual orientation

- **Primary continuous reading text:** [Project Gutenberg 1260, *Jane Eyre: An Autobiography*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1260). The accessible Gutenberg text descends from a late nineteenth-century illustrated printing; it should not be called a facsimile of the 1847 first edition.
- **First-edition-oriented transcription:** [University of Virginia, Literature in Context, *Jane Eyre*](https://anthologydev.lib.virginia.edu/work/BronteC/bronte-jane-eyre) identifies its basis as the 1847 Smith, Elder text and includes additional editorial apparatus. Editorial choices and later prefaces need to be identified rather than folded invisibly into the first edition.
- **First-edition material witness:** [The Morgan Library & Museum, *Jane Eyre* first edition, 1847](https://www.themorgan.org/printed-books/72468). Three volumes under "edited by Currer Bell." [British Library manuscript catalog](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-002056383) records a surviving fair-copy manuscript and 1847 publication. Sources differ on the exact October retail date; the lectures use **1847** rather than asserting a contested day.
- **Historical setting and childhood source:** [The British Library, "Jane Eyre and the rebellious child"](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/en/articles/jane-eyre-and-the-rebellious-child/) and [The British Library's overview](https://www.britishlibrary.cn/en/works/charlotte-brontes-jane-eyre/) connect Lowood to the Clergy Daughters' School and explain the novel's challenge to Victorian treatment of children. The fictional Lowood is not a line-for-line documentary account of Cowan Bridge.
- **Brontë family and pseudonyms:** [Brontë Parsonage Museum, introduction](https://www.bronte.org.uk/the-lives-of-the-brontes). "Currer Bell" is Charlotte's pseudonym; do not conflate her novel with Emily's *Wuthering Heights* or Anne's works.
- **Later critical response:** [W. W. Norton, *Wide Sargasso Sea* edition description](https://wwnorton.co.uk/books/9780393960129-wide-sargasso-sea-f91be854-ca5e-4803-9804-0ba277579158) places Jean Rhys's 1966 novel in explicit relation to *Jane Eyre*. It is a later creative reimagining, not recovered evidence of Brontë's intentions.

## Questions that remain open

1. How much should a reader trust the first-person narrator when she reports Rochester's views of Bertha, and what claims can be established independently within the fictional world?
2. To what extent does inheritance make Jane's final consent independent, and which unequal dimensions of her relationship remain?
3. What is the narrative and theological status of the heard voice across the distance? The text dramatizes the experience without furnishing an external test.
4. Why conclude with St. John's vocation instead of with marriage, and how does that choice qualify a purely romantic reading?

**Editorial scope:** These lectures rely on a named complete English reading text, a first-edition-oriented text, and selected institutional history and critical records. They are not a full collation of the surviving manuscript, the 1847 three-volume edition, all subsequent editions, and all translations.
