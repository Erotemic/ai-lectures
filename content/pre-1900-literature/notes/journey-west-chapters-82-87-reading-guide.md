# Journey to the West: chapters 82–87 — reading and source guide

**Scope:** Six original, scene-first English narrative manuscripts `555000`–`560000`, retelling the Chinese selected witness's chapters 82–87. Spoken dialogue, transitions, imagery, and chapter pacing are independently composed, not copied from an English translation. These are events in a novel, not documented events in the life of the historical Xuanzang. Descriptive word counts do not impose narration-duration targets.

**Primary text and broader reading:** [*Xiyou ji*, Chinese Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98), with individual chapter links below. For the novel's history and major themes, consult [*Journey to the West* (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_West), a contextual overview rather than the witness used to establish scene boundaries. For the chapter-9 River Child variant and the difference between the transmitted witness and an early printed edition, see [the expanded-retelling source map](journey-west-expanded-retelling-source-map.md).

## Chapter 82 — A peach in Bottomless Cave (`555000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第082回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC082%E5%9B%9E).

- **Opening continuity:** Xuanzang has been abducted from Zhenhai Monastery to **Bottomless Cave under Trap-Sky Mountain**; the demon had substituted an embroidered shoe in chapter 81. **Bajie goes first to scout**, not Wukong, and the three disciples have not entered the underground household.
- Bajie discovers an immense underground cave complex, arranged like another inhabited country, and reports the scale of the hiding place to his companions. Wukong descends, disguised as a small insect, and follows the sounds of a household preparing a forced marriage.
- Xuanzang is guarded in the demon's home. The demon has already determined that the monk will be her husband; his frightened politeness must not be narrated as willing consent. The incongruity of a lavish vegetarian feast in a prison is a central scene.
- Wukong first plans to enter the demon's body through **bubbles on a wine cup** offered by Xuanzang. The bubbles break and the disguise is exposed, so the first scheme fails; do not tell this scene as an immediately successful rescue.
- The monkey then invents a more effective stratagem **using a peach**. His bodily infiltration pressures the demon to move Xuanzang toward the cave opening, but the standoff is not finished at the chapter break.
- **Exact closing limit:** The captor remains alive and dangerous, Xuanzang has not been safely returned to the pilgrims, and the connection to Li Jing and Nezha has not yet been discovered or acted upon. The second shoe substitution and divine intervention belong to chapter 83.

## Chapter 83 — The tablets of Li Jing and Nezha (`556000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第083回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC083%E5%9B%9E).

- As Wukong presses the demon toward rescue, she again uses an **embroidered shoe** as a false substitute and evades the attackers, taking Xuanzang away from the expected escape route. The earlier monastery shoe was not a single-use illusion.
- Wukong investigates the cave more carefully and finds **tablets honoring the Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King Li Jing and Nezha**. The clues create grounds for a formal accusation in Heaven, not proof that the heavenly figures personally ordered this abduction.
- The accusation first produces indignation from Li Jing; **Nezha explains the background**. The spirit is the **golden-nosed white rat**, an animal demon who had once been spared and later treated the heavenly king and his son as adoptive family in gratitude.
- The narration should distinguish **kinship obligations, gratitude, and official responsibility** rather than flatten the rescue into a simple story of a heavenly demon. Wukong's complaint forces a guardian of Heaven to acknowledge a link to a predator on earth.
- Li Jing and his troops intervene; the demon is constrained, **Xuanzang is recovered**, and the travelers are once more able to leave the region. Retain the contrast between Wukong's anger and the disciple's obligation to finish the mission.
- **Closing limit:** The Bottomless Cave episode ends here. The next threat is a mortal king with a vow against monks; do not run that kingdom into chapter 83.

## Chapter 84 — The kingdom that vowed to kill monks (`557000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第084回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC084%E5%9B%9E).

- The travelers reach **Dharma-Destroying Kingdom**, where the king has vowed to execute **10,000 Buddhist monks**. He has killed **9,996**, leaving exactly four deaths he expects to complete the tally. This count makes the approaching four travelers particularly vulnerable.
- A warning associated with **Guanyin** comes before the disguises. Wukong decides that a direct battle at the gates would put Xuanzang at needless risk; **merchant clothes**, modified appearances, and an innkeeper's **large luggage chest** provide a way through the city.
- The chest, containing the concealed pilgrims, becomes a coveted object and is **stolen by robbers**. Its recovery by royal officers turns concealment itself into a potential trap.
- While the court sleeps, Wukong **shaves the king and other members of the royal household and officials**. The scene turns a king's boast about the slaughter of monks into a humiliating display of shaved heads, using comic reversal rather than the killing of a ruler.
- **Exact closing limit:** The shaving has happened, but the king has not yet recognized or renounced his crimes. **His change of heart and the renaming of the country begin in chapter 85**, so chapter 84 should not end in full reconciliation.

## Chapter 85 — The shaven court and Hidden Mist Mountain (`558000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第085回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC085%E5%9B%9E).

- **Opening continuity:** The shaven royal court faces the impossibility of hiding what has occurred. The king repents his vow, ceases his campaign against monks, and **renames the country Dharma-Honoring Kingdom**. This completes the chapter-84 episode before the company returns to the road.
- Beyond the city, the mountain episode begins with the disciples' familiar arguments over food, fear, and who should scout. Wukong's teasing can be comic while the danger remains serious.
- A **leopard demon** of **Hidden Mist Mountain** sends false advances and decoys to draw the disciples away. These tactics serve to separate the escort, not to prove that any particular disciple is weak by nature.
- The demon **captures Xuanzang** and takes him to a mountain cave where a **woodcutter** is already being held. The woodcutter has an aged mother at home, making the rescue consequential beyond the pilgrim company.
- **Exact closing limit:** The chapter ends with Xuanzang captive. Neither the fake severed head nor the leopard's defeat should be placed in chapter 85; both are chapter-86 events.

## Chapter 86 — The false head and the woodcutter's mother (`559000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第086回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC086%E5%9B%9E).

- The leopard attempts to break the disciples' resolve by displaying **counterfeit severed heads**. The trick appears to confirm Xuanzang's death, and the disciples respond with grief, anger, and uncertainty. Retain the successive deceptions rather than telling the whole episode as one unconvincing prop.
- Wukong distrusts appearances, infiltrates the cave, and discovers **both Xuanzang and the captive woodcutter alive**. The rescues are linked: the monk's liberation does not exhaust the moral obligations of the scene.
- The cave's sleep and guard arrangements allow Wukong to turn the demon's concealment against him. The lair is destroyed and the **leopard** revealed and defeated, while the captives escape.
- The woodcutter is restored to his **elderly mother**, who had feared that her son had vanished forever. Their reunion gives the episode a domestic conclusion outside a king's court or a divine tribunal.
- **Closing limit:** This ordeal resolves before the pilgrims enter Fengxian Prefecture in chapter 87. The leopard should not remain a threat in the drought story.

## Chapter 87 — Rain over Fengxian (`560000`)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第087回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC087%E5%9B%9E).

- The travelers enter **Fengxian Prefecture**, a region devastated by **three years without rain**. Scarce food, dry wells, flight, and hunger affect ordinary families. The prefect seeks help and promises a reward; Wukong refuses to treat water as a commodity sold to him.
- Summoning the **Dragon King** does not immediately help, because official rain must be authorized by Heaven. Wukong goes up to inquire and finds a punishment tied to the prefect's furious violation of an offering: the emperor's displeasure has been extended to the whole district.
- Three symbols give apparently impossible conditions: a **chicken must peck away a mountain of rice**, a **dog must lick away a mountain of flour**, and a **candle flame must burn through a golden lock**. These are part of the **novel's moral cosmology**, not physical claims about drought or atmospheric causation.
- Wukong brings the message back and urges **repentance in action, public charity, and repaired conduct** rather than another grand petition that leaves society unchanged. Reports of the population's transformation reach Heaven; the conditions are recognized as fulfilled.
- Heavenly rain officers authorize and deliver **a measured, abundant downpour**. The people see their fields and streets filled with water and thank the divine officers and pilgrims. The story closes with a **Sweet Rain, Universal Relief temple**, farewells, and the pilgrimage continuing west.
- **Exact closing limit:** Fengxian's drought is resolved and the travelers have left. **Chapter 88 begins with Jade Flower Kingdom**, a distinct episode; do not import its characters or incidents into chapter 87.
