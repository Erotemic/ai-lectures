## Chapter 64 — thorny passage and the tree poets

- The party leaves **Jisai** after the recovery of the luminous relic, refusing gifts of personal wealth while accepting travel provisions. Avoid repeating the dragon-palace fight in chapter 64. The forest is a new obstacle.
- The **Eight-Hundred-Li Thorn Ridge** defeats ordinary progress; the horse cannot pass and Wukong calls on **Zhu Bajie** to clear the growth. Bajie's labor matters for the party's passage and supplies a physical counterpoint to the ensuing discussion of refined poetry.
- A mysterious older figure presents himself as an inviting local earth god and carries **Xuanzang away to an assembly of older gentlemen**. Wukong is immediately doubtful. Their apparent religious and literary learning should be given time to develop on its own terms, rather than summarized as an instant attack.
- The company enjoys challenging conversation in verse and in discussions of **Buddhist and Daoist ideas**, with Xuanzang answering as a monk who can appreciate the exchange without accepting it as religious authority. His intellectual pleasure makes the predicament more convincing.
- **Apricot Immortal** arrives with attendants, composes verse, and becomes the proposed bride. The elders' praise and flattery turn into pressure that would make Xuanzang abandon his celibate religious vocation. His resistance must be clear; he does not marry her.
- The disciples locate Xuanzang at dawn. **Bajie fells the spirit-trees** after the men and woman prove to be plant spirits. Xuanzang objects to the killing; neither the learned conversation nor the violence is simply erased by the outcome. The party travels west afterward.

## Chapter 65 — the false Little Thunderclap

- The mountain and temple seem to promise the real destination. **Xuanzang's longing to arrive** makes him vulnerable to an apparently Buddhist institution; **Wukong mistrusts the place** before the trap is sprung.
- A figure claiming the Buddha's authority commands worship. Wukong recognizes a false appearance, and the monster **Yellow Brows** imprisons him in **golden cymbals**. The other three pilgrims are captives in the complex; this is not yet the true Western Paradise.
- Wukong cannot simply break or prop apart the vessel. The Celestial officers arrive; **Kang Jinlong**, the Horned Golden Dragon star, extends his horn through a small opening so Wukong can escape. The maneuver causes painful effort rather than an effortless single strike.
- Yellow Brows is powerful even when his disguise is obvious. His **white bag** swallows Wukong's allies, including celestial helpers, after outwardly successful attack sequences. His relic is a repeated reversal, not a one-time trick.
- While the captives' lives remain in danger, Wukong hides, looks for the party's **travel papers and baggage**, and attempts to free his master. His rescues are defeated; the party is recaptured. Chapter 65 ends with Wukong seeking help elsewhere, not with the demon subdued.

## Chapter 66 — Wudang, Little Zhang, and the melon

- **At Mount Wudang**, Wukong seeks help from the Northern Lord, who cannot personally abandon his assigned post. The borrowed divine guardians and later **Little Zhang, the Great Sage State Preceptor**, cannot overcome Yellow Brows' magic bag. The botched rescue attempts reinforce that sheer numbers are ineffective.
- **Maitreya** descends with a specific answer: Yellow Brows is his own attendant, who escaped with his master's **Later-Heaven Human-Seed Bag** and a striker used as a weapon. This is an accountability problem within the divine world; the false sanctuary is empowered by genuine religious possessions.
- Maitreya's plan uses a **melon field** and Wukong's shape-changing abilities instead of another army. Wukong enters the fruit; Yellow Brows eats it. Inside the demon's belly, the monkey's movement and threats force surrender.
- Maitreya reveals himself and reclaims his servant and treasures. **Yellow Brows is not slain** by Wukong. The monkey still must release the pilgrims and dispose of the hostile demon followers; the false temple is destroyed.
- The original destination remains ahead. A tale about false holy appearances should leave the travelers restored to motion, not suggest they already obtained the scriptures.

## Chapter 67 — Tuoluo Village and two obstacles

- The village's people fear a **serpent monster**, and the old host initially doubts that travelers could protect them. The narrative spends time on hospitality, bargaining, Wukong's deliberate testing of a host, and Bajie's appetite; there is more here than the snake fight.
- A storm announces the creature. **Wukong and Bajie** struggle with it, discover that its true body is a **great red python**, and pursue it after it withdraws. Wukong ventures **inside the serpent** and impales it with the iron staff. This action is not to be replaced by an external blow alone.
- The villagers are grateful, but the pilgrims have another obstacle: a road closed by a **massive, rotten accumulation of persimmons**. Killing the creature does not make the road passable.
- **Bajie assumes an enormous boar's form** and roots through the obstruction while the village helps clear the pathway. This long, dirty labor should be narrated as the second half of the resolution; Xuanzang's party needs a road as much as a slain monster.

## Chapter 68 — the king's sickness and Wukong's joke

- On emerging into **Zhuzi Kingdom**, the travelers enjoy the contrast between the filthy mountain passage and a well-organized city. Xuanzang's visit concerns the **passports and proper reception**, not yet the queen's abduction.
- Zhuzi's **king is very ill** and hears Xuanzang speak of the eastern court and the mission. Wukong and Bajie move through the city and discover a public proclamation seeking a physician.
- **Wukong conceals the royal notice in Bajie's clothing**, setting the guards upon an indignant pig who insists he is not a healer. The comic action establishes Wukong's confidence and the court's bureaucratic reaction; do not turn Bajie into the actual prescriber.
- Wukong answers the royal call. Because the king cannot endure seeing unfamiliar faces, the monkey proposes **three threads taken from his hair** to obtain a pulse at a distance. Chapter 68 ends with this examination **about to begin**. The diagnosis and cure are in chapter 69.

## Chapter 69 — Black-Gold Pills and the missing Golden Sage

- Wukong takes the remote pulse and diagnoses a **sickness caused by separation**, expressed through a poetic comparison of **paired birds divided by a storm**. The cause is initially understood by Wukong and is not openly explained to the court.
- Physicians send **eight hundred and eight medicinal ingredients** in great quantities, more than are needed. Wukong selects **rhubarb, croton, pot soot (Hundred-Grass Frost), and dragon-horse urine**, making three dark pills. Bajie repeatedly complains and nearly betrays the recipe later; the horse's speech explains why he does not casually yield his powerful fluid.
- The pills require **rainwater not yet fallen to the ground**. Wukong brings the **Dragon King of the Eastern Sea** to deliver the moisture in a fine spray; there is no need for a full rainstorm. The apparently sacred rain has the dragon's very practical explanation.
- The king's vigorous purging and recovery are part of the tale's logic, not a recommendation for real medical treatment. The physician episode is presented as the novel's fictional miracle and comic medicine, never as instructions for dosing real medicines.
- Over a banquet, the now healthy king explains the underlying sorrow: three years earlier **Sai Taisui of Qilin Mountain** abducted his wife, the **Golden Sage**, during a public festival. The king feared a threat to his kingdom if he refused. The queen **has not been rescued** by the end of chapter 69.
- The king reveals an underground refuge beneath the palace in which he hoped to survive renewed threats. A **large threatening figure arrives** in a gust of wind, and **Wukong rises to challenge or question him**. **Chapter 69 stops before the visitor identifies himself.** The opening of **chapter 70** establishes that he is **Sai Taisui's advance guard** rather than the demon king himself; do not import that exchange into chapter 69.
