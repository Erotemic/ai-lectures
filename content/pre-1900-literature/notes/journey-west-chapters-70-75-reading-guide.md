# Journey to the West: chapters 70–75 — reading and source guide

**Scope:** original, scene-first English narrative manuscripts `543000`–`548000`, corresponding to selected Chinese witness *Xiyou ji* chapters 70–75. The manuscripts are not translations of an English edition; dialogue and transitions are newly written from review of the corresponding Chinese chapter. Duration is descriptive, not a target. This is a fictional novel, not a historical report.

**Edition:** Chapter numbering and texts here follow the linked Chinese [Wikisource *Xiyou ji*](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98). See [the source map](journey-west-expanded-retelling-source-map.md) for the special status of the River Child insertion and the disputed Wu Cheng'en attribution. [*Journey to the West* (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_West) provides background, not the plot authority for this chapter-level narration.

## Chapter 70 — The bells on Qilin Mountain (543000)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第070回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC070%E5%9B%9E).

- **Open after chapter 69, not before:** Zhuzi's king has recovered from illness but the kidnapped Golden Sage has not been rescued. A demon approaches the royal palace, and chapter 70 reveals him to be **Sai Taisui's advance guard**, not the king himself.
- Wukong defeats the envoy's martial challenge. The visitor and the monarch's fear disclose the wider threat from Qilin Mountain.
- The king offers the rescuing pilgrim useful finery and supplies. Wukong hears from the demon messenger **Youlaiyouqu** about the captive woman and the enchanted garment that prevents her abductor from approaching without being pricked.
- The messenger's account includes suffering at the demon household and the threat of Sai Taisui's treasure bells. Wukong kills the messenger and assumes the role needed to approach the mountain.
- The three purple-gold bells command smoke, fire, and sand. Wukong reaches the inner court, but his attempt to steal them produces a dangerous sound and betrays his presence.
- **Closing limit:** The queen is still captive; the *real* bells remain with Sai Taisui, and the confrontation continues into chapter 71.

## Chapter 71 — The Golden Sage returns (544000)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第071回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC071%E5%9B%9E).

- Wukong regains access to the Golden Sage's rooms, disguises himself as the young attendant **Chun Jiao**, and makes a second attempt to get the bells.
- Insect magic and staged discomfort distract the demon; false objects stand in for the bells while Wukong takes the authentic treasures.
- Sai Taisui's alarming reputation is exposed when Wukong uses the three bells against him. Their blasts of smoke, fire, and sand reverse the danger.
- **Guanyin intervenes** to save the creature, whom she identifies as her golden-haired mount. Within the novel, the monarch's earlier act against the Peacock Wisdom King's chicks explains a punishment measured in three years. Wukong objects that the punishment has brought terrible harm to the queen and to other victims; retain his objection rather than endorsing the explanation as a straightforward moral justification.
- The genuine bells are returned to their divine owner. Wukong carries the Golden Sage back to the kingdom; the protective prickly garment is removed by the holy figure **Zhang Ziyang**, allowing the reunion with the king.
- **Closing limit:** Zhuzi's story resolves by the end of chapter 71; the seven spider sisters first appear in chapter 72.

## Chapter 72 — The seven sisters (545000)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第072回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC072%E5%9B%9E).

- After Zhuzi the master insists on begging food himself. The ordinary house by a bridge proves to be the home of seven spider spirits, who bind him and hang him in their cave.
- The sisters release silk from their bodies to fill the approach with a web; Wukong consults the local land god about Spider-Silk Cave and the hot spring nearby.
- He follows them to their bath, **steals their clothing rather than striking them there**, and learns their intention to eat Xuanzang.
- Bajie ignores restraint, goes into the spring, and pursues the seven in the guise of a slippery fish; the bathing scene and the women's alarm are causal to the next chapter.
- The seven's insect-children attempt to attack the rescuers. Wukong uses magical hair-grown birds to overcome them, frees Xuanzang, and destroys the cave.
- **Closing limit:** The spider sisters survive the episode and flee. Their appeal to their Daoist associate happens in chapter 73.

## Chapter 73 — Yellow Flower Temple (546000)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第073回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC073%E5%9B%9E).

- Xuanzang enters a beautiful Daoist establishment with an open mind; its priest is preparing medicinal pills. The fugitive spider sisters recognize his new guests and enlist his vengeance.
- He gives Xuanzang, Bajie, and Sha poisoned tea. Wukong sees the difference in the host's cup and refrains from drinking, but his warning comes too late for his companions.
- In the ensuing fight, the seven sisters attempt to trap Wukong with silk. **Wukong kills all seven**; do not imply they escape after chapter 73.
- The Daoist reveals a vast multitude of eyes beneath his ribs and imprisons Wukong in golden light. The monkey escapes by becoming a pangolin and tunneling beneath its reach.
- A woman in mourning tells him to seek **Pilampo** on Purple Cloud Mountain; she is **the Old Mother of Mount Li** in disguise, not an actual recently bereaved wife.
- Pilampo's **tiny needle**, associated with her rooster-star son, overcomes the light. The priest becomes a centipede; she **spares and takes him**, rather than permitting Wukong to kill him. She provides an antidote to the three pilgrims. The temple burns and the travelers depart.
- **Closing limit:** The episode is resolved. A warning about the demons of Lion-Camel Ridge belongs to chapter 74.

## Chapter 74 — The Lion-Camel patrolman (547000)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第074回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC074%E5%9B%9E).

- A white-haired elder warns of the tremendous demon population and three rulers. He is **Taibai Jinxing**, a heavenly messenger. Xuanzang falls from his horse in fright, and Wukong briefly transforms into a handsome young monk to question him.
- Wukong scouts the hill and lands as a fly upon a watchman's cap. The patrolman's repeated warning about monkey-to-fly transformations almost makes Wukong reveal himself.
- Wukong imitates a fellow patrolman but lacks a gilt identity badge. He sees the guard's name **Little Wind-Driller (Xiao Zuanfeng)** and improvises a superior token naming himself **Chief Wind-Driller (Zong Zuanfeng)**.
- The guard explains the three lords: a blue-maned lion, a yellow-tusked elephant, and a great roc. The roc has a **yin-yang bottle**; the demonic power reaches into a conquered Lion-Camel Kingdom beyond the mountain.
- Wukong kills and impersonates the guard, then convinces successive sentry groups that the Great Sage is sharpening a gigantic staff and intends to annihilate the defenders. The lesser demons flee.
- **Closing limit:** Wukong enters the rulers' cave **without yet confronting them**. The cave's interior and the fate of the disguise occur in chapter 75.

## Chapter 75 — The yin-yang bottle and the swallowed monkey (548000)

Primary witness: [西遊記/第075回](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E8%A5%BF%E9%81%8A%E8%A8%98/%E7%AC%AC075%E5%9B%9E).

- The outer cave is piled with human remains and scenes of slaughter. Beyond that are an inner garden and the three kings. Wukong continues to pose as Little Wind-Driller.
- A hair-grown fly terrifies the kings; Wukong laughs, and the **third king, the roc**, catches the monkey mouth appearing through his disguise. The body beneath the seeming human face reveals monkey hair and tail.
- **Thirty-six demons** carry out the yin-yang two-energy bottle. Inside it, Wukong faces fire, snakes, and fire-dragons. Neither growing nor shrinking defeats the vessel, and his ankle begins to soften.
- Remembering **Guanyin's three life-saving hairs** given near the Eagle-Grief Stream episode, Wukong makes a diamond drill, bamboo implement, and cord, drills the bottle open, escapes, and returns to Xuanzang.
- Wukong brings Bajie to challenge the rulers. The **lion** fights Wukong; Bajie joins rashly, sees the lion's terrible open mouth and hides. Wukong deliberately allows the lion to swallow him.
- Bajie sincerely believes Wukong dead and proposes dividing the luggage and returning home; Xuanzang mourns. Meanwhile Wukong talks from inside the lion's stomach, threatens to cook the organs, resists vomiting, and intercepts the lion's medicinal wine.
- **Exact closing limit:** After drinking the wine Wukong tumbles drunkenly inside the lion, making him fall in agony. The outcome is deferred to **chapter 76**. Do not end this lecture with his release or the eventual defeat of the three rulers.

## Next narrative hinge: chapters 76–81

Start with **chapter 76, not with a completed Lion-Camel victory**. Xuanzang and Sha believe Wukong lost; Bajie is pressing the party to abandon the pilgrimage. Wukong is alive, active, and temporarily beyond the lion's power to expel. Chapters 76–77 complete the sprawling three-demon confrontation, then the road moves toward new dangers.
