# Mathematics · Find a Path

Choose a subject or begin with a question. The mathematics library has useful
introductory courses, long specialist studies, and several treatments of the
same idea for different purposes. This guide connects them without asking a
listener to take every course in source-directory order.

The starting hub groups the subjects into six families. Four learning paths
provide a smaller set of choices:

| Path | What it develops | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proof and Linear Algebra | Claims and proofs; sets and functions; linear maps, spectral directions, and dual measurements | School algebra |
| Limits to Motion | Convergence, calculus, several-variable geometry, and ordinary differential equations | School algebra and functions; linear algebra before the several-variable stage |
| Proofs, Programs, and Mathematical Structure | Proof objects, equality, trusted checking, algorithmic reasoning, and universal properties | Sets, functions, logic, and short proofs |
| Reliable Answers | Conditioning, backward error, residuals, stopping rules, and convex optimality certificates | Linear algebra and elementary calculus |

Each path is divided into stages. Chapter links lead to the corresponding
subject course, where the listener can continue into its deeper branches.
The operator family gives direct access to G2 topic studies, the G3 paper
course, Davis–Kahan theorem families, matching, statistical applications, and
the visual presentation lectures. These remain substantial specialist studies;
the introductory paths do not certify their background as mastered.

This pack owns navigation, not lecture manuscripts. `track.json` intentionally
has an empty lecture inventory. `navigation.json` selects existing qualified
lecture IDs, so the original manuscripts, companions, source records, and
recordings stay with their subject packs. The `start` collection is the guide;
its `browse_sections` organize links to existing collections. Its
`guide_for: ["Mathematics"]` identifies the subject it introduces.

Path order is an editorial recommendation. It creates no new teaching
prerequisite or formal proof edge, and it does not infer knowledge from
listening. A listener can enter at any stage and use the linked preparation
when necessary. A portable installation may lack a referenced subject pack;
that should be shown as unavailable rather than supplied by a substitute.

The [organization audit and handoff](../../docs/planning/math-library-organization.md)
records coverage, overlap, assumptions, existing gaps, and proposed further
organization. New lectures should remain in their subject packs. Review guide
references after a subject changes its route names or consolidates manuscripts.
