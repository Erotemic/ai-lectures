Camille Jordan and Lord Rayleigh History ZIP overlays applied. This ZIP is
incremental to that sequence and does not repackage earlier lecture changes.

**Narration:** 4,418 spoken words (~30.5 minutes at the reference rate of 145
words/minute). This is a substantive history lecture on Ritz's finite-dimensional
variational method and the separate spectral-line combination principle. It
also treats the 1909 Ritz–Einstein debate and the limitations of Ritz's
classical atomic models. The narration develops an admissible string trial
space from a parabola through a two-function generalized eigenproblem and
connects the construction to residuals, subspace geometry and spectral gaps.

matrices, determinant and Ritz roots, and checks the Rayleigh bound against
π². It separately explains the weak-versus-strong PDE residual, static
variational energy, requirements on a conforming function space, and the
spectral-term arithmetic. Trial functions for plate bending require different
energy and boundary constraints than fixed-end strings.

original publications support the variational and spectral contributions;

1995 spectroscopy study distinguishes accurate empirical predictions from
Ritz's later-rejected mechanical models; and Frisch/Pietsch (2016) supports
careful treatment of the radiation-asymmetry debate. The sources stay in
