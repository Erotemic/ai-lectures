# Human-side revisions to Newton and Fourier

This incremental, text-only overlay revises two existing History-track narrations, their source metadata, the shared source ledger, the cumulative track word counts, the History README and the evergreen lecture style guide. It does not add new portrait IDs or replace the existing 42-entry catalog with an earlier snapshot.

## Isaac Newton — `035000`

- Reworks the narration so mathematical discussions are followed by historically situated human scenes, including his interrupted upbringing, model-building, Cambridge friendship with Wickins, surviving sin list, apple narrative, Halley and Hooke, the optics and calculus disputes, alchemical and religious research, 1693 crisis, family life, and Mint investigations.
- Moves an appended block of mechanics and applications into the relevant sections of the lecture, eliminating the earlier false ending before that block.
- Adds a **display-only mathematical and historical companion**, absent from the previous Newton portrait, with inverse-square orbit geometry, calculus and root-finding examples, and a source-evidence guide.
- Uses direct testimony where available, and avoids attributing modern medical diagnoses or dramatic legends as established facts.

## Joseph Fourier — `041000`

- Preserves the heat-equation derivations, Fourier series and transforms, diffusion examples, and history of Earth-temperature research.
- Integrates his teenage mathematical ambition, intended religious training, revolutionary imprisonment, Egyptian administrative duties, Napoleon-era political reversals, Academy criticism, friendship with Sophie Germain, and the warmth/health anecdotes recorded by Arago.
- Distinguishes Arago's firsthand recollections from the medical explanations he suggested.

## Permanent editorial guidance

`LECTURE_STYLE_RULES.md` now defines an affirmative standard for human historical portraits: concrete evidenced scenes, relationships, ordinary working life, social context, achievements and failures, and precise boundaries around uncertain anecdotes. These should be woven into the mathematical exposition rather than appended as trivia.

## Sources and scope

New source records include Arago's memorial eulogy; Fourier's letters to Sophie Germain; a study of Fourier during the Hundred Days; the Newton Project's biographies and transcriptions; the Chymistry of Isaac Newton; the Royal Mint historical collection; and firsthand accounts reported by later biographers. New claims have anchors in `metadata/035000.json` and `metadata/041000.json`.

The overlay is intended to be applied **after the Fourier–Hypatia overlay**, which contains the latest 42-portrait History catalog. No binary media or synthesized audio is included.
