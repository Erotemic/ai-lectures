## Editorial consistency across the arc

- A spectrum is received radiation. An inferred emission time is a calculation based on an expansion history. Avoid calling the latter 'directly measured'.
- A thermal CMB spectrum supports a hot stage; angular peaks and polarization add independent constraints on the plasma and its fluctuations.
- Primordial light-element abundances test physics earlier than photon last scattering. Stellar chemical processing and selection of suitable gas matter to the inference.
- The same datum may have two classes of uncertainty: statistical measurement uncertainty and physical/model uncertainty. Quote precisely what each error bar covers.
- A result can be highly reliable within a model without making all models equally plausible. Distinguish historically contradicted explanations, live research alternatives and presently unconstrained proposals.
- Time labels (minutes, 380,000 years, 13.8 billion years) are part of the standard background history and cannot be assigned equal epistemic status to spectral line wavelengths or detector counts.
- Recorded sources are reference anchors, not proof that the exact full text of every paper has been verified. Avoid upgrading claim audit flags without source-passage checking.

## Next reserved lectures

The next substantive batch from the original curriculum plan is `006000` to `009000`:

1. **`006000` Missing Mass: Dark Matter or Different Gravity?** Compare rotation curves, lensing including cluster mergers, weak-lensing shear, CMB peak pattern and structure growth in a single evidence matrix. Teach at least one quantitative rotation-curve example and identify the observational limitations of modified-gravity versus particle explanations.
2. **`007000` Does the Vacuum Accelerate?** Teach the geometric meaning of supernova distances and BAO, conditional sound-horizon calibration, the Hubble tension, Lambda versus evolving dark energy, and the actual data combinations in DESI DR2/2026 follow-up studies. Separate statistical preference from established discovery.
3. **`008000` When the First Galaxies Appeared.** Reconstruct early stellar and galaxy populations from JWST spectroscopy, photometry, stellar modeling and selection. Avoid treating a photometric-redshift candidate as a spectroscopically established distance or age.
4. **`009000` Starlight, Stardust, and the Origin of Heavy Elements.** Explain stellar burning, supernova nucleosynthesis, neutron-capture pathways, chemical tagging, meteorite isotope constraints and neutron-star mergers. Distinguish directly observed emission from inferred elemental yields.
