# The World Reconsidered: Lectures 002000–005000

**Status:** Four substantive standalone drafts integrated into the existing
`tts-world-reconsidered` track. Source references and claim anchors recorded;
actual audio rendering and listening review outstanding.

## Editorial approach

These lectures pursue four different questions, making them usable as
individual recordings or as a consecutive flagship sequence after the causal
inference lecture. Each begins with a concrete problem, builds an explanatory
model and uses research and the lives of the researchers to examine where the
model succeeds and where it does not. Mathematical companions and citations
remain outside the spoken markers. Nothing relies on having heard Lecture 001000.

## Research decisions

- **Perception:** The student-memory comparison by Talarico and Rubin
  (2003) gives a direct design separating confidence from consistency. The
  1974 Loftus–Palmer paper supplies actual group counts and question wording.
- **Rationality:** The Bayes example and university conjunction are explicitly
  synthetic. Separate a formal violation of probability from differences in
  natural-language interpretation, stakes or information representation.
- **Cooperation:** Axelrod's original tournament is described with its known
  noise limitation. Ostrom's empirical governance work cannot be collapsed
  into either a blanket endorsement or rejection of state governance.
- **Institutional harm:** The reported 26/40 Milgram result applies to one
  experimental condition. The study of untreated syphilis at Tuskegee did not
  involve researchers infecting the participants. The Challenger investigation
  addresses a distinct type of institutional safety failure. These histories
  are not claimed to be morally equivalent.
