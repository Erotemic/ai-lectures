# Deep Time: Matter, Expansion, First Galaxies, and Element Origins — Evidence Map

**Research date:** 14 November 2026. **Covers:** `006000`–`009000`. **Status:** Four new substantive audio-first manuscripts with display-only methods companions. The narrative and claims below were researched against primary study/experiment pages and technical reviews; this document is an editorial map, not a reanalysis of raw instrument data. Audiobook synthesis and listening were not undertaken.

## Purpose

The four lectures carry cosmic reconstruction from *how matter gravitates* through *how expansion changes*, *when early galaxies are seen*, and *how enriched matter was produced*. These topics depend on each other, but the evidence should not be blurred together:

1. **Instrumental event:** a detector reads a wavelength, count, angle, time, spectrum, or correlated pattern.
2. **Calibrated observable:** reduction, foreground subtraction, sample selection, noise and response modeling turn raw readings into velocities, lensing fields, redshifts, distance moduli, luminosities, or abundance proxies.
3. **Dynamical model:** general relativity, population synthesis, reaction networks, and gas dynamics connect observables to mass, pressure, age, chemical composition, or stellar life cycle.
4. **Historical reconstruction:** the model states what happened before the recorded light was emitted.
5. **Extrapolated explanation:** a dark-sector particle, scalar field, modified gravitational dynamics, first stellar initial mass function, or unknown heavy-element production site accounts for the inferred history.

A successful explanation need not have the same confidence at all five stages. Avoid using precise error bars from a conditional fit as if they measured the underlying ontology with equal precision.

## Evidence and competing accounts by lecture

### 006000 — Missing Mass

| Trace | Secure or strong conclusion | What remains underdetermined | Alternatives / discriminating tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Doppler rotation curves plus stellar/gas mass models | Many disk galaxy motions exceed naive baryon-only Newtonian predictions | Stellar M/L, geometry, dynamics, feedback correlations | MOND low-acceleration phenomenology explains flat curves and baryonic acceleration relation; relativistic completion, clusters and CMB are hard tests |
| 153-disk SPARC radial acceleration relation | Close tracking of observed centripetal acceleration with baryonic distribution | Why relation is tight in physical models | Predict scatter and environmental dependence before observing new galaxy samples |
| Bullet Cluster lensing/X-ray positional offset | Gravitational potential not centered on most hot plasma | Precise projected mass inversion; collision dynamics | Collisionless matter in GR fits naturally; modified dynamics must model lensing and any required unseen matter |
| CMB peaks + primordial baryon abundance | Independent constraints on baryon and gravitating nonbaryonic components within LCDM | Particular dark particle and possible extensions of gravity | Fit full CMB TT/TE/EE, BBN, structure, lensing together |
| LZ 2026 high-energy event | One candidate nuclear-recoil-like interaction, global 2.6 sigma | Origin of event; look-elsewhere effects | Repeat, better background control and independent detector signatures |
| Euclid 12 Nov 2026 foundation release | Many new strong-lens candidates and large calibrated imaging/spectral archive | Full weak-shear cosmological inference | Await shear products planned in 2027; do not cite DR1-Foundation as a final dark-matter cosmology fit |

### 007000 — Expansion and dark energy

| Trace | Strongest warranted conclusion | Open question | Status of alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type Ia supernova light curves plus redshifts | Cosmic acceleration supported in large-scale metric framework | Physical mechanism and model dependence | Constant Lambda fits; time-dependent field or modified gravity are research possibilities |
| DESI BAO galaxy/quasar correlation peak | Precise distance ratios over redshift; sound ruler ties to early physics | Independent sound-horizon calibration | Test against BBN/CMB, different tracers and high-z forest |
| DESI 2026 DR2 Ly-alpha AP full-shape | 1% AP constraint at z=2.33, shifting central result toward Lambda-CDM | Cross-sample residuals, evolutionary dark energy | Joint model preference 2.7 sigma with CMB and 3.2 including SN in that analysis; cannot be called a discovery |
| Planck early-model vs local distance ladders | Method-dependent H0 values differ | Physics versus calibration/systematics and covariance | SH0ES 2025 higher calibration; CCHP 2025 TRGB lower; both need method-level description |

Sources to recheck when updating the lecture: DESI DR2 official list and July 30 2026 release, DES Year-5 supernova paper, SH0ES 2025 JWST reanalysis, and CCHP 2025 status report. Do not substitute a newer press release's sigma summary for the combination and model specified by an original paper.

### 008000 — Earliest galaxies

| Trace | Strongest warranted conclusion | More model-sensitive layer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| JWST NIRSpec Lyman break and line candidates in MoM-z14 | A source at spectroscopic z=14.44 | Its ~280 Myr model cosmic age, source population, stellar mass, early formation efficiency |
| ALMA [O III] 88-micron line in JADES-GS-z14-0 | Strong oxygen signal and precise z=14.1793 | Metallicity, ionization conditions and integrated stellar formation history |
| Survey bright-galaxy counts | Early luminous-galaxy population exists | Luminosity function normalization, survey selection, small-area cosmic variance |
| Stellar color/continuum | UV brightness and SED constraints | Stellar mass, age, IMF, burst history, dust vs old stars, AGN share |

Do not call a high-redshift galaxy *the first galaxy*. Do not turn a precise line redshift into a precise age of its oldest stars. Large mass estimates based on broadband photometry are historical reconstructions with astrophysical priors, not weighings.

### 009000 — The chemical inheritance of the universe

| Trace | Physical conclusion | Remaining ambiguity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laboratory nuclear reactions, neutrinos, stellar spectra | Hydrogen/helium burning and stellar production of carbon/oxygen | Reaction rates and site-dependent yields |
| Isotope ratios and stellar abundances | Multiple slow/rapid neutron-capture pathways | Relative sites across epochs and gas mixing |
| GW170817 GWs and optical/IR counterpart | Compact merger with kilonova consistent with r-process ejecta | Composition distribution, source rate and universal element fractions |
| VLT spectrum with Sr identification | A particular species identified in merger ejecta | Abundance of heavier r-process species and galaxy-integrated inventory |
| JWST GRB230307A proposed [Te III] feature | Spectral feature consistent with tellurium in one interpretation | 2026 continuum/dust reinterpretation disputes a simple merger explanation |

This archive gives a strong basis for multiple nucleosynthetic processes, but the source fractions of individual elements are a chemical-evolution inverse problem. The 2026 reanalysis is a deliberately included example of legitimate scientific disagreement about a transient rather than a settled counterexample to GW170817.

## Suggested maintenance triggers

- New particle direct-detection analyses that cross conventional discovery thresholds and survive independent corroboration.
- New Euclid shear/cosmology release; the November 2026 foundation release did not include the final shear cosmology products.
- New DESI/other-survey cross-validation, especially systematic reanalysis of Type Ia supernova calibration and Lyman-alpha full-shape constraints.
- Spectroscopic high-redshift sample changes and revised luminosity-function completeness, not merely new photometric candidates.
- New lines conclusively tied to neutron-capture elements in a merger, or published reinterpretations of existing spectra.
