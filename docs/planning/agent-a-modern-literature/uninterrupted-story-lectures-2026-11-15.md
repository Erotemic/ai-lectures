# Literature storytelling: consolidate by narrative, not by timer

The former twenty-five plot-orientation episodes were cut at relatively short intervals. This revision recombines them into **nine continuous story lectures**, one per novel, before their nine existing analyses. The first `To Kill a Mockingbird` account is about thirty minutes at the recorded narration rate; the other eight are measured at their naturally authored lengths. None has a fixed runtime obligation.

## Decisions

- Keep the first story ID for each novel (`000200`, `001200`, `002200`, `003300`, `004200`, `005200`, `006200`, `007200`, `008200`).
- Remove the redundant segment IDs and files; don't leave them in the playable manifest or as unindexed top-level manuscripts.
- Keep all existing analyses exactly as they were.
- Combine or remove recaps where they interrupted a single story, and preserve the relevant factual source anchors and bibliography records.
- Leave generous ID gaps for genuinely warranted additional recordings, but do not use them as an implicit target for how often to split a narrative.
- Word counts and predicted TTS duration are descriptive metadata only.

## Future authoring

Tell each work's story from beginning through ending before moving to analysis. A literature title need not be artificially limited to a ten-minute recording. Listen to the completed narration for flow and pronunciation; if the storytelling feels thin, enrich the plot and character context rather than filling time.

The prior planning documents remain as historical evidence of the earlier segmented campaign; this note supersedes their episode-count recommendations.
