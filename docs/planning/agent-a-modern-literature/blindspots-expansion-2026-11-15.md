# 20th-Century Literature: blind-spot repair, first expansion

This batch adds three complete story-and-analysis pairs to the pre-existing nine pairs. Earlier analyses and plot narrations are unchanged. The paired units are **Kokoro**, **Season of Migration to the North**, and **The God of Small Things**.

## Why these works

The previous nine novels were rich in US and English-language traditions, plus Nigeria and Colombia, but offered little engagement with twentieth-century East Asian fiction, Arabic-language African fiction, or Indian fiction rooted in specific South Asian social structures. These additions cover different languages, regions, and formal problems. *Kokoro* brings the modern Japanese confession and Meiji transition; *Season* gives a Sudanese Arabic frame narrative challenging both colonial representation and local patriarchy; *Small Things* brings a nonchronological Kerala family story marked by caste, gender and language.

## Further priorities

The next pair should consider Indigenous American literature, particularly Leslie Marmon Silko's *Ceremony*, with a careful account of Laguna Pueblo contexts and without reproducing ceremonial knowledge as a universal template. Later pairs should consider writing from the Caribbean, Eastern Europe, China, and the African continent beyond English-language publishing, alongside major queer and feminist movements. Continue to test blind spots within themes and technique, not just author geography.
