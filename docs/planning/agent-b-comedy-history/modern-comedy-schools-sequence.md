## Intellectual map

| ID | Question | Research tradition, opposed claims, limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 030500 | What are the major contemporary schools, what is each trying to explain, and how do their claims differ? | Long-form conceptual field guide; recurring museum case, explicit methodological distinctions, rival predictions, seven revision exercises |
| 031000 | How does a verbal joke make us change an interpretation? | Raskin (1985) script semantics; Attardo and Raskin (1991) General Theory of Verbal Humor; semantic construction versus pragmatics and audience reception |
| 032000 | Is humor surprise, satisfying comprehension, safe transgression, or some combination? | Suls (1972) incongruity-resolution; Ruch on nonsense; McGraw/Warren (2010/2012) benign violation and distance; laboratory findings versus performance generalizations |
| 033000 | Is a good sense of humor a stable beneficial personality property? | Martin et al. (2003) four humor styles; Heintz/Ruch (2019) extended style taxonomy; Lu (2023) cross-cultural review; measurement and causal limits |
| 034000 | How do comedy, cultural capital, power, gender and exclusion interact? | Billig (2005) ridicule; Kuipers/Friedman on taste; Krefting (2014) charged humor; compare disciplinary and emancipatory claims |
| 035000 | What can comedy reveal about the contradictions in our ideals? | Critchley (2002) comic human frailty; Zupančič (2008) contradiction and exception; Carroll (2014) analytic philosophy, amusement and ethical value |
| 036000 | How do artistic schools change who performs and what the audience expects? | British alternative comedy (late 1970s onward); anti-comedy, deadpan and cringe as strategies, not one unified movement; Ngai (2012) zany aesthetics and labor |
| 037000 | What changes when the audience remakes and distributes the joke? | Shifman (2013) internet memes; Phillips (2015) trolling and media systems; satire, irony, context collapse and shifting targets |

**Taxonomy warning:** These traditions are not seven mutually exclusive theories. The sequence distinguishes explanations of cognitive response, semantic architecture, socially situated practice, political critique, philosophical interpretation, performance movements, and digital media circulation. Some are empirical models; some are interpretive frameworks; some are historical artistic scenes. No lecture claims to provide a formula for being funny.

## Editorial constraints met

- Every lecture stands alone, begins with an original, audible example, develops one line of inquiry at listener pace, explains rival frameworks and failure cases, and ends with a usable writing/analysis exercise.
- Spoken content contains no inline citation syntax, equations, or URLs. Companions carry definitions and editorial caveats. All fictional scenes are original and marked as teaching examples.
- The author/source metadata uses named scholarly sources with bibliographic links and marks publisher-only summaries and abstracts as such, rather than implying access to inaccessible full texts.
- All outputs are UTF-8 text only. No changes to central reader/manifest registries, common curriculum documents, or other track namespaces.
