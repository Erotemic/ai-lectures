# Agent A — Writers in Argument, 020000–023000

**Status:** Four complete standalone narrated lectures; the shared `tts.toml`, local overrides, track discovery, and other agents' files are outside this overlay.

The goal is not to construct one false universal writing method. This sequence studies four historically situated interventions in how fiction can work:

| Lecture | Core question | Difference worth retaining |
|---|---|---|
| 020000, Le Guin | Why must a story be an arrow of heroic conflict? | Social relations and maintenance can be narrative engines; conflict remains possible |
| 021000, Gardner | Does fiction owe readers a morally serious vision? | Separates craft of immersive clarity from controversial ethical universals |
| 022000, Barth | What can a writer do when familiar forms are already familiar? | Form can show its own making, provided the experiment generates discovery |
| 023000, Saunders | How does a reader's understanding change sentence by sentence? | Revising for unfolding curiosity need not reduce a story to a fixed formula |

## Editorial method

These are **comparative interpretations**, not a record of a four-way historical debate. The writers did not produce these four lectures jointly or necessarily respond directly to every other argument presented here. Original bathhouse examples are invented teaching material, not descriptions of their published plots.

The sources are per-lecture sidecars and track-local `metadata/sources.json`. Historical claims are not to be conflated with the lecturer's own synthesis. Companion notes are not narrated. Lecture length is determined by the developed material; measured word counts in the manifest are descriptive indexing fields, not policy thresholds or targets.
