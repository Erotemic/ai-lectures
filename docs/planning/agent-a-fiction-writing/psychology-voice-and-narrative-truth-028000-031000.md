# Agent A — Psychology, Voice, and Narrative Truth (028000–031000)

## Scope

Four completed, standalone studies continuing the twentieth- and twenty-first-century fiction schools sequence. They analyze how literary forms claim access to minds, belief, lives, and rival voices. The examples are invented demonstrations rather than reconstructions from the named authors' books.

- **028000, Psychological Realism and the Opacity of Other Minds:** Dorrit Cohn's distinct modes for representing consciousness, free indirect style, and Zadie Smith's critique of a dominant lyrical realism. Four rewrites of an inheritance scene show different narrator/character distances.
- **029000, Irony, New Sincerity, and the Risk of Belief:** Wallace's 1993 historical argument, Kelly's 2010 critical formulation, and a reunion speech that tests whether wit can coexist with moral risk. Avoids treating all postmodernism as cynical or all sincere writing as spontaneous.
- **030000, Autofiction, Documentary Form, and the Contract of Truth:** Lejeune (1975), Doubrovsky and *Fils* (1977), documentary evidence, memory, disclosure and consent. The notebook exercise separates factual claims, interpretation and invention without asserting a unified contemporary autofiction definition.
- **031000, Polyphony, Dialogism, and the Novel of Voices:** Bakhtin's 1929 and revised 1963 Dostoevsky books, social language, and independent conceptual perspectives. A failing elevator provides one event with rival vocabularies and consequences. Polyphony is not treated as mere narrator count.

## Editorial and factual boundaries

- Every manuscript introduces its problem without depending on a previous episode.
- Length is determined by the material. Narration counts are descriptive metadata only, not quotas, targets, or acceptance criteria.
- Historical claims have traceable source IDs, and the reading list is in track-local source metadata. The manuscript text does not pass off a reconstructed conversation as a historic quotation.
- Original fiction scenes are clearly interpretive teaching devices; the explicit claims made by a named scholar are distinguished from the lecture author's applications.
- No other track, shared reader, TTS config, or global editorial policy is modified.

## Future possible directions (not drafted)

Experiments in time and fragmentation; contemporary short-story traditions; the influence of translation on fictional voice; the novel of ideas; unreliable narrators and testimony.
