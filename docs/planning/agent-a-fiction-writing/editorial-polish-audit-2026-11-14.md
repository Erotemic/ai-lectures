# Agent A fiction-writing editorial and reference audit (2026-11-14)

## Scope and method

Reviewed the complete 28-manuscript Agent A fiction track (001000–027000, including 007500), the track index, README, 28 per-lecture metadata sidecars, and the track-local reference registry. Verified selected disputed bibliographic facts externally against author, library, scholarly-publisher and documentary records. Original fictional teaching examples are authorial illustrations, not historical evidence.

Changes are restricted to `content/fiction-writing/` and `docs/planning/agent-a-fiction-writing/`. They do not alter shared TTS settings, lesson-duration policy, or any other agent's work. Measured word counts and read-time estimates are descriptive, not editorial requirements.

## Corrections implemented

1. **001000 and 002000:** replaced the Wikipedia citation for Forster with a linked 1927 primary text hosted by Project Gutenberg; changed the metadata pointers accordingly.
2. **003000:** added *Always Coming Home* (1985) as an actual literary example of constructed social worlds rather than relying solely on a generic definition of worldbuilding.
3. **009000:** replaced a general claim about Carver/Lish editing with an edition-specific account of *Beginners* and *What We Talk About When We Talk About Love*. Added the editors' Library of America textual note in the companion bibliography and sidecar.
4. **016000:** changed the historically overbroad title 'Invention of Interior Life' to 'Forms of Interior Life'; the lecture already explains that earlier authors represented interiority.
5. **018000:** repaired two machine-readable historical-claim anchors that did not actually appear in the narrated manuscript.
6. **020000 and 024000:** distinguished the 1986 date associated with Le Guin's essay, its documented appearance in *Women of Vision* in 1988, and its inclusion in *Dancing at the Edge of the World* in 1989. Added bibliography pointers and claim-level records.
7. **021000 and 025000:** supplied an authoritative first-edition catalog reference for Gardner's posthumous *The Art of Fiction* (1984), distinguishing it from a contemporary 1983 pre-publication review.
8. **027000:** replaced the mostly print-only further-reading list with directly linked primary and publisher/catalog resources.
9. **README, `track.json`, metadata:** updated titles, reference IDs, historical claims, and measured narration counts; all 28 lecture IDs and filenames are retained.

## Audit results and boundaries

- 28 lecture manuscripts have manifest entries, sidecars, companion references, and tracked sources.
- Reference IDs in sidecars and claim-level metadata were checked against `metadata/sources.json`. Two unmatched claim text anchors were corrected.
- Source claims are not treated as direct quotes unless the manuscript actually quotes the source. The comparisons among authors remain explicitly the lecturer's constructions, not historical debates among these authors.
- Historical research was targeted, not an exhaustive, full-text verification of every interpretive sentence or live-URL test for all reference links. Some sources are paywalled bibliographic abstracts rather than full-text evidence; the descriptions should not imply otherwise.
- This polishing pass adds no lecture-length minimum or maximum, no additional global tests, and no new lectures.
