# Pilot: What Would Have Happened Otherwise?

**Status:** substantive draft; integrated with the audio reader; not yet
synthesized or spot-listened.

The first concrete lecture of *The World Reconsidered* is
`draft-stages/tts-world-reconsidered/001000-what-causes-something.md`.
It is a standalone question-centered teaching narrative, with a companion
showing the exact assumptions and historical tables, and eight linked readings.

It makes the broader curriculum's first central question practical: how can
we distinguish a causal effect from an observed sequence or association?

**Core worked example:** synthetic two-stratum recovery table where treatment
has a 10-point advantage within each severity stratum but a lower crude
recovery rate because selection into treatment depends on severity. The
lecture then introduces standardization, its assumptions, and why adjustment
can fail when a variable is a mediator or collider.

**History:** Snow’s 1854 Broad Street investigation and water-company comparison
are treated as separate forms of evidence; the claim that handle removal ended
the epidemic is explicitly corrected from the primary source. Semmelweis and
Bradford Hill's 1940s clinical trial provide the human and institutional
context for interventions and controlled comparisons.

**Editorial constraints:** no prior-lecture dependency, no invented historical
dialogue, no unexplained symbols in speech, no citation URLs in the spoken
region. Content is authoritative at the specified scope, but the recorded
human stories and causal assumptions should remain open to future source-based
revision. Synthesized audio and listening feedback have not yet been collected.
