# Agent B modern-world: claim-to-source attribution protocol

This file documents the strengthened editorial method used for lectures 015000–018000. It is track-specific, not a modification of the repository-wide style guide.

1. **One verifiable ledger entry per factual spoken paragraph.** A row names the exact paragraph by an anchored substring and supplies direct document URLs, not just book titles. A row may have multiple supporting items when the paragraph combines independent facts.
2. **Specific locators.** Prefer a document title, section heading, named primary text, article section, or known page range. Never fabricate a page number for a book that has not been opened.
3. **Source-access honesty.** `direct_page` and `direct_article` refer to text actually inspected in public web results; `abstract_only` and `catalog_only` support only their published description; `excerpt_only` means a partial excerpt was available. These labels do not assert exhaustive review.
4. **Different evidence types answer different questions.** A treaty supports its terms, not its complete implementation. A constitutional preamble reports state intentions, not suffrage equality. A climate reconstruction estimates drought, not local relief policy. A museum object may establish a historical practice while its collection history exposes conquest-related gaps.
5. **Dates and contested interpretations.** When material conflicts, look for corroboration from an independent source, qualify or omit the detail, and record the conflict. Example: one Columbia source introduction has “1856” for the Taiping rising, while the dedicated Columbia chronology dates the rebellion 1850/51–1864. The latter is used for dates, the former for its primary land-program text.
6. **No invented real-world testimony.** Conceptual scenes must be marked as invented; a historical witness must have a traceable document, date, translation and provenance.
7. **No citation quota.** A sufficient citation means a reader can follow a specific substantive claim back to reliable evidence. Citation count and manuscript duration are not targets.

## Verification limitations

This process checks citation IDs, article/publisher pages and known visible excerpts, and runs structured mechanical tests. It is not a full scholarly peer review. For sources that require subscription access, summaries are constrained to publisher abstracts. Future editorial checks should obtain full texts or replace weak citations before advancing unusually specific claims.

## Follow-up quality check

Read each cited paragraph and ask: does the linked source actually state this, or is the sentence (a) a defensible deduction, (b) a contemporary political assertion, (c) a debated historiographic interpretation, or (d) beyond what the cited source documents? Rewrite or qualify category (d) and make (a)–(c) explicit.
