## Directions for future substantive lectures (not yet indexed)

- **003000 — Trade worlds before and after the Portuguese entry.** Gujarat, the Swahili coast, Malacca, Aceh, Ottoman Red Sea interests, taxation, alliances, seasonal sailing, and the limits of treating 'the Indian Ocean' as one market. Distinguish direct contact from material evidence passed through intermediaries.
- **004000 — Land empires and borderlands.** The Ottoman, Safavid, and Mughal projects, with careful founding dates; wars, diplomacy, religious plurality, fiscal organization, local intermediaries, and reasons to resist the gunpowder-empire shorthand.
- **005000 — Silver, mines, and the price of connection.** The Andes, forced labor, economic institutions, oceanic shipping and Asian demand, including the risk of reading later global networks back into 1500.
- **006000 — World religions and reform in motion.** Catholic and Protestant reformations, Safavid Shi'ism, the Sikh tradition, Sufi institutions, Buddhist and Hindu networks, Indigenous traditions, and local agency in coercive conversion and voluntary adoption.
- **007000 — Captivity, plantations, and different forms of bondage.** Changing African, American, European, and Asian labor regimes; resist a timeless global 'slavery' category and reconstruct who was constrained, by whom, and how.
- **008000 — Knowledge institutions across regions.** Printing and manuscripts, examinations, Islamic scholarship, navigation, astronomy, medicine, Indigenous ecological knowledge, and the politics of what an archive preserves.

These are **prospective questions**, not completed manuscripts, duration requirements, or a fixed obligatory sequence.
