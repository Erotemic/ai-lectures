# Deep Time evidence map: human origins and dispersals (022000–025000)

Research snapshot: 14 November 2026. Four delivered manuscripts. This plan is an evidence audit and later revision target, not a substitute for original publications. `reference_recorded` in metadata identifies a bibliographic association rather than an independent passage-by-passage verification or demonstration of scholarly consensus.

## Course sequence and learning commitments

1. **022000 — How We Date Human Origins.** Teach stratigraphic order, minimum/maximum bounds, argon/thermoluminescence/OSL/ESR/radiocarbon assumptions, error-bar interpretation and fossil taxonomic placement. Running examples: Jebel Irhoud (2017), Omo I and revised tuff correlation (2022), and Thomas Quarry I (2026). No assertion that the oldest known fossil uniquely establishes species origin.
2. **023000 — Hominins, Tools, and Adaptation.** Teach bipedality as an anatomical reconstruction; Lomekwi 3 and Nyayanga as material and chronological observations; knapping and bone marks as physical traces; uncertainty about toolmaker taxon; Dmanisi morphological variation; Bianfu Cave (2026) proteomic attribution. Avoid a linear-progress species ladder or attributing whole assemblages to a known individual.
3. **024000 — What Ancient DNA Can and Cannot Tell Us.** Teach authenticity of fragmentary molecules, negative controls, damage profiles, mapping bias, allele-sharing/model tests, recombination clocks, reference sampling and ethical constraints. Make distinction between biological ancestry and language/culture explicit. Discuss Ranis/Zlatý kůň and Belgian/French Neanderthal genomic results.
4. **025000 — How People Peopled the World.** Teach site-date minimum arrival bounds, paleoshoreline reconstruction, Sahul chronology alternatives and ancestral-branching model assumptions, White Sands chronology updates through October 2026, coastal versus interior routes, and missing-lineage scenarios.

## Explicit methodological distinctions

- **Age of a dated sample vs age of the target event:** volcanic mineral, sediment bleaching, fossil death, genome coalescence, lineage split, admixture and first arrival are different events.
- **Directly observed vs inferred:** an isotope ratio, bone marker, footprint, peptide or read alignment is a measurement. An ancestor, maker, route, settlement wave or social behavior is an inference from that measurement plus a causal model.
- **Absolute and relative claims:** a layer above a fossil yields a minimum fossil age (in years before present), conditional on no disturbance. The oldest site found is a lower bound on occupation duration, not a declaration of initial arrival.
- **Alternative models require differentiating predictions:** for Sahul, look for stratigraphically secure archaeological samples and independently calibrated ancient genomic signals; for the Americas, sample inundated paleoshorelines and improved ice-corridor ecologies. Repeatedly adding extinct waves as unconstrained hidden variables is not evidence.
- **Avoid using genetic ancestry to assign modern identities:** neither ancient genome components nor migration arrows identify the language, social group or self-understanding of a past person. Sample provenance and descendant-community authority must be respected.
- **Sparse sampling is a scientific parameter:** warm-climate DNA loss, underwater sites, taxonomic ambiguity and the uneven geography of excavations shape what can be discovered. Quantify sampling uncertainty when possible; do not present missing archives as observed absences.

## High-value follow-up research gaps

- Detailed critique of dates and deposit models at earliest Homo and early sapiens sites; compare individual site Bayesian stratigraphic models and reworking tests.
- Tools-per-species attribution: survey taphonomic studies that contrast purposeful knapping with geofacts and compare site-scale temporal averaging.
- Population graph nonidentifiability: a follow-on lecture could work a small allele-sharing example showing multiple graphs with similar fitted data.
- Sahul arrival timing: place archaeology and mito/nuclear/genome models on one shared event-time diagram with assumption annotations rather than forcing their dates to agree.
- 2026 White Sands: review latest pollen radiocarbon calibration, stratigraphic horizons and paleoclimate inference for the 23.5–21.7 ka claim.
- Indigenous archaeological partnerships, culturally governed collections, and ethical limits on ancient-DNA destructive sampling are substantial topics for deeper treatment.
