# Fiction Writing and Worldbuilding: a two-year lecture-and-practice curriculum

**Status, 14 November 2026:** opening lectures 001000–003000 authored and packaged. The remaining titles are proposals, not promised or existing manuscripts.

## Teaching objective

Give a writer the ability to develop original fiction with characters whose decisions matter, scenes that causally alter the work, structures that sustain narrative attention, dialogue with readable subtext, worlds that generate human circumstances, and revisions guided by real reader response. Useful to prose, theater and narrative games, but not reducible to LLM prompting, screenwriting formulas, or human-centric game editors. All lectures must be self-contained audio-first explanations, not a sequence of instructions requiring earlier listening.

The earlier two-year curriculum had six broad Year 1 areas—fundamentals, character, structure, scene/subtext, reading as a writer, workshop—and six broad Year 2 areas—long-form architecture, advanced character and dialogue, worldbuilding and theme, genre traditions, revision, thesis workshop. Keep those organizing commitments here. Progress from exercises in scenes toward a sustained work. Practice is a support for lectures, not a substitute for teaching.

## Editorial requirements

A recording should develop one mechanism carefully, with an original worked example that changes over the lecture, a contrasting failure case, independent listening context, and a display-only companion. Treat the major workshop exercises as ways to falsify or improve a craft hypothesis, not as proof that a single recipe makes a story work. Source claims about the history of fiction and named authors; do not attribute invented illustrative dialogue to real works. Do not quote protected literary passages at length. Preserve room for experimental, episodic, lyric and nonlinear fiction; dramatic craft principles are analytical lenses rather than universal requirements.
