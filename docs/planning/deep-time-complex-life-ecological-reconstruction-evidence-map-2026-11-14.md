# Deep Time 018000–021000: Complex Cells, Animal Diversification, Extinctions, and Ecosystems

Research snapshot: 14 November 2026. These four manuscripts continue the chronological curriculum from universal common ancestry through the restructuring of marine and terrestrial communities. Manuscripts are audio-first; equations, references, source caveats and confidence classification sit in the display-only companions and source metadata.

## Editorial position

Three histories intersect in this arc: phylogeny reconstructed from living genomes, the incomplete archive of fossils in rocks, and inferred environmental mechanisms such as oxygenation, warming and sediment dynamics. Explain what each archive can establish independently. A gene-family age does not directly date a nucleus; a fossil's first occurrence establishes a minimum age for its lineage; a chemical anomaly requires a causal pathway to explain an extinction. Treat convergence between independent archives as a reason for confidence, not as permission to erase their individual qualifications.

Use a qualitative confidence scale attached to individual claims: **directly observed**, **strongly supported historical reconstruction**, **model-dependent reconstruction**, **actively contested mechanism**, or **presently inaccessible**. None of these is an arbitrary numerical confidence percentage.

## Dated research findings worth retaining

**Eukaryogenesis:** Imachi et al. (2020) cultured an Asgard archaeon with syntrophic physiology and cellular protrusions. Tobiasson et al. (2026) found dominant Asgard contribution to conserved core eukaryotic systems through phylogenomic gene-origin tests. A distinct ancient gene-duplication clock analysis favored host complexity arising before mitochondrial integration. These are related but non-identical analyses; neither reconstructs a physically observed transition. The fossil-habitat study of Lechte et al. (2026) concerns approximately 1.75–1.4 Ga eukaryote environments rather than the exact origin date.

**Multicellularity and animals:** Miao et al. (2024) described the approximately 1.635 Ga filamentous Qingshania; the multicellular-eukaryote classification is a reasoned interpretation of the preserved cells. Bobrovskiy et al. (2018) reported steroid biomarkers supporting an animal affinity for Dickinsonia, with later studies continuing to examine biological origins of associated lipids. Li and colleagues (2026) reported Jiangchuan fossils bearing late-Ediacaran and animal-like characteristics. Distinguish the date of the fossil from the date of origination of its lineage.

**Extinction causation:** Alvarez et al. (1980) measured iridium and proposed an impact; later physical observations, particularly the Chicxulub crater and impact structures, reinforced the explanation. A 2026 Chicxulub drilling synthesis helps connect crater material to the boundary. For the end-Permian crisis, Kaiho et al. (2026) reported mercury-isotope variation interpreted as eruptive pulses; the observational measurements and their volcanic-source interpretation should remain distinct. Recovery patterns are also a different question from the cause of collapse.

**Ecological transformation:** A 2024 Cambridge/Cardiff forest discovery records a ~390 Ma landscape with early trees, sedimentary context and root/branch evidence. A 2026 spore-character study proposed a later 457–450 Ma molecular-clock estimate for origin of land plants, revising the use of older spore characters as calibrations. This does not make previously described spore specimens vanish. Research published in 2025 and 2026 addresses the staged radiation of flowering plants and the multiple phases of insect interactions with non-flowering plants.

## Questions for future comparison or revision

1. Does increasing sampling of living Asgard archaea alter placement of eukaryotic ancestors or inferred order of host innovations? Can studies reconcile gene-tree and fossil clocks?
2. How do preservation and depositional differences change apparently abrupt species appearances? Which Ediacaran fossils have independent anatomical or molecular biomarkers?
3. Do high-resolution end-Permian proxy series identify regionally synchronized stress intervals? What alternative sources or cycling processes might produce similar isotope excursions?
4. How do spore-character reconstructions influence minimum and maximum fossil constraints? Which older grains remain evidence of land plants under the revised interpretation?
5. Where are ancient food-web edges directly evidenced, and where have they been imported from living analogues? Avoid presenting a complete reconstructed network as a collection of observations.
