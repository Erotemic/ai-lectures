# History expansion: Grace Hopper and Ada Lovelace

**Baseline:** the History track with the prior 32 portraits, including `031000-runge` and `032000-kutta`.

**Additions:** Two standalone portraits `033000` and `034000`, in a new `computing` section. No previous identifiers or existing transcripts are changed. Both presentations are text-only: a TTS-spoken narrative and a display-only technical companion in the same Markdown document.

## Grace Hopper (`033000`)

The portrait covers the 1934 doctorate under Øystein Ore, archimedean Newton–Hopper polygons and polynomial irreducibility; teaching at Vassar; entry into the Naval Reserve; Harvard Mark I and technical documentation; programming and subroutine libraries at UNIVAC; the historical A-0 assembler/linker/loader distinction; FLOW-MATIC, COBOL and CODASYL; Navy standardization, numerical representation and reliability.

Teaching examples: Eisenstein's criterion as illustrative background (explicitly not Hopper's original result), Horner evaluation, reusable routine labor model, loop invariants, decimal business arithmetic and nanosecond distance.

## Ada Lovelace (`034000`)

The portrait covers her mathematical education, Mary Somerville and Augustus De Morgan; the Difference Engine's finite differences; the proposed Analytical Engine's store/mill/control; the 1843 translation of Menabrea and Notes A–G; the Bernoulli-number example; symbolic representation, music and the later creativity question; and the collaborative evidence from Babbage letters and research on program priority.

Teaching examples: finite differences for squares, store/mill pseudocode, Bernoulli recurrence and its first terms, loop invariants and finite-precision counterexample. The modern recurrence is identified as an explanatory reconstruction rather than a transcription of Note G.
